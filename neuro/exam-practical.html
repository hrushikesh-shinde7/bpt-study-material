ExamThe Neuro practical exam: long case, short case, spots and journal

The Neuro practical is 80 marks: a long case (45), a short case with two skills (20), five spots (10) and the journal (5), marked on knowledge, hands-on skill and attitude. This pack follows that scheme and links each skill to the page that explains it.

Syllabus
Practical syllabus: clinical skills (sensory testing and re-education, muscle testing and re-education, electrical modalities, tone management, posture, transfers, functional re-education, gait, coordination, balance, fitness, outcome measures) and documentation of 8 cases with ICF
Priority
Essential. 80 practical marks plus 20 internal assessment.
Asked as
Long case 45 Short case 20 Spots 10Journal 5
Usual split
Long case 10 + 10 + 10 + 15; short case 2 × 10; spots 5 × 2; journal 5

1The marking scheme

PartWhat is markedMarks
Long case(a) Subjective and physical examination 10; (b) evaluation and physiotherapy diagnosis (ICF) 10; (c) plan of care and goal setting 10; (d) demonstration of one important test and one treatment on the patient 15. Marked in cognitive, psychomotor and affective domains45
Short caseDemonstration of two physiotherapy intervention skills for effective patient management, 10 each20
Spots5 spots, 2 marks each, 3 minutes per spot: EMG and nerve conduction studies, orthoses, prostheses, neurological assessment and scales10
JournalDocumentation of cases with assessment, evaluation, diagnosis (ICF), prognosis, intervention and outcome5

Internal assessment adds 20 marks. In the terminal and preliminary exams the spots carry 15 marks (5 × 3) and the journal is not marked.

2Long case: assessment proforma

Subjective

  • Demographics, dominance, occupation, family, home (stairs, toilet type), referral.
  • Chief complaints in the patient's words; history of present illness (onset, progression, investigations, treatment); past, personal, family and social history; drugs; red flags.
  • The patient's own goals.

Objective

  • General: vital signs, consciousness (GCS), posture, deformity, skin, aids.
  • Higher functions, cranial nerves, sensation, tone (Modified Ashworth), power (MRC or voluntary control), reflexes, coordination, balance (Berg, TUG), gait, function (Barthel or FIM) (see assessment and scales).
  • Condition-specific: ISNCSCI in SCI, Hoehn and Yahr in PD, GMFCS in CP, muscle chart in LMN lesions.

Then

  • Investigations seen; ICF table and functional diagnosis; problem list; goals; plan; outcome measures.

3Presenting the long case (3–5 minutes)

  1. One sentence: "Mr A, 54, bank manager, right MCA infarct 2 weeks ago, now…"
  2. Key positive findings in ICF order: body functions and structures, activities, participation, environmental and personal factors.
  3. The functional diagnosis in one line.
  4. Two or three priority problems with short- and long-term goals.
  5. The plan, then demonstrate the test and the treatment you chose (part d, 15 marks): explain, position, perform, check the response.

Choose a test and treatment that fit the main problem (for example the Modified Ashworth Scale and sit-to-stand training in stroke; ISNCSCI key muscles and a sliding-board transfer in paraplegia).

4Short case: demonstrating skills (2 × 10 marks)

Skills from the syllabus

  • Sensory testing and sensory re-education; muscle testing or voluntary control and muscle re-education.
  • Electrical modalities for re-education or pain (see electrotherapy).
  • Management of tone; facilitatory and inhibitory techniques (see tone and approaches).
  • Postural assessment and correction; transfer training; functional re-education; gait assessment and training (see transfers and gait).
  • Coordination testing and training; balance strategies (see balance).
  • Fitness training; outcome measures and quality-of-life questionnaires.

Steps for any skill demonstration

  1. Introduce yourself, explain, take consent; check precautions (blood pressure, skin, pain).
  2. Position the patient and yourself; drape; guard.
  3. Demonstrate with clear commands; give feedback; show progression.
  4. Re-check the response and state the dose (sets, repetitions, frequency) and why.

5Spots (5 × 2 marks, 3 minutes each)

SpotWhat to say

OrthosisName (AFO type, KAFO, cock-up, knuckle-bender), parts, principle, indications, one precaution (see orthoses)
ProsthesisLevel (below-knee, above-knee), parts (socket, suspension, pylon, foot), the usual indication (for example amputation for a diabetic foot), one gait problem

6The journal (5 marks)

  • Eight cases using the ICF model: four upper motor neuron (stroke, SCI, brain injury, degenerative or demyelinating disease), two lower motor neuron (peripheral nerve or plexus injury, GBS), two paediatric (CP, myopathy, meningocele).
  • For each: assessment, evaluation, diagnosis (ICF), prognosis, intervention and outcome, with measures at the start and end.
  • Neat, complete, signed and up to date; the examiner may ask about any case in it.

7The affective domain: what examiners watch

  • Introduction, consent and explanation; respect, dignity and draping (see caring).
  • Safety: guarding, gait belt, brakes on, checking blood pressure and skin, stopping for warning signs.
  • Hand hygiene; tidy equipment; thanking the patient.
  • Calm, clear answers; saying "I would check with the team" rather than guessing.

Last two weeks

  • Practise the assessment proforma on classmates until it takes under 20 minutes.
  • Rehearse presenting three typical cases aloud (stroke, SCI, CP) in ICF order.
  • Practise each short-case skill with a partner, saying the commands and doses aloud.
  • Revise spots with real reports, orthoses and scales; complete and check the journal.
