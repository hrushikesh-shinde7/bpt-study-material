TP8Lifting and handling, wheelchairs and adaptive devices

Wheelchair prescription was part of the S26 long case (a boy with Duchenne muscular dystrophy from a rural area), and lifting, wheelchairs and aids appear in the management of almost every severe case. Fit the device to the person, their body, their home and their roads, train them to use it, and review it as things change.

My revision

1Safe lifting and patient handling

  • Assess before you move anyone: the task, the person (weight, ability, pain, lines and tubes, cognition), the environment (space, floor, height of surfaces) and the equipment available.
  • Plan and communicate: explain, agree on commands ("ready, steady, stand"), and let the patient do as much as possible.
  • Use equipment instead of lifting: slide sheets for moving in bed, transfer boards, stand-aids, and hoists for dependent patients.
  • Body mechanics: wide base, bend the knees and hips, keep the load close, keep the back upright, turn with the feet not the trunk, work at a good height.
  • Never lift under the arms (the "drag lift"): it injures the patient's shoulder, especially a hemiplegic shoulder, and the carer's back.

Classic answer vs current guidance: lifting technique

Classic

Teach correct lifting technique to prevent back injury in carers and staff.

Current

Training in lifting technique alone did not prevent back pain in workers (Cochrane, Verbeek et al., 2011). Reduce manual lifting with equipment (hoists, slide sheets, boards) and plan each move.

Write in the exam

"Risk assessment, use of handling equipment, patient participation, and good body mechanics; avoid manual lifting of dependent patients."

Transfers by level of help

  • Independent: stand-pivot, or a sliding (transfer) board for paraplegia.
  • One helper: stand-pivot towards the stronger side (hemiplegia), blocking the weak knee and guiding at the pelvis.
  • Two helpers or more support: slide sheets and a board; a stand-aid if the person can bear some weight.
  • Dependent: a mechanical hoist with the right sling. More on transfers: transfers and gait.

2The wheelchair

Parts

  • Frame (folding or rigid); seat and backrest (sling or solid); cushion.
  • Rear wheels with push rims; front castors; brakes; tyres (solid or pneumatic).
  • Armrests (fixed, removable, desk-length); footrests or elevating legrests; footplates.
  • Push handles; anti-tip bars; lap belt and postural supports.

Types

TypeBest for
Standard folding manual chairOccasional or short-term use; easy transport
Lightweight rigid (active) chairActive long-term users with good arms (paraplegia)
Attendant-propelled chairUsers who cannot self-propel; small rear wheels
Hemi-height or one-arm drive chairHemiplegia: low seat to propel with the good foot, or a double rim or lever on the good side
Tilt-in-space and reclining chairsPoor trunk and head control, pressure relief, fatigue (MND, high tetraplegia, severe CP)
Powered chairWeak arms or long distances (high tetraplegia, DMD, MND); joystick, chin or breath controls
Rough-terrain or hand-propelled tricycleRural and outdoor use in India

3Wheelchair prescription (S26)

The WHO eight steps of wheelchair provision

  1. Referral and appointment
  2. Assessment
  3. Prescription (selecting the chair)
  4. Funding and ordering
  5. Product preparation
  6. Fitting and adjusting
  7. User training
  8. Follow-up, maintenance and repairs

Assessment

  • The person: diagnosis and prognosis (stable or progressive), sitting balance, trunk and head control, arm strength, contractures and deformities (pelvic obliquity, scoliosis), skin and pressure risk, continence, breathing, cognition and vision, body size and growth.
  • Life and place: home access and space, toilet, school or work, terrain (rough rural roads), transport, who pushes or helps, budget, local repair services.

Measurements (person seated, on the cushion to be used)

DimensionRule of thumbIf wrong
Seat widthHip width plus about 5 cmToo wide: leaning and hard to propel; too narrow: pressure on the hips
Seat depthButtock-to-back-of-knee length minus about 5 cmToo deep: slides into a slumped posture; too short: high thigh pressure
Seat and footplate heightThighs level; footplate clears the ground by about 5 cmFootplate too high: pressure under the buttocks
Back heightBelow the shoulder blades for active users; higher, with head support, for poor trunk controlToo low: poor trunk support; too high: blocks arm movement
Armrest heightElbow height (arm by the side, elbow at 90°) plus about 2.5 cmToo low: slumping; too high: shoulders hunched

Then choose the type, the seating (solid seat and back, lateral trunk supports, hip guides, pommel, headrest, lap belt at the hips) and a pressure-relieving cushion. The full DMD answer is on the DMD page.

4Wheelchair modifications by condition

ConditionModifications
HemiplegiaHemi-height seat; propel with the good arm and foot; arm trough or lap tray for the weak arm; brake extension on the weak side
ParaplegiaLight rigid frame, low back, good cushion, removable armrests for board transfers
Tetraplegia (C5–C6)Push-rim projections or coated rims, gloves, higher back, power chair for long distances; C4 and above: power chair with chin or breath control and tilt
Cerebral palsyCustom seating: pelvic belt, hip guides, pommel, lateral supports, headrest, tray; tilt for poor head control
DMD and other progressive weaknessManual chair for distance early; power chair with tilt, recline, elevating legrests and spinal supports later
MNDTilt-in-space, head support, plan the power chair early because the need changes quickly

5Pressure care and wheelchair skills

  • Cushion: foam, gel or air cell, matched to the risk; check it is not bottomed out.
  • Weight shifts: every 15–30 minutes, holding each for at least a minute: push-up, forward lean, side lean, or tilting the chair.
  • Skin checks twice daily with a mirror; manage moisture and posture.
  • Skills to teach: brakes and footrests, propulsion with long smooth strokes, turning, ramps, kerbs (back-wheel balance with help, then alone), transfers in and out, falls and getting back in, folding and loading, simple maintenance (tyres, brakes).

6Adaptive devices and home changes

Walking aids

  • Sticks (single-point, tripod, quadripod), crutches (axillary, elbow), walkers (standard frame, rollator).
  • Height: the handgrip at the level of the wrist crease (or greater trochanter) with the arm by the side, giving about 20–30° of elbow bend; axillary crutch tops about 5 cm below the armpit, weight taken through the hands.
  • A stick goes in the hand opposite the weak leg.

Daily-living aids

  • Eating: built-up or angled cutlery, plate guard, non-slip mat, rocker knife.
  • Dressing: button hook, sock aid, long-handled shoe horn, elastic laces, Velcro.
  • Reaching: long-handled reacher.
  • Bathroom: raised toilet seat or commode chair, grab rails, shower chair or bath bench, non-slip mats.
  • Communication and control: communication boards or devices, call bells, environmental controls for severe disability.

Home modifications

  • Ramps: no steeper than 1 in 12 for short rises (gentler for longer ramps), at least 1200 mm wide, with handrails on both sides at 760–900 mm (Indian accessibility standards, 2021).
  • Wide doorways and turning space; rails by the toilet and bed; remove loose rugs; good lighting; a ground-floor room where possible.

Getting devices in India

The ADIP scheme (Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment) supplies aids such as wheelchairs, tricycles and callipers free or at subsidised cost to eligible people with a disability certificate (40% or more), through ALIMCO, national institutes and NGOs; income limits apply.

Answer skeleton: wheelchair prescription (part of a long case)

  1. Assess the person (stage, trunk, arms, deformity, skin, breathing, growth) and the setting (home, school, terrain, carer, budget).
  2. Choose the type and its features, with reasons.
  3. Measure (seat width, depth, height, back and armrest height) and specify seating and cushion.
  4. Fit, train the user and family, and plan review and repairs.

Test yourself

3 mList the parts of a wheelchair.

Frame; seat and backrest; cushion; rear wheels with push rims; front castors; brakes; armrests; footrests or legrests with footplates; push handles; anti-tip bars; lap belt.

3 mWrite the principles of safe patient handling.

Assess the task, the person, the environment and the equipment; plan and communicate; let the patient help; use equipment (slide sheets, boards, hoists) instead of lifting; good body mechanics (wide base, load close, bend knees, no twisting); never lift under the arms.

7 mExplain the steps involved in wheelchair prescription. (S26)

WHO steps: referral, assessment, prescription, funding and ordering, preparation, fitting, user training, follow-up. Assessment: diagnosis and progression, trunk and head control, arm strength, deformity, skin, breathing, cognition, growth; home, school, terrain, carer, budget. Prescription: type (manual or power, folding or rigid, tilt), seating (solid seat and back, lateral supports, hip guides, pommel, headrest, belt), cushion. Measurements: seat width = hips + 5 cm; depth = buttock to knee − 5 cm; footplate 5 cm off the ground; armrest = elbow height + 2.5 cm; back height by trunk control. Fitting and training in use, transfers, pressure relief and care; review with growth or disease progression.

LAQPractice: a 30-year-old man with a C6 complete tetraplegia (AIS A) is ready for discharge to a first-floor flat. Plan his wheelchair, transfers and adaptive devices. (5 + 5 + 5)

Wheelchair (5): light manual chair with push-rim projections or coated rims and gloves for indoors, power chair for distance; high back, good air or gel cushion; tilt or a recline if he cannot do weight shifts; measured to fit. Transfers (5): sliding-board transfers with assistance using a level transfer and removable armrests; a hoist for car or floor transfers; carer training in safe handling. Devices (5): universal cuff and built-up cutlery, tenodesis use, wrist splints, shower chair, raised commode, pressure-relieving mattress; a ramp at 1:12 with rails or a lift, wider doors, ground-level access; ADIP scheme for equipment.
