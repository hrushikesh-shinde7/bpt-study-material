4Quality of life scales, independence measures and other outcome measures

Scales came up in 6 papers as 3-mark questions: the Glasgow Coma Scale four times, the Modified Ashworth Scale twice, and the ASIA scale and Hoehn and Yahr stages once each, and every long-case answer is stronger with a named measure. Pick a measure for each ICF level you are treating, score it at the start and again later, and write the numbers in your goals.

My revision

1Why and how to use outcome measures

  • Purposes: describe the problem and its severity, predict outcome, set measurable goals, show change after treatment, and communicate with the team.
  • Match the ICF level: body functions (tone, strength, motor control, cognition), activities (balance, walking, self-care) and participation and quality of life.
  • Use them properly: standard instructions, equipment and scoring; same tester and conditions on retest; record the date and score.
  • Put the numbers into goals: "Berg from 38 to 46 in 6 weeks".

What makes a good measure

PropertyMeaning
ReliabilityGives the same result when repeated (test–retest) and between testers (inter-rater)
ValidityMeasures what it claims to measure
ResponsivenessDetects real change over time
Minimal clinically important differenceThe smallest change that matters to the patient
Floor and ceiling effectsToo many people score at the lowest or highest possible score, so change cannot show
FeasibilityTime, equipment, training, cost; language and literacy (use validated Indian versions where they exist)

2Independence measures

Barthel index

  • Ten items: feeding, bathing, grooming, dressing, bowels, bladder, toilet use, transfers (bed to chair), mobility on level surfaces, stairs.
  • Items are scored in steps of 5 (0, 5, 10 or 15) by how much help is needed; total 0–100. It weights mobility and continence heavily.
  • Interpretation (Shah et al., 1989): 0–20 total dependence; 21–60 severe; 61–90 moderate; 91–99 slight dependence; 100 independent in these tasks (not free of disability).
  • Quick, valid and widely used, but has a ceiling effect in milder patients.

Functional Independence Measure (FIM)

  • 18 items: 13 motor (self-care, sphincter control, transfers, locomotion) and 5 cognitive (communication, social cognition).
  • Each item 1 (total assistance) to 7 (complete independence); total 18–126.
  • More detailed than Barthel and includes cognition; needs training to score.

Modified Rankin Scale (stroke outcome)

GradeMeaning
0No symptoms
1No significant disability despite symptoms; usual activities
2Slight disability: cannot do all previous activities, but looks after own affairs
3Moderate disability: needs some help, walks without assistance
4Moderately severe: cannot walk or attend to bodily needs without help
5Severe: bedridden, incontinent, needs constant care
6Dead

3Quality of life scales

  • Health-related quality of life is the person's own view of how health affects physical, mental and social well-being; it is asked, not observed.
  • SF-36 (generic): 36 questions in 8 domains: physical functioning, role limits from physical health, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning, role limits from emotional problems, and mental health. Each domain scores 0–100 (higher is better), with physical and mental summary scores.
  • EQ-5D (generic): 5 dimensions (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain or discomfort, anxiety or depression) plus a 0–100 visual scale of overall health.
  • Disease-specific: Stroke Impact Scale; PDQ-39 for Parkinson's disease; MS quality of life scales. They pick up changes that matter in that condition.

5The four scales asked for 3 marks

Glasgow Coma Scale (W16, S19, S20, S26)

ResponseScores
Eye opening (E, 1–4)4 spontaneous; 3 to sound; 2 to pressure; 1 none
Verbal response (V, 1–5)5 oriented; 4 confused; 3 words; 2 sounds; 1 none
Motor response (M, 1–6)6 obeys commands; 5 localises; 4 normal flexion; 3 abnormal flexion; 2 extension; 1 none

Total 3–15: severe 3–8, moderate 9–12, mild 13–15. Report the parts ("E2 V2 M4 = 8"); the motor score predicts outcome best. Used in head injury, stroke, meningitis and any reduced consciousness. Full detail: brain injury.

Modified Ashworth Scale (S19, W21)

GradeMeaning
0No increase in tone
1Slight increase: a catch and release, or minimal resistance at the end of the range
1+Slight increase: a catch followed by minimal resistance through less than half of the remaining range
2More marked increase through most of the range, but the part moves easily
3Considerable increase; passive movement is difficult
4The part is rigid in flexion or extension

Patient relaxed; move the part through its full range in about one second; grade the first movement. Full detail: tone.

ASIA Impairment Scale (S19)

  • A, complete: no sensory or motor function in S4–S5.
  • B, sensory incomplete: sensation but no motor function below the level, including S4–S5.
  • C, motor incomplete: motor function below the level, but less than half of the key muscles below the neurological level reach grade 3.
  • D, motor incomplete: half or more of those key muscles reach grade 3 or more.
  • E, normal: normal sensation and power in a person who had deficits.

Full detail with sacral sparing: spinal cord.

Hoehn and Yahr stages (W20)

  • 1: one side only. 2: both sides, balance normal. 3: both sides, some postural instability, physically independent. 4: severe disability, still walks or stands without help. 5: wheelchair-bound or bedridden unless helped.
  • The modified scale adds 1.5 (one side plus trunk) and 2.5 (both sides, recovers on the pull test). Full detail: Parkinson's disease.

Test yourself

Answer on paper first, then open the answer.

3 mWrite the components of the Glasgow Coma Scale and two conditions where it is used. (W16)

Eye opening 1–4 (spontaneous, to sound, to pressure, none); verbal 1–5 (oriented, confused, words, sounds, none); motor 1–6 (obeys, localises, normal flexion, abnormal flexion, extension, none); total 3–15, severe 3–8. Used in head injury and stroke (also meningitis, overdose).

3 mWrite the Modified Ashworth Scale. (S19, W21)

0 no increase; 1 catch and release or minimal resistance at the end of range; 1+ catch then minimal resistance through less than half the range; 2 more marked increase through most of the range, part moves easily; 3 considerable increase, passive movement difficult; 4 rigid.

7 mDescribe independence measures used in neurological rehabilitation.

Purpose (measure dependence in daily activities, set goals, show change). Barthel index: 10 items, 0–100, Shah bands (0–20 total, 21–60 severe, 61–90 moderate, 91–99 slight dependence, 100 independent); quick but has a ceiling effect. FIM: 18 items (13 motor, 5 cognitive), each 1–7, total 18–126; includes cognition, needs training. Modified Rankin Scale: 0–6 global disability after stroke. Condition-specific: Spinal Cord Independence Measure. Mention reliability, validity, responsiveness and the use of scores in goals.

LAQPractice: a 60-year-old man is 3 months after a left MCA stroke, walks with a stick and needs help to bathe. Choose and justify outcome measures for each ICF level, and write two goals using them. (5 + 5 + 5)

Body functions (5): Fugl-Meyer (motor recovery), Modified Ashworth (spasticity), MMSE or MoCA (cognition). Activities and participation (5): Berg and TUG (balance and fall risk), 10-metre walk (speed), Barthel or FIM (independence), Stroke Impact Scale (quality of life). Goals (5): "TUG from 22 s to under 15 s in 6 weeks"; "Barthel from 75 to 90 in 8 weeks, bathing with supervision only".
