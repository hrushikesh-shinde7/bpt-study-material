Causes of respiratory acidosis

Respiratory acidosis: pH below 7.35 with PaCO₂ above 45 mmHg, caused by alveolar hypoventilation: CO₂ is not breathed out fast enough. The kidneys compensate over days by retaining HCO₃⁻ (acute: +1 mmol/L per 10 mmHg rise in PaCO₂; chronic: +3.5–4).

Brain (drive)Sedatives, opioids, anaesthesia, head injury, stroke
Nerves and musclesGuillain–Barré syndrome, myasthenia gravis, high spinal cord injury, muscular dystrophy, exhausted breathing muscles
Chest wallFlail chest, kyphoscoliosis, obesity hypoventilation
Lungs and airwaysCOPD exacerbation, late severe asthma, severe pneumonia or pulmonary oedema, airway obstruction
VentilatorMinute ventilation set too low

Physio: in acute type 2 failure, gentle chest care, support NIV, no exercise training, and report drowsiness. In chronic CO₂ retention, aim for SpO₂ 88–92%.

Reasons for desaturation with exercise

  1. Worse V/Q mismatch as cardiac output rises and blood flows through poorly ventilated lung.
  2. Diffusion limitation: faster blood flow shortens the time red cells spend in the capillary. This matters in ILD and in emphysema with low DLCO.
  3. Hypoventilation: dynamic hyperinflation in COPD, tired breathing muscles.
  4. Low mixed venous oxygen: working muscles extract more oxygen, so blood returns to the lungs more desaturated.
  5. Pulmonary hypertension; right-to-left shunt (for example through a patent foramen ovale).
  6. Measurement error: cold fingers, poor circulation, movement (use an ear probe).

Physio: measure SpO₂ in the 6MWT; give oxygen during training if SpO₂ falls below about 88%; use interval training. ILD desaturates the most.
