Phase 1 and 2 cardiac rehabilitation after valve replacement

Valve replacement (mechanical valve with lifelong warfarin, or tissue valve) is usually done through a median sternotomy, so rehabilitation follows the CABG plan with valve-specific points. Common in India after rheumatic heart disease (mitral valve).

Phase 1 (in hospital, about 5–7 days)

  • Before surgery: education, breathing exercises, supported cough, baseline walk.
  • Day 0–1: deep breathing with a hold, supported huff and cough, positioning, ankle pumps, sitting out of bed.
  • Day 2 to discharge: walking 3–4 times a day from the room to the corridor; stairs before discharge; RPE below 13, HR no more than rest + 30.
  • Monitor rhythm: atrial fibrillation is common; heart block can follow aortic valve surgery (pacing wires, sometimes a pacemaker).
  • Sternal precautions (6–8 weeks): no heavy lifting, pushing or pulling, one-sided arm loading; arm movements close to the body; gentle shoulder movement, posture.

Phase 2 (outpatient, about 8–12 weeks)

  • Exercise test or 6MWT first; many patients were inactive for months before surgery, so start low.
  • Aerobic: 3–5 days a week, building to 30–60 min at RPE 11–14; use RPE and the talk test when AF or beta-blockers make HR unreliable.
  • Resistance: legs early; arms after the sternum heals (6–8 weeks), progressing slowly.
  • Education: anticoagulation, infection prevention, symptoms to report, return to work and driving.

Valve-specific points

  • Mechanical valve, warfarin (INR checks): easy bruising and bleeding: gentle handling, fall prevention, no contact sport or vigorous percussion.
  • Infective endocarditis: keep teeth and skin healthy; report fever, night sweats or new breathlessness.
  • Mitral disease often leaves pulmonary hypertension and AF: progress more slowly.
  • TAVI (catheter valve): no sternotomy, so no sternal precautions; older, frailer patients; groin care; walk within a day.
