Signs and symptoms of respiratory distress in children

  • Fast breathing for age (WHO, counted for a full minute while calm): ≥ 60/min under 2 months; ≥ 50 at 2–11 months; ≥ 40 at 1–5 years.
  • Increased work of breathing: nasal flaring; chest in-drawing (subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal); tracheal tug; neck muscle use; head bobbing in infants; see-saw breathing.
  • Noises: grunting (breathing out against a closed glottis to keep alveoli open), stridor, wheeze.
  • Effects: cyanosis and low SpO₂, fast heart rate, cannot feed or talk, sweating.
  • Danger signs (tiring): drowsiness, apnoea, slowing breathing in an exhausted child, silent chest: urgent help.

Precautions and contraindications for postural drainage in a neonate

Chest physiotherapy in neonates is given only when clearly needed (retained secretions, collapse, a blocked tube), never as routine; preterm babies have fragile ribs and brain blood vessels.

Precautions

  1. Minimal handling: cluster care with nursing; short sessions; rest between.
  2. Continuous monitoring: SpO₂, heart rate, breathing, colour, temperature; keep the baby warm.
  3. Timing: before feeds or well after, to avoid vomiting and aspiration.
  4. Modified positions without head-down tilt: flat or head-up; a 30° head-up position caused less reflux than head-down (Cochrane).
  5. Gentle techniques: vigorous percussion has been linked to injury; use fingertips or a soft cup, gentle vibrations, only when indicated.
  6. Suction: correct catheter size and pressure (no stronger than −120 mmHg in newborns), pre-oxygenate if needed, short passes; watch for bradycardia and desaturation.

Contraindications (or senior review first)

  • Unstable baby: low BP, uncontrolled arrhythmia, severe desaturation with handling.
  • Recent or extending intraventricular haemorrhage; very preterm baby in the first days of life.
  • Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (handling triggers desaturation).
  • Undrained pneumothorax or air leak.
  • Rib fractures, osteopenia of prematurity, low platelets or bleeding disorder (no percussion or vibration).
  • Raised intracranial pressure; immediately after feeds; recent abdominal or cardiac surgery without surgical clearance.

Modified techniques

Positioning (side-lying, prone only with continuous monitoring), gentle vibrations, assisted breathing, careful suction; developmental positioning.
