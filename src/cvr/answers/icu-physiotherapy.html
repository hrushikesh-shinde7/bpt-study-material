Short-term goals for a patient on a ventilator

A ventilated patient lies still, cannot cough normally, and loses muscle within days. Short-term goals (days to the end of ventilation) aim to protect the lungs and body while the cause is treated, and to prepare for weaning.

GoalHow
1. Clear airways, expand lungs, improve oxygenationPositioning (affected area uppermost), manual techniques on expiration, manual or ventilator hyperinflation, suction when needed
2. Prevent VAP, collapse and aspirationHead of bed 30–45°, turns every 2–4 hours, humidification, clearance
3. Keep joint range; prevent contracturesPassive ROM of all joints daily; ankle splints (foot drop); hand position
4. Prevent pressure injuries and DVTRegular turns, heels floated; ankle movements, early mobilisation
5. Limit muscle wastingActive-assisted and active exercise as soon as sedation allows; bed cycling; sitting out
6. Support weaningUpright positioning for trials; inspiratory muscle training in weaning failure
7. Orientation and communicationExplain each step; communication board; day–night routine; family involvement

Each goal is written as a measurable target, for example "clear breath sounds at the right base within 48 h", "full passive ankle dorsiflexion maintained", "sits over the edge of the bed by day 3 if the safety criteria are met".

Precautions: check settings, vitals and sedation first; avoid disconnection at high PEEP or FiO₂; no positive pressure with an undrained pneumothorax.

Chest physiotherapy for a patient on mechanical ventilation (SIMV)

On SIMV the ventilator gives a set number of breaths timed with the patient's effort, and the patient breathes alone in between (usually with pressure support), so both passive and active techniques are possible.

  1. Assess and check safety: mode, FiO₂, PEEP, pressures, SpO₂, HR, BP, ABG, X-ray, sedation (RASS); time the session with pain relief and away from feeds.
  2. Positioning: head of bed 30–45°; side-lying with the affected area uppermost for drainage; turns every 2–4 hours; upright sitting as soon as possible.
  3. Manual techniques on expiration: shaking, vibrations, percussion where secretions are retained (not with rib fractures, low platelets or instability).
  4. Hyperinflation by bag (slow deep breath, 2–3 s hold, quick release, pressure ≤ ~40 cmH₂O) or by the ventilator, to re-open collapsed lung and move secretions; then suction (≤ 15 s, ≤ −200 mmHg).
  5. Breathing exercises on the patient's own breaths when awake: deep breaths with a hold; inspiratory muscle training during weaning.
  6. Limbs: passive ROM when sedated, then active-assisted and active; splints for ankles and hands.
  7. Mobilisation ladder: rolling → sitting up → edge of bed → standing → marching → chair → walking with the ventilator, within the safety criteria (FiO₂ ≤ 0.6, SpO₂ ≥ 90%, RR ≤ 30, PEEP ≤ 10).
  8. Reassess: breath sounds, SpO₂, secretions, ventilator pressures; record.

Precautions

High PEEP or FiO₂: closed suction and ventilator hyperinflation; undrained pneumothorax: no positive pressure; raised ICP: head up 30°, neutral neck, short sessions; unstable BP or arrhythmia: gentle treatment only. Stop if SpO₂ falls below about 88–90%, HR or rhythm change sharply, MAP falls, or the patient fights the ventilator.

Acute-phase physiotherapy in a mechanically ventilated ARDS patient

ARDS (Berlin 2012): within a week of a known insult (pneumonia, sepsis, trauma, aspiration); bilateral opacities on X-ray; not explained by heart failure; PaO₂/FiO₂ on PEEP ≥ 5: mild 201–300, moderate 101–200, severe ≤ 100. The lungs are stiff, oedematous and easily torn, and oxygenation depends on PEEP.

Medical care that shapes physiotherapy

Lung-protective ventilation (tidal volume 6 mL/kg predicted weight, plateau pressure ≤ 30 cmH₂O), PEEP, careful fluids, and prone positioning 16 hours or more a day when PaO₂/FiO₂ is below 150 (lowered deaths in the PROSEVA trial).

Physiotherapy in the acute phase

  1. Minimal, clustered handling while oxygenation is poor; agree the plan with the ICU team.
  2. No disconnection: closed (in-line) suction only when secretions are present; ventilator hyperinflation rather than bagging; avoid high pressures.
  3. Positioning: head up 30–45°, regular turns; help the team turn the patient prone safely (protect eyes, face, shoulders, knees and lines).
  4. Limbs: passive ROM and positioning against contractures; pressure-area care (prone positioning causes facial and knee sores).
  5. Monitor SpO₂, ventilator pressures and BP during every intervention; stop if SpO₂ falls and does not recover.

As she improves

When FiO₂ and PEEP fall into the safe range (FiO₂ ≤ 0.6, PEEP ≤ 10): active exercise, sitting over the edge of the bed, standing and walking with the ventilator; IMT during weaning; after extubation, breathing exercises and graded exercise with follow-up rehab.

Complications to prevent: ventilator lung injury, pneumothorax, VAP, ICU-acquired weakness, contractures, pressure injuries, DVT, delirium, later post-intensive care syndrome.
