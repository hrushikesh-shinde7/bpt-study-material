Goals of management of restrictive lung diseases

Restrictive lung diseases (interstitial lung disease, pleural thickening, kyphoscoliosis, obesity, neuromuscular weakness) reduce lung volumes and compliance, causing rapid shallow breathing and exertional desaturation.

Short-term goals:

  • Safe exercise with SpO₂ kept at about 88% or more (oxygen if needed).
  • Breathing control, positions of ease and pacing during daily activities.
  • Control of a troublesome dry cough; chest mobility and posture (chest wall causes).

Long-term goals:

  • Better exercise tolerance (6MWT) and quality of life through pulmonary rehabilitation.
  • Independent home exercise and energy conservation; avoid the causative dust; return to suitable work.

Three spirometric findings in restrictive lung conditions

  1. FVC reduced (FEV1 reduced in proportion).
  2. FEV1/FVC normal or high: 0.70 or more, often above 0.80 (unlike obstruction, where it falls below 0.70).
  3. Total lung capacity reduced on lung volume testing (needed to confirm restriction); DLCO reduced in interstitial lung disease.

Flow–volume loop: small and narrow. On the spirogram, restriction lowers the plateau, while obstruction slows the rise. Example (S25): FVC 60%, FEV1 82%, FEV1/FVC 94% predicted = restrictive (old TB scarring).

Physiotherapy for a 43-year-old farmer with ILD (mMRC 2, dry cough, generalised weakness)

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) scars the tissue between the air sacs: lungs become stiff and small (restriction) and oxygen transfer is poor, so patients desaturate markedly on exertion. In a farmer, think of hypersensitivity pneumonitis (farmer's lung, from mouldy hay or grain); heterogeneous opacities fit the HRCT picture.

Assessment

Exposure history (dust, mould, masks), smoking; mMRC 2; dry cough; breathing pattern, fine basal crackles, clubbing; SpO₂ at rest and during a 6-minute walk test; HR, Borg; muscle strength; ADL and farm work. Outcomes: 6MWT distance and lowest SpO₂, mMRC, SGRQ.

Goals

Short-term: safe exercise with SpO₂ about 88–90% or more; breathing control and pacing in ADL; cough control. Long-term: better 6MWT and quality of life; independent home programme; avoid further exposure; return to lighter farm work.

Pulmonary rehabilitation for ILD

  • Aerobic: walking or cycling 3–5 days a week, 20–30 min, at moderate breathlessness (Borg 3–4); interval training if SpO₂ falls quickly.
  • Oxygen during exercise to keep SpO₂ about 88–90% or more.
  • Resistance training of legs and arms 2–3 days a week for the generalised weakness.
  • Breathing control and forward-lean recovery positions; pacing; energy conservation for farm work.
  • Dry cough: sip water, swallow, nose breathing, cough suppression.
  • Thoracic mobility and posture; inspiratory muscle training may help.

Unlike COPD, airway clearance is rarely needed; the limit is oxygen transfer, so monitor SpO₂ continuously.

Education and precautions

Avoid the mould or dust (mask, change of task), stop smoking, vaccines, oxygen safety. Stop if SpO₂ falls below about 85% despite oxygen, or with chest pain, dizziness or severe breathlessness; avoid heavy straining if pulmonary hypertension develops.
