Cheyne–Stokes respiration

Cheyne–Stokes respiration is a cyclical crescendo–decrescendo breathing pattern. Breaths gradually deepen (and quicken), then become shallower, then stop in an apnoea lasting several seconds, and the cycle repeats.

Mechanism: delayed feedback between the lungs and the chemoreceptors (a slow circulation, or a damaged, over-sensitive respiratory centre). Over-breathing lowers CO₂ too far, so breathing stops (apnoea). CO₂ then rises and breathing restarts, overshooting again.

Causes:

  • Heart failure (slow circulation delays the chemoreceptor feedback).
  • Stroke, brain injury, raised ICP.
  • High altitude, opioids.
  • Normal in sleeping infants and elderly people.

Waves of breaths rising and falling in size, separated by flat apnoea gaps, as in the sketches printed with the next answer.

In heart failure it signals a poor prognosis; report it, and keep the patient sitting up.

Three abnormal breathing patterns

Draw the shape of each pattern and give one cause.

  1. Kussmaul breathing: deep, fast, laboured, regular breathing. It blows off CO₂ to compensate for metabolic acidosis (diabetic ketoacidosis, kidney failure).
  2. Cheyne–Stokes respiration: breaths gradually deepen, then become shallow, then an apnoea of several seconds; repeats. Seen in heart failure, stroke and brain injury, at high altitude and with opioids.
  3. Biot's (ataxic) breathing: groups of quick, equal breaths separated by irregular pauses (ataxic: completely irregular). Seen in medulla damage (meningitis, raised ICP, brainstem stroke, opioids); a grave sign.

Others: apneustic (deep gasping breath held, short expiration: pons damage); paradoxical (abdomen moves in on inspiration in diaphragm paralysis, or a flail segment moves in); tachypnoea (above ~20/min); bradypnoea (below ~12/min: opioids, raised ICP).
