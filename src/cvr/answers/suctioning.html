Hazards and complications of suctioning

Memory: "low oxygen, slow heart, high head pressure", then trauma, spasm, collapse, infection.

  1. Hypoxaemia: oxygen is sucked out and PEEP is lost on disconnection (pre-oxygenate; short passes; closed suction).
  2. Bradycardia and arrhythmias: vagal stimulation of the trachea plus hypoxia (watch the monitor; stop if HR falls).
  3. Raised intracranial pressure and BP swings from coughing and distress.
  4. Mucosal trauma and bleeding (correct pressure, no suction on the way in).
  5. Atelectasis (negative pressure pulls air out of alveoli).
  6. Bronchospasm; infection (sterile technique); pain, anxiety, coughing fits; laryngospasm and nasal bleeding with the nasal route; rarely cardiac arrest.

Safe limits (AARC 2022): each pass ≤ 15 s, pressure no stronger than −200 mmHg in adults, catheter less than half the tube's inner width.

Suctioning and manual hyperinflation (bagging): indications, procedure and precautions

Suctioning

Removing airway secretions with a catheter on negative pressure when the patient cannot cough them out: open or closed (in-line) through a tube, nasopharyngeal, or oral (Yankauer). Shallow suction (tube length) routinely; deep only if shallow fails.

Indications: secretions seen or heard in the tube; coarse crackles over the trachea; saw-tooth flow graph, rising peak pressure; falling SpO₂ from secretions; weak cough; sputum sample. Suction only when needed.

Procedure ("15 s / −200 mmHg / half the tube"):

  1. Explain; hand hygiene, apron, eye protection, sterile glove; check pressure (≤ −200 mmHg adult, −120 children); catheter under half the tube width (8.0 mm tube → 12 Fr).
  2. Pre-oxygenate (30–60 s) if SpO₂ drops with suction.
  3. Insert without suction; withdraw with suction, rolling the catheter, in under 15 s.
  4. Reconnect; let SpO₂ and HR recover; usually ≤ 2–3 passes; mouth last.
  5. Record sounds, SpO₂, secretions, blood, tolerance. No routine saline.

Manual hyperinflation

Disconnect and give larger, slower breaths with a 2-litre bag (oxygen 15 L/min, manometer, PEEP valve if needed), then a quick release that imitates a cough. Aims: re-open collapsed alveoli through collateral channels, move secretions centrally, improve compliance and oxygenation.

Technique: side-lying with the affected area up; slow deep inspiration → hold 2–3 s → quick release with vibrations or shaking on expiration; peak pressure ≤ about 40 cmH₂O; a few breaths in sets; suction when secretions are central; reassess.

Precautions and contraindications

  • Undrained pneumothorax (absolute for hyperinflation); bullae; fresh bronchial stump after lung surgery.
  • Unstable BP or arrhythmia; uncontrolled raised ICP (sedate, short passes); severe bronchospasm; haemoptysis; pulmonary oedema; low platelets (gentle shallow suction).
  • PEEP above about 10 or high FiO₂: disconnection collapses the lung, so use closed suction and ventilator hyperinflation (similar effect without disconnection).

Hazards of suction: hypoxaemia, bradycardia, raised ICP, mucosal trauma, atelectasis, bronchospasm, infection.
