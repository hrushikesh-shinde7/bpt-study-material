Clinical features of tetralogy of Fallot

Tetralogy of Fallot: large VSD, overriding aorta, right ventricular outflow obstruction (pulmonary stenosis) and right ventricular hypertrophy. Blue blood crosses the VSD into the aorta.

  1. Cyanosis from the first months of life, worse with crying, feeding or exertion.
  2. Hypercyanotic ("tet") spells: sudden deep cyanosis, fast breathing, irritability, sometimes fainting or fits, often after waking, feeding or crying.
  3. Squatting after exertion in older children (raises systemic resistance, sends more blood to the lungs).
  4. Clubbing of fingers and toes; poor growth; exercise intolerance.
  5. Harsh ejection systolic murmur at the left upper sternal edge; single second heart sound.

Radiological features of tetralogy of Fallot

  1. Boot-shaped heart (coeur en sabot): upturned, rounded apex from right ventricular hypertrophy.
  2. Concave pulmonary bay: a small pulmonary artery segment on the left heart border.
  3. Oligaemic lung fields: reduced lung vessel markings, dark lungs (less blood reaches the lungs).

Heart size is usually normal; some patients have a right-sided aortic arch. Echocardiography confirms the diagnosis.
