Clinical features of bronchiectasis

Bronchiectasis is permanent abnormal widening of the bronchi, with a daily wet cough and repeated infections.

  • Chronic cough with large amounts of mucopurulent sputum, worse in the morning and on changing position; in a pot it settles in three layers (froth, mucus, pus); foul-smelling when infected.
  • Recurrent chest infections: more and darker sputum, fever, tiredness.
  • Haemoptysis, from streaks to large bleeds.
  • Breathlessness, wheeze, chest pain, fatigue, weight loss, bad breath, sinus symptoms.
  • Signs: finger clubbing; coarse crackles over the affected area that change after coughing; wheeze; reduced expansion and tracheal shift over a fibrosed lobe.

Physiotherapy in bronchiectasis (right lower lobe)

Bronchiectasis is permanent widening of the bronchi (often after TB in India). Mucus pools in the dilated airways and keeps the vicious cycle of infection and inflammation going; physiotherapy breaks it by clearing the mucus.

Assessment

Daily sputum (amount, colour, smell, blood), cough, mMRC, exacerbations, past TB, urinary leakage with cough; chest expansion, coarse crackles over the right base, SpO₂, 6MWT; X-ray or HRCT to locate the lobe.

1. Daily airway clearance (the core)

  • 10–30 min, once or twice a day, more during infections; bronchodilator → nebulised saline → clearance → inhaled antibiotic.
  • ACBT: breathing control → 3–4 deep breaths with a 3-s hold → breathing control → 1–2 huffs from mid lung volume; repeat until the huff is dry.
  • Autogenic drainage or PEP (Flutter, Acapella) for independent use at home and work.

2. Postural drainage for the right lower lobe

  • Posterior basal: prone, pillow under the hips, foot of the bed raised 20–30°.
  • Lateral basal: lying on the left side, turned a quarter forward, foot raised 20–30°.
  • Superior (apical) segment: prone flat with two pillows under the hips.
  • About 10 min in each productive position, with ACBT; percussion or vibration only if helpful.

3. Exercise and other measures

Pulmonary rehabilitation (endurance and strength, 6–12 weeks); breathing control and posture; pelvic floor training for stress incontinence (tighten before coughing); hydration; inspiratory muscle training if weak.

Precautions

No head-down tilt with haemoptysis, raised ICP, reflux, severe breathlessness, unstable heart or after meals; stop percussion with fresh bleeding; infection control (safe sputum disposal, clean devices daily).

Education: clearance every day even when well; sputum diary and action plan for exacerbations; stop smoking; vaccines.

Compare the effects of ACBT and autogenic drainage in bronchiectasis

Both are active, independent airway clearance techniques that need no equipment and are preferred over passive postural drainage with percussion (BTS/ACPRC 2009).

ACBT is a cycle of breathing control, thoracic expansion exercises and the forced expiration technique (huffs). Autogenic drainage (AD) uses controlled breathing at low, mid and high lung volumes ("unstick, collect, evacuate") to move mucus from small to large airways, suppressing coughs until the mucus is central.

Effects

Both increase sputum clearance and reduce cough; short-term studies show similar results, so choose the technique the patient will actually do. ACBT suits most patients and is quicker to teach; AD suits patients whose airways collapse or who leak urine when coughing (like the S19 clerk).

Ergonomic and energy-conservation advice for left lung bronchiectasis with a normal right lung

Positioning and sleep

  • Rest and sleep lying on the right side. The diseased left lung is then uppermost, so it drains by gravity, and the healthy right lung is lowermost, where it gets more blood flow and better gas exchange.
  • Use the left lung drainage positions (right side-lying, foot of bed raised for the lower lobe) with ACBT for daily clearance.

At work and home (ergonomics)

  • Clear the chest in the morning and before work or travel; plan a short huffing break during the day in private; carry a sputum container and tissues.
  • Sit upright with the desk at elbow height; avoid long slumped sitting, which squashes the lower chest; take movement breaks.
  • Keep often-used things between waist and shoulder height; avoid prolonged bending and lifting overhead.

Energy conservation (the 4 Ps)

  • Plan heavy tasks across the week and at the best time of day.
  • Pace: slow, steady work; rest before becoming breathless; breathe out on effort.
  • Prioritise: drop or share non-essential tasks.
  • Position: sit for cooking and ironing; use trolleys; avoid heavy lifting.

Environment and health

Avoid dust, smoke and crowded places during infections; wear a mask; stop smoking; drink enough water to keep sputum thin; keep up exercise; know the action plan for an exacerbation.
