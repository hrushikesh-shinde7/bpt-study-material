Physiotherapy management of hypertension

Hypertension is persistently raised arterial BP on two or more correct readings: 140/90 mmHg or more (ISH, India); grade 1 140–159/90–99, grade 2 ≥ 160/100. It is usually silent but damages the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes and arteries.

Why exercise lowers BP

Training improves the artery lining, relaxes small arteries, lowers sympathetic activity and helps weight loss, so resting resistance falls; one session lowers BP for hours (post-exercise hypotension).

Assessment

BP in both arms with the right cuff, HR; medicines and their timing; target-organ disease; risk factors (salt, weight, inactivity, alcohol, smoking, stress); BMI, waist; 6MWT or step test with BP before and after.

Exercise prescription

AerobicBrisk walking, cycling, swimming; moderate (talk test, RPE 12–13); about 30 min on most days
Dynamic resistance2–3 days a week; major muscles; moderate loads (~50–80% 1RM), about 3 × 10; breathe out on effort
IsometricHandgrip 4 × 2 min at 30–40% of maximum, 1–3 min rest, 3 days a week (or wall squats): the largest BP fall, about 8/4 mmHg
Flexibility, relaxationStretching; yoga with slow breathing; relaxation for stress

Warm-up and a longer cool-down (10 min) to avoid a sudden BP drop; progress duration first, then intensity.

Precautions

  • Do not start if resting BP is above about 180/110 or with symptoms of a crisis; recheck and refer.
  • Stop if systolic rises above about 250 or diastolic above about 115, systolic falls as work rises, or with chest pain, dizziness or headache.
  • No breath-holding (Valsalva), very heavy lifting or long head-down positions.
  • Drugs: beta-blockers → use RPE; diuretics → fluids; vasodilators → longer cool-down.

Lifestyle advice

Salt below 5 g a day (avoid pickles, papad, packaged snacks); lose 5% or more of weight; vegetables, fruit, pulses; limit alcohol; stop smoking; manage stress and sleep; take medicines; home BP checks. Target below 130/80.
