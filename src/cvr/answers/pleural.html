Radiological findings of pleural effusion

  • Blunting of the costophrenic angle (needs about 200 mL or more on a PA film; less on a lateral film).
  • A homogeneous white (opaque) area at the base with a curved upper border rising towards the axilla: the meniscus sign.
  • Loss of the diaphragm outline on that side.
  • Large effusion: mediastinum and trachea pushed to the opposite side; whole hemithorax white ("white-out").
  • Lying (AP) films: diffuse haziness instead of a level; hydropneumothorax shows a straight air–fluid level.

Physio meaning: lie on the unaffected side and do localised thoracic expansion to the affected side; large effusions are drained first.

Pneumothorax, and the precautions, contraindications and physiotherapy for a patient with an intercostal drain

Pneumothorax is air in the pleural space, so the lung collapses partly or fully.

  • Types: primary spontaneous (tall, thin young men; smokers); secondary (COPD bullae, TB, CF, ILD; more dangerous); traumatic; iatrogenic; tension (a one-way valve lets air in but not out, the mediastinum shifts and venous return stops: emergency).
  • Features: sudden one-sided chest pain and breathlessness; reduced expansion and breath sounds, hyper-resonant percussion; tension: distress, low BP, fast pulse, trachea pushed away.
  • X-ray: thin pleural line with no lung markings beyond it.
  • Treatment (BTS 2023): guided by symptoms: observation, aspiration, ambulatory device or intercostal drain; VATS pleurodesis for recurrence; tension: needle decompression then a drain.

Physiotherapy with an intercostal drain

  1. Check before and after each session: swing, bubbling, drainage, kinks; bottle upright and below chest level.
  2. Breathing control; deep breathing and thoracic expansion to help the lung re-expand once drained.
  3. Supported cough at the drain site; adequate pain relief.
  4. Early mobilisation carrying the bottle low; shoulder ROM on the drain side; posture correction.

Precautions and contraindications

  • Undrained pneumothorax: no positive-pressure techniques (IPPB, NIV, PEP, manual hyperinflation): they can create a tension pneumothorax.
  • Never clamp a bubbling drain; never lift the bottle above the chest.
  • No vigorous percussion over the drain or fresh wounds; avoid straining and breath-holding until healed.
  • No flying until the doctor confirms resolution; stop smoking (recurrence).

Stop and call for help: sudden worse breathlessness, falling BP, tracheal shift (tension); drain falls out or disconnects; sudden heavy blood in the drain.
