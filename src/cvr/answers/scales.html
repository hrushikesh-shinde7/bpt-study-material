Borg CR10 and modified Borg scale

The Borg CR10 is a category–ratio scale from 0 to 10. Word anchors (categories) are spaced so that the numbers behave like ratios: 4 feels about twice 2. The modified Borg scale is its version for breathlessness, widely used in pulmonary rehab.

Borg's own CR10 words: 0.5 extremely weak, 2 weak, 5 strong, 7 very strong, 10 extremely strong; ratings above 10 are allowed.

Uses: before and after the 6MWT; training intensity in pulmonary rehab at 3–5; stop above 7; pacing. (The original RPE scale, 6–20, rates overall effort; 13 is "somewhat hard".)

Grades of the NYHA scale

The New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification grades limitation of physical activity by symptoms (breathlessness, fatigue, palpitations, angina) in heart disease, especially heart failure.

ClassDescription
INo limitation: ordinary activity causes no undue symptoms
IISlight limitation: comfortable at rest; ordinary activity (brisk walking, stairs) causes symptoms
IIIMarked limitation: comfortable at rest; less than ordinary activity (walking on the level, dressing) causes symptoms
IVSymptoms at rest; any activity increases discomfort

Uses: assessing severity and prognosis, guiding drug and device treatment, and setting the starting level of cardiac rehabilitation (class IV: stabilise first; classes I–III: supervised exercise). For angina, the similar Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) classes I–IV are used.

ConditionQuestionnaireDetails
COPDSt George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ)Symptoms, activity and impact; 0–100, higher is worse; 4 points is a meaningful change. (Also CRQ; CAT, 8 items, 0–40, ≥ 10 = high impact.)
Heart failureMinnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ)21 items, 0–105, higher is worse. (Also KCCQ, 0–100, higher is better.)
Peripheral arterial diseaseWalking Impairment Questionnaire (WIQ)Walking distance, speed and stairs; 0–100, higher is better. (Also VascuQoL, 25 items.)

Generic questionnaires (SF-36, EQ-5D, WHOQOL-BREF) compare across diseases but change less after treatment; disease-specific ones detect small changes after rehabilitation. Use one of each.
