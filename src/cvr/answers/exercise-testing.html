Bruce protocol

The Bruce protocol is a symptom-limited, incremental treadmill test in which speed and gradient both rise every 3 minutes, with ECG, BP and symptoms monitored throughout.

Modified Bruce (elderly, deconditioned, early after MI) adds two easier starting stages at 1.7 mph, 0% and then 5%, avoiding the big first jump.

Result: exercise time, stage reached (METs), ischaemic ST changes, arrhythmias, BP response. Uses: diagnosing coronary disease, risk stratification, functional capacity before cardiac rehabilitation.

Use the cycle when balance, weight-bearing or ECG quality is a problem, and the treadmill when walking capacity is the target.

The endurance shuttle walk test uses a constant speed set from the ISWT and records endurance time; it is very sensitive to rehabilitation. Record SpO₂, HR and Borg before and after both tests.

Contraindications for exercise testing (absolute; three relative and three absolute)

Memory: "acute heart, acute clot, tight valve".

Absolute:

  1. Acute MI within 2 days; ongoing unstable angina.
  2. Uncontrolled arrhythmia causing symptoms.
  3. Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis.
  4. Decompensated heart failure.
  5. Acute pulmonary embolism, pulmonary infarction or DVT.
  6. Acute myocarditis, pericarditis or endocarditis; acute aortic dissection.
  7. Disability that makes testing unsafe.

Relative (test only if the benefit outweighs the risk):

  1. Resting BP above 200/110 mmHg.
  2. Left main coronary stenosis; moderate to severe aortic stenosis with unclear symptoms; HOCM with a severe gradient.
  3. Fast arrhythmia with uncontrolled rate; complete or advanced heart block.
  4. Recent stroke or TIA; mental impairment; uncorrected anaemia, electrolyte imbalance or hyperthyroidism.

Absolute and relative contraindications to exercise testing

Exercise testing measures the response of the heart, lungs and muscles to graded exercise (treadmill, cycle, CPET, field tests). It is used for diagnosis, prognosis, functional capacity and exercise prescription. It is safe when patients are screened (history, examination, resting ECG, BP, medicines) and the contraindications are respected. Memory: "acute heart, acute clot, tight valve".

Absolute contraindications (do not test)

  1. Acute MI within 2 days, or ongoing unstable angina.
  2. Uncontrolled arrhythmia causing symptoms or haemodynamic compromise.
  3. Active endocarditis; acute myocarditis or pericarditis.
  4. Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis.
  5. Decompensated heart failure.
  6. Acute pulmonary embolism, pulmonary infarction or deep vein thrombosis.
  7. Acute aortic dissection.
  8. Physical or mental disability that prevents safe, adequate testing.

Relative contraindications (test only if the benefit outweighs the risk)

  1. Known left main coronary stenosis.
  2. Moderate to severe aortic stenosis with unclear symptoms.
  3. Fast arrhythmia with uncontrolled ventricular rate.
  4. Complete or advanced heart block.
  5. Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy with a severe gradient.
  6. Recent stroke or TIA.
  7. Mental impairment limiting co-operation.
  8. Resting BP above 200/110 mmHg.
  9. Uncorrected anaemia, electrolyte imbalance or hyperthyroidism.

Safety: trained staff, defibrillator and emergency drugs at hand, continuous ECG and regular BP and symptom (RPE) checks, and monitoring for several minutes into recovery. With relative contraindications, use a gentler protocol (modified Bruce) and closer supervision.

Exercise tolerance tests: advantages and disadvantages of each

Exercise tolerance tests measure how much exercise a patient can do and how the body responds. Purposes: diagnosis (ischaemia, arrhythmia, desaturation, exercise-induced bronchospasm); prognosis and risk stratification; functional capacity (METs, distance); exercise prescription (safe heart rate, walking speed, oxygen need); outcome of rehabilitation.

Types: laboratory tests (treadmill, cycle ergometer, with ECG; cardiopulmonary exercise testing with gas analysis) and field tests (6-minute walk, shuttle walks, step tests).

Choosing: treadmill or CPET for diagnosis and precise capacity; the cycle for poor balance or obesity; the 6MWT and shuttle tests for chronic lung disease and rehabilitation outcomes. Screen every patient for the contraindications first and monitor HR, BP, SpO₂, ECG and Borg score during each test.
