Head injury

Primary injury at impact (contusion, diffuse axonal injury, haematomas); secondary injury from low O₂, low BP, raised ICP, seizures, fever, sugar swings: the whole ICU plan prevents it. GCS: mild 13–15, moderate 9–12, severe 3–8 ("GCS 8, intubate").

ICU picture: sedated, ICP monitor or EVD, craniectomy; decorticate or decerebrate posturing; sympathetic storms; aspiration, VAP, collapse; agitation on waking. Treat ICP above ~22; CPP (MAP − ICP) 60–70; normal CO₂; head up 30°; mannitol or hypertonic saline.

Physio: head up 30°, neck straight; side-lying as ICP allows; vibrations and compressions on expiration (did not change ICP or CPP in sedated patients); suction only when needed, after sedation and pre-oxygenation, short passes (ICP rose ~20 → 26 and settled in ~10 min); positioning out of abnormal posture, ankle splints, gentle PROM, serial casts later; mobilise once ICP is stable (sit, edge of bed, tilt table); calm consistent handling as he wakes.

Precautions: ICP above target or being treated: no active mobilisation, clustered handling; no head-down, neck flexion or rotation, tight ties, high-pressure bagging; craniectomy: no pressure on the bone-free side, helmet; clamp or level EVD; spine not cleared: log-roll.

Tetanus

Clostridium tetani toxin from wounds blocks inhibitory neurones → uncontrolled contraction + autonomic overactivity; onset 3–21 days. Trismus (lockjaw), risus sardonicus, opisthotonus; painful spasms triggered by touch, noise, light; fully conscious; laryngospasm, rigid chest → respiratory failure; aspiration; BP and HR swings.

Wound care, immunoglobulin, metronidazole, benzodiazepines, magnesium, ventilation and early tracheostomy; dark quiet room; vaccinate after recovery (no immunity).

Physio: plan with the team, treat when sedation is adequate; short sessions; no sudden touch or noise; positioning, turns, gentle vibrations, suction with sedation; gentle PROM only when spasms are controlled; recovery: active exercise, jaw opening, sitting, walking, strength. Warn before touching; slow firm handling; stop if spasms start; new back pain = vertebral fracture?

Spinal cord injury: breathing

C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive

C1–C2: lifelong ventilator or phrenic pacing; C3–C5: partial diaphragm, often ventilated then wean; C6–C8: diaphragm works, intercostals and abdominals lost: low VC, very weak cough, paradoxical upper chest; thoracic: abdominals T6–T12, weaker cough the higher the level.

Spinal shock; neurogenic shock (T6 and above: low BP, slow HR); excess secretions (unopposed vagus), collapse and pneumonia; gut distension; VC improves over weeks.

Physio: manually assisted cough (upward and inward push timed with the cough), cough assist, suction; diaphragmatic breathing; VC is often better lying than sitting (abdomen supports the diaphragm): abdominal binder when sitting; respiratory muscle training (↑ VC, Cochrane 2013); hyperinflation if ventilated; log-roll turns, vibrations; PROM within spinal limits, splints, 2–3-hourly turns; staged sitting, tilt table.

Precautions: unstable spine: log-roll only; suction or turning → severe bradycardia; autonomic dysreflexia (T6 and above): very high BP, pounding headache, flushing above the level → sit up, loosen clothing, check catheter and bowel, call for help; postural hypotension; no assisted cough over a fresh abdomen or ileus.

Neuromuscular disease

Myasthenic crisis (infection, surgery, drugs; IVIG or plasma exchange; too much anticholinesterase = cholinergic crisis with secretions); motor neurone disease (NIV improves survival without severe bulbar weakness); Duchenne (scoliosis, night hypoventilation); critical illness polyneuropathy; neurotoxic snakebite.

Warning signs: VC below ~20 mL/kg, weak cough, choking, nasal voice; fast breathing, accessory muscles, abdomen moving in on inspiration, breathless lying flat; morning headache, daytime sleepiness; rising PaCO₂ is late. Peak cough flow below ~270 L/min: cough may fail in an infection (teach augmentation); below ~160: ineffective.

Physio: assisted cough, cough assist, breath stacking (bag with one-way valve), suction; support NIV (mask fit); positioning, clearance, swallow referral; ROM, gentle non-fatiguing strengthening, energy conservation; myasthenia: short sessions ~1 h after pyridostigmine, stop at first fatigue. Avoid fatigue; respect choices about ventilation in progressive disease.
