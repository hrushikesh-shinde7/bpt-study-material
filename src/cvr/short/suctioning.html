Asked: 3 marks (5 papers): hazards or complications of suctioning. 7 marks: suctioning and bagging (manual hyperinflation): indications, procedure, precautions (S22, S24).

Types

Suctioning: removing airway secretions with a catheter on negative pressure when the patient cannot cough them out.

  • Open endotracheal or tracheostomy: disconnect, sterile catheter; loses PEEP and oxygen.
  • Closed (in-line): catheter in a sealed sleeve; keeps PEEP and FiO₂, less cross-infection: high PEEP, FiO₂, infection.
  • Nasopharyngeal or nasotracheal: no artificial airway; lubricated catheter or nasal airway. Oropharyngeal: Yankauer.
  • Shallow (to tube length) routinely; deep (to resistance, pull back 1 cm) only if shallow fails (AARC 2022).

Indications (only when needed): secretions seen or heard; coarse crackles over trachea; saw-tooth flow graph, rising peak pressure or falling VT; falling SpO₂ from secretions; weak cough after other clearance; aspiration; sputum sample.

Procedure (open)

15 s / −200 mmHg / half the tube

Pass ≤ 15 s; pressure no stronger than −200 mmHg adults (−120 children, newborns); catheter under half the tube's inner width (under 70% newborns): 8.0 mm tube → 12 Fr.

  1. Assess and explain (even if sedated); vitals, SpO₂.
  2. Hand hygiene, apron, eye protection, sterile glove; check pressure; choose catheter.
  3. Pre-oxygenate 30–60 s if SpO₂ drops with suction.
  4. Insert without suction to planned depth.
  5. Withdraw with suction, rolling the catheter, in under 15 s.
  6. Reconnect at once; let SpO₂ and HR recover; usually ≤ 2–3 passes.
  7. Mouth last with a separate catheter (never mouth → tube).
  8. Reassess and record sounds, SpO₂, secretions, blood, tolerance.

No routine saline down the tube; thick secretions need better humidification and hydration. Closed system: advance, suction on withdrawal, flush.

Hazards (3m W16, S17, S18, W19, S26)

Low O₂, slow heart, high head pressure

  • Hypoxaemia (oxygen sucked out, PEEP lost): pre-oxygenate, short passes, closed suction.
  • Bradycardia, arrhythmias (vagal, hypoxia): watch monitor, stop if HR falls.
  • Raised ICP, BP swings (coughing, distress): sedation, few passes.
  • Mucosal trauma, bleeding: right pressure, no suction going in, shallow.
  • Atelectasis (alveolar air sucked out): small catheter, short passes, hyperinflate after.
  • Bronchospasm; infection (sterile, closed); pain, anxiety, coughing fits; laryngospasm and nasal bleeding (nasal route); rare cardiac arrest.

Care in: raised ICP; high PEEP or FiO₂; low platelets or haemoptysis; unstable rhythm; SCI or GBS (vagal bradycardia); bronchospasm; recent lung resection (no deep suction near stump). No nasal route with base-of-skull fracture, CSF leak, facial fractures, nasal bleeding.

Manual hyperinflation (7m S22, S24)

Disconnect and give larger, slower breaths with a rebreathing bag, then a quick release: imitates a cough. Aims: re-open collapsed alveoli (collateral channels), move secretions centrally (fast expiratory flow), short-term better compliance and oxygenation.

  • Two people: one bags, one shakes or vibrates. 2-L bag, O₂ ~15 L/min, manometer, PEEP valve if needed.
  • Side-lying, affected area up (or supine).
  • Slow deep breath → hold 2–3 s → quick release with vibrations on expiration; peak pressure ≤ ~40 cmH₂O.
  • Sets of a few breaths; suction when secretions are central; reconnect, reassess.

Contraindications: undrained pneumothorax (absolute); bullae; fresh bronchial stump; unstable BP or arrhythmia; PEEP above ~10 or high FiO₂ (disconnection collapses the lung); uncontrolled ICP; severe bronchospasm; haemoptysis; pulmonary oedema.

Evidence (Paulus 2012): short-term gains only; ventilator hyperinflation works about as well without disconnection, so preferred with high PEEP or FiO₂.
