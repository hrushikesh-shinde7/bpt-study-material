Asked: not asked 2016–2026; possible 3- or 7-mark short note. Also linked to bronchiectasis and paediatric questions.

Definition and cause

Cystic fibrosis: inherited, autosomal recessive (both parents carriers); CFTR gene mutation → chloride and water do not move across cell surfaces → thick sticky secretions in lungs, pancreas, liver, gut and reproductive tract; very salty sweat.

Features and diagnosis

  • Lungs: thick mucus, chronic infection (Staph aureus, later Pseudomonas), progressive bronchiectasis, haemoptysis, pneumothorax, clubbing.
  • Gut, pancreas: meconium ileus at birth, fatty bulky stools, malabsorption, poor weight gain; CF-related diabetes later.
  • Other: nasal polyps, sinusitis, liver disease, male infertility, salt loss in heat, low bone density.
  • Diagnosis: newborn screening; sweat chloride ≥ 60 mmol/L (30–59 intermediate); genetic test. Under-diagnosed in India: found late in children with repeated chest infections and poor growth.

Medical points for physio

CFTR modulators (elexacaftor–tezacaftor–ivacaftor) correct the protein in many patients: big gains in lung function and survival where available. Mucoactives: dornase alfa, hypertonic saline before clearance. Antibiotics (oral, inhaled, IV), pancreatic enzymes, high-calorie diet, salt supplements, transplant. Order: bronchodilator → hypertonic saline or dornase → airway clearance → inhaled antibiotic.

ICF: thick secretions, infections, ↓ lung function and exercise tolerance, poor growth, posture and back pain → sport, play, daily treatment burden → school, work, social life (cross-infection rules); CF centre and family (facilitators), cost of drugs (barrier); adolescent adherence.

PT management (lifelong)

  • Infants: modified postural drainage without head-down, gentle percussion or vibration, assisted autogenic drainage, PEP mask, active play (bouncing on a ball, rolling).
  • Children: PEP mask, flutter or Acapella, blowing games, ACBT once they can huff.
  • Teenagers and adults: ACBT, autogenic drainage, PEP or oscillating PEP, chest-wall oscillation vest: the one they will actually do.
  • Dose: once or twice daily, more in infections; review technique regularly.
  • Exercise and sport at all ages: fitness, mucus clearance, bones, QoL; it does not replace airway clearance for most.
  • Posture, thoracic mobility, back pain; pelvic floor (leakage with cough common in women); inhaler technique; NIV in advanced disease; rehab before and after transplant.

Precautions

Cross-infection: people with CF must be kept apart from each other (no shared rooms, gyms or groups); clean equipment; hand hygiene. Haemoptysis: stop percussion and positive-pressure devices, report. Pneumothorax: no positive pressure until drained. Heat: extra fluids and salt. CF diabetes: check sugar around exercise. No head-down in infants or with reflux.

Exam points

Autosomal recessive, CFTR, thick secretions, sweat chloride ≥ 60; lifelong daily airway clearance adapted to age; exercise; strict cross-infection control.
