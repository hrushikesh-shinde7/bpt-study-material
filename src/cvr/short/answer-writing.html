~2 minutes a mark. Slow writer: long answers first. Choose the case you know best.

Short answers

3 marks (half a page): one-line definition → 3–5 points, a small table or labelled sketch → one line of physio relevance. Example (ABI): definition; highest ankle ÷ highest brachial; 0.91–1.40 normal, ≤ 0.90 PAD, below 0.40 severe, above 1.40 calcified; use before compression.

7 marks (1.5–2 pages): definition (2 lines) → principle, classification or why (flowchart) → 6–8 headed points or a table with doses → labelled diagram → precautions, contraindications → one concluding line (evidence or outcome measure).

The 15-mark case

  1. Read the split (5 + 5 + 5, 3 + 4 + 8) and give space in proportion.
  2. Underline clues: age, job, earning role, home (stairs, lives alone), post-op day, drains, settings, tests, what the patient wants.
  3. Functional diagnosis in one sentence, then the ICF table with a clinical reasoning column.
  4. Problem list by priority → SMART goals with time frames.
  5. Management by phase or impairment with technique, dose, reason; precautions and stop criteria; education, home programme.
  6. Outcome measures; discharge or progression criteria.

Doses with units (reps, sets, times a day, minutes, RPE, % HRR): a plan without doses loses marks.

Template: "[Age]-year-old [job] with [condition, day or stage], presenting with [3–4 impairments], leading to [activity limitations], restricting [participation], with [facilitator] and [barrier]." Mistakes: symptoms as participation; no contextual factors; describing disease not function; impairments not linked to treatment.

Goals and documentation

SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound; written as what the patient will do. Short-term: days to ~2 weeks ("clears sputum with ACBT within 3 days"). Long-term: ~6–12 weeks ("climbs to the 2nd floor with one rest by 8 weeks"). Patient-specific: bank manager drives 1 h to work by ~4 weeks; tiffin cook cooks for ten clients by 8–12 weeks; farmer walks to the field; milkman delivers door to door; schoolchild attends daily and plays. Link each goal to an impairment and each treatment to a goal ("because...").

SOAP: S what the patient reports; O measured findings and treatment given; A your interpretation and progress; P next steps. Also consent, precautions, adverse events, dated outcome measures.

Presentation: headings, numbered points, tables; underline keywords; labelled diagrams (ICF, ACBT cycle, drainage positions, CR phases, rule of nines, spirogram); always precautions; keep pathology short; leave lines for additions. Before handing in: every part answered? doses with units? time frames? precautions? diagram labelled? question numbers right?
