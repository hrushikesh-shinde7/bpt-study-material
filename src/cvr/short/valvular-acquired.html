Asked: 15 marks: pre- and post-implantation assessment and physio for a pacemaker and ICD in cardiomyopathy (S22). 7 marks: Phase 1 and 2 rehab after valve replacement (W21). 3 marks: NYHA grades (S21). Heart failure inside IHD cases (EF 25%, EF 40%).

Definitions and grades

Valve disease: stenosis (does not open fully) or regurgitation (does not close). Rheumatic heart disease: valve damage after rheumatic fever (untreated strep throat), mitral valve commonest. Heart failure: heart cannot pump enough for the body, or only at high filling pressure. Cardiomyopathy: dilated, hypertrophic, restrictive. Pacemaker stops the rate going too low; ICD detects dangerous fast rhythms and paces or shocks.

EF: reduced ≤ 40%; mildly reduced 41–49%; preserved ≥ 50%. NYHA (3m S21): I no limitation; II slight (ordinary activity → symptoms); III marked (less than ordinary → symptoms); IV symptoms at rest.

Mitral stenosisMid-diastolic rumble at apex; fast HR shortens filling → breathlessness, AF
Mitral regurgitationPansystolic at apex → axilla
Aortic stenosisEjection systolic, right 2nd space → neck; symptomatic severe AS = no exercise (fainting, sudden death)
Aortic regurgitationEarly diastolic, left sternal edge

"Open valves stenose, closed valves leak": in systole the aortic is open and mitral shut → systolic murmurs = AS or MR; diastolic = MS or AR.

Causes, pathophysiology, features

Causes: rheumatic fever (poverty, crowding), calcification (AS), endocarditis, bicuspid valve; HF from CAD, hypertension, valves, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, alcohol. Jones major: carditis, migrating polyarthritis, chorea, erythema marginatum, nodules. Pacemaker for heart block, sick sinus; ICD after arrest or EF ≤ 35% with symptoms.

Output cannot rise in exercise → fatigue; back-pressure → breathlessness, orthopnoea, night breathlessness, leg oedema, big liver; salt and water retention; skeletal muscle weakness (main target of training); arrhythmias.

Tests: echo (EF, valve area), ECG, Holter, X-ray (big heart, congestion, big left atrium in MS), BNP, exercise test or CPET (not in symptomatic severe AS), INR on warfarin.

Treatment: HFrEF four drugs (ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) + diuretics, ICD, CRT; RHD: benzathine penicillin, balloon valvotomy, repair or replacement (mechanical = lifelong warfarin; tissue; TAVI); device below the left clavicle with venous leads. Device complications: lead displacement (arm movement), pocket haematoma, pneumothorax, inappropriate shocks.

Pacemaker and ICD (S22: 34, teacher, cardiomyopathy)

Assess: EF, NYHA, drugs, device type and settings (rate response, ICD detection rate), recent shocks; symptoms, daily weight, oedema, orthopnoea; HR, rhythm, BP, SpO₂, crackles; wound and device-side shoulder; 6MWT with Borg. Outcomes: 6MWT, NYHA, MLHFQ or KCCQ, HADS.

ICF: ↓ tolerance, fatigue, breathlessness; wound pain, restricted shoulder; fear of shocks → lifting, reaching overhead (blackboard), stairs, brisk walking → teaching, household, driving rules; device clinic and family (facilitators).

  • Pre-implant: baseline shoulder range and 6MWT; explain the device, arm precautions, shock plan; breathing and mobility plan.
  • First 4–6 weeks: device-side arm not above shoulder height, no heavy lifting (leads fix); gentle shoulder movement below shoulder height, elbow, wrist, hand from day 1; walk from day 1; breathing exercises.
  • After 4–6 weeks: full shoulder range; exercise training.
  • ICD shock: stop, sit or lie; one shock and well → tell the clinic; more than one or unwell → emergency.

Exercise in heart failure; valve replacement (7m W21)

Training for all stable HF (ESC 2021, Class I): better capacity and QoL, fewer admissions. Aerobic 3–5 days a week, 20–60 min, start RPE 11–13, slow progress, intervals for the unfit; light resistance 2–3 days, 10–15 reps; IMT. ICD: train ≥ 10 beats below the detection rate. Fixed-rate pacemaker or chronotropic incompetence: use RPE and BP.

Valve replacement (sternotomy, as CABG): Phase 1 (5–7 days): pre-op teaching; deep breaths, supported huff, sit out day 0–1; walking room → corridor, stairs; RPE below 13, HR ≤ rest + 30; watch AF and heart block (after aortic surgery); sternal precautions. Phase 2 (8–12 weeks): test first, start low; aerobic 3–5 days to 30–60 min at RPE 11–14; leg resistance early, arms after ~6–8 weeks. Mechanical valve: warfarin (gentle handling, fall prevention, no contact sport or vigorous percussion); endocarditis prevention (teeth, skin); MS leaves pulmonary hypertension and AF (slower); TAVI: no sternal limits, groin care, frailer patients.

Precautions

Devices: no shortwave or microwave diathermy; no TENS, IFT or stimulation near the device or across the chest without device-team advice; no ultrasound over it; no strong magnets. No exercise if: decompensated HF (breathless at rest, new orthopnoea, 2 kg gain in 3 days, more oedema), symptomatic severe AS, uncontrolled arrhythmia, rest chest pain, recent shocks. HCM: avoid dehydration and heavy straining. Educate: daily morning weight, salt and fluid advice, never miss penicillin in RHD, carry the device card, phone away from the pocket.
