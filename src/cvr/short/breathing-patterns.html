Asked: 3 marks: Cheyne–Stokes respiration (S16); three abnormal breathing patterns (S22). Draw the shape and write one cause: a full answer for each pattern.

Breathing patterns

TachypnoeaAbove ~20/min: fever, pneumonia, oedema, PE, anxiety, pain, ILD
BradypnoeaBelow ~12/min: opioids, sedatives, raised ICP, hypothermia, hypothyroidism
KussmaulDeep, fast, laboured, regular: metabolic acidosis (DKA, kidney failure) blowing off CO₂
Cheyne–Stokes (S16)Breaths gradually deepen then become shallow, then an apnoea of seconds; repeats: heart failure (slow circulation delays chemoreceptor feedback), stroke, brain injury, altitude, opioids; normal in sleeping infants and elderly
Biot's (ataxic)Groups of quick equal breaths with irregular pauses (ataxic: completely irregular): medulla damage (meningitis, raised ICP, brainstem stroke, opioids): grave sign
ApneusticDeep gasping inspiration held, short expiration: pons damage
Central neurogenic hyperventilationSustained deep rapid breathing: midbrain, upper pons
ParadoxicalPart moves the wrong way: flail chest (in on inspiration); diaphragm paralysis or fatigue (abdomen in on inspiration); obstruction
Prolonged expirationPursed lips: COPD, asthma
Sighing, upper chestDysfunctional breathing, hyperventilation

Orthopnoea (lying flat), PND (wakes breathless), platypnoea (upright, better lying).

Chest signs

  • Barrel chest (↑ AP diameter): hyperinflation (emphysema, chronic asthma).
  • Pectus excavatum or carinatum, kyphoscoliosis: restriction when severe.
  • Accessory muscles, intercostal indrawing, tracheal tug: ↑ work of breathing.
  • ↓ Expansion one side = disease on that side.
  • Trachea pulled towards collapse or fibrosis; pushed away by large effusion or tension pneumothorax.
  • Clubbing: bronchiectasis, CF, abscess, empyema, cancer, ILD, cyanotic heart disease (not COPD alone).
  • Central cyanosis (tongue, lips) = low arterial O₂; peripheral only = poor circulation or cold.

Added sounds

  • Wheeze: musical, narrowed airways (asthma, COPD); fixed single wheeze = local obstruction.
  • Coarse crackles: secretions in larger airways (bronchiectasis, infection); change after a cough: a sign for airway clearance.
  • Fine crackles: late inspiratory "Velcro" (fibrosis); fluid (pulmonary oedema).
  • Pleural rub: creaking with breathing (pleurisy).
  • Stridor: harsh inspiratory noise, upper airway narrowing: emergency.
