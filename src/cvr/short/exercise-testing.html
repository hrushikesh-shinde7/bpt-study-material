Why test; types

Why: diagnosis (ischaemia, arrhythmia, desaturation, bronchospasm); prognosis and risk stratification; functional capacity (METs, distance); prescription (safe HR, walking speed, oxygen need); outcome. Types: laboratory (treadmill, cycle with ECG; CPET with gas analysis) and field (6MWT, shuttle, step tests).

Contraindications

Acute heart, acute clot, tight valve

Absolute: MI within 2 days; ongoing unstable angina; uncontrolled symptomatic arrhythmia; active endocarditis; symptomatic severe aortic stenosis; decompensated heart failure; acute PE, pulmonary infarction or DVT; acute myocarditis or pericarditis; acute aortic dissection; disability preventing safe testing.

Relative: left main stenosis; moderate–severe AS with unclear symptoms; tachyarrhythmia with uncontrolled rate; complete or advanced heart block; HOCM with severe gradient; recent stroke or TIA; mental impairment; resting BP above 200/110; uncorrected anaemia, electrolytes, hyperthyroidism.

Bruce protocol (3m S16)

Symptom-limited treadmill test; speed and gradient rise every 3 min; ECG, BP, symptoms; result = time, stage (METs), ischaemia, arrhythmia, BP response.

Modified Bruce (elderly, deconditioned, early post-MI): two extra starting stages at 1.7 mph, 0% then 5%, avoiding the big first jump.

Cycle ergometer (3m S22): for: exact workload in watts, independent of weight; still upper body (easy ECG, BP); safer with poor balance, obesity, orthopaedic problems; cheaper, quieter. Against: thighs tire first, so peak VO₂ ~10–20% lower than treadmill; unfamiliar; less walking-specific.

Field tests (3m W22)

6MWT: flat straight course ≥ 30 m; as far as possible in 6 min at own pace, rests allowed, standard encouragement each minute; record distance, SpO₂, HR, Borg before and after, stops; practice test. Don't test: unstable angina or MI in the past month; caution resting HR above 120, BP above 180/100. Stop: chest pain, intolerable breathlessness, cramps, staggering, sweating, pallor. MCID ~25–33 m (often 30 m) in chronic lung disease.

ISWT: 10 m course round two cones, paced by beeps, speed up every minute through 12 levels; ends when the cone is missed: maximal, relates to peak VO₂. ESWT: constant speed from ISWT; endurance time; sensitive to rehab.

Harvard step test: 20-inch (~50 cm) step, 30 steps/min for 5 min; 30-s pulse counts at 1–1.5, 2–2.5, 3–3.5 min. Index = (seconds × 100) ÷ (2 × sum of counts): below 55 poor, 55–64 low average, 65–79 high average, 80–89 good, ≥ 90 excellent. Example: 300 s, counts 70 + 60 + 50 → 30000 ÷ 360 = 83 (good). Healthy people only.
