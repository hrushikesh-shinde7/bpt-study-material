Asked: 7 marks: complications of bed immobility in ICU (W17), effects of bed rest on body systems (S24), cardiovascular and pulmonary effects (S26). 3 marks: one effect on three systems (S19, W21).

Why bed rest harms

Lying flat removes two loads: gravity along the body (trains BP reflexes and fluid balance) and muscle work against gravity (keeps muscle, bone, fitness). Every system adapts downwards within days; fastest in the old and critically ill.

Numbers: 3 weeks of bed rest cut VO₂max by ~27% (Dallas 1968); rectus femoris shrank ~12.5% in the first week of critical illness (Puthucheary 2013); strict bed rest loses ~1–1.5% strength a day.

ICU-acquired weakness (W17): generalised symmetrical limb weakness in critical illness with no other cause; MRC sum score below 48/60; risks: sepsis, organ failure, long ventilation, immobility, high sugar, steroids, paralysing drugs; slows weaning, affects walking and work for months.

Prevention: early mobilisation

  • Safety check (ICU criteria) → turns every 2–4 h, head up, upright sitting.
  • ROM: passive → active-assisted → active; bed cycling, bridging, resisted exercise.
  • Edge of bed → standing (tilt table if BP falls) → chair → marching → walking (with the ventilator if needed).
  • Deep breathing, airway clearance, IMT. Monitor HR, BP, SpO₂, RR, symptoms before, during, after.

Current: bed rest is a treatment with side effects: use only when needed and briefly; early mobilisation is safe and shortens delirium and recovery.
