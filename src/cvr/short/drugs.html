Asked: not on its own; needed in every case answer and viva. One line on a drug's effect on your plan (for example "on a beta-blocker, so intensity by RPE 11–13") shows clinical reasoning.

Heart and BP drugs

Beta-blockers (metoprolol, bisoprolol)↓ HR, BP, angina, arrhythmia → Karvonen overestimates: RPE, talk test; fatigue; non-selective → bronchospasm in asthma
ACEi, ARBs (ramipril, losartan); sacubitril–valsartan↓ BP, protect heart and kidney → postural and post-exercise hypotension, longer cool-down; ACEi dry cough
Diuretics (furosemide, spironolactone)Dehydration, postural dizziness, low K⁺ (cramps, arrhythmia); treat after the morning dose has worked
Nitrates (GTN, isosorbide)Relieve angina → carry GTN, take it sitting; headache; before a known trigger
CCBs (amlodipine; diltiazem, verapamil)Ankle swelling, hypotension; slower HR response (diltiazem, verapamil)
Antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel, ticagrelor)Bruising, bleeding; never advise stopping
Anticoagulants (heparin, warfarin, apixaban)Gentle handling, care with percussion, suction, falls; INR often 2–3 (higher for mechanical valves)
Thrombolytics (streptokinase, tenecteplase)High bleeding risk day 1: no vigorous chest physio
StatinsReport new muscle aches or weakness
DigoxinToxicity: nausea, confusion, visual change, slow irregular pulse (worse with low K⁺)
AmiodaroneSlow HR; lung fibrosis, thyroid problems (new cough, breathlessness)
Inotropes, vasopressorsHigh or rising doses: no active mobilisation

Respiratory drugs

SABA (salbutamol)Reliever: give 15–30 min before clearance or exercise; keep at hand; tremor, tachycardia
LABA (salmeterol, formoterol)12–24 h; not for sudden relief (except formoterol combinations in asthma)
Antimuscarinics (ipratropium; tiotropium)Bronchodilation, less mucus (COPD); dry mouth
Inhaled steroids (budesonide, fluticasone)Rinse mouth (thrush, hoarse voice); spacer
Oral steroids (prednisolone)Long courses: osteoporosis (care with percussion, falls), weakness, high sugar, thin skin, slow healing
Theophylline, doxofyllineNarrow margin: palpitations, nausea, tremor, seizures
MontelukastAsthma add-on; mood or sleep changes
Mucoactives (NAC, carbocisteine, hypertonic saline, dornase)Clearance after them; hypertonic saline → bronchodilator first
AntibioticsInhaled ones after clearance

Order (CF, bronchiectasis): bronchodilator → mucoactive → clearance → inhaled antibiotic or steroid.

Other drugs

  • Insulin, sulfonylureas: hypos during and hours after exercise: check sugar, carry glucose. Metformin, gliptins, SGLT2 inhibitors: low hypo risk; SGLT2 dehydration.
  • Anti-TB: isoniazid neuropathy (pyridoxine); rifampicin orange urine and tears, interactions; ethambutol blurred vision; liver toxicity: report.
  • Opioids, analgesia: treat 30–60 min after oral doses; drowsiness, slow breathing; epidurals: leg weakness, low BP sitting up.
  • ICU sedatives, paralysis: no movement or cough; active work in sedation breaks. Pyridostigmine: treat ~1 h after the dose.

Timing physio around medicines

Bronchodilator 15–30 min before clearance or exercise; analgesia before post-op chest physio; exercise 1–2 h after a meal with insulin, sugar before and after; GTN before a known trigger; after the morning diuretic has worked; coordinate sedation (tetanus, ICU) with the nurse.
