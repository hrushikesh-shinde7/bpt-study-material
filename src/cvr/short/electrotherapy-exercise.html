Pain

TENS after chest surgery: electrodes either side of and parallel to the thoracotomy, sternotomy or abdominal wound (not on it); conventional ~80–100 Hz, short pulses, strong comfortable tingling, during breathing exercises and coughing. Better than standard analgesia alone (review of 11 trials); an add-on. Also: pillow-supported cough, wound-up side-lying, relaxed sitting; cold for acute chest-wall pain; careful heat for neck and shoulder spasm; relaxation.

Wounds and weak muscles

Wound modalities: high-voltage pulsed current (best evidence for slow pressure ulcers), ultrasound, UVC, low-level laser: parameters on the Diabetes sheet.

NMES of quadriceps: strong contractions without breathlessness: severe COPD, heart failure, ICU, after major surgery. Mixed evidence (COPD gains in 2016 meta-analysis; 2014 review no clear benefit; small gain after surgery 2023): a bridge to active exercise. Electrodes over quadriceps, smooth strong contraction, on–off cycles, 20–30 min several times a week.

Precautions

Pacemaker or ICD: no shortwave or microwave; TENS only away from chest and device with advice. Ischaemic limb: no heat (burns); check sensation and circulation. Anticoagulants: gentle. DVT: no massage or heat on that limb. Cancer: nothing over a tumour. Never over the anterior neck (carotid sinus), pregnant abdomen, broken or numb skin, or in a patient who cannot report discomfort.

Therapeutic exercise

MobilityShoulder after thoracotomy, mastectomy, CABG (within precautions); thoracic mobility; burns scar stretch

VenousAnkle pumps, heel raises, walking, elevation, ankle range, with compression
LymphaticRhythmic exercise in compression, deep breathing, slowly progressive resistance

Buerger–Allen (classic): legs up ≥ 45° until pale (1–3 min) → hang down until pink (2–5 min) moving ankles and toes → flat, covered, ~5 min; repeat several cycles, a few times a day. Weak evidence; walking is first-line; avoid in rest pain, ulcer, gangrene.
