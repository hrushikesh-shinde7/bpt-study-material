Asked: 3 marks: any six contraindications to postural drainage (S18); 7 marks: precautions and contraindications in a neonate (W18). Drainage positions are part of every bronchiectasis long answer.

What it is

Postural drainage (gravity-assisted positioning): the affected segment's bronchus points down towards the trachea so gravity helps secretions reach the large airways, where they are huffed out. Always combined with a breathing technique (ACBT), ± percussion, vibration, shaking. Classic: 11–12 positions, many head-down. Modified: same side-lying and prone positions without head-down tilt (infants, reflux, breathlessness).

Dose: ~10 min per productive position, session 20–40 min, once or twice a day; more in infection; not after meals.

Lie on the healthy side for lateral segments; turn towards prone for posterior, towards supine for anterior; foot up for lower lobes, middle lobe and lingula. Angles vary between books (foot raised ~35–45 cm); direction matters most.

Indications: large sputum (bronchiectasis, CF, abscess); retained secretions or collapse of a known lobe; patients too weak or young for active techniques (modified positions).

Contraindications to head-down tilt (3m S18)

  • Recent or active haemoptysis
  • Raised ICP, recent head injury or neurosurgery
  • Uncontrolled hypertension, unstable heart, arrhythmia, pulmonary oedema, heart failure
  • Severe breathlessness or bronchospasm
  • Reflux, hiatus hernia, recent oesophageal surgery, just after meals
  • Aortic aneurysm, recent eye surgery
  • Gross obesity, abdominal distension, late pregnancy
  • Untreated pneumothorax, large effusion or empyema, rib fractures, painful recent surgery

Use modified positions instead. Neonates (W18): no head-down (reflux, IVH in preterms), minimal handling: see Neonatal.

To percussion and vibration: rib fractures, flail chest, osteoporosis, bone metastases, recent spinal surgery; haemoptysis, low platelets; unstable BP or rhythm; bronchospasm; burns, fresh grafts, wounds, new pacemaker; surgical emphysema; intolerable pain.

Percussion, vibration, shaking

  • Percussion: rhythmic clapping with cupped hands, relaxed wrists, over a thin towel, 30 s to a few minutes, alternating with BC or TEE: air cushion sends an energy wave that loosens mucus.
  • Vibration: fine rapid shaking from tensed arms with gentle compression, on expiration only: ↑ expiratory flow.
  • Shaking: coarser rhythmic bouncing compression on expiration after a deep breath: thick mucus.
  • Adjuncts only, if secretions and helpful. Infants: fingertips or soft cup, no tilt. Ventilated: on expiration, often after hyperinflation.

Current (BTS/ACPRC 2009): active techniques preferred; head-down only when helpful and safe; percussion not routine. Write: "gravity-assisted positions for the affected lobe with ACBT; modified positions where contraindicated; percussion and vibration as adjuncts".
