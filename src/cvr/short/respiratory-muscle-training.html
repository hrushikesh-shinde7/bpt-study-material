Asked: 3 marks: two types of inspiratory muscle training (S18). 7 marks: exercise programme to strengthen respiratory muscles (W22); diaphragm recruitment (S21, see Breathing exercises).

Why and who

Breathing muscles may be weak or overloaded (stiff lungs, obstruction, hyperinflation) → breathlessness, poor exercise tolerance, weak cough, weaning failure. Like limb muscles they respond to progressive overload.

Measure: MIP (PImax) and MEP with a mouth pressure meter. MIP stronger than ~−80 cmH₂O rules out important weakness; in COPD MIP weaker than ~60 marks those who gain most.

Who: COPD with weak inspiratory muscles, heart failure, before high-risk heart, lung or abdominal surgery, ICU weaning failure, early neuromuscular disease and SCI, dysfunctional breathing, athletes.

Types (3m S18)

Threshold loadingSpring-loaded valve opens only at a set pressure; load independent of flow; easy to set as % MIP; most used
Inspiratory resistiveBreathing in through a small opening; load depends on flow, so a set pattern or target device is needed
Normocapnic hyperpnoeaHigh ventilation for 10–30 min with a rebreathing bag (normal CO₂): endurance; needs equipment
Tapered-flow resistiveElectronic device varies resistance through the breath: heavier loads, costly
Expiratory trainingThreshold device on expiration: cough, voice, swallowing (neuromuscular, Parkinson's, MS)

Classic: small weights on the upper abdomen in supine; manual diaphragmatic resistance.

Dose (threshold IMT)

  • Intensity: start ~30% MIP, progress to 50–60%+; re-measure MIP every 1–2 weeks.
  • Volume: ~30 breaths (or 15 min), twice a day.
  • Frequency: 5–7 days a week, ≥ 6–8 weeks, then maintenance.
  • Technique: upright, nose clip if needed, quick strong breath in through the device, slow relaxed breath out, rest between sets.

Evidence: alone, IMT improves strength, endurance, breathlessness and walking, most in weak patients (2011); added to full pulmonary rehab it raised strength but not exercise capacity (2018); helps ICU weaning failure (2015). An add-on, not a replacement for whole-body exercise.

Write: "IMT at ≥ 30% MIP, ~30 breaths twice a day for ≥ 8 weeks, for inspiratory weakness, alongside general exercise".

Programme to strengthen respiratory muscles (7m W22)

  1. Assess: MIP, MEP, pattern, expansion, peak cough flow, Borg, mMRC, posture.
  2. Diaphragmatic breathing with facilitation and manual resistance, half-lying → sitting → standing.
  3. Threshold IMT at the dose above.
  4. Expiratory training and huffing if cough is weak; abdominal strengthening (bracing, curl-ups as allowed).
  5. Accessory and postural muscles: scapular and upper-limb exercise (arm ergometer, resisted patterns), thoracic mobility.
  6. Whole-body aerobic training: the ventilatory load also trains breathing muscles.
  7. Endurance: normocapnic hyperpnoea; singing or wind-instrument games for children.
  8. Review every 1–2 weeks; re-measure MIP; adjust.

Precautions

Recent or untreated pneumothorax; unhealed chest surgery; acute COPD or asthma exacerbation; unstable heart failure, recent MI; perforated eardrum; raised ICP; active haemoptysis. Stop with fatigue (harmful in neuromuscular disease), dizziness, breathlessness.
