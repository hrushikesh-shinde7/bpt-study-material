Anatomy and coronary arteries

Middle mediastinum; apex beat left 5th space, mid-clavicular line. Right side: RA → tricuspid → RV → pulmonary valve → lungs (low pressure). Left: LA → mitral → LV → aortic valve → body (high pressure, thickest wall). Pericardium: effusion or tamponade restricts filling.

Veins → coronary sinus. Sympathetic β1 speeds and strengthens; vagus slows.

Conduction

SA node (right atrium, natural pacemaker 60–100/min) → atria contract (P wave) → AV node (delay ~0.1 s lets ventricles fill: PR interval; backup 40–60/min) → bundle of His → right and left bundle branches → Purkinje fibres → ventricles contract (QRS; backup 20–40/min). T wave = ventricular repolarisation. Lower pacemakers are slower: why complete heart block gives a slow pulse.

Cardiac cycle and sounds

Systole: isovolumetric contraction, ejection. Diastole: isovolumetric relaxation, rapid filling, atrial top-up. LV 120/0–10, aorta 120/80; RV 25/0–5, PA 25/10 mmHg.

S1 mitral and tricuspid close (start of systole); S2 aortic and pulmonary close, splits on inspiration; S3 rapid filling into a failing ventricle (normal in the young); S4 atrial kick into a stiff ventricle (hypertension, ischaemia).

LV perfusion happens mainly in diastole (~⅔ of each beat at rest); tachycardia shortens diastole while demand rises: why fast rates bring angina and beta-blockers help.

Contractility↑ sympathetic; ↓ damaged muscle

Frank–Starling: more filling → stronger contraction (to a limit); the failing heart is on a flatter curve. BP = CO × peripheral resistance; MAP ≈ diastolic + ⅓ pulse pressure (120/80 → ~93). Baroreceptors (carotid sinus, aortic arch) correct a fall on standing; fail after bed rest, drugs, autonomic damage → postural hypotension. O₂ demand rises with HR, BP, wall tension, contractility: rate–pressure product = HR × systolic.

Physiology behind physio decisions

  • Diastolic coronary flow, RPP → stay below the angina HR; long warm-up; no sudden bursts.
  • Valsalva swings BP → breathe out on effort; no breath-holding when lifting.
  • RCA territory → inferior MI: watch slow HR and block when mobilising.
  • Frank–Starling, failing heart → lower intensity, intervals, watch weight gain.
  • Baroreceptors → sit up in stages after bed rest; postural BP.
  • Muscle pump → ankle pumps; cool down slowly (pooling).
  • Autonomic HR control → beta-blockers, pacemakers, transplant: use RPE.
