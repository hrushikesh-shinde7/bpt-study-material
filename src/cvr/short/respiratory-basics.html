Airways and lobes

Nose warms, humidifies, filters; carina at the sternal angle (T4–T5); right main bronchus wider, shorter, more vertical (aspiration goes right). ~23 generations: 0–16 conducting (dead space ~150 mL), 17–23 respiratory. Type I alveolar cells exchange gas; type II make surfactant (stops small alveoli collapsing); macrophages clear particles. Mucociliary escalator slowed by smoking, dry gas, infection.

Right lung 3 lobes (oblique + horizontal fissures), left 2 (upper with lingula, lower). Segments: right upper apical, posterior, anterior; middle lateral, medial; lower superior + 4 basal (medial, anterior, lateral, posterior). Left upper apicoposterior, anterior; lingula superior, inferior; lower superior, anteromedial, lateral, posterior basal.

Surface markings: oblique fissure from T3 spine round to the 6th costal cartilage (lower lobes mostly behind); horizontal fissure along the 4th costal cartilage; lung border 6th rib (MCL), 8th (MAL), 10th (back); pleura two ribs lower. Nerves: vagus constricts, β2 dilates (salbutamol), phrenic C3–C5. Pulmonary artery ~25/10 mmHg.

Mechanics

Pump handleUpper ribs 1–6, sternum forward and up: ↑ AP diameter
Bucket handleLower ribs 7–10 swing up and out: ↑ transverse
CaliperFloating ribs 11–12 outwards
Diaphragm descent↑ vertical: biggest share (~¾ of quiet VT)

Zone of apposition: diaphragm against the lower ribs lifts and widens them. Hyperinflation flattens it → Hoover's sign; forward lean re-domes it.

Muscles: quiet inspiration: diaphragm, external and parasternal intercostals, scalenes. Forced inspiration: SCM, scalenes, upper trapezius, pectorals (arms fixed), serratus, back extensors. Quiet expiration: passive recoil. Forced expiration and cough: abdominals, internal intercostals, latissimus.

Pressures: pleural −5 at rest, −7.5 end-inspiration; alveolar −1 in, +1 out (cmH₂O). Compliance: lungs ~200, lungs + chest ~110 mL/cmH₂O; low in fibrosis, oedema, obesity, deformity; high in emphysema. Resistance mainly in medium bronchi. Work of breathing 3–5% of energy at rest.

Volumes (young adult man)

TV 0.5 LIRV 3.0 LERV 1.1 LRV 1.2 L
IC = TV + IRV 3.5FRC = ERV + RV 2.3VC = IRV + TV + ERV 4.6TLC 5.8

RV, FRC, TLC need body box or dilution. FRC falls after surgery, with pain, obesity, lying flat → basal airway closure → collapse; restore with upright positions, deep breaths, walking. Minute ventilation = VT × rate (~6 L/min).

Gas exchange, transport, control

Diffusion slows with a thick (fibrosis, oedema) or small (emphysema, resection) membrane. O₂: 98.5% on Hb, 1.5% dissolved; 1 g Hb carries 1.34 mL (anaemia ↓ delivery despite normal SpO₂). CO₂: bicarbonate ~70%, carbamino ~23%, dissolved ~7%. Bohr: CO₂ and acid release O₂ in tissues. Haldane: O₂ binding releases CO₂ in lungs (why high O₂ raises CO₂ in COPD). Dissociation curve: flat top; SpO₂ stays above 90% until PaO₂ ~60, then falls fast: why 60 mmHg defines failure.

Control: medulla (rhythm); pons (pneumotaxic fine-tuning); central chemoreceptors (CO₂ via CSF acidity: main drive); peripheral (carotid, aortic: low O₂, strongly below ~60); stretch (Hering–Breuer), irritant, J receptors; cortex (voluntary) and limbic (anxiety).

Physiology behind techniques

Collateral ventilation (pores of Kohn, canals of Lambert, channels of Martin) → deep breaths with hold, SMI, PEP. Equal pressure point → mid-volume huffs. Gravity → positioning, drainage. Zone of apposition → forward lean, binder. FRC → upright, walking. Surfactant → regular deep breaths. Mucociliary clearance → humidification, hydration, quitting. Chemoreceptors, Haldane → 88–92% in COPD. Cortical control → breathing control, pursed lips, retraining.
