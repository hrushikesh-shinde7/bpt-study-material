Asked: not on its own in 2016–2026 (inhalation burns, ventilated pneumonia and ARDS come closest). Possible: 7 marks on organophosphate poisoning or shock; 3 marks on X-ray of pulmonary oedema or MODS and SOFA.

Pulmonary oedema

Cardiogenic (failing LV, fluid overload: high capillary pressure) or non-cardiogenic (leaky capillaries: ARDS, drowning, smoke, altitude, head injury). Severe breathlessness, orthopnoea, night breathlessness, sweating; pink frothy sputum; fine bibasal crackles. X-ray: bat's wing haze from both hila, Kerley B lines, big heart, effusions. Sit up, oxygen, diuretics, nitrates; CPAP or NIV relieves breathlessness and cuts intubation (Cochrane 2013).

Physio: high sitting, legs down; breathing control, reassurance; help with the CPAP mask. Froth is oedema, not secretions: no airway clearance (unless infection). Later mobilise and heart failure rehab. Never flat or head-down; watch BP (diuretics, nitrates).

Shock

Tissues not getting enough O₂: fast pulse, systolic below ~90 or MAP below 65, cold clammy skin (warm in early sepsis), urine below 0.5 mL/kg/h, confusion, lactate above 2.

Sepsis-3: life-threatening organ dysfunction from a dysregulated response to infection. Septic shock: needs vasopressors for MAP ≥ 65 with lactate above 2 despite fluids. Fluids, noradrenaline, antibiotics within 1 h.

Physio: unstable: no active mobilisation; positioning, pressure care, gentle passive movement if BP allows; chest physio only for clear need, short. As support weans: follow the safety traffic lights and the mobilisation ladder. Survivors: months of weakness → strength and endurance training. Turning and sitting can drop BP: stop if MAP falls, pulse races, confusion, clamminess.

Multiple organ failure (MODS)

Progressive failure of two or more organ systems needing support; after sepsis, trauma, burns, pancreatitis, major surgery, shock. SOFA scores six: breathing (P/F), clotting (platelets), liver (bilirubin), circulation (MAP, pressors), brain (GCS), kidney (creatinine, urine).

Physio by organ: lungs: ARDS plan; circulation: mobilise only with stable MAP on low support; low platelets and liver: no percussion, gentle suction; dialysis: femoral lines don't stop exercise (secure them), watch BP during dialysis; brain: orientation, family, routine; muscles: early ROM, splints, mobilisation. Post-intensive care syndrome: weakness, fatigue, memory problems, anxiety, nightmares → follow-up rehab.

Poisoning

Organophosphates block acetylcholinesterase. Muscarinic: pinpoint pupils, sweating, salivation, lacrimation, vomiting, diarrhoea, urination, slow heart, bronchorrhoea, bronchospasm. Nicotinic: fasciculations → paralysis incl. breathing muscles. Brain: confusion, seizures, coma. Intermediate syndrome at 24–96 h: neck flexor, proximal, cranial and breathing weakness. Atropine until the chest is dry, HR above 80, systolic above 80, pupils not pinpoint, axillae dry.

Physio: frequent suction and positioning (aspiration pneumonia common), head up; daily neck flexion test (lift the head off the pillow): new weakness warns of the intermediate syndrome; then mobilise. Every self-poisoning needs mental health review.

Snakebite (krait, cobra): descending paralysis from ptosis; antivenom, ventilation: treat like neuromuscular weakness. Paraquat: lung fibrosis, high O₂ worsens it: oxygen only as prescribed. Kerosene (children): aspiration pneumonitis. Overdose: aspiration, pressure palsies (foot drop) after long lying.

Aspiration, drowning, smoke

Aspiration pneumonitis (chemical, Mendelson's, hours) vs pneumonia (mouth bacteria, days); reduced consciousness, stroke, vomiting, tube feeds flat. Usually the right lung (wider, more vertical bronchus): basal segments upright; posterior upper and superior lower segments supine. Physio: head up 30–45°, side-lying, suction and clearance of those segments, swallow referral, oral care.

Drowning (WHO 2002): respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion. Hypoxaemia, surfactant washout → collapse, oedema, ARDS; aspiration; brain injury; hypothermia; neck injury in diving. Physio as ARDS (no high pressures), spinal precautions, later neuro rehab.

Smoke: upper airway swelling (early intubation), lower airway injury; CO: oximeter falsely normal, check COHb, high-flow reservoir mask; cyanide. Physio: upright, humidified O₂, deep breathing, ACBT, suction, walking (see Burns). PE: sudden breathlessness, pleuritic pain; mobilise once anticoagulated and stable.
