Asked: 15-mark cases, usually post-TB (functional diagnosis + short-term + long-term, or reasons for impairments + rehab plan). 7 marks: right lower lobe physiotherapy, ACBT vs autogenic drainage, management, ergonomic and energy-conservation advice, mucus removal techniques. 3 marks: clinical features.

Definition and types

Bronchiectasis: permanent, abnormal widening of the bronchi with daily wet cough, sputum and repeated infections. Widened scarred airways cannot clear mucus, so airway clearance is the core of physio.

Shape (Reid): cylindrical (mildest), varicose (beaded), cystic or saccular (most severe, most sputum, fluid levels). Extent: localised (one lobe: after TB, foreign body, pneumonia) or diffuse (CF, immune deficiency, ciliary disorders). Severity: Bronchiectasis Severity Index, FACED.

Causes: post-TB (commonest in India), severe pneumonia, childhood measles or whooping cough; blockage (foreign body, tumour, nodes); cystic fibrosis; primary ciliary dyskinesia (Kartagener's: + sinusitis, reversed organs); immune deficiency; ABPA; rheumatoid arthritis; aspiration; idiopathic.

Vicious cycle (Cole): reasons for impairments (S19)

Infection (e.g. TB) damages the wall → inflammatory enzymes destroy elastic and muscle → permanent dilatation → cilia destroyed, mucus cannot clear → mucus pools, bacteria colonise → more infection and inflammation → repeat. Physio breaks the cycle by removing pooled mucus.

  • Copious productive cough: pooled mucus. Foul sputum: anaerobes in stagnant mucus.
  • Breathlessness, ↓ exercise tolerance: obstruction by mucus, narrowed airways, deconditioning.
  • Haemoptysis: enlarged fragile bronchial arteries.
  • Stress incontinence: repeated coughing raises abdominal pressure on the pelvic floor.
  • Fatigue, weight loss, low mood, embarrassment. Tracheal shift towards a fibrosed, collapsed lobe (S21: shift to the left = left disease).

Clinical features (3m W19, W20)

Chronic cough with large amounts of mucopurulent sputum, worse in the morning and on changing position; sputum settles in three layers (froth, mucus, pus); recurrent infections; haemoptysis; breathlessness, wheeze, fatigue, weight loss, sinus symptoms. Signs: clubbing, coarse crackles that change after coughing, wheeze, ↓ expansion over a fibrosed lobe, halitosis.

Investigations

  • HRCT (gold standard): bronchus wider than its artery (signet ring), non-tapering airways, cysts: tells which segments to drain.
  • X-ray: tram lines, ring shadows, cysts with fluid levels; collapse or fibrosis pulling the trachea.
  • Sputum culture (Pseudomonas = worse disease; infection control); spirometry (usually obstructive); ABG, SpO₂; tests for cause (immunoglobulins, aspergillus, sweat test, TB).

Medical and surgical management

Airway clearance taught by a physio for every productive patient, and pulmonary rehab for reduced exercise capacity (ERS 2025, strong). Antibiotics (14 days in exacerbations; long-term macrolide or inhaled); nebulised hypertonic or normal saline before clearance; bronchodilators; treat the cause; vaccines. Surgery: lobectomy for localised resistant disease or major haemoptysis; bronchial artery embolisation.

Complications

Exacerbations (more clearance, action plan); haemoptysis (stop percussion, vibration and head-down; gentle breathing control; report; large bleed = emergency); respiratory failure, cor pulmonale; abscess, empyema, pneumothorax; stress incontinence (pelvic floor, "the knack"); social isolation.

PT assessment

Sputum: daily amount (teaspoons, cups), colour, smell, thickness, blood; best time and position. Cough effectiveness; mMRC; exacerbations a year; current routine; past TB; ask about incontinence; job and home. Posture, clubbing, pattern, chest shape; expansion, trachea; coarse crackles over the lobe, rechecked after treatment; SpO₂ at rest and exercise; 6MWT or shuttle.

Outcome measures: sputum weight or volume, Leicester Cough Questionnaire, QOL-B, SGRQ, 6MWT, mMRC.

ICF (S19: 50, clerk, post-TB right lower lobe, foul sputum, cough and leak on laughing or talking)

  • Body function: copious foul sputum, poor clearance; paroxysmal cough; breathlessness, ↓ exercise tolerance; pelvic floor weakness; ↓ right lower chest expansion; anxiety about work.
  • Activity: talking or laughing long, walking and stairs at her pace, housework.
  • Participation: clerk work with the public; social gatherings (cough, sputum, leakage).
  • Environment: desk job, TB treated, care access (facilitators); dusty crowded office, no private place to clear sputum (barriers). Personal: 50, motivated, worried.

Goals: short-term (2–4 weeks): independent ACBT in the right lower lobe position twice a day; clearer chest; walks 20 min; correct pelvic floor contraction and "the knack". Long-term (3 months+): daily routine kept, fewer exacerbations, better 6MWT, leakage stopped, full workdays and social life.

Airway clearance (the core)

Dose: 10–30 min, once or twice a day, more in exacerbations; the technique the patient will actually do. Order: bronchodilator → nebulised saline → clearance → inhaled antibiotic. Combine with the lobe's drainage position if tolerated.

ACBT: breathing control → 3–4 thoracic expansion breaths with a 3-s hold (air gets behind mucus via collateral channels) → breathing control → 1–2 huffs from mid lung volume (mouth open, "steaming a mirror") → breathing control; huff from high volume or cough when mucus is central. Repeat until clear or two cycles bring nothing.

Autogenic drainage: breathing at low volume ("unstick"), mid ("collect"), high ("evacuate"); open-mouth breath out; cough suppressed until mucus is central. PEP, flutter, Acapella: breathing out against resistance splints airways; oscillation vibrates mucus; good for home and work. Postural drainage with percussion and vibration: if helpful and not contraindicated.

Right lower lobe (W16, S19): prone foot-up (posterior basal), left side quarter forward foot-up (lateral basal), prone flat (superior); ACBT in each.

No head-down tilt (S18, any 6): haemoptysis; raised ICP or recent head injury; uncontrolled BP, unstable heart, arrhythmia, pulmonary oedema; severe breathlessness or bronchospasm; reflux, hiatus hernia, after meals; aortic aneurysm, recent eye surgery; obesity, distension, late pregnancy; untreated pneumothorax, large effusion, rib fractures. Use flat or sitting positions instead.

Exercise, pelvic floor, ergonomics

  • Pulmonary rehab as in COPD (6–12 weeks, ≥ 2 supervised sessions a week); clear the chest before exercise; IMT if weak.
  • Pelvic floor: slow holds up to 10 s × 10 and quick contractions, 3 times a day; tighten before coughing ("the knack").
  • Breathing control, posture, thoracic mobility; drink enough water.
  • Left lung disease, normal right (7m W22): rest on the right side (diseased lung up drains; healthy lung down gets more blood flow and better gas exchange); clear the chest before work, private huffing break; upright sitting, desk at elbow height; pacing, sit for chores, trolleys; avoid dust and crowds, mask.

Precautions and stop

Haemoptysis: no percussion, vibration or head-down. Bronchodilator before clearance in bronchospasm. Infection control: safe sputum disposal, separate Pseudomonas patients, clean devices daily; confirm TB is not infectious. Stop and report: fresh bleeding beyond streaks, falling SpO₂, severe breathlessness or wheeze, sudden chest pain.

Education and prognosis

Clearance every day, even on good days; sputum diary and action plan; stop smoking; daily exercise; clean nebulisers; pelvic floor daily; vaccines. Worse with Pseudomonas, frequent exacerbations, low FEV1, many lobes, low weight.

Exam plan

  1. Restate: age, job, cause (TB), which lobe, sputum, mMRC, X-ray detail.
  2. ICF or reasons (5–7): ICF + vicious cycle + reason for each impairment.
  3. Short-term (5): clearance plan for that lobe (technique, positions, dose), saline, breathing control, pelvic floor.
  4. Long-term (5–8): rehab doses, home routine, devices, energy conservation, action plan, outcome measures.
