Asked: 7 marks: physiotherapy for hypertension (S18). 15 marks: schoolteacher with chest pain and hypertension (S23). "Known hypertensive" appears in most cardiac and vascular cases, so the lifestyle table is reused often.

Definition and grades

Hypertension: persistently raised BP on two or more correct readings: ≥ 140/90 (most guidelines, ISH 2020, India) or ≥ 130/80 (ACC/AHA).

ISH: grade 1 140–159/90–99; grade 2 ≥ 160/100. Primary (~90%); secondary: kidney disease, renal artery stenosis, hyperaldosteronism, thyroid, coarctation, sleep apnoea, pregnancy, steroids, NSAIDs.

Risk factors: age, family history, abdominal obesity, salt (pickles, papad, packaged snacks), low potassium, inactivity, alcohol, stress, smoking, diabetes.

Pathophysiology and features

BP = cardiac output × peripheral resistance; mostly stiff narrow small arteries + salt and water retention + sympathetic overactivity. Target organs: heart (LVH, failure, CAD), brain (stroke, dementia), kidneys, eyes, arteries (PAD, aneurysm, dissection).

Why exercise lowers BP: better artery lining, relaxed small arteries, less sympathetic activity, weight loss; one session lowers BP for hours (post-exercise hypotension).

Usually silent ("silent killer"); headache, dizziness, nosebleed; crisis: severe headache, blurred vision, chest pain, confusion.

Measure correctly: seated, back supported, arm at heart level, right cuff, 5 min rest, two readings; home or 24-h readings. Kidney, sugar, lipids, ECG (LVH), echo, eyes.

Medical management and drugs

ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium channel blocker, thiazide diuretic (often combined); beta-blocker for specific reasons. Target below 130/80 (ACC/AHA 2025); ESC 2024 systolic 120–129 if tolerated.

Assessment, ICF, goals

Assess: BP both arms (right cuff), HR, drugs and timing, target-organ disease, risk factors; activity, salt, alcohol, smoking, stress, sleep; BMI, waist; 6MWT or step test with BP before and after. Outcomes: home BP, weight, waist, 6MWT, activity minutes.

S23 teacher (chest pain + hypertension, conservative): treat as stable angina with hypertension: confirm the cause with the doctor (ECG, troponin), BP below 180/110, no rest pain; education (BP, angina, GTN, warning signs); walking at RPE 11–13 to 30 min most days, below the angina HR; light resistance breathing out; relaxation; low salt, weight control; home BP. Participation: sit for part of class, plan stairs. See IHD.

Goals: short-term (4 weeks): correct home BP, walk 30 min most days, less salt. Long-term (3–6 months): BP at target, ≥ 5% weight loss, regular aerobic, resistance and isometric training, stress habits.

Exercise prescription (7m S18)

AerobicBrisk walking, cycling, swimming; moderate (talk test, RPE 12–13); ~30 min most days (ACC/AHA 2025: 75–150 min a week)
Resistance2–3 days a week; ~50–80% 1RM, ~3 × 10; breathe out on effort
IsometricHandgrip 4 × 2 min at 30–40% max, 1–3 min rest, 3 days a week (or wall squats): largest fall, ~8/4 mmHg (2023 analysis of 270 trials)
RelaxationStretching, yoga with slow breathing, relaxation

Warm-up and a 10-min cool-down; progress duration, then intensity; combine with diet and weight loss.

Precautions and lifestyle

Do not start if resting BP above ~180/110 (recheck, refer) or crisis symptoms. Stop if systolic above ~250 or diastolic above ~115, systolic falls as work rises, chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness, headache. No Valsalva, very heavy lifts or long head-down; keep breathing in isometric holds.

Lifestyle (ACC/AHA 2025): sodium below 2,300 mg (ideally 1,500); lose ≥ 5% weight; DASH-style diet (vegetables, fruit, pulses, low-fat dairy); 75–150 min exercise a week; alcohol ≤ 2 (men) or 1 (women) a day; stop smoking; manage stress; treat sleep apnoea. Control cuts stroke and heart disease; lifestyle alone controls many with stage 1.
