Recognising arrest; chain of survival

Cardiac arrest: unresponsive with no normal breathing (none, or occasional gasps: gasping is not breathing; start CPR). Each minute without CPR lowers survival.

Chain (adult, outside hospital): call early → high-quality CPR → early defibrillation → advanced care → post-arrest care → recovery. In hospital: recognise deterioration early.

Adult BLS steps

Check, Call, Compress, Shock

  1. Safety of the scene.
  2. Response: tap shoulders, shout "Are you all right?"; don't shake the neck.
  3. Call: in hospital the code team; outside 108 or 112 on speaker; send for an AED.
  4. Breathing (± carotid pulse if trained) for ≤ 10 s.
  5. No normal breathing → compressions at once, 30:2.
  6. AED: switch on, follow prompts; restart compressions right after each shock.
  7. Continue until normal breathing, the team takes over, or exhaustion; swap every 2 min.

High-quality CPR

HandsHeel on the centre of the chest (lower half of sternum), other on top, arms straight, shoulders over hands
Rate100–120/min
Depth5–6 cm
RecoilFull; don't lean
PausesUnder 10 s
Ratio30:2 without an advanced airway
BreathsHead tilt–chin lift (jaw thrust if neck injury); 1 s each, just visible chest rise; no over-ventilation
Advanced airwayContinuous compressions, 1 breath every 6 s
SurfaceStart at once even on a bed (2025); backboard helps but don't delay

Compression-only CPR for untrained or unwilling rescuers: hard, fast, continuous; India's COLS guideline for lay people.

AED

Switch on, follow prompts; bare, dry chest; pads below the right clavicle and left side below the armpit (as pictured), not over a pacemaker; stand clear for analysis; shock if advised ("Stand clear", check, press); restart compressions immediately for 2 min, no pulse check; reanalysis every 2 min. Shockable: VF and pulseless VT.

Children, choking, recovery position

Child (1 to puberty)Infant (under 1)
Depth⅓ chest, ~5 cm⅓ chest, ~4 cm
TechniqueOne or two handsTwo thumbs encircling, or heel of one hand (two-finger method dropped, 2025)
Ratio30:2 one rescuer, 15:2 two rescuers; rate 100–120

Children arrest mostly from breathing problems: breaths matter more.

Choking: mild (can cough, speak): encourage coughing. Severe adult or child: 5 back blows then 5 abdominal thrusts, alternate (AHA 2025); pregnant or obese: chest thrusts. Infant: 5 back blows face-down on the forearm, then 5 chest thrusts; no abdominal thrusts. Unresponsive: floor, call, CPR; look in the mouth before breaths, no blind finger sweeps.

Recovery position (breathing normally, no spinal injury): on the side, upper hip and knee bent in front, hand under cheek, head slightly back; keep checking breathing.

In physio settings

Know the AED, trolley, emergency number and trained staff; phone at hand in rehab. Warning signs: chest pain, sudden breathlessness, dizziness, pallor, sweating, new irregular pulse, falling BP → stop, sit or lie down, vitals, call early. VF on a monitor + unresponsive = arrest (a loose lead also looks chaotic: check the patient). After heart surgery follow the unit protocol but never withhold compressions for fear of the sternum. Check DNR wishes in advance. BLS certificates last ~2 years.
