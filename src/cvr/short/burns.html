Asked: a 15-mark case in most papers (ICF 5 + short-term 5 + long-term 5, or 5 + 7 + 3 with an add-on). 7 marks: anti-deformity positioning, splinting, care after grafting, inhalation burns, wound-healing factors and modalities, lower-limb burns. 3 marks: rule of nines, depth types, three splints, stages of wound healing, inhalation burn complications.

Definition and types

Burn: injury to skin and deeper tissue from heat, electricity, chemicals, radiation or friction. Described by cause, depth and %TBSA. Inhalation injury: airway and lung damage from hot gases, smoke or toxic products.

Causes: flame (stove, dupatta catching fire, firecrackers), scald (children), contact, electrical, chemical (alkali goes deeper).

Rule of nines (3m W18, S21)

Head and neck 9; each arm 9; front of trunk 18; back of trunk 18; each leg 18; perineum 1. Patient's palm with fingers ≈ 1% (small or patchy burns). Children: Lund and Browder chart (bigger head, smaller legs). Count only partial and full thickness. Show the working: both arms 18 + front of head 4.5 + upper front trunk 9 = 31.5%.

Burn unit if: partial thickness over 10%, any full thickness, face, hands, feet, perineum or major joints, electrical, chemical or inhalation injury, other serious injury.

Burn to impairments (clinical reasoning)

  • Jackson's zones: coagulation (dead), stasis (can be saved or lost: resuscitation, infection), hyperaemia (recovers).
  • Capillary leak → oedema; over ~20% TBSA → burn shock in 24–48 h.
  • Hypermetabolism → muscle breakdown for months; ↓ immunity → infection, sepsis.
  • Inhalation: upper airway swelling, lower airway and alveolar damage, CO and cyanide poisoning.
  • Scar contracts for months (myofibroblasts) → flexion and adduction. Position of comfort = position of contracture.
  • Impairments: pain, ↓ ROM, contracture, hypertrophic scar; weakness, ↓ endurance, ↓ ADL; ↓ ventilation, secretions.

Wound healing (3m S19; 7m S25)

Haemostasis (minutes–hours) → inflammation (days 1–5) → proliferation (day 4–week 3: granulation, collagen, epithelium, contraction) → remodelling (week 3 to 1–2 years; scar never as strong as skin).

Slow healing: local: infection, poor blood supply, oedema, dead tissue, pressure and shear, too dry or wet, radiotherapy. General: age, poor nutrition (protein, vitamin C, zinc), diabetes, smoking, anaemia, obesity, steroids, organ failure. Modalities: see Diabetes and wounds.

Clinical features

  • Pain worst in superficial partial; swelling peaks 24–48 h (hands, face, neck). Circumferential deep chest burns restrict breathing; on a limb they cut off circulation.
  • Suspect inhalation injury: closed space; face and neck burns; singed nasal hair; soot in mouth or nose; black sputum; hoarse voice, stridor, wheeze; confusion (CO). Swelling worsens over day 1, so early intubation.
  • Electrical: small entry and exit wounds, deep muscle damage; arrhythmia, compartment syndrome, dark urine (myoglobin, kidney injury), nerve damage, fractures.

Investigations: burn chart; ABG with carboxyhaemoglobin (SpO₂ reads falsely normal in CO poisoning); chest X-ray (later collapse, ARDS); bronchoscopy; ECG, CK, urine myoglobin after electrical injury; Hb, albumin, swabs.

Medical and surgical management

  • First aid: stop the burning; cool running water 20 min (no ice, toothpaste, butter); cover with cling film; keep warm.
  • Airway first; high-flow oxygen for CO; fluids if ~20% TBSA or more; analgesia; tetanus; high-protein feeds; silver dressings.
  • Parkland: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA in 24 h, half in the first 8 h. Current: start 2–4 mL and titrate to urine output 0.5 mL/kg/h.
  • Escharotomy (tight eschar on chest or limb), fasciotomy; early excision and split-thickness grafting (meshed; donor site often thigh); full-thickness grafts and flaps for face, hands, joints; later contracture release, Z-plasty.

Complications and physio role

  • Contracture (neck, axilla, elbow, hand, knee, ankle): positioning, splints, ROM from day 1.
  • Hypertrophic scar (within wound) and keloid (beyond it): pressure, silicone, massage, stretch.
  • Chest: pneumonia, collapse, ARDS. Infection and sepsis (hand hygiene, gowns).
  • Heterotopic ossification (elbow): warm, painful, stiffening joint: gentle active only, no forceful passive.
  • Compartment syndrome: elevate, report urgently. Exposed extensor hood: splint the finger straight (boutonnière risk).
  • Weakness, pressure sores, DVT; neuropathy (electrical); anxiety, body image; Marjolin's ulcer (cancer in an old scar).

PT assessment

Subjective: how and where (closed space, electrical, first aid), pain, itch, cough and sputum, grafts and donor sites, job and hand dominance, earning role, house and toilet, goals and fears about appearance.

Objective: vitals (CO caution), breathing, chest expansion (eschar), auscultation, cough; burn chart (sites, depth, joints crossed); oedema; active then passive ROM with goniometer; strength, sensation; bed mobility, walking, self-care, hand function; scar in rehab.

Outcome measures: Vancouver Scar Scale, POSAS, goniometry, BSHS-B, VAS, grip, 6MWT, Barthel or FIM.

ICF (S21: housemaid, stove burns, face, upper chest, both arms, day 3)

  • Body function: pain; hand and face oedema; ↓ neck, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand range, contracture risk; ↓ chest expansion, inhalation risk; microstomia risk; ↓ strength; anxiety about appearance.
  • Activity: feeding, washing, dressing, toileting (both hands), reaching, lifting.
  • Participation: housemaid work (sweeping, washing, cooking), family and social roles.
  • Environment: burn-unit care, family (facilitators); lost wages, floor-level stove, Indian toilet (barriers). Personal: 35, manual worker, distress about the face.

Goals: short-term (2–3 weeks): clear chest, ↓ oedema, full range kept with no contracture, grafts taken, independent feeding and toileting. Long-term (3–6 months): full range, flat pliable scar, full self-care, back to work, kitchen safety.

PT management by phase

  • Emergent (first 72 h): head up ≥ 30°; deep breathing, thoracic expansion, ACBT, assisted cough, suction; elevate burnt limbs with active finger and ankle pumps; anti-deformity positions and splints from day 1; active ROM of every burnt joint hourly while awake (after analgesia); sit out and walk once stable.
  • Acute (until wounds close): continue; exercise at dressing changes. After grafting: immobilise the graft ~3–5 days (surgeon decides), move all other joints, then gentle active ROM; no shear. Donor site: move early. Legs: compression bandage before standing.
  • Rehabilitation (scar matures 1–2 years): slow sustained stretch until the scar blanches, 20–30 s or longer, several times a day; pressure garments ~23 h a day for 12–18 months (20–25 mmHg), silicone gel, massage, moisturiser; night splints, serial casts; strength, aerobic, hand therapy; work tasks; sun protection; counselling.

Splints (3m S19; role 7m S18): resting hand (safe position), neck conformer, axillary, elbow and knee extension, ankle–foot, mouth; dynamic splints and serial casts later. They hold the position the patient cannot, protect tendons and grafts, keep range gained; worn at rest and night; check fit daily.

Special cases

  • Inhalation burns (7m W22): sit upright; humidified oxygen (high-flow for CO); deep breathing, ACBT, huff, assisted cough, suction; early mobilisation; if ventilated: positioning, clearance; report stridor or falling SpO₂ early.
  • Lower limb (7m W25): superficial: elevate, hourly ankle pumps and active movement, walk early in compression. Deep: hip extension with slight abduction, knee straight, ankle 90°, splints, heels free; after grafting stand and walk in compression bandages; later stretches (hip flexors, hamstrings, calf) and strengthening. Check toes in circumferential burns.
  • Pregnancy (S24): two patients; left side-lying or wedge (no flat supine after 20 weeks); DVT prevention; breathing exercises; no heat or electrotherapy over the trunk; report bleeding, pain, contractions, reduced fetal movement.

Precautions and stop

No movement of fresh grafts until allowed; no shear. No aggressive stretch over exposed tendons. No forceful passive range with heterotopic ossification. Check cardiac stability after electrical burns. Infection control. Stop and report: stridor, harder breathing, falling SpO₂, falling BP or fast irregular pulse, a bleeding or lifting graft, a tight numb limb.

Education and prognosis

Stretch daily for 1–2 years; wear splints and garments; moisturise, massage, sunscreen; don't scratch. Prevention: raised cooking platform; never refuel a lit stove; tuck the dupatta; stop, drop and roll. Revised Baux = age + %TBSA (+ 17 if inhalation injury).

Exam plan

  1. Restate: age, job, cause, sites, %TBSA worked out, depth, day, inhalation or electrical.
  2. ICF (5–6) with functional diagnosis and reasons.
  3. Short-term (5): chest care, oedema, positioning table, splints, hourly ROM, graft care, early walking.
  4. Long-term (5): stretching, scar care, strength, hand therapy, ADL, work, psychology, prevention.
