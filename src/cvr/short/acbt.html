ACBT: three parts

A flexible cycle the patient does independently: breathing control → thoracic expansion → breathing control → forced expiration technique; breathing control always between active parts.

Breathing controlGentle relaxed lower-chest tidal breathing, shoulders relaxed, as long as needed. Rest: prevents bronchospasm, fatigue, desaturation
Thoracic expansion3–4 deep breaths, 3-s hold (or sniff), relaxed out; hand on the chest for feedback; may add percussion or shaking. Air travels by collateral channels (pores of Kohn, canals of Lambert, channels of Martin) behind mucus; hold evens filling; interdependence opens neighbours
FET1–2 huffs from mid to low volume (open mouth and glottis, "steaming a mirror"), then breathing control; high-volume huff or cough when mucus is central. Airways narrow downstream of the equal pressure point; fast air shears mucus mouthwards

Equal pressure point: in a forced expiration airway pressure falls from alveoli to mouth; where it equals pleural pressure the airway is squeezed and air speeds up. High volume → EPP in central airways; lower volume → EPP moves peripherally. So a long mid-volume huff reaches further than a cough from a full breath.

Procedure, dose, indications

Explain; bronchodilator first if prescribed; sitting or the lobe's drainage position; BC until relaxed → 3–4 TEE with hold → BC → 1–2 mid-volume huffs → BC; high-volume huff or one cough when central; repeat. Stop when huffs are dry and non-productive in two consecutive cycles or the patient tires. Dose: 10–30 min, once or twice a day; more in infections; post-op a few cycles every 1–2 h.

Indications: excess or retained secretions (bronchiectasis, CF, COPD, infection, asthma with plugs); collapse; post-op prevention; to make coughing easier; independent home routine.

Contraindications and care: few true ones; not possible if unconscious, confused, very young. Bronchospasm: longer BC, stop if wheeze rises. Haemoptysis: no vigorous huffs or percussion. Untreated pneumothorax, unstable heart, severe post-op pain: modify (supported huff) or avoid forced parts. Fatigue, desaturation: shorter cycles, oxygen.

Asthma rationale (S17): BC prevents bronchospasm and fatigue; TEE gets air behind plugs; huffs avoid coughing fits that narrow sensitive airways; patient sets the pace.

Teach a huff: medium breath, then out fast but not violently through an "O" mouth: a breathy rush, not a cough; a tube keeps the glottis open; support the wound.

Autogenic drainage (7m S20; 3m W19)

Controlled breathing at different lung volumes (Chevaillier) to create the highest airflow without airway collapse, moving mucus from small to large airways.

  1. Unstick: small breaths at low lung volume (expiratory reserve): loosens peripheral mucus.
  2. Collect: breaths at mid volume (tidal): gathers mucus in middle airways.
  3. Evacuate: deeper breaths at high volume, then a gentle huff.

Upright; slow nasal breath in, hold 2–3 s, sigh out through open mouth and throat, fast but no wheeze; feel or hear crackles to change phase; suppress the cough until mucus is central; 30–45 min. Indications: bronchiectasis, CF (from ~8–12 years), COPD with sputum, collapsing or wheezy airways, cough-induced incontinence. For: independent, no equipment, gentle. Against: hard to learn, needs concentration, slow, not for young children (assisted form for infants).
