V/Q and West's zones (3m S25)

Ventilation ~4 L/min, perfusion ~5 L/min: overall V/Q ≈ 0.8. Upright, both are greater at the base, but perfusion rises more steeply (gravity): apex V/Q ≈ 3, base ≈ 0.6. Basal alveoli start smaller (less negative pleural pressure), on the steep part of the pressure–volume curve, so they ventilate more.

  • Zone 1 (apex): PA > Pa > Pv: no flow; absent normally, appears with low BP or high PEEP.
  • Zone 2 (middle): Pa > PA > Pv: flow depends on Pa − PA.
  • Zone 3 (base): Pa > Pv > PA: continuous flow.

If "zones" means airways: conducting (generations 0–16, no exchange, dead space ~150 mL) and respiratory (17–23, gas exchange). Write both if unclear.

Altered V/Q (3m S23)

Low V/Q (shunt if no ventilation): secretions, plugs, bronchospasm, collapse, consolidation, pulmonary oedema, ARDS, lying flat, obesity, pain after surgery. High V/Q (dead space if no perfusion): PE, low output or blood loss, emphysema, high PEEP, pulmonary vascular disease. Both lower PaO₂; low V/Q responds to oxygen and positioning; true shunt barely responds until alveoli re-open.

Effects of positions

UprightLargest FRC, diaphragm moves best: almost everyone
SupineDiaphragm pushed up, FRC ↓, dorsal collapse, aspiration: avoid long (but tetraplegia breathes better lying; binder when sitting)
Side-lying, spontaneous adultLower lung gets more V and Q: good lung down for O₂; affected lung up to drain
Side-lying, controlled ventilationAir to upper lung, blood to lower: matching may worsen; check SpO₂
InfantVentilation to the upper lung: good lung up
ProneEven ventilation, re-opens dorsal lung: severe ARDS (P/F below 150), ≥ 16 h (PROSEVA)
Head-downDrains lower lobes; ↑ work of breathing, reflux, ICP
Head-up 30–45°Less reflux and VAP; raised ICP
Forward leanDomes a flat diaphragm; arms fixed for accessory muscles: COPD

Not "good lung down" with a lung abscess or bleeding (spill into the good lung) or in infants.

Sitting upright (7m W21)

Respiratory: diaphragm descends → bigger FRC and VT, fewer collapsed basal alveoli, better V/Q and O₂, less work of breathing, deeper breath for a stronger cough, less reflux and aspiration. Cardiovascular: keeps BP reflexes trained (prevents postural hypotension); legs down ↓ venous return (orthopnoea, pulmonary oedema). Neuro and other: alert, oriented, communicates, safer swallowing; head-up ↓ ICP; first step to standing; off-loads the back. Watch for slumping (squashes abdomen, loads sacrum).

ICU positioning (7m W22, W25)

Benefits: better V/Q and O₂, lung volume, re-opens collapse; gravity drainage; less work of breathing; head-up ↓ aspiration and VAP; turns prevent pressure injuries, contractures, nerve compression; upright keeps reflexes and muscles, prepares for mobilisation; comfort, dignity.

Every turn: check SpO₂, HR, BP; secure tube, lines, drains; allow settling (SpO₂ can dip for minutes).

Positions for breathlessness (3m S22): high side-lying; forward-lean sitting (forearms on knees or table); relaxed sitting back with arms supported; forward-lean standing on a wall or rail; leaning back on a wall. Why: diaphragm domed by the abdomen, arms fixed for accessory muscles, less muscle work and anxiety.
