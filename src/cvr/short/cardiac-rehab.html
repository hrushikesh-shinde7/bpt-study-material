Definition, aims, indications

Cardiac rehabilitation: a coordinated, multi-part programme that restores physical, psychological and social function and slows the disease through exercise, education and risk-factor control (AHA/AACVPR). WHO: all activities so patients, by their own efforts, regain as normal a place as possible in the community.

Aims: safe return to daily life, work, leisure; ↑ exercise capacity; risk-factor control; fewer events and admissions; better QoL and mood; lifelong self-management.

Indications: MI or ACS; after CABG or PCI; stable angina; stable HFrEF; valve repair or replacement (incl. TAVI); heart transplant; also pacemaker or ICD, cardiomyopathy, adult congenital disease, PAD (walking programme), high-risk people. Age, low EF, ICD and diabetes are reasons to stratify and supervise, not to exclude.

Contraindications (ACSM)

Unstable angina; resting BP above 180/110; symptomatic drop over 20 mmHg on standing; significant aortic stenosis; uncontrolled arrhythmia or sinus rate over 120; decompensated heart failure; 3rd-degree block without pacemaker; active peri- or myocarditis; recent embolism, acute thrombophlebitis, aortic dissection; fever; uncontrolled diabetes or potassium; severe orthopaedic or psychological problems.

Core components (AHA/AACVPR 2024)

Patient assessment (ECG, exercise test or 6MWT, risk stratification, goals); nutrition (salt below 5 g); weight (Indian cut-offs); risk factors (BP, lipids, diabetes, tobacco, medicines); psychosocial (depression, stress, family, work); aerobic training (FITT-VP); strength training 2–3 days; activity counselling (≥ 150 min/week, less sitting); quality improvement.

Phases I–IV

I inpatientStable day to discharge: prevent bed-rest effects, breathing exercises after surgery, graded self-care and walking, stairs, education, GTN, home plan, referral. ~3 METs; RPE below 13; HR ≤ rest + 20 (MI) or + 30 (surgery)
II early outpatientWithin weeks of discharge, 8–12 weeks, centre, home or hybrid: exercise test, risk stratification, monitored aerobic and strength training, risk factors, psychology, return to work, driving, sex. 40–80% HRR or RPE 11–16, start low; 3–5 days; resistance 2–3 days
III communityMonths: less supervision, self-monitoring with pulse and RPE
IV lifelongIndependent: ≥ 150 min/week moderate + 2 days strength; periodic review

Across conditions

  • MI, CABG: Phase I limits; sternal precautions 6–8 weeks; legs first.
  • PCI: no sternal limits; protect the puncture site ~1 week; faster progress.
  • Heart failure: start RPE 11–13, intervals, long warm-up and cool-down; watch weight gain; IMT.
  • Valve surgery: sternal precautions; AF; anticoagulation (bleeding, falls).
  • Pacemaker, ICD: arm below shoulder 4–6 weeks; HR ≥ 10 below the detection rate.
  • Transplant: denervated heart, HR rises slowly: RPE, long warm-up. AF, beta-blockers: RPE and talk test. PAD: walk to moderate claudication.

EF 50 / 40, METs 7 / 5

Risk (AACVPR): low = EF ≥ 50% and ≥ 7 METs; high = EF below 40%, symptoms below 5 METs, complex arrhythmias or complicated course; sets ECG monitoring and progression.

Outcome measures and evidence

Capacity: exercise test (METs, VO₂), 6MWT, ISWT, sit-to-stand. Risk factors: BP, lipids, HbA1c, weight, waist, smoking. Symptoms: CCS, NYHA, Borg. QoL: MacNew, Seattle Angina, MLHFQ or KCCQ, SF-36, EQ-5D. Mood: HADS, PHQ-9. Behaviour: steps, adherence, return to work. Events: readmissions, MI, revascularisation.

Evidence: MI ↓ ~28%, admissions ↓ ~35%, probably fewer deaths, better QoL (Cochrane 2026: 107 trials, 26,886 people); home and digital programmes as good as centre-based; Yoga-CaRe better health, not fewer events. Few eligible join (especially in India and women): refer at discharge.
