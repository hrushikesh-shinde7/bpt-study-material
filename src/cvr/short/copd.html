Definition and types

COPD: preventable lung disease with persistent, usually progressive airflow limitation (airways and alveoli), causing breathlessness, cough and sputum. Spirometry: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.70.

Chronic bronchitis: productive cough on most days for 3 months a year for 2 years in a row. Emphysema: permanent enlargement of air spaces beyond the terminal bronchioles with wall destruction.

Causes; effects of smoking (3m S17, W22)

Causes: tobacco (pack-years = packs a day × years; bidi, hookah); chulha smoke (biomass cooking, non-smoking Indian women); occupational dust (sweepers, farmers, miners, textile); outdoor pollution; past TB; alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.

Smoking: paralyses and destroys cilia; enlarges mucus glands and goblet cells; inflames and narrows airways; proteases destroy alveolar walls; FEV1 falls faster; CO lowers O₂ carriage; more infections.

Pathophysiology to impairments

  • Inflamed, narrowed small airways, big mucus glands, damaged cilia → cough, sputum, infections.
  • Lost recoil → airways collapse on breathing out → air trapping, hyperinflation → flat diaphragm at a mechanical disadvantage → accessory muscles.
  • Faster breathing in exercise → less time to breathe out → dynamic hyperinflation: the main cause of exertional breathlessness.
  • V/Q mismatch → low O₂, later high CO₂ → hypoxic vasoconstriction → cor pulmonale.
  • Breathlessness spiral: breathless → inactive → weak limb muscles → more breathless. Physio breaks it at deconditioning.

Clinical features

Slowly worsening exertional breathlessness, morning cough, sputum, wheeze, weight loss. Signs: barrel chest, accessory muscles, pursed lips, tripod posture, ↓ expansion, hyper-resonance, quiet sounds with prolonged expiration, Hoover's sign (lower ribs move in), cyanosis, ankle oedema and raised JVP in cor pulmonale.

Exacerbation: sudden worse breathlessness, cough or sputum (more or purulent), usually infection; danger signs: breathless at rest, drowsy or confused (rising CO₂), accessory muscles.

Investigations

  • PFT (3m W20, W19): FEV1 ↓, FVC normal or slightly ↓, FEV1/FVC below 0.70, little bronchodilator reversibility (asthma: large), PEF ↓, scooped flow–volume loop; RV, TLC, RV/TLC ↑; DLCO ↓ in emphysema.
  • X-ray emphysema (3m W18, W24): hyperlucent lungs, fewer peripheral markings; low flat diaphragms, over 6 anterior or 10 posterior ribs visible; horizontal ribs, wide spaces; long narrow (tubular) heart; bigger retrosternal space; bullae. Bronchitis: near normal or "dirty chest", tram lines; big hilar arteries in pulmonary hypertension.
  • ABG: low PaO₂, high PaCO₂ in type 2 failure → target SpO₂ 88–92%. Oximetry on exercise; 6MWT or shuttle; ECG and echo (right heart); sputum, Hb (polycythaemia).

Medical management

  • Stop smoking, reduce smoke and dust: the only thing that slows decline.
  • LAMA and LABA, salbutamol reliever; inhaled steroid in group E with high eosinophils. Vaccines.
  • Long-term oxygen (ATS 2020): PaO₂ ≤ 55 mmHg (SpO₂ ≤ 88%), or 56–59 (SpO₂ 89%) with oedema, polycythaemia or right heart strain; ≥ 15 h a day.
  • Exacerbation: nebulised bronchodilators, oral steroids, antibiotics if purulent, controlled oxygen, NIV for acidotic hypercapnia, intubation if NIV fails. Surgery: bullectomy, volume reduction, transplant.
  • Hypoxic drive (3m W25): classic: chronic CO₂ retention leaves breathing driven by low O₂. Current (BTS): CO₂ rises mainly from worse V/Q matching and the Haldane effect. Write: "uncontrolled oxygen can cause CO₂ narcosis; give controlled oxygen (Venturi or low-flow cannula) to SpO₂ 88–92% and check ABG". Never withhold oxygen.

Complications

Exacerbations; respiratory failure (drowsy, flap: position, support NIV, report); cor pulmonale; pneumothorax from bullae; muscle wasting; steroid osteoporosis; anxiety, depression.

PT assessment

Subjective: mMRC; cough and sputum (amount, colour, morning load); exacerbations; pack-years; oxygen; inhaler technique; sleep position; breathless ADL; home floor, lift, toilet; job and dust; earning role.

Objective: forward-lean posture, chest shape, rate and pattern, accessory muscles, cyanosis, clubbing (think of another cause), oedema; expansion at three levels, trachea; auscultation; SpO₂ at rest and exertion; 6MWT or ISWT with SpO₂ and Borg; quadriceps and grip; PImax.

Outcome measures: mMRC, CAAT, 6MWT, ISWT and ESWT, modified Borg, SGRQ, CRQ.

ICF (W16: 50, smoker 30 years, 2nd floor, no lift; exacerbation; moderate obstruction; hypoxaemia and hypercapnia)

  • Body function: breathlessness, altered pattern; sputum, poor clearance; airflow obstruction, hyperinflation; low O₂ and high CO₂; ↓ exercise tolerance; weak limb and breathing muscles; anxiety.
  • Activity: walking, climbing to the 2nd floor, bathing, dressing, carrying. Participation: work, home roles, social and religious gatherings.
  • Environment: 2nd floor without lift, smoke and dust (barriers); family, medicines, rehab (facilitators). Personal: 50, 30-year smoker, worried about income.

Goals: short-term: breathing control and relieving positions, clears sputum with ACBT, SpO₂ 88–92%, one flight with pacing, correct inhaler use, quit plan. Long-term (6–12 weeks rehab): mMRC better by one grade, better 6MWT, reaches the 2nd floor with one rest, tobacco-free, action plan.

Tests set goals (7m W24): acidotic high CO₂ → acute goals only, no training; compensated high CO₂ → 88–92%, night NIV; desaturation on walking → train with oxygen, intervals; FEV1 grade → realistic goals; ↑ RV → pursed lips, forward lean, slow breathing; flat diaphragm → don't force abdominal breathing; bullae → no high-pressure techniques; consolidation → target that lobe.

Acute exacerbation (in hospital)

  • Relieving positions: high side-lying; sitting leaning forward on a table or knees; standing leaning on a wall. They let the flat diaphragm work better.
  • Breathing control; pursed-lip breathing on effort; ACBT with huffs; PEP or flutter if helpful; postural drainage only if it clearly helps (head-down can worsen breathlessness; BTS 2009).
  • Support NIV (mask tolerance, upright, clear secretions in breaks). Early sitting and walking with oxygen, SpO₂ 88–92%.
  • On SIMV (S20, RLL pneumonia): affected lung uppermost, suction, manual techniques, careful hyperinflation (air trapping, pneumothorax), early sitting, IMT in weaning. Refer to pulmonary rehab.

Pursed-lip breathing (3m S20, W21, W22)

In through the nose; out slowly through loosely pursed lips ("blowing on hot tea"), out about twice as long as in, not forced. Why: back-pressure splints small airways open → less air trapping and hyperinflation → slower, deeper breathing, better O₂ → less breathlessness and panic on walking and stairs.

Pulmonary rehabilitation (long-term)

Exercise + education + behaviour change, 6–12 weeks, ≥ 2 supervised sessions a week plus home. Better breathlessness, exercise capacity, quality of life; fewer readmissions after an admission (ATS 2023).

Why strengthen limbs (3m W24): COPD wastes muscle (inactivity, hypoxia, inflammation, steroids, poor food); weak thighs tire early and make lactate, which drives breathing, so many stop from leg fatigue; training improves strength, stairs, standing, carrying, breathlessness at the same load, bones and falls, and is less breathless than endurance work.

Energy conservation (7m S19)

Plan heavy tasks across the week and at the best time of day. Pace: slow, rest before very breathless, breathe out on effort. Prioritise and delegate. Position: sit to wash, dress, cook; keep things between waist and shoulder; avoid bending and reaching overhead. Aids: trolley, long sponge, shower stool, raised toilet seat. Stairs: one step per out-breath, rest on landings, fewer trips. Lowers the O₂ cost of daily tasks, so he stays independent.

Precautions and stop

SpO₂ 88–92% if CO₂ is high; no head-down when breathless or after meals; no high PEP or aggressive bagging with bullae; no vigorous percussion on steroid-thinned ribs; no training during an exacerbation. Stop if: SpO₂ below ~85–88% despite oxygen, Borg above 7, chest pain, dizziness, confusion, new irregular pulse, BP falls.

Education and prognosis

Stop tobacco; avoid chulha smoke and dust; inhaler technique; daily walking and strength work; daily clearance; pacing; small frequent meals; vaccines; action plan for early exacerbation signs. Worse prognosis: low FEV1, frequent exacerbations, low BMI, smoking.

Exam plan

  1. Restate: age, job, smoking, home, PFT grade, gases, acute or stable.
  2. ICF (5) with reasons: hyperinflation, sputum, deconditioning.
  3. Short-term (3–5): positions, breathing control, ACBT, controlled oxygen, NIV support, early walking.
  4. Long-term (5–7): pulmonary rehab doses, IMT, energy conservation, stairs, quitting, action plan, outcome measures.
