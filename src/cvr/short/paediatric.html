Asked: 7 marks: precautions and contraindications for postural drainage in a neonate (W18); paediatric asthma rehab (S18, see Asthma). 3 marks: signs of respiratory distress in children (W24).

Neonatal chest physio: precautions (7m W18)

  • Only when needed (retained secretions, collapse, blocked tube): never routine.
  • Minimal handling: cluster care with nurses; short sessions; rest between.
  • Monitor continuously: SpO₂, HR, breathing, colour, temperature; keep warm.
  • Timing: before feeds or well after (vomiting, aspiration).
  • No head-down tilt: flat or head-up; 30° head-up gave less reflux (Cochrane).
  • Gentle techniques: fragile ribs (osteopenia) and brain vessels; vigorous percussion linked to injury.
  • Suction: right catheter and pressure, pre-oxygenate, short; watch bradycardia, desaturation.

Contraindications

Unstable baby (low BP, arrhythmia, desaturates with handling); recent or extending intraventricular haemorrhage, very preterm in first days; persistent pulmonary hypertension; undrained pneumothorax; rib fractures, osteopenia, low platelets (no percussion or vibration); raised ICP; just after feeds; post-op without surgical clearance. Modified techniques: positioning, gentle vibration, assisted breathing, suction.

Respiratory distress in children (3m W24)

60, 50, 40

Fast breathing (WHO, full minute, calm child): ≥ 60 under 2 months; ≥ 50 at 2–11 months; ≥ 40 at 1–5 years.

  • Work of breathing: nasal flaring; chest in-drawing (subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal); tracheal tug; neck muscles; head bobbing; see-saw breathing.
  • Noises: grunting (breathing out against a closed glottis to keep alveoli open), stridor, wheeze.
  • Effects: cyanosis, low SpO₂, fast HR, cannot feed or talk, sweating.
  • Danger (tiring): drowsy, apnoea, slowing breathing in an exhausted child, silent chest: urgent help.

Neonatal conditions

  • RDS (premature): no surfactant → alveoli collapse; grunting, flaring, recession, cyanosis within hours; X-ray ground glass + air bronchograms. Antenatal steroids, CPAP, surfactant. Physio: not routine (stiff, not wet lungs); positioning, developmental care, minimal handling.
  • Meconium aspiration (term or post-term): airway block with air trapping and pneumothorax, chemical pneumonitis, surfactant loss, pulmonary hypertension; patchy shadows, over-inflation. Supportive care; routine tracheal suction of non-vigorous babies no longer advised (ILCOR). Physio: clearance only when secretions retained; minimal handling.
  • Chronic lung disease of prematurity (BPD): oxygen need at 36 weeks post-conception; wheeze, infections, poor growth. Clearance during infections, positioning, developmental care (tummy time), home-oxygen education.
  • Paediatric ARDS: after pneumonia, sepsis, drowning, trauma: positioning (prone as the ICU directs), careful clearance, minimal disconnection, early rehab.

Pneumonia, pneumonitis, bronchiolitis

Pneumonitis: aspiration of milk or stomach contents, kerosene ingestion (classic in India), infection. Pneumonia: major killer under 5 in India. Bronchiolitis (viral, under 2): wheeze, crackles, poor feeding.

Physio: bronchiolitis: conventional chest physio (percussion, vibration) does not help and forced expiration causes vomiting (Cochrane 2023); slow passive expiration may help a little in moderate cases. Pneumonia: no routine clearance; positioning, mobility, play; clear only retained secretions. Aspiration: head-up positioning, feeding advice with speech therapy.
