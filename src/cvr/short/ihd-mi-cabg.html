Asked: a 15-mark case in almost every paper (ICF 5 + cardiac rehab 10, or 3 + 5 + 7). 7 marks: Phase I after MI, risk stratification, cardiac markers, Phase II after PCI for a sportsman, lifestyle education. 3 marks: risk factors, CABG grafts and indications, Karvonen, ECG of acute and old MI.

Definitions

  • IHD (CAD): heart muscle gets less blood and oxygen than it needs, usually from coronary atherosclerosis.
  • Angina: temporary ischaemia, no permanent damage. MI: death of heart muscle from prolonged ischaemia.
  • MI (Fourth Universal Definition, 2018): troponin rise and/or fall with a value above the 99th percentile, plus evidence of ischaemia (symptoms, new ECG changes or Q waves, new wall-motion defect, clot on angiography).
  • ACS = unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI. PCI (angioplasty, PTCA): balloon and stent from inside. CABG: a graft carries blood around the block. NYHA and CCS grades: Scales.

Risk factors (3m S17, W17)

Non-modifiable: age (men over 45, women over 55), male sex, family history of early heart disease (male relative under 55, female under 65), South Asian origin.

Modifiable: smoking and all tobacco, hypertension, diabetes, abnormal lipids (high LDL, low HDL), obesity (waist), inactivity, salty and fried diet, alcohol, stress and depression.

For "3 + 3" write: age, male sex, family history; smoking, hypertension, diabetes.

Plaque to impairments (clinical reasoning)

Plaque narrows the artery → ruptures, clot forms → O₂ supply falls below demand = ischaemia (brief: angina) → over 20–30 min: MI, from the inner wall outwards → ↓ pumping (EF, cardiac output), arrhythmias, pain and fear → bed rest (and after CABG the sternotomy and drains) add deconditioning, shallow breathing and DVT risk.

Impairments: ↓ exercise tolerance, fatigue, breathlessness; atelectasis and DVT risk; ↓ ADL, work and confidence.

Scar is firm in 6–8 weeks: early activity is safe, heavy straining is not. Rate–pressure product = HR × systolic BP = the heart's O₂ demand; angina starts at the same value in the same patient.

Extra after CABG: wound pain → shallow breaths and weak cough; basal atelectasis; guarding → stiff shoulders and thoracic spine, rounded posture; painful or swollen graft site.

Clinical features

  • Angina: central tightness or heaviness spreading to the left arm, jaw, neck or back; brought on by exertion, cold, meals, emotion; eased by rest or GTN in minutes.
  • MI: pain over 20 min not relieved by rest or GTN; sweating, nausea, breathlessness, sense of doom; fast or slow pulse, low BP, basal crackles if the LV fails.
  • Inferior MI → bradycardia or heart block: the right coronary supplies the SA and AV nodes.
  • Silent MI in diabetes, the elderly and women: breathlessness, tiredness, epigastric pain.
  • After CABG: wound and graft-site pain, drains, ↓ basal breath sounds, weak cough, swollen leg or forearm, stiff shoulders (S19: "cannot raise his hands above his head").

Investigations: what changes physio

  • STEMI: new ST elevation in 2 neighbouring leads, ≥ 1 mm (V2–V3: 2 mm men ≥ 40, 2.5 mm men under 40, 1.5 mm women). Sequence: tall T → ST elevation → Q waves → T inversion.
  • NSTEMI, unstable angina: ST depression or T inversion.
  • Old (evolved) MI (3m S24): pathological Q waves (≥ 0.03 s and 1 mm deep; older rule over 0.04 s or over ¼ of R), T inversion, ST back to baseline.

  • Troponin: most specific; rises in hours, peaks ~1 day, stays up ~2 weeks. Mobilise once stable or falling. CK-MB is normal in 2–3 days, so a new rise means re-infarction.
  • Echo EF below 40% = high risk; BNP high = heart failure (lower intensity, slower progress). Angiography: triple vessel or left main → CABG. Exercise test or 6MWT: safe HR range for Phase II.
  • Markers in rehab (7m W25): confirm MI; infarct size and risk group; when to start; a fresh rise during rehab means stop and reset; some rise after CABG is expected; BNP guides intensity.

Medical and surgical management

  • STEMI: primary PCI within 90 min (120 if transferred), otherwise thrombolysis within 30 min; aspirin plus a second antiplatelet and an anticoagulant; oxygen only if SpO₂ is below 90%; nitrates; opioid only for severe pain. MONA for every MI is the old answer.
  • Drugs: beta-blocker → blunted HR, so use RPE and the talk test; nitrates → dizzy on standing, keep GTN at hand; antiplatelets → bleeding; ACE inhibitors, diuretics → low BP.
  • After PCI: femoral → leg straight a few hours, check the groin before walking; radial → walk early, no heavy wrist loading for ~1 week. Driving 1 week after PCI for ACS if EF ≥ 40%, otherwise 4 weeks (DVLA).
  • CABG indications (3m W25): left main disease; triple vessel disease (especially with diabetes or low EF); 2 vessels including the proximal LAD; angina uncontrolled when PCI is unsuitable; failed PCI; with valve surgery or mechanical complications of MI.
  • Grafts (3m W24): LIMA to LAD (stays open longest), RIMA, radial artery, great saphenous vein. Median sternotomy, wired; on- or off-pump.
  • Course: ICU and extubation on day 0, sitting out day 1, ward walking days 2–4, home ~day 5–7.

Complications and what physio does

  • Arrhythmia (first 48 h; AF after CABG, days 2–4): pulse before every session; stop and report a new one.
  • Heart failure, shock: sit upright, keep activity low, report. Re-infarction: stop, rest, GTN, report.
  • Rupture, embolism, stroke: emergency. Pericarditis (sharp, worse lying flat) or Dressler's: report.
  • DVT, PE: ankle pumps, early walking, stockings. Lung collapse after CABG: deep breaths, supported huff, sit and walk early.
  • Sternal infection or instability (clicking, discharge; more likely with diabetes, obesity, both IMAs): report at once.
  • Graft leg swelling: elevate, walk. Stiff shoulders: early pain-free movement.

PT assessment

  • Subjective: chest pain (site, spread, triggers, relief), breathlessness, palpitations; event and treatment (date, PCI or CABG, post-op day, grafts, ICU course); risk factors; drugs; social history for ICF: job, driving, earning role, stairs, toilet type, family, tobacco, diet; goals and fears.
  • Objective: HR and rhythm, BP lying and standing, RR, SpO₂ at rest and with activity; lines and drains; chest expansion, auscultation at the bases, cough; wounds; shoulder range, posture, calf; bed mobility, walking distance with vitals, stairs.
  • Outcome measures: Borg RPE, 6MWT, ISWT, NYHA and CCS, DASI, HADS, MacNew or HeartQoL.

ICF: draw the 6 boxes first (W17 case)

  • Health condition: triple vessel CAD; CABG day 2 (LIMA, RIMA, radial).
  • Body structure: wired sternum; both IMAs taken; forearm wound.
  • Body function: incisional pain; ↓ lung expansion, atelectasis risk; weak cough; ↓ exercise tolerance; AF risk; guarded shoulders; anxiety.
  • Activity: bed mobility and sit-to-stand without the arms, walking, stairs, upper-body dressing, lifting.
  • Participation: cannot drive to work (~4 weeks) or run his business; family and social roles.
  • Environment: facilitators: family, rehab services, medicines; barriers: daily drive, stress (ask about stairs and toilet).
  • Personal: 50, businessman, motivated; smoking, diet, stress.

Functional diagnosis in one line: age, job, surgery and day + main impairments + the activities and roles they limit + one facilitator and one barrier.

Goals

Short-term (by discharge, ~1 week): pain low enough to breathe deeply and cough with support; clear chest; independent sternal-safe bed mobility; corridor walking and one flight within Phase I limits; pain-free shoulder movement; knows the home programme and warning signs.

Long-term (6–12 weeks): walks 30 min continuously at RPE 12–13; full shoulder range, upright posture; back to driving (~4 weeks) and work; risk factors controlled; exercise for life.

Before CABG: explain ICU and drains; deep breaths with a hold; supported huff and cough with a pillow; sternal-safe bed mobility; ankle pumps; baseline walk.

Phase I

Start when stable: no new chest pain, no new ECG changes or dangerous arrhythmia, troponin stable or falling, no uncontrolled heart failure (usually day 1). After CABG: day 1.

13 / 20 / 30 / 120

RPE below 13; HR ≤ rest + 20 and ≤ 120 after MI; HR ≤ rest + 30 after surgery (ACSM).

Dose: bouts of 3–5 min with 1–2 min rest, ~20 min in all; 3–4 times a day on days 1–3, then twice; build to 10–15 min continuous first, then intensity; activities up to ~3 METs.

  • CCU (1–2 METs): breathing exercises, ankle pumps, active limbs, sitting up, self-feeding, commode. After CABG add deep breaths with a 3-s hold, 5–10 an hour, supported huff, sitting out on day 1, log-roll, both arms moving together.
  • Ward (2–3 METs): standing, walking in the room then the corridor (3–4 times a day), bathroom, posture, elevate the graft leg.
  • Before discharge (~3 METs): 10–15 min walk, one flight of stairs, own pulse and RPE, sternal and wound care, home walking plan.

Monitor HR, rhythm, BP, SpO₂, RPE and symptoms before, during and after. Teach warning signs and GTN use. Refer to Phase II.

Progress only if (S24): HR rise within limits; systolic rises a little (a fall over 10 mmHg or diastolic ≥ 110 stops it); no new arrhythmia, angina, dizziness or marked breathlessness; RPE under 13; SpO₂ steady; pain controlled, sternum stable, Hb and chest X-ray checked; recovers within minutes and not unduly tired next morning; consider age, fitness, EF, diabetes.

Current evidence: mobilise within 12–24 h once stable (long bed rest is the old answer). After CABG the incentive spirometer is an adjunct only, never alone (AARC 2011).

Karvonen: age 50, resting HR 70 → max 170, HRR 100 → 40–60% = 110–130/min. On a beta-blocker use RPE 11–13 and the talk test. Breathe out on effort; no Valsalva.

After PCI, young sportsman (7m W24): no sternal limits; protect the puncture site ~1 week; symptom-limited test first; 40–60% rising to 60–80% HRR, intervals later; sport skills at low intensity first; competitive sport only after cardiology review (ESC 2020).

Sternal precautions: for 6–8 weeks no heavy lifting, pushing, pulling or one-sided arm loading; keep arms close to the body and pain-free ("keep your move in the tube"). No heavy lifting for ~3 months.

Phases III–IV: ≥ 150 min a week of moderate activity + resistance on 2 days; self-monitoring.

Do not start exercise (ACSM)

Unstable angina; resting BP over 180/110; symptomatic fall over 20 mmHg on standing; significant aortic stenosis; uncontrolled arrhythmia or sinus rate over 120; uncompensated heart failure; third-degree block without a pacemaker; active peri- or myocarditis; recent embolism or DVT; fever; uncontrolled diabetes.

Stop the session

Diastolic ≥ 110; systolic falls over 10 mmHg as work rises; significant arrhythmia; second- or third-degree block; angina, marked breathlessness, ischaemic ECG; dizziness, confusion, pallor, cold sweat; sternal movement or wound bleeding.

Angina in a session: stop, sit, GTN; not better 5 min after the first dose → emergency help. No exercise within 1–2 h of a heavy meal or in extreme heat or cold.

Lifestyle and education (S16, W20, S23, S25)

  • Stop all tobacco (bidi, gutkha, second-hand smoke). Salt under 5 g a day; less fried food, vanaspati, ghee, sugar.
  • ≥ 150 min of activity a week. BMI under 23; waist under 90 cm (men), 80 cm (women). BP under 130/80. Control sugar and lipids; limit alcohol; manage stress.
  • Obesity grades (S21): WHO normal 18.5–24.9, overweight 25–29.9, obese I 30–34.9, II 35–39.9, III ≥ 40. Asian Indian: normal 18.5–22.9, overweight 23–24.9, obese ≥ 25.
  • Return: driving ~4 weeks after CABG; sex when 3–5 METs are symptom-free (~6–8 weeks after CABG); no heavy lifting ~3 months; work ~6 weeks (desk work sooner).
  • Do's and don'ts (S25): walk daily at RPE 11–13, take medicines, carry GTN, join Phase II. Don't hold the breath, exercise to exhaustion, stop antiplatelets, smoke, lift heavy or drive until cleared.

Prognosis: rehab cuts MI by ~28% and admissions by ~35% (Cochrane 2026). Phase I discharge: stable vitals, walking at ~3 METs, one flight of stairs, knows the home programme.
