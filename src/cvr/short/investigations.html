Asked: 3 marks in most papers: X-ray of effusion, emphysema, COPD, TOF; spirometry in restriction, obstruction, COPD; ECG of MI; ABI. 7 marks: ABG, PFT and X-ray in COPD goal setting (W24). Practical spots: X-ray, ABG, PFT, ECG, devices.

Chest X-ray: read in order

  1. Details and quality: name, date, PA or AP (AP enlarges the heart), rotation (spinous processes midway between clavicle ends), inspiration (~6 anterior or 10 posterior ribs), exposure.
  2. A airway (trachea central?); B lungs and pleura zone by zone, costophrenic angles; C cardiac (CTR below 0.5 on PA), borders, mediastinum; D diaphragm (right slightly higher; flat in hyperinflation; free air); E everything else (bones, sternal wires, surgical emphysema, lines and tubes).

Silhouette sign: a white area blurring a border lies next to it: lost right heart border = right middle lobe; lost hemidiaphragm = lower lobe.

ConsolidationWhite, air bronchograms, volume kept → positioning, mobility; clearance only if sputum retained
CollapseWhite, smaller lobe; fissure, trachea, mediastinum pulled towards; raised diaphragm → target that lobe
EffusionDense base, meniscus, blunted angle; large pushes away → good side down, expansion; drain first if large
PneumothoraxBlack, no markings beyond a pleural line; tension pushes away → no positive pressure until drained

Pulmonary function tests

FVC total forced exhalation; FEV1 first second; FEV1/FVC normally ~0.75–0.80; PEF fastest flow (asthma diary); TLC, RV, FRC (body box or dilution): high in hyperinflation, low in restriction; DLCO low in emphysema, ILD, vascular disease, normal in asthma.

Bronchodilator response: FEV1 or FVC rise ≥ 12% and 200 mL (ATS/ERS 2005); 2022 standard: over 10% of predicted. Large = asthma. Flow–volume loop: scooped expiration in obstruction; small narrow loop in restriction; flattened in upper airway obstruction. S25: FVC 60%, FEV1 82%, ratio 94% predicted = restrictive (old TB scarring).

ECG

25 mm/s: small square 0.04 s, large 0.2 s; 10 mm = 1 mV. P atria, QRS ventricles depolarise, T ventricles repolarise. PR 0.12–0.20 s (3–5 small); QRS below 0.12 s; QTc below ~0.44 (men), 0.46 (women). Rate: 300 ÷ large squares between R waves (regular), or QRS in 10 s × 6 (irregular). Order: rate, rhythm, P before every QRS, PR, QRS width, ST and T.

Sinus tachycardiaAbove 100: find cause; resting above 120 → postpone
Sinus bradycardiaBelow 60: beta-blockers, fit people; stop if dizzy or BP falls
AFNo P, irregularly irregular: exercise if rate controlled, use RPE; anticoagulant bleeding
EctopicsEarly beats (ventricular wide): frequent, multiform or runs in exercise → stop
VTFast wide regular: emergency; no pulse → CPR
VFChaotic, no QRS: arrest → CPR, defibrillate
Heart block1st: PR above 0.20; Mobitz I: PR lengthens then drops; Mobitz II: sudden drops; 3rd: P and QRS unrelated. Mobitz II, 3rd: no exercise until paced
ST changeElevation in neighbouring leads = acute MI; depression = ischaemia → stop, report (see IHD)
PacedSpikes before P or QRS: device precautions

Bedside cardiovascular tests

  • Pulses: radial, brachial, carotid (one side), femoral, popliteal, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial; 0 absent, 1+ weak, 2+ normal, 3+ bounding.
  • Postural hypotension: BP after 5 min lying, then 1 and 3 min standing: fall ≥ 20 systolic or ≥ 10 diastolic within 3 min (2011). Bed rest, diuretics, antihypertensives, diabetes, SCI.
  • ABI: highest ankle ÷ highest arm per leg: 0.91–1.40 normal, ≤ 0.90 PAD, below 0.40 severe, above 1.40 calcified (see PAD).
  • Claudication distance: treadmill ~3.2 km/h at 12% (or graded, or 6MWT): pain-free and maximal walking distance.
  • Capillary refill: press nail bed 5 s, colour back within ~2–3 s.
  • Auscultation: see Chest signs; ABG: see ABG.
