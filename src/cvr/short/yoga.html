Asked: not on its own in 2016–2026; possible 3- or 7-mark note or viva. Fits one or two lines in the long-term plan of COPD, asthma and cardiac answers.

What yoga includes

  • Asanas: postures for stretch, strength, balance.
  • Pranayama: slow deep breathing, anulom-vilom (alternate nostril), bhramari (humming), ujjayi; forceful kapalbhati, bhastrika; breath retention (kumbhaka).
  • Relaxation, meditation: shavasana, yoga nidra.
  • Ministry of AYUSH Common Yoga Protocol.

How it may help: slower deeper breathing and control; less anxiety and sympathetic drive; flexibility and posture; culturally familiar activity.

Evidence

After MIYoga-CaRe (India, 3,959, 2020): better self-rated health and return to activity; no significant fall in major events; good where usual rehab is unavailable
AsthmaCochrane 2016: small gains in QoL and symptoms; no clear lung function change
COPD2019 meta-analysis (11 trials, 586): ~25 m more 6MWT, slightly better FEV1, mainly breathing-focused yoga
HypertensionModest BP falls with regular breathing and relaxation: an add-on
Heart failureSmall trials: better QoL and tolerance with gentle supervised yoga

Claim vs evidence: yoga does not cure asthma or reverse heart disease or replace medicines. Write: "yoga as an adjunct to standard rehab: breathing-focused pranayama, gentle asanas and relaxation, avoiding breath-holding, straining and head-down postures".

Sample supervised session (stable COPD, cardiac Phase II onwards)

  1. Loosening 5–10 min: neck, shoulders, wrists, ankles, gentle trunk.
  2. Asanas 15–20 min, breathing normally: tadasana, vrikshasana near a wall, ardha kati chakrasana, supported trikonasana, vajrasana or sitting forward bends, bhujangasana if allowed; rests.
  3. Pranayama 10 min: slow breathing with longer exhalation, anulom-vilom without retention, bhramari.
  4. Relaxation 10 min: shavasana or yoga nidra.

3–5 times a week, as part of, not instead of, aerobic and strength training.

Avoid or adapt

  • Breath-holding and straining (Valsalva): heart disease, hypertension, aneurysm, after eye or brain surgery.
  • Kapalbhati, bhastrika: uncontrolled BP, heart disease, recent chest or abdominal surgery, hernia, pregnancy, epilepsy, asthma or COPD flare.
  • Inversions (sirsasana, sarvangasana, halasana): hypertension, heart failure, glaucoma, reflux, neck problems, breathless patients.
  • After sternotomy: no arm weight-bearing, prone on the chest or strong back bends for ~6–8 weeks.
  • Steroid osteoporosis: no loaded flexion or twisting.
  • No session: unstable angina, uncontrolled arrhythmia, exacerbation, fever.

Continue all medicines; stop with chest pain, dizziness, marked breathlessness; learn from a trained teacher in a supervised group.
