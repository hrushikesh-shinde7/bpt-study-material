Asked: 3 marks: reasons for desaturation with exercise (S21), causes of respiratory acidosis (S24). Inside COPD and ICU cases; ABG, PFT and X-ray in COPD goal setting (W24, 7 marks); a practical spot.

Normal values

pH7.35–7.45; below = acidaemia, above = alkalaemia
PaCO₂35–45 mmHg (4.7–6.0 kPa): respiratory part, set by ventilation
HCO₃⁻22–26 mmol/L: metabolic part
PaO₂80–100 mmHg (10.7–13.3 kPa), lower in the elderly; below 60 = respiratory failure
SaO₂95–98%
Base excess−2 to +2: negative in metabolic acidosis

Four steps

  1. pH: acidaemia or alkalaemia?
  2. Cause: PaCO₂ opposite to pH = respiratory; HCO₃⁻ same direction as pH = metabolic.
  3. Compensation: the other system moves the same way as the cause; partial (pH still abnormal) or full (pH normal, never over-corrected).
  4. Oxygenation: PaO₂ against FiO₂ (P/F ratio).

ROME

Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal.

Acute resp. acidosisHCO₃⁻ +1 per 10 mmHg PaCO₂ rise
Chronic resp. acidosisHCO₃⁻ +3.5–4 per 10
Acute resp. alkalosisHCO₃⁻ −2 per 10 fall
Metabolic acidosisExpected PaCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8 (± 2) (Winter's)
Metabolic alkalosisPaCO₂ +0.7 per 1 mmol/L HCO₃⁻

Worked examples

  • pH 7.28, PaCO₂ 62, HCO₃⁻ 28: respiratory acidosis; expected HCO₃⁻ ~26 acute, ~32 chronic; 28 between → acute-on-chronic (COPD exacerbation).
  • pH 7.48, PaCO₂ 30, HCO₃⁻ 22: acute respiratory alkalosis (anxiety, early asthma, PE, pain).
  • pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 28, HCO₃⁻ 12: metabolic acidosis; expected PaCO₂ 1.5 × 12 + 8 = 26 ± 2 → appropriate (DKA, Kussmaul).

Causes (3m S24: respiratory acidosis)

Respiratory acidosis (hypoventilation)Brain: sedatives, opioids, head injury, stroke. Nerves and muscles: GBS, myasthenia, high SCI, dystrophy, exhausted muscles. Chest wall: flail chest, kyphoscoliosis, obesity hypoventilation. Lungs: COPD exacerbation, late severe asthma, pneumonia, oedema, obstruction. Ventilator too low
Respiratory alkalosisAnxiety, pain, fever, hypoxaemia (pneumonia, PE, early asthma), altitude, pregnancy, ventilator too high
Metabolic acidosisDKA, lactic acidosis (shock, sepsis), kidney failure, diarrhoea, poisons
Metabolic alkalosisVomiting, NG suction, diuretics, low K⁺, antacids

Respiratory failure

Type 1 (hypoxaemic): PaO₂ below 60, PaCO₂ normal or low: gas exchange (pneumonia, oedema, ARDS, PE, ILD). Type 2 (hypercapnic): PaO₂ below 60 + PaCO₂ above ~45–50: pump failure or high load (COPD, neuromuscular, chest wall, sedation). P/F normal ~400–500 (95 ÷ 0.21 ≈ 450); ARDS 201–300 mild, 101–200 moderate, ≤ 100 severe. Low pH = acute (NIV); normal pH + high HCO₃⁻ = chronic.

Exercise desaturation (3m S21) and physio meaning

  • Worse V/Q as output rises; diffusion limitation (faster flow, less transit time; ILD, emphysema with low DLCO); hypoventilation (dynamic hyperinflation, tired muscles); low mixed venous O₂ (more extraction); pulmonary hypertension, shunt through a PFO; measurement error (cold fingers, movement: use ear probe).
  • SpO₂ in the 6MWT; oxygen in training if below ~88%; intervals; ILD desaturates most.
  • Acute type 2: gentle chest care, support NIV, no training, report drowsiness. Chronic CO₂ retention: SpO₂ 88–92%. Kussmaul breathing is compensation, not a breathing problem: inform the team. Recheck after big changes.
