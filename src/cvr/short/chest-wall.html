Flail chest

3 ribs, 2 breaks, 1 floating segment

A chest-wall segment with no bony connection: usually 3 or more neighbouring ribs each broken in 2 or more places. Paradoxical movement: sucked in on inspiration, pushed out on expiration.

Causes: road accidents, falls from height, crush; lesser falls in the elderly. Features: severe pain, shallow breathing, poor cough, low O₂; lung contusion is the main cause of hypoxia (often worsens over 1–2 days); haemo- or pneumothorax. X-ray/CT: multiple fractures, contusion, pleural air or fluid.

Medical: pain control is the key (epidural, paravertebral, nerve blocks); oxygen; NIV in some; ventilation for failure (positive pressure splints from inside); rib fixation; chest drain.

Flail chest: physio

  • Pain: treat after analgesia; supported positions; hand or pillow splint for cough.
  • Volumes: sit upright; deep breaths with 3-s hold, 5–10 every hour; expansion avoiding pressure on fractures.
  • Secretions: ACBT with gentle huffs, supported cough, suction if needed.
  • Ventilated: positioning, suction, careful chest handling, early mobilisation when stable.
  • Early sitting and walking; shoulder range on the injured side.

Precautions: no percussion, vibration or shaking over fractures; no positive pressure with an undrained pneumothorax; watch worsening hypoxia, haemoptysis, spine and head injury.

Scoliosis

Sideways spinal curve with vertebral rotation, Cobb angle ≥ 10°. Types: idiopathic (adolescent commonest, mostly girls), congenital, neuromuscular (CP, muscular dystrophy, SMA, SCI), degenerative, secondary (after thoracoplasty or empyema).

Features: uneven shoulders and waist, prominent scapula, rib hump on forward bending (Adam's test), scoliometer. Large thoracic, early-onset and neuromuscular curves stiffen the chest and limit lung growth: restrictive pattern, night hypoventilation.

Medical (SOSORT 2016): observation, scoliosis-specific exercise, bracing for moderate curves in growing children, fusion for large or progressive curves.

Physio: scoliosis-specific exercises (Schroth, SEAS): self-correction, stabilisation, breathing into the concave side; thoracic expansion to the concave side, rib mobility, IMT, aerobic fitness; neuromuscular: seating, clearance, assisted cough, night ventilation; after fusion: breathing, early walking, log-roll, spinal precautions. Follow brace schedules; no forceful mobilisation of progressive or fused curves.

Kyphosis

Excess thoracic forward curve (normal ~20–40°). Postural (flexible); Scheuermann's (rigid, adolescents, ≥ 5° wedging in 3 vertebrae); osteoporotic (wedge fractures); ankylosing spondylitis (rigid, fused costovertebral joints → very ↓ expansion); Pott's spine (angular gibbus); congenital.

Features: rounded back, forward head, back pain; ↓ expansion and vital capacity in severe or rigid curves; exertional breathlessness; falls (osteoporosis).

Physio: spinal extension and back-extensor strengthening, chest stretches, scapular retraction, posture awareness; lateral costal and diaphragmatic breathing; ankylosing spondylitis: daily chest expansion and spinal mobility; osteoporosis: weight-bearing, balance, falls prevention, no loaded flexion; bracing for Scheuermann's. Careful manual techniques on a rigid, fragile thorax.
