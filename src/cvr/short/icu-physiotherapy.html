ICU assessment (write this first)

Chart: diagnosis, course, days ventilated, trend; ventilator settings; HR, rhythm, BP and MAP, SpO₂, RR, temperature, ICP; ABG and X-ray (collapse, consolidation, effusion, pneumothorax, tube position); drugs (sedation, analgesia, BP support, paralysis); Hb, platelets, clotting, lactate, electrolytes, sugar; lines and drains (femoral lines limit hip flexion).

Bedside: GCS; RASS (−5 to +4); delirium (CAM-ICU); pattern, ventilator synchrony, chest movement, auscultation, secretions, cough on suction; ROM, tone, contractures, skin, oedema; when awake MRC sum score (0–60; below 48 = ICU-acquired weakness), bed mobility, sitting balance, ICU Mobility Scale (0 lying, 10 walking); pain (behavioural scale).

Safety before mobilising (Hodgson 2014)

Traffic lights: green go; yellow discuss; red no active mobilisation without senior agreement.

  • Respiratory green: tube or trach (not a barrier); FiO₂ ≤ 0.6, SpO₂ ≥ 90%, RR ≤ 30, PEEP ≤ 10. Red: SpO₂ below 90%, oscillation, prone.
  • Cardiovascular green: MAP above target with little support; rate below 120 in a stable tachyarrhythmia. Red: hypertensive emergency drugs, symptomatic low MAP, bradycardia needing drugs, rate above 150, active ischaemia.
  • Neurological green: RASS −1 to +1, follows commands. Red: RASS above +2 or below −2, raised ICP being treated, uncleared spine, uncontrolled seizures.
  • Other red: active bleeding; unstable pelvic, spinal or long-bone fracture; big open wound; femoral sheaths.

Near the limit in several areas (W24: FiO₂ 0.6, PEEP 10, SpO₂ 90%) → decide with a senior ICU doctor. Stop: SpO₂ below ~88–90% not recovering; sharp HR change, new arrhythmia, MAP below target; RR above ~35, fighting, distress; tube or line displaced; fall.

Physio for the ventilated patient (S17, S21, S26; goals W17)

Short-term goals (W17): clear airways and expanded lungs, better oxygenation; prevent VAP, collapse, aspiration; keep joint range, prevent contractures, pressure injuries, DVT; limit wasting, start active exercise when safe; support weaning; orientation and communication.

  • Check settings, vitals, ABG, X-ray, sedation, safety; time with analgesia, away from feeds.
  • Position: head of bed 30–45°; side-lying, affected area up; turns every 2–4 h; sit up early.
  • Manual techniques on expiration: shaking, vibration, percussion for retained secretions (not with fractures, low platelets, instability).
  • Hyperinflation by bag or ventilator to re-open lung and move secretions, then suction.
  • Awake on SIMV: deep breaths with a hold on own breaths; IMT in weaning.
  • Limbs: passive ROM when sedated → active-assisted → active; ankle and hand splints.
  • Mobilisation ladder: rolling → sitting up → edge of bed → standing → marching → chair → walking with the ventilator.
  • Reassess breath sounds, SpO₂, secretions, pressures; record.

Precautions: high PEEP or FiO₂ → closed suction, ventilator hyperinflation; undrained pneumothorax → no positive pressure; unstable BP → gentle only; raised ICP → head up 30°, neutral neck, short sessions, no head-down; low platelets → no percussion; recent lung surgery → no deep suction or high pressure near the stump.

GBS on SIMV (S23: 45, teacher, PEEP 6, FiO₂ 0.4)

Immune attack on peripheral nerves → ascending symmetrical flaccid weakness; ventilated for weak muscles, not lung disease (modest settings).

  • Impairments: weak breathing muscles, low VC (20/30/40 rule: VC below 20 mL/kg, MIP weaker than −30, MEP below 40); weak cough, secretions, collapse risk; swallowing weakness; autonomic instability (suction → bradycardia); flaccid paralysis, areflexia, sensory loss, nerve pain; ankle contracture, pressure, DVT risk; anxiety, cannot communicate.
  • Physio: 2–4-hourly turns, head up, hyperinflation with vibrations, suction (pre-oxygenate, watch HR); track VC and MIP for weaning; tracheostomy common; weaning with more spontaneous breathing, short non-fatiguing IMT sets, upright; gentle passive ROM, ankles 90°, functional hands; then active work without fatigue (overwork weakens recovering muscle); pressure care, tilt table, communication board, reassurance.

ARDS (S24 case; acute phase 7m S25)

Berlin 2012: within 1 week of an insult; bilateral opacities; not explained by heart failure; P/F on PEEP ≥ 5: mild 201–300, moderate 101–200, severe ≤ 100. (2024 global definition adds high-flow oxygen and SpO₂/FiO₂.)

Medical: lung-protective ventilation (VT 6 mL/kg, plateau ≤ 30), PEEP, careful fluids, prone ≥ 16 h a day if P/F below 150 (PROSEVA). Complications: lung injury, pneumothorax, VAP, ICU-acquired weakness, contractures, pressure injuries (prone), DVT, delirium, post-intensive care syndrome.

Acute physio: minimal clustered handling; no disconnection (closed suction, ventilator hyperinflation, no high pressures); head up, turns; help with safe prone turns (eyes, face, shoulders, knees, lines); passive ROM and positioning. As she improves (FiO₂ 0.4, PEEP 5 = green): active exercise, edge of bed, standing and walking on the ventilator, IMT, then graded exercise and follow-up rehab.

Evidence and exam plan

Early mobilisation is safe within criteria: physio during sedation breaks → more independent at discharge (59% vs 35%), less delirium (Schweickert 2009); TEAM 2022: more aggressive early mobilisation did not help survival and caused more adverse events: dose to suit the patient. ABCDEF bundle: pain, awakening and breathing trials, sedation choice, delirium, early mobility, family.

Ventilated case skeleton: read the case and settings (low, moderate, high support) → impairments or ICF (chest, muscles and joints, circulation and skin, consciousness, communication) → complications → physio: safety check → positioning → clearance → limbs → mobilisation ladder → weaning → after extubation → precautions.
