NPF: what and who

Neurophysiological facilitation: external touch and pressure (proprioceptive and tactile) producing reflex changes in breathing: deeper breaths, more movement in a chosen area, rate changes (Bethune 1975, unconscious adults).

For patients who cannot follow commands: unconscious or sedated (incl. ventilated with own breathing), head injury, stroke, confused, young children. Aims: deeper breathing, lung expansion, basal and segmental ventilation, secretion movement, oxygenation. Dose: hold through several breaths (30 s – 1 min), repeat while it works; watch breathing, SpO₂, HR.

NPF techniques (7m W22)

TechniqueHowResponse
Perioral pressureFirm maintained finger pressure on the upper lipMore upper-abdominal movement, deeper breaths, sighs; mouth closure, swallowing (sucking reflex)
Intercostal stretchPush the upper border of a rib down to widen the space above; holdMore expansion there (intercostal stretch reflex)
Vertebral pressureFirm pressure on spinous processes: upper T2–T5 or lower T7–T10Upper: more abdominal movement, deeper breaths. Lower: more apical movement
Co-contraction of abdomenLower ribs and pelvis on one side drawn towards each otherUpper-abdominal movement, posterior basal expansion, firmer abdominal wall
Anterior stretch basal liftSupine; hands under lower posterior ribs lift gently and holdPosterior basal expansion, abdominal movement
Maintained manual pressureFirm steady hand on an area of chest wallMore movement under the hand on inspiration

Precautions: no pressure over fractured ribs, unstable spine, osteoporosis, painful wounds or burns; stop if agitated, HR or BP unstable, SpO₂ falls; raised ICP: short, gentle. Evidence: small studies, effects last minutes: combine with positioning and other techniques.

PNF for respiration (7m W24, S25)

Limb PNF principles (manual contacts, stretch, resistance, commands, irradiation from strong patterns) applied to breathing muscles in patients who can co-operate.

Repeated quick stretch with resistanceHands on lower lateral ribs (or upper abdomen); quick stretch in and down at end-expiration, light resistance through inspiration, re-stretch during it ("push my hands out"): stretch reflex, more expansion
Bilateral D2 flexion with inspirationArms up and out on breathing in, D2 extension on breathing out, can be resisted: irradiation, upper chest expands
Trunk lift and chopLift (diagonal up, extension, rotation) on inspiration; chop (diagonal down, flexion) on expiration and cough
Scapular posterior elevationSide-lying, resisted on inspiration: upper and lateral expansion
Rhythmic initiationPassive → active-assisted → active → resisted breathing rhythm: teaches a coordinated pattern
Approximation; timing for emphasisRib compression at end-expiration; hold a strong pattern to direct expansion to a weak area

For "three techniques" (W24): repeated quick stretch with resistance, bilateral D2 flexion with inspiration, trunk lift and chop, each with method and effect.

Indications: weak or uncoordinated breathing (stroke, SCI, neuromuscular), post-op and post-ICU low expansion, basal collapse. Precautions: rib fractures, wounds and drains under the hands, pain, unstable cardiovascular status.
