Definition and types

Asthma: chronic inflammatory airway disease with variable symptoms (wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, cough) and variable airflow limitation, often reversing with treatment or on its own. Exacerbation: worsening beyond usual, needing a change in treatment.

Types: allergic (childhood, eczema, rhinitis, family history); non-allergic (adult onset); occupational (flour, grain dust, chemicals: farmers); exercise-induced bronchoconstriction; asthma–COPD overlap in smokers.

Control: rare daytime symptoms and reliever use, no night waking, no activity limit. Asthma Control Test (ACT), Childhood ACT.

Risk factors and triggers

Risk: family history, atopy, obesity, smoking and passive smoke, prematurity, early viral infections. Triggers: infections; dust mites, pollen, mould, animal fur, cockroaches; smoke, chulha smoke, mosquito coils, incense; cold air, weather change (winter, rains); exercise; strong smells; stress; aspirin, beta-blockers; work dusts.

Pathophysiology to impairments

  • Trigger → mast cells, eosinophils → three causes of narrowing: bronchospasm, mucosal swelling, mucus plugs.
  • Obstruction mainly on breathing out → air trapping, hyperinflation, wheeze, breathlessness.
  • Uneven ventilation → low O₂; CO₂ low at first (fast breathing). Normal or rising CO₂ = tiring: danger.
  • Years of inflammation → remodelling: some obstruction becomes fixed.
  • Fear of attacks → avoids exercise → deconditioning; some develop fast upper-chest (dysfunctional) breathing.

Features: episodic wheeze, breathlessness, tightness, cough, worse at night and early morning, triggered; normal between attacks. In an attack: fast breathing, accessory muscles, prolonged expiration with wheeze, cannot speak, anxiety; silent chest = life-threatening. Children: night cough, cough with play, avoid running, miss school.

Investigations

Spirometry with bronchodilator (FEV1 rise over 12% and 200 mL); peak-flow diary (variability; basis of the action plan); exercise challenge (EIB); FeNO, eosinophils, allergy tests; ABG and SpO₂ in an attack (rising CO₂ → call the team); chest X-ray (normal or hyperinflated; excludes pneumothorax, pneumonia).

Action plan zones: green above 80% of best peak flow; yellow 50–80%; red below 50% (doctor sets the medicines for each zone).

Medical management

  • Inhaled steroid-containing treatment for everyone; GINA no longer recommends salbutamol alone at any step. Adults: low-dose ICS–formoterol as reliever (and maintenance at higher steps). Severe: LAMA, leukotriene antagonists, biologics.
  • Inhaler technique with a spacer (mask for young children).
  • Attack: repeated reliever by spacer or nebuliser, oral steroids, oxygen to SpO₂ 94–98%, ipratropium, magnesium; ICU if life-threatening.
  • Trigger avoidance, stop smoking, treat rhinitis, written action plan. W22 girl on SOS salbutamol only with monthly attacks → needs medical review.

Complications

Life-threatening attack and respiratory failure (sit up, oxygen, call for help); pneumothorax (stop, report); mucus plugging and collapse; deconditioning, obesity; dysfunctional breathing (sighing, light-headed); steroid side effects; missed school or work, anxiety.

PT assessment

History: triggers, attacks and admissions, night symptoms, reliever use, inhaler technique, smoking, job and dust, home, school or work absence, sport and play. Observe: pattern, rate, accessory muscles, posture, speaking in sentences. Auscultation (wheeze, prolonged expiration). Peak flow, SpO₂ (ABG if severe). 6MWT or shuttle (children: shuttle or step test) with SpO₂ and peak flow before and after. Nijmegen questionnaire if dysfunctional breathing.

Outcome measures: ACT or Childhood ACT, AQLQ (PAQLQ for children), peak-flow diary, exercise test, school or workdays missed, attacks a year.

ICF (W22: 7-year-old girl, monthly attacks, SOS salbutamol, kept from play)

  • Body function: episodic breathlessness and wheeze; ↓ exercise tolerance from inactivity; possible poor breathing pattern; anxiety.
  • Activity: running and active play, carrying her bag, stairs in attacks.
  • Participation: missed school; kept from outdoor play and sport; less time with friends.
  • Environment: fearful parents and teachers, reliever-only medicine (barriers); caring family, school, health services (facilitators). Personal: wants to play.

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): parents and teacher understand asthma and the plan; correct spacer technique; breathing control and warm-up; carries her own lighter bag. Long-term (3–6 months): plays outdoors and joins sport, better shuttle or 6MWT, fewer missed days and attacks, uses the plan independently.

Acute exacerbation (7m S22)

  • High sitting or forward-lean sitting with arms supported.
  • Breathing control and relaxation: slow, gentle lower-chest breathing, relaxed shoulders, calm reassurance; no deep forced breathing during bronchospasm.
  • Oxygen and nebulised bronchodilator as prescribed; airway clearance after the bronchodilator, once spasm eases: ACBT with gentle huffs.
  • No percussion or vibration in acute bronchospasm. Fluids; mobilise as it settles.
  • Before discharge: inhaler technique, action plan, triggers, quitting, follow-up. Discharge when peak flow is over ~75% of best.

Rationale of ACBT (7m S17): breathing control between parts prevents bronchospasm and fatigue; thoracic expansion gets air behind plugs (collateral ventilation); huffs move mucus without the narrowing and coughing fits of forceful coughs; patient controls the pace, so back to breathing control if wheeze rises.

Long-term management

  • Breathing retraining (Papworth, Buteyko-based): nose breathing, slower rate, relaxed lower chest, less over-breathing; 10–15 min daily; better symptoms and QoL (Cochrane 2020).
  • Aerobic training 2–3 times a week for 8–12 weeks: fitness and QoL without worsening asthma; swimming suits (warm humid air).
  • Exercise-induced symptoms: 10–15 min warm-up with short bursts, pre-exercise inhaler if prescribed, nose breathing, avoid cold dry air and pollution, gradual cool-down.
  • IMT if weak; posture, thoracic mobility, relaxation; self-management: spacer, peak-flow diary, action plan, triggers.
  • Energy conservation (W19 teacher): pace ("blow as you go"), plan heavy jobs across the week, prioritise, sit for chores, sit for part of each class, limit stair trips, dustless chalk, microphone; damp dusting, mask, no sprays.

Children (7m S18, S25; W22)

Education with the family: triggers, spacer with mask, action plan. Breathing games: bubbles, windmills, cotton-ball races, "smell the flower, blow the candle". Exercise as play: warm-up games, interval play, skipping, cycling, swimming, 2–3 times a week, progressing; body-weight strength games, posture.

Parent and teacher counselling (W22): asthma can be controlled and well-controlled children play sport; exercise is good, warm-up and reliever prevent attacks; know early signs and plan steps; inhaler and spacer at school, teacher knows how to help; no smoking at home, dust control, no coils or incense; let her carry a light bag; medical review (reliever-only no longer recommended); emergency if she cannot speak, lips blue, reliever not helping.

Outcome measures (3m W22): Childhood ACT, PAQLQ, peak-flow diary, 6MWT or shuttle, school days missed, attacks and reliever use, time in active play.

Precautions and stop

No percussion, vibration, forced breathing or head-down drainage in acute bronchospasm; reliever within reach every session; no exercise in cold dry air, pollution, an attack or a chest infection. Get help: cannot finish sentences, very fast breathing, SpO₂ below 92%, silent chest, blue lips, drowsy or confused, reliever not working or needed more than 4-hourly.

Education (7m W21: why it matters)

Most severe attacks and deaths follow poor control: preventers not taken, relievers over-used, poor technique, no plan. Self-management with a written action plan and review cuts admissions, emergency visits, night symptoms and missed days. Teach: preventer even when well; spacer, rinse mouth after steroids; peak-flow diary; triggers (hot-wash and sun-dry bedding, no smoking); exercise with warm-up; daily breathing practice; flu vaccine; mask for farmers.
