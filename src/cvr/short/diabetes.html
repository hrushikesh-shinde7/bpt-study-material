Definitions and types

  • Diabetes mellitus: raised blood glucose from lack of insulin, poor response to it, or both. Type 1: autoimmune, young, insulin, ketoacidosis. Type 2: insulin resistance + failing secretion; obesity, inactivity, family history; commonest, younger in Indians. Gestational; secondary (pancreas, steroids).
  • Diabetic foot: infection, ulcer or tissue destruction of the foot from neuropathy and/or PAD.
  • Diabetes insipidus (AVP deficiency or resistance): cannot concentrate urine → large volumes (over ~3 L a day) of dilute urine, thirst; sugar normal.

Wagner: 0 at-risk foot; 1 superficial ulcer; 2 deep to tendon, capsule or bone; 3 abscess or osteomyelitis; 4 forefoot gangrene; 5 whole-foot gangrene. IWGDF risk: 0 no LOPS or PAD (yearly checks) → 3 LOPS or PAD + past ulcer, amputation or end-stage kidney disease (every 1–3 months).

Why the diabetic foot ulcerates

  • Sensory neuropathy: no protective feeling: injuries, tight shoes, burns unnoticed.
  • Motor neuropathy: weak small foot muscles → claw toes, prominent metatarsal heads → high forefoot pressure → callus.
  • Autonomic neuropathy: no sweating → dry cracked skin → bacteria enter.
  • Ischaemia slows healing; high sugar weakens immunity, infection spreads.

Pressure under a callus → bleeding and breakdown beneath = neuropathic ulcer → + infection + ischaemia → gangrene, amputation.

Other complications: silent MI, stroke, PAD; retinopathy (bleeding with straining, head-down); nephropathy; postural hypotension and fixed HR (autonomic); frozen shoulder, stiff hands, Charcot foot.

Features: thirst, polyuria, tiredness, weight loss, blurred vision, slow healing, infections (type 2 often silent). Neuropathic foot: numb, burning, warm, dry, callus, claw toes, pulses present, painless ulcer under forefoot or heel. Ischaemic foot: cold, pale, no pulses, painful toe and heel ulcers.

Investigations and foot tests

HbA1c (3-month average; target ~7%, individual); glucometer before and after exercise (insulin, sulfonylureas); 10 g monofilament (loss of protective sensation); 128 Hz tuning fork, ankle reflexes; pulses, ABI (falsely high if calcified), toe pressures; lipids, kidney, ECG, eyes; water deprivation test for insipidus.

Medical management

Lifestyle; metformin; sulfonylureas (hypoglycaemia); DPP-4 inhibitors; SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists (protect heart and kidney); insulin. BP and lipid control; yearly eye, kidney, foot checks. Foot ulcer: off-loading (total contact cast), debridement, infection control, revascularisation, sugar control. Insipidus: desmopressin.

Wound healing (3m S19; 7m S25)

Haemostasis (minutes–hours) → inflammation (days 1–5) → proliferation (day 4–week 3) → remodelling (week 3 to 1–2 years). Diabetes slows inflammation and proliferation most.

Local factors: infection, poor blood supply, oedema, dead tissue, pressure and shear, too dry or wet, size and depth, radiotherapy. General: age, nutrition (protein, vitamin C, zinc), high sugar, smoking, anaemia, obesity, steroids, chemotherapy, organ failure, immunity, stress. In the diabetic foot: sugar, ischaemia, unnoticed pressure, infection, smoking.

Assessment

Type, duration, medicines (insulin, sulfonylureas), HbA1c, hypos, complications; activity, job, diet, smoking, BMI and waist. Feet: skin, nails, callus, deformity, footwear, monofilament, vibration, pulses, ulcer (site, size, depth, Wagner). BP lying and standing; resting HR; 6MWT; strength; balance (single-leg stance, TUG). Outcomes: HbA1c, weight, waist, 6MWT, ulcer area, balance.

ICF (practice: shopkeeper, 12 years, HbA1c 9%, numb feet, callus, walks barefoot): loss of sensation, callus, ↓ fitness, weight, balance → unsafe walking, long standing → work days at risk; barefoot habit and shop snacks (barriers), family and clinic (facilitators).

Exercise for glycaemic control (7m S16, W18)

Why: contracting muscle takes up glucose without insulin; training raises insulin sensitivity for a day or more; lowers weight, BP, lipids, heart risk.

Aerobic≥ 150 min a week moderate–vigorous over 3–7 days; no more than 2 days in a row without exercise
Resistance2–3 times a week, non-consecutive; 1–3 sets of 10–15 reps
Flexibility, balance2–3 days a week; stretches 10–30 s × 2–4; balance with neuropathy
Less sittingA few minutes of light activity every 30 min

Rule of 15

Sugar below 70 or symptoms (sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion): 15 g fast sugar (glucose tablets, 3 tsp sugar in water, half glass juice), recheck in 15 min, repeat, then a snack. Hypos can come hours after exercise.

Goals: short-term: daily foot checks, footwear always, walk 20 min most days with safe readings, knows hypo plan. Long-term: 150 min/week + resistance twice; lower HbA1c and weight; no ulcers; better balance.

Active plantar ulcer: off-load (cast, boot, crutches); non-weight-bearing exercise (cycling with the other leg, arm ergometer, swimming once healed). Neuropathy without ulcer: moderate walking is safe with good footwear and checks.

Parameters vary between books: write a range with the effect. Check circulation first; no heat or electrotherapy over ischaemic or numb skin unchecked.

Pressure sores (7m W19)

Damage from pressure (± shear) over bony points: sacrum, heels, trochanters, ischial tuberosities, occiput, elbows. At risk: bed- or chair-bound, numb skin, ICU, poor nutrition, incontinence, elderly.

NPIAP stages: 1 non-blanching redness, intact skin; 2 partial-thickness, shallow pink wound or blister; 3 full-thickness, fat visible; 4 muscle, tendon or bone exposed; unstageable (slough or eschar covers base); deep tissue injury (persistent maroon or purple). Braden scale 6–23: ≤ 18 = at risk.

Physio: reposition on an individual schedule ("at least 2-hourly, or as skin and surface require"); 30° tilted side-lying; head of bed ≤ 30°; float heels; foam or alternating-pressure mattress, chair cushion, weight shifts; early mobility, exercise; lift, don't drag (slide sheets); electrical stimulation has the best modality evidence for slow stage 2 and stage 3–4 sores; nutrition, continence; daily skin checks, never lie on a red area.

Insipidus: free access to water and toilet; dehydration signs; night falls; ICU after head injury or pituitary surgery: check fluid balance.

Exercise precautions

Check sugar before and after (insulin, sulfonylureas); carry fast sugar; avoid the peak of insulin action; treat a low first; no exercise with very high sugar and ketones (type 1). Advanced retinopathy: no vigorous exercise, heavy lifting, jumping, head-down, breath-holding. Neuropathy: footwear, feet checked after sessions, balance. Autonomic: slow position changes, RPE not HR, avoid heat. Silent heart disease: report unusual breathlessness. No heat or ice on numb skin.

Lifestyle: active daily, break sitting every 30 min; fewer refined carbs and sweets; BMI under 23, waist under 90/80 cm; medicines; lifelong foot care; stop tobacco.
