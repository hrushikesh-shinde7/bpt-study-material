MET (3m W22, S24, S25)

1 MET = energy used sitting quietly at rest = 3.5 mL O₂/kg/min (~1 kcal/kg/h). 4 METs = four times resting.

(2024 Compendium.) Light below 3, moderate 3–5.9, vigorous ≥ 6 METs. Phase I up to ~3 METs; good capacity ≥ 7 METs; sex when 3–5 METs tolerated.

Energy systems (7m S24)

ATP–PCCreatine phosphate remakes ATP, no O₂: maximal efforts up to ~10 s: sprint, jump, single heavy lift
Anaerobic glycolysisGlucose → lactate without O₂, fast, little ATP: 10 s–2 min: 400 m, fast stair flights
AerobicCarbohydrate and fat with O₂ in mitochondria, slow, large yield: over 2 min and rest: walking, cycling, marathon

Fat dominates at rest and low intensity; carbohydrate as intensity rises. RQ: carbohydrate 1.0, fat 0.7.

  • Karvonen (W25): target HR = rest + intensity × (max − rest); max = 220 − age (or 208 − 0.7 × age). Age 60, rest 70: max 160, HRR 90, 50% → 115.
  • % HRmax; % VO₂max or VO₂ reserve (CPET = gold standard); METs; RPE 6–20 or CR10 (3–5 in pulmonary rehab); talk test (talk not sing = moderate); ~80% of 6MWT or shuttle speed; ventilatory or lactate threshold; ≥ 10 beats below the ischaemic threshold; % 1RM for strength.
  • Use RPE and talk test with beta-blockers, AF, pacemakers, heart transplant.

Training principles and adaptations

FITT-VP; overload, specificity, progression, individuality, reversibility (gains fade in weeks), warm-up and cool-down.

Acute: ↑ HR, stroke volume, output, systolic BP (diastolic similar), ventilation, muscle blood flow and O₂ extraction. Chronic: ↓ resting and submaximal HR, ↑ stroke volume and max output, ↓ resting BP; less ventilation per load; more capillaries and mitochondria, ↑ VO₂max; strength and size with resistance; better insulin sensitivity, HDL, weight.

Ageing and fitness (7m S16)

Heart: ↓ max HR, stiffer arteries (↑ systolic), slower HR response, ↓ VO₂max. Lungs: ↓ recoil, stiff chest wall, ↑ RV, ↓ VC, earlier airway closure, weaker muscles, slightly ↓ PaO₂. Muscle, bone: sarcopenia (fast fibres), bone loss, stiff joints, poor balance.

Programme (WHO 2020): 150–300 min moderate (or 75–150 vigorous) a week; strength ≥ 2 days; balance and strength ≥ 3 days (falls); start low, go slow; longer warm-up and cool-down; check drugs and heart first.
