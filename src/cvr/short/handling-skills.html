Asked: practical exam only: short-case demonstrations and viva on stockings, bandaging, dressings, taping, splints and orthoses, exercise and monitoring equipment, and monitoring adherence (practical list 3, 5, 7).

Compression stockings

Anti-embolism stockings (bed-bound, DVT prevention) vs graduated compression (walking patients with venous disease or lymphoedema; highest at the ankle). Classes differ by country: follow the prescription.

Check first: ABPI (none below ~0.5; specialist 0.5–0.8), skin, sensation, uncontrolled heart failure. Measure in the morning: narrowest ankle, widest calf, heel to below the knee crease.

Donning: morning before getting up (or after 15–20 min elevation); turn inside out to the heel; foot in, heel centred; ease up a little at a time (rubber gloves, frame aid); smooth wrinkles; top band two fingers below the knee crease, never rolled down (tourniquet). Off: peel down inside out; at night unless prescribed; check skin daily; wash; replace every few months.

Bandaging and dressings

  • Venous ulcer: after ABPI; ankle 90°; pad bony points; toes to below the knee incl. heel, spiral or figure-of-eight, ~50% overlap, firmest at the ankle; check toes after.
  • Lymphoedema: multilayer short-stretch (see Mastectomy). Stump: figure-of-eight, firmest at the end, no circular turns (see Amputation).
  • Always loosen and report: pain, numbness, tingling, pale or blue digits, swelling above the bandage.
  • Dressings: work around them safely: no shear on dressings or grafts, support the wound on coughing, keep dry; burns exercise at dressing changes. Report: soaking, loose or wet dressing, spreading redness, heat, pus, smell, fever, wound opening. Types: gauze, film, hydrocolloid, foam (heavy exudate), alginate (very wet), silver (infected).
  • Taping: kinesiology tape for mild lymphoedema, posture, chest-wall pain; limited evidence; clean intact skin, test patch; not over wounds, drains, grafts, irradiated or numb skin; remove if itching or blisters, peel along the hair.

Exercise and monitoring equipment

TreadmillShow emergency stop and safety clip; start slow and flat; light hand support; small steps up; slow before stopping
Cycle, arm ergometerKnee slightly bent at the bottom; low resistance first; arm crank at or below shoulder height (after sternotomy only when allowed)
HR monitorChest strap beats wrist; check against pulse; unreliable on beta-blockers and in AF
Pulse oximeterWarm finger, no nail polish, steady trace; ear probe or recheck at rest when walking
SphygmomanometerRight cuff, arm at heart level, seated and rested; recheck high readings
Weights, bandsLoad from 1RM or reps; breathe out on effort; progress with "2 for 2"
Pressure biofeedbackPad under the lower back (~40 mmHg) or abdomen; keep or lower pressure while drawing in: core and posture
Balance board, Swiss ballBalance and trunk training in deconditioned and post-ICU patients; near a rail, supervised

Monitoring adherence

How: exercise and symptom diaries, step counts, attendance, check inhaler and breathing technique each visit, open questions ("How many days did you walk last week?"). Barriers: cost, travel, work hours, fear of breathlessness or chest pain, low mood, poor understanding, family beliefs ("rest is best"), other illnesses. Helps: goals the patient chose; short picture programme in his language; link to routines; family; phone or WhatsApp reminders, video reviews; praise; adjust the plan.
