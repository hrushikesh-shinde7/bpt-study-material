Oxygen is a drug

Given to correct or prevent hypoxaemia, prescribed with a target SpO₂; it does not treat breathlessness itself.

Indications: acute hypoxaemia (pneumonia, asthma, heart failure, PE, post-op), critical illness, CO poisoning, exercise desaturation, long-term oxygen (see COPD). Not for breathless patients with normal SpO₂ or MI with SpO₂ ≥ 90%.

Low-flow oxygen therapy (7m S20)

  • Definition: flow lower than the patient's inspiratory flow → mixed with room air → FiO₂ varies.
  • Devices: nasal cannula, simple mask, reservoir mask, tracheostomy mask (oxygen catheter in older books).
  • Uses: mild–moderate hypoxaemia in stable patients; home and ambulatory oxygen; exercise desaturation.
  • Advantages: simple, cheap, comfortable; can eat, talk, walk.
  • Disadvantages: FiO₂ not exact (higher with slow shallow breathing, lower with fast), so less suitable when CO₂ must be controlled; nasal dryness.
  • Humidification: not needed at low flow; consider for high flow over ~24 h, tracheostomy, dryness.
  • Monitor: SpO₂ against target, RR, drowsiness (rising CO₂), ABG if at risk.

Hazards (3m S25)

  • CO₂ retention in at-risk patients (V/Q and Haldane effect; drive plays a small part): drowsy, confused, acidotic.
  • Absorption atelectasis (nitrogen washed out, oxygen absorbed, poorly ventilated alveoli collapse).
  • Oxygen toxicity (FiO₂ above ~0.6 for long). Retinopathy of prematurity.
  • Fire: no smoking, flames, chulha; no grease on fittings; face burns in smokers.
  • Masking deterioration: good SpO₂ hides worsening breathing; watch the rate.
  • Dry nose, nosebleeds, ear and nose pressure sores, mask claustrophobia, aspiration if vomiting; tubing trips, empty or heavy cylinders.

Physio role

Check prescription and target; never change oxygen without it. SpO₂ during walking; oxygen to keep SpO₂ above ~88–90% in training; portable cylinders or concentrators. Keep oxygen on during clearance and positioning; watch SpO₂ after turns and suction. Teach LTOT users: ≥ 15 h a day, no smoking, clean cannula, pacing. Oximeter errors: cold fingers, poor circulation, movement, nail polish, CO (falsely normal), over-reading in darker skin.
