Asked: 15-mark cases after lobectomy, segmentectomy or pneumonectomy (functional diagnosis or ICF + short-term + long-term). 7 marks: complications, intercostal drain and underwater seal, incisions and thoracotomy complications, pneumothorax with drain care, pneumonectomy, lobectomy. 3 marks: muscles cut in posterolateral thoracotomy, three incisions, indications for a chest drain.

Definitions and operations

Thoracic surgery on lung, pleura, chest wall, oesophagus or mediastinum always lowers lung volume and cough strength and stiffens the shoulder and trunk on that side. Intercostal drain (ICD): a tube between the ribs into the pleural space, to an underwater seal that lets air and fluid out but not back.

Operations: pneumonectomy (whole lung), lobectomy (commonest for cancer), segmentectomy, wedge, sleeve resection, decortication (empyema peel), pleurectomy and pleurodesis, bullectomy, thoracoplasty, transplant.

Indications: lung cancer, localised bronchiectasis, abscess, aspergilloma, TB complications, hydatid cyst (W16), empyema, recurrent pneumothorax, chest trauma.

Surgery to impairments

  • Anaesthesia and lying flat → ↓ FRC, basal airway closure.
  • Pain from incision, rib spreading and drains → shallow breaths, poor cough → sputum retention → atelectasis → pneumonia.
  • Lung removed → ↓ TLC and exercise reserve; remaining lung must expand.
  • Cut shoulder-girdle muscles and guarding → stiff shoulder, trunk bent towards the incision.
  • Bed rest and drains → ↓ mobility and fitness, DVT risk.

Features: incision and drain pain worse on breathing and coughing; shallow fast breathing; ↓ expansion and sounds on the operated side; crackles; weak painful cough; drains, oxygen, epidural; stiff shoulder; fatigue.

Tests: pre-op FEV1 and DLCO (predicted post-op function), stair climb, shuttle or CPET (prehab); post-op X-ray (re-expansion, collapse, surgical emphysema); ABG, SpO₂; Hb.

Intercostal drain (7m W20; 3m S25)

  • Indications: large or breathless pneumothorax; tension (after needle decompression); any pneumothorax on a ventilator; haemothorax; empyema; large or malignant effusion (and before pleurodesis); chylothorax; after chest surgery.
  • Safe triangle: front edge of latissimus dorsi, side edge of pectoralis major, line at nipple level (5th space), apex below the axilla.
  • Underwater seal: tube ends ~2 cm under water; air bubbles out, water stops it being sucked back. Bottle always below the chest.
  • Swing = tube patent and in the pleura. Bubbling = air leak. No swing = kinked, blocked, or lung fully expanded. Low suction may be added.

Medical: epidural, paravertebral or intercostal block, PCA; oxygen; antibiotics; enhanced recovery (walk day 1); chemo or radiotherapy for cancer.

Complications (7m S17, S18)

  • Sputum retention, atelectasis, pneumonia (↓ sounds, crackles, fever, ↓ SpO₂): deep breathing, ACBT, supported cough, walking.
  • Respiratory failure; DVT and PE; arrhythmia (AF): check pulse first.
  • Air leak, bronchopleural fistula (persistent bubbling; after pneumonectomy coughing thin fluid): report, no high-pressure techniques.
  • Haemorrhage (heavy blood in drain, ↓ BP): stop and report. Surgical emphysema (crackling under skin): report, check drain.
  • Empyema, wound infection; nerve injury (recurrent laryngeal: hoarse, weak cough; phrenic: raised diaphragm).
  • Stiff shoulder, posture, scoliosis: shoulder and trunk exercises from day 1. Post-thoracotomy pain syndrome: desensitise, TENS.

PT assessment

Pre-op: lung function, exercise tolerance, smoking, sputum, shoulder range, job, hand dominance; teach the plan. Post-op chart: operation, incision, drains (number, site, swing, bubbling, amount and type), analgesia, oxygen, X-ray. Respiratory: pattern, rate, SpO₂, expansion, auscultation, cough. Pain at rest and on coughing (VAS). Shoulder range, posture. Bed mobility, sitting, standing, walking with SpO₂. Outcome measures: 6MWT, shoulder ROM, pain, EORTC QoL.

ICF (W16: 40, right-handed labourer, right lower lobectomy for hydatid cyst, posterolateral thoracotomy, day 2, ICD, productive cough)

  • Body function: incision and drain pain; ↓ lung volume, shallow breathing, atelectasis risk; retained secretions, weak cough; ↓ right shoulder range and strength; ↓ exercise tolerance. Reasons: pain and anaesthesia, cut latissimus, serratus and intercostals, lung removed.
  • Activity: bed mobility, walking with the drain, self-care with the right arm, lifting. Participation: labour work (heavy, overhead), family income.
  • Environment: hospital, family (facilitators); heavy job, dust, daily wages (barriers). Personal: 40, right-handed, young.

Goals: short-term (~1 week): clear chest, effective supported cough, lung re-expanded, pain controlled, near-full shoulder flexion and abduction, walks and climbs one flight. Long-term (6–8 weeks): normal breathing and chest mobility, full shoulder strength for overhead work, better 6MWT, graded return to labour.

PT management, day by day

  • Before surgery: explain; teach breathing, ACBT, supported cough, bed mobility, shoulder exercises; stop smoking; prehabilitation (aerobic + strength for a few weeks) cuts lung complications and stay (Cochrane).
  • Day 0–1: sit upright, high side-lying either side (after lobectomy); deep breaths with 3-s hold, thoracic expansion to the operated side, ACBT; supported huff and cough (pillow or arm over the incision and drain); ankle pumps; sit out, march or walk with the drain; active-assisted shoulder movement.
  • Day 2–4: walk 3–4 times a day, stairs before discharge; active shoulder flexion and abduction, trunk side-bending away from the incision, rotation; mirror posture; clearance until the chest is clear.
  • After discharge: daily walking, shoulder stretch and strength, thoracic mobility, exercise training; heavy work after ~6–8 weeks.
  • Dose: deep breaths 5–10 every hour; shoulder 10 reps 3–4 times a day. Incentive spirometer is an add-on only (AARC 2011).

Pneumonectomy (7m S23; cases W21, S26)

  • Approach (S26): posterolateral thoracotomy, 4th or 5th space; skin → latissimus dorsi → serratus anterior → lower trapezius and rhomboids → intercostals → parietal pleura; vessels and main bronchus divided, stump closed.
  • The space fills with fluid over weeks; mediastinum shifts towards it.
  • Position: supine, sitting, or a quarter turn towards the operated side. Never fully on the remaining lung (fluid could spill through a leaking stump into the only lung); no head-down.
  • One lung does all gas exchange: deep breathing, supported huff, early walking matter even more. Shoulder exercises from day 1; mirror posture (scoliosis risk).
  • Report: AF, crackles in the remaining lung (fluid overload), fever, coughing thin watery or bloody fluid (worse lying on the good side) or bubbling for days (W21) = bronchopleural fistula.

Physio with a chest drain; chest trauma

  • Check swing, bubbling, drainage, kinks before every session. Bottle upright and below chest level; carry it low when walking.
  • Never clamp a bubbling drain. Disconnection: chest end under sterile water (or brief clamp per protocol), call the nurse. Drain out: sealed dressing, call for help.
  • Support the drain site for coughing; gentle shoulder work near it.
  • Rib fractures (W17 electrician): pain control (blocks, epidural) is the key; on the ventilator positioning, suction, careful manual techniques away from fractures; then breathing, supported cough, walking. No percussion over fractures.

Precautions and stop

Drain rules above. After pneumonectomy no full side-lying on the remaining lung, no head-down, no vigorous manual techniques. Fresh stump: no aggressive bagging or high PEP. No percussion over fractures, incisions or drains. Epidural: check leg power and BP before standing. Stop and report: sudden breathlessness or chest pain, heavy fresh blood in drain or sputum, new irregular pulse or falling BP, spreading surgical emphysema.

Education and discharge

Deep breathing and huffing for weeks at home; daily walking; shoulder and trunk exercises until full; no heavy lifting at first; stop smoking; report fever, breathlessness, wound changes. Discharge: drains out, chest clear, walks and climbs stairs, shoulder near full, knows the home programme.
