Asked: an ICU question in every paper since 2023. 3 marks: respiratory complications, long-term complications, CPAP vs PEEP, three indications. 7 marks: modes (with ICU physio role), weaning criteria. 15 marks: GBS on SIMV (S23), with settings to interpret.

What it is

A machine moves gas into the lungs with positive pressure when the patient cannot breathe well enough; it buys time, it cures nothing. Invasive (ETT, tracheostomy) or non-invasive (mask CPAP or BiPAP).

Minute ventilation = VT × rate (~5–8 L/min).

Indications (3m S24)

  • Type 1 failure (PaO₂ below ~60 despite oxygen): pneumonia, ARDS, pulmonary oedema.
  • Type 2 failure (high CO₂ with acidosis): COPD when NIV fails, severe asthma, overdose.
  • Pump failure: GBS, myasthenic crisis, high SCI, flail chest, exhausted muscles.
  • Airway protection: GCS ≤ 8, head injury, stroke, poisoning, airway burns.
  • Circulation: cardiac arrest, cardiogenic shock. Planned: surgery, heavy sedation, ICP control.

Warning signs: RR above ~35, exhaustion, falling SpO₂ despite oxygen, rising CO₂ with falling pH, drowsiness, falling VC in neuromuscular disease.

Modes (7m S17, W20)

CMVSet volume or pressure at a set rate; patient cannot add breaths: deeply sedated or paralysed (W24)
Assist-controlSet breath + minimum rate; every triggered breath is a full machine breath: rests tired muscles; fast breathing → air trapping
SIMVSet machine breaths timed with effort; spontaneous breaths between (usually with PS): partial support (S20, S23, S24)
Pressure controlSet pressure and time; volume varies with stiffness: ARDS
Pressure supportPatient starts every breath; machine adds pressure: comfortable; weaning to 5–8
CPAPOne continuous pressure, no machine breaths: weaning, sleep apnoea, cardiogenic oedema, post-op collapse
BiPAPIPAP on inspiration, EPAP on expiration by mask: COPD acidosis, post-extubation, neuromuscular at night

Why the mode matters to physio: CMV or A/C at a high rate = muscles resting and wasting → positioning, passive movement, clearance. SIMV, PSV, CPAP = patient breathing → active exercise, breathing exercises, IMT; watch fatigue.

CPAPPEEP
WhatA mode: pressure through the whole breathA setting: pressure at end-expiration, added to any mode

UseSleep apnoea, cardiogenic oedema, post-op collapse, weaningAlmost every ventilated patient (~5); higher in ARDS

CPAP = PEEP for a patient who breathes alone

Effects: ↑ FRC, alveoli and small airways kept open, better oxygenation and compliance, less collapse; CPAP also ↓ work of breathing and LV load. High levels: ↓ venous return and BP, barotrauma, ↑ ICP; mask sores, air swallowing. Auto-PEEP: trapped air when expiration is incomplete (COPD, asthma): ↓ BP, harder triggering.

Complications (3m S22, W21)

  • Lungs: ventilator-induced lung injury (over-stretch, high pressure, repeated opening and closing), pneumothorax, VAP, collapse, O₂ toxicity, auto-PEEP, fighting the ventilator → head up, turns, clearance; report sudden high pressure or falling SpO₂.
  • Airway: tube blocked, displaced or pulled out, right main bronchus intubation; laryngeal and tracheal damage, later stenosis; sinusitis → humidification, suction, secure the tube on every turn.
  • Circulation: ↓ venous return and output (hypotension), fluid retention → check BP.
  • Muscles: diaphragm wasting, ICU-acquired weakness, contractures, pressure injuries, DVT → mobilisation, ROM, positioning, IMT.
  • Other: stress ulcers, delirium, poor sleep, anxiety, PTSD → orientation, communication, day–night routine.

Long-term (W21): tracheal stenosis or softening, repeated VAP, diaphragm wasting and ventilator dependence, weakness, contractures, pressure injuries, psychological effects. Ventilation over ~1–2 weeks → tracheostomy (comfort, less sedation, easier weaning).

Weaning (7m W19)

Daily readiness screen: cause improving; SpO₂ ≥ 90% on FiO₂ ≤ 0.4–0.5 and PEEP ≤ 5–8 (P/F above ~150–200); stable HR and BP, little or no BP support, no ischaemia; awake, follows commands, can cough; breathing alone, pH ≥ 7.25, few secretions, no fever, normal electrolytes.

RSBI (rate ÷ VT in L)below 105
MIPstronger than −20 to −30 cmH₂O
Vital capacityover ~10 mL/kg
Minute ventilationbelow ~10 L/min

RSBI: a tiring patient breathes fast and shallow: 35 breaths of 0.25 L = 140 (likely fail); 20 of 0.5 L = 40 (likely succeed).

Spontaneous breathing trial: 30–120 min on low pressure support (5–8) or T-piece (ACCP/ATS 2017: low PS). Stop if: RR above 35 for over 5 min; SpO₂ below 90%; HR above 140 or change over 20%; systolic above 180 or below 90; agitation, sweating, accessory muscles, see-saw breathing. Passed → extubation (cough and secretion check); NIV after extubation in high risk.

Outcome (2007): simple (first trial); difficult (up to 3 trials or 7 days); prolonged (more than 3 trials or 7 days).

Physio in weaning: keep airways clear (positioning, manual techniques, hyperinflation, suction); sit upright for trials; IMT for those who fail; early mobilisation; after extubation deep breathing, huffing, supported cough, walking.

Reading settings in a case

Note mode, FiO₂, PEEP, set and actual VT, rate, peak pressure, SpO₂, ABG, and the trend (rising FiO₂ or PEEP = worse lungs).

S23 GBS: SIMV, PEEP 6, FiO₂ 0.4Moderate support for weak muscles, lungs fine: active treatment as strength returns
S24 ARDS: SIMV, PEEP 5, FiO₂ 0.4Mild–moderate support: in- and out-of-bed activity if stable
W24 pneumonia: CMV, PEEP 10, FiO₂ 0.6High support, sedated: limit for mobilising; avoid disconnection (closed suction)

Alarms: high pressure = cough, secretions, biting, kinked tube, bronchospasm, fighting, pneumothorax; low pressure or volume = disconnection or cuff leak. If unclear or SpO₂ falls: stop and call; never silence an alarm you don't understand.
