Definition (ATS 2012)

Dyspnoea: "a subjective experience of breathing discomfort that consists of qualitatively distinct sensations that vary in intensity": measured by the patient's own rating. Sensations: air hunger, work or effort, chest tightness; intensity (sensory) and distress (emotional). Measure: modified Borg 0–10 in activity; mMRC 0–4 in daily life. Orthopnoea (lying flat: heart failure), PND (wakes breathless), platypnoea (sitting or standing, relieved lying).

How it is perceived (W25)

  • Motor drive and effort: a copy of the motor command (corollary discharge) reaches the sensory cortex; weak or overloaded muscles need a bigger command = effort.
  • Chemoreceptors: central (CO₂, acidity, medulla), peripheral (low O₂, carotid and aortic bodies) → more drive, air hunger.
  • Mechanoreceptors: airway stretch and irritant receptors, J receptors (stiff wet lungs), chest wall spindles and tendon organs report movement.
  • Mismatch (neuromechanical dissociation): high drive, lungs can't respond (hyperinflation, stiffness, weakness) → main source.
  • Emotion: limbic system adds fear; anxiety speeds breathing → more hyperinflation → more breathlessness.

Causes: ↑ demand (exercise, fever, acidosis, hypoxia), ↑ load (obstruction, stiffness, hyperinflation), weak muscles, anxiety.

↑ CapacityExercise training (desensitises, less ventilation per load); IMT if weak

PalliationLow-dose opioids in advanced disease

Positions of ease (3m S22)

High side-lying (pillows, top arm supported); forward-lean sitting (forearms on thighs or pillows on a table); relaxed sitting back with arms on armrests; forward-lean standing on a wall, sill, rail or frame; relaxed standing against a wall. Why: abdomen pushes a flat diaphragm into a dome (better contraction); fixed arms let accessory muscles pull on ribs; supported positions cut muscle work and anxiety; upright ↑ lung volume.

Energy conservation (S19, S22, W22; 3m W17, S25)

The 4 Ps: Plan, Pace, Prioritise, Position

  • Plan: spread heavy jobs over the week; best time of day (after clearance and medicines); gather things first.
  • Pace: slow and steady; rest before very breathless; "blow as you go" on the effort.
  • Prioritise: drop or share non-essential jobs.
  • Position: sit to bathe, dress, iron, cook; things between waist and shoulder; avoid bending and overhead work; trolley, long sponge, shower stool, raised toilet seat.
  • Pursed lips when walking; stairs one step per out-breath, rest on landings; avoid smoke, dust, heat, cold; loose clothes; fan.

One-sided disease (W22, left bronchiectasis): lie on the right (healthy lung down: more air and blood; diseased lung up: drains); desk at elbow height; huffing breaks; avoid dust.

Activity pacing (3m S20, W21)

Activity in planned manageable amounts with rests, staying below the breathlessness that forces a stop: no "boom and bust". Break tasks into steps (bed in stages); alternate heavy and light; stop, rest, recover, restart at moderate breathlessness (Borg 3–4); stairs one step per out-breath; interval walking at a slower pace; sit to wash and dress, rest between, dressing gown instead of towelling.
