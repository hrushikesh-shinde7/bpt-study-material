Asked: 7 marks: principle of jet and ultrasonic nebulisers (S20); indications, contraindications and types of nebulisation (S24). 3 marks: humidification and uses (S17), why intubated patients need it (W17), three nebuliser conditions (S19), definitions (S21), MDI (W25).

Definitions (3m S21)

Humidification: adding water vapour (a gas) to inspired gas, to warm and moisten it. Nebulisation: turning a liquid (drug) into an aerosol of fine droplets to breathe in. Aerosol: tiny liquid or solid particles in a gas.

Humidification (3m S17, W17)

Normally the nose warms and humidifies air so that a few cm below the carina it is 37 °C, 100% RH, ~44 mg H₂O/L (isothermic saturation boundary).

Intubated (W17): the tube bypasses the nose → cold dry gas → thick sticky secretions, slow cilia, damaged lining, blocked tube, collapse, heat and water loss. AARC 2012: humidify every invasively ventilated patient.

HME ("artificial nose")Passive: traps heat and moisture from expiration; ≥ 30 mg/L; not with thick, copious or bloody secretions, big leak, hypothermia, nebulisation, or low VT (dead space)
Heated humidifierActive, heated water chamber, heated-wire circuit; 33–44 mg/L at 34–41 °C: long ventilation, thick secretions, high-flow cannula, NIV
Bubble humidifierOxygen through room-temperature water: little real humidity
Saline nebulisationLoosens sputum; tracheostomy care

Uses: intubated and tracheostomy patients, long high-flow oxygen, thick secretions, NIV, dry airway. Hazards: overheating and airway burns, water in tubing (washed in, infection, false triggers), contaminated water, HME resistance, dead space, blockage.

Nebulisers (7m S20, S24)

Droplet size: over 10 µm mouth and throat; 5–10 µm large airways; 1–5 µm small airways and alveoli (respirable); under 1 µm breathed out.

JetGas 6–8 L/min through a narrow opening: low pressure (Bernoulli, Venturi) draws liquid up and shatters it; baffle removes big drops. Cheap, any drug, suspensions; noisy, slow (10–15 min), residual drug
UltrasonicPiezoelectric crystal vibrates; surface waves throw off droplets. Quiet, fast; warms some drugs, poor for suspensions (budesonide), costly
Vibrating meshPlate pushes liquid through a mesh of tiny holes. Efficient, little waste, quiet, portable, in-line on ventilators; expensive, careful cleaning

Indications (S24; S19): acute asthma or COPD (bronchodilators); CF and bronchiectasis (hypertonic saline or dornase before clearance; inhaled antibiotics); ventilated patients (in-line); croup; sputum induction; patients who cannot use inhalers.

Contraindications and care: drug allergy; hypertonic saline can cause bronchospasm (bronchodilator first, check peak flow); COPD with CO₂ risk: drive with air, not oxygen, oxygen by cannula (BTS); tachycardia, tremor, low K⁺; aerosol infection risk: clean, dry, don't share.

Technique: sit up; 2–5 mL; mouthpiece (mask for children, very breathless); normal mouth breathing with occasional deep breaths; until it splutters; rinse mouth after steroids; wash and dry.

Inhalers (3m W25)

MDI: pressurised canister + propellant; metering valve gives a fixed dose (e.g. salbutamol 100 µg). Cap off, shake; breathe out away; seal lips; start a slow deep breath (3–5 s) and press once at the start; hold up to 10 s; wait 30–60 s before a second puff; rinse after steroids. For: small, quick, cheap. Against: needs coordination, throat deposition, cold spray.

Spacer: puff into the chamber, breathe over a few breaths: less coordination, less throat drug; mask for young children; in acute asthma MDI + spacer = nebuliser (Cochrane 2013).

DPI (Rotahaler, Accuhaler, Turbuhaler): released by a fast deep breath; no propellant or coordination; not for young children or severe attacks; don't breathe into it.
