Asked: 3 marks: thoracic mobility exercises (W17), diaphragmatic breathing (W18), pursed-lip breathing effects and rationale (S20, W21, W22). 7 marks: diaphragm recruitment and strengthening (S21), breathing retraining (S23).

Types and aims

Breathing control: relaxed tidal breathing, calms panic. Diaphragmatic: more diaphragm, less accessory work, basal ventilation. Pursed-lip: slower breathing, less air trapping (COPD). Thoracic expansion: re-expand an area, loosen secretions. SMI and incentive spirometry: post-op expansion. Thoracic mobility: free a stiff chest and spine. Breathing retraining: correct dysfunctional breathing (asthma, anxiety).

Diaphragmatic breathing (3m W18; 7m S21)

Technique: half-lying, head and knees supported (diaphragm domed, good length); later sitting, standing, walking. Shoulders relaxed; hand on the upper abdomen below the ribs; breathe in slowly through the nose, hand rises, upper chest quiet; out gently, not forced; 5–10 breaths several times a day; watch dizziness.

Recruit and strengthen: quick stretch in and up on the upper abdomen at end-expiration, then light resistance on inspiration; a sniff recruits strongly; progress supine → sitting → standing (binder in tetraplegia); manual resistance, classic small weights on the abdomen, best evidence threshold IMT ≥ 30% MIP; use in walking, stairs, speech.

Indications: post-op, neuromuscular disease and SCI (diaphragm may be the main muscle left), weaning, upper-chest breathing. Severe COPD: with a flat diaphragm, forced abdominal breathing can raise work and breathlessness (Gosselink 1995): prefer pursed lips, positions of ease; stop if worse.

Pursed-lip breathing (3m S20, W21, W22)

In through the nose; out slowly through loosely pursed lips ("hot tea"), out ~twice as long, not forced. Back-pressure splints small airways → less air trapping → slower deeper breathing, better VT and SpO₂ → less breathlessness and anxiety on walking and stairs. See COPD.

Thoracic expansion; lung expansion

Localised expansion: hand over the area (lateral costal, posterior basal, apical; uni- or bilateral); gentle pressure at end-expiration, "breathe into my hand", 3-s hold, relaxed out; 3–4 breaths then breathing control. Post-op, effusion, empyema, collapse, stiff chest, scoliosis (concave side).

SMI: slow deep breath to the top, hold 3–5 s, relaxed out (collateral channels). Incentive spirometer: visual feedback; volume-oriented better than flow-oriented (balls tempt fast breaths); 5–10 breaths hourly + walking. Not if cannot be taught, will not co-operate, or VC below ~10 mL/kg (then IPPB or CPAP). AARC 2011: not alone or routine; deep breathing, cough and walking are the core.

Thoracic mobility (3m W17)

  • Trunk rotation with breathing out, back to centre breathing in, 10 each way.
  • Side-bending with arm overhead while breathing in (thoracotomy, pleural disease, scoliosis).
  • Bilateral arm elevation up on inspiration, down on expiration.
  • Pectoral stretch in a doorway with deep breaths, hold 20–30 s.
  • Thoracic extension over a rolled towel or the chair back.

Ankylosing spondylitis, kyphosis, scoliosis, after chest surgery and chest burns, COPD posture.

Breathing retraining (7m S23)

For dysfunctional breathing: fast, upper-chest, mouth breathing, sighing, over-breathing; symptoms (breathlessness, tightness, dizziness, tingling) out of proportion; screen with the Nijmegen questionnaire; common in asthma and anxiety.

  • Education: over-breathing lowers CO₂ and causes symptoms; reassure.
  • Nose breathing at rest and light activity; lower-chest breathing, relaxed shoulders.
  • Slower, smaller, gentler breaths with a pause after expiration.
  • Relaxation; control sighs and yawns; speech phrasing.
  • Practice 10–15 min once or twice a day, then in daily activity (Papworth; Buteyko adds reduced breathing).

Evidence: better asthma QoL and symptoms (Cochrane 2020); UK trial 2018 with a DVD programme; does not replace inhalers.
