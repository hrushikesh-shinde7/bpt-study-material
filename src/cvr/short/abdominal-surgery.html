Definition and incisions (3m S16, W16, S26)

Operations on stomach, bowel, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, spleen, kidney, abdominal wall: open (laparotomy) or laparoscopic. Post-operative pulmonary complications (PPCs): atelectasis, pneumonia and other lung problems that delay recovery.

Upper abdominal incisions (upper midline, subcostal, rooftop) are near the diaphragm: more pain on breathing and more lung complications.

Risk and pathophysiology

Risk of PPCs: upper abdominal or thoracic site, open surgery, long or emergency operation, nasogastric tube; older age, smoking, COPD or asthma, obesity, poor fitness, poor nutrition, heart failure, sleep apnoea.

Anaesthesia relaxes the diaphragm → ↓ FRC, basal airway closure → wound pain stops deep breaths and coughs; diaphragm weak for days → opioids and lying flat → retained secretions → atelectasis, low O₂, then pneumonia. Bed rest → DVT, weakness; cut abdominal muscles weaken trunk support.

Features: wound pain, shallow fast breathing, weak cough, ↓ basal sounds, low SpO₂; drains, catheter, NG tube; ileus; fatigue. Tests: pre-op lung function, fitness, Hb, albumin; post-op X-ray (basal collapse, raised diaphragm, effusion), SpO₂ or ABG, blood counts.

Medical: epidural or wound catheters; ERAS (pre-op education, short fasting, few drains, early eating, mobilise day 0–1, early tube removal); DVT prophylaxis; nutrition. Gastrectomy: upper midline or rooftop, high PPC risk, small frequent meals.

Complications (7m S22)

  • Atelectasis, sputum retention, pneumonia: pre-op teaching, deep breathing, ACBT, supported cough, upright, early walking.
  • DVT, PE: early walking, ankle pumps, leg exercises, stockings.
  • Wound infection, dehiscence, incisional hernia: support the wound on coughing, log-roll, no heavy lifting ~6 weeks.
  • Ileus, urinary retention: early walking. Pain, weakness: graded exercise. Pressure sores: turning, sitting out.

Pre-operative physiotherapy (7m W16, W20)

  • Assess to find high-risk patients; record a baseline.
  • Educate: what happens after surgery, why breathing and early walking matter, pain relief.
  • Teach and practise: deep breathing with a 3–5 s hold, ACBT, supported cough (hands or pillow on the wound), log-roll, ankle pumps, getting out of bed.
  • Prehabilitation (high risk): aerobic, strength and IMT for a few weeks. Stop smoking early.
  • Evidence: LIPPSMAck-POP (441 upper abdominal patients): one 30-min pre-op session roughly halved PPCs (HR 0.48).

Rationale of goals (3m W21): breathing and cough learned while pain-free and alert are done correctly after surgery; log-roll and early walking taught early → less fear, sooner walking, fewer DVTs; baseline finds high-risk patients; education lowers anxiety and improves co-operation; prehab → better tolerance and faster recovery.

Post-operative physiotherapy

  • Day 0–1: sit upright or high side-lying; deep breaths with a hold, 5–10 every hour; ACBT if sputum; supported huff and cough; ankle pumps, quadriceps and gluteal sets; log-roll, sit over the edge, sit out, walk with help.
  • Day 2 to discharge: walk 3–4 times a day, stairs if needed; breathing exercises until fully mobile; gentle trunk mobility, posture.
  • After discharge: daily walking; gentle core activation, progressed after ~6 weeks; no heavy lifting ~6 weeks.
  • Incentive spirometry only as an adjunct (AARC 2011).

Principles (7m W25): control pain first and support the wound; restore lung volume (upright, deep breaths, walking); clear secretions; mobilise early (ERAS); prevent DVT; protect the wound (log-roll, no lifting or sit-ups ~6 weeks); restore posture (patients bend forward) and core; watch for fever, low SpO₂, wound leak, distension; educate and plan discharge.

Exploratory laparotomy (7m W21, S23)

Long midline incision, often emergency (perforation, peritonitis, obstruction, trauma, bleeding): sepsis, ICU, NG tube, drains, catheter, stoma, distended abdomen pushing the diaphragm up: high PPC risk. ICU: positioning, clearance, suction, early mobilisation. Ward: upright, deep breaths hourly, ACBT and huff with a pillow on the wound, ankle pumps, log-roll, sit out and walk from day 1 if stable. Dehiscence and hernia risk: support the wound, no straining or lifting, binder if the surgeon uses one; support a stoma when coughing. Later: graded walking, posture, gradual core work.

Day 2 after cholecystectomy (7m S24)

Laparoscopic: 4 ports, low PPC risk, home in 1–2 days; gas irritates the diaphragm → shoulder-tip pain (phrenic, C3–5), settles in days, walking helps. Open (Kocher's): painful breathing, low right basal volume, high PPC risk.

  1. Assess: pain, SpO₂, temperature, right basal sounds, sputum, drains, mobility.
  2. Time it with analgesia.
  3. Position: high sitting, or left side-lying (right side up): more basal volume.
  4. Breathing: deep breaths with 3-s hold, expansion to the right lower chest, 5–10 hourly: re-opens alveoli.
  5. Clearance: huff and cough with a pillow on the wound.
  6. Mobilise: log-roll, walk 3–4 times a day: best for lung volume, DVT, ileus.
  7. Ankle pumps 10 an hour; posture, trunk and right shoulder movement; home advice, no heavy lifting.

Precautions, assessment, education

Precautions: always support the wound; check drains and catheter, bags below the wound; epidural: leg power and BP before standing; watch postural drop, bleeding, sudden breathlessness (PE); no percussion near wounds and drains; no heavy lifting or strong abdominal work ~6 weeks.

Assessment: pre-op type and site, risk factors, breathing, cough, mobility; post-op chart, pain on coughing, rate, SpO₂, expansion, auscultation, sputum, bed mobility, walking. Outcomes: chest complications, SpO₂ on air, walking distance, time to independence, length of stay.

Home: walk daily; pillow on the wound for coughing and laughing; roll onto the side to get up; no buckets or gas cylinders ~6 weeks; stop smoking; report fever, wound discharge, calf pain, breathlessness.
