Asked: in 18 of 20 papers, mostly short answers. 3 marks (almost every paper): ABI and its grades, three special tests for arterial insufficiency, claudication, evaluation parameters. 7 marks: Buerger's disease, claudication, PAD assessment and physiotherapy. Cases: 25-year-old smoker with claudication (Buerger's); diabetic clerk with claudication (W22).

Definitions and types

  • PAD: narrowing or block of the limb arteries (usually legs) from atherosclerosis.
  • Intermittent claudication: cramping muscle pain (usually calf) after a fairly fixed walking distance, gone within minutes of rest.
  • Buerger's disease (thromboangiitis obliterans): inflammatory, non-atherosclerotic disease of small and medium arteries and veins of hands and feet in young smokers.
  • Presentations: asymptomatic; claudication; chronic limb-threatening ischaemia (rest pain, ulcer, gangrene); acute limb ischaemia (embolus or thrombosis: emergency).
  • Others: Raynaud's (white → blue → red with cold or stress); aneurysm (aorta, popliteal).

Method: supine, rest 5–10 min; Doppler systolic pressure in both arms (take the higher); at dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial of each ankle (higher of the two); ABI = ankle ÷ higher arm, each leg.

Fontaine: I no symptoms; IIa claudication over ~200 m; IIb under ~200 m; III rest pain; IV ulcer or gangrene (III–IV = CLTI).

Special tests for arterial insufficiency (3m W16, S18, W20)

  • Pulses: femoral, popliteal, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial; grade 0 absent, 1+ weak, 2+ normal, 3+ full, 4+ bounding.
  • Buerger's test: raise the leg; angle where the foot blanches (below ~20° = severe); then hang it down: slow dusky red (dependent rubor).
  • Capillary refill: over ~2 s is delayed. Venous filling time: over ~20 s after elevation.
  • Treadmill test: pain-free and maximum walking distance. Allen's test for the hand (Buerger's).
  • Duplex Doppler (site of block), CT or magnetic resonance angiography before surgery; sugar, HbA1c, lipids, kidney function.

Causes and Buerger's criteria

PAD: smoking (strongest), diabetes (below-knee disease, neuropathy), age over 50–65, hypertension, lipids, kidney disease.

Buerger's: smoker, onset before 45–50, below-knee artery blocks, arm involvement or migrating superficial phlebitis, no other atherosclerotic risk factor (Shionoya; Olin). The 25-year-old smoker with claudication and cold sweaty feet (S16, W17) is Buerger's: say so in line one.

Why walking hurts and exercise helps

Plaque narrows the artery → flow enough at rest → walking raises calf O₂ demand, supply cannot rise → ischaemia, metabolites → pain; rest lowers demand → pain goes → he walks less → weaker, less fit → distance shrinks → severe narrowing: rest pain, ulcers, gangrene.

Exercise helps: better muscle O₂ use, collaterals open, better artery lining, more efficient walking. The block stays, but he walks much further.

Rest pain is worse at night (no help from gravity) and eased by hanging the leg down. Site: aorto-iliac → buttock, thigh; femoro-popliteal (commonest) → calf; tibial → foot.

Clinical features

  • Calf, thigh or buttock pain on walking, relieved by standing still; rest pain in toes at night; cold numb feet.
  • Cool, pale, thin, shiny, hairless skin; thick nails; wasted calf; weak or absent pulses; pallor on elevation, rubor on dependency; slow refill; ulcers on toes, heel, pressure points; gangrene.
  • Buerger's: young male smoker, feet and hands, instep claudication, Raynaud's-like changes, migrating phlebitis, painful fingertip and toe ulcers.
  • Acute ischaemia: 6 P's: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paraesthesia, paralysis, perishing cold: call the doctor at once.

Medical and surgical management

Stop all smoking (in Buerger's the only proven treatment). Antiplatelet, statin, BP and sugar control (heart attack and stroke risk). Cilostazol for claudication. Supervised exercise is first-line. Revascularisation (angioplasty ± stent, femoro-popliteal bypass) if still limiting or limb-threatening. Wound care; amputation if unsalvageable. Acute ischaemia: anticoagulation, embolectomy.

Complications and prevention (W22)

  • Arterial ulcer → daily foot inspection, footwear, never barefoot, nail care.
  • Infection, gangrene, amputation → treat wounds early, sugar control, stop smoking, refer early.
  • Heart attack and stroke (same atherosclerosis) → risk-factor control, exercise.
  • Deconditioning, low mood → graded walking. Burns and pressure sores (numb ischaemic skin) → no hot-water bottles, padded heels. Neuropathic ulcer in diabetes → monofilament, offloading.

PT assessment (3m S21: evaluation parameters)

History: claudication site, onset distance or time, stopping time, recovery; rest pain; smoking (pack-years, bidi); diabetes, BP, heart; job, walking demands, stairs. Inspection: colour, hair, nails, wasting, ulcers (site, size, base, edge). Palpation: temperature both sides, graded pulses. Tests: ABI, Buerger's, capillary refill, venous filling. Sensation: 10 g monofilament if diabetic. Ankle ROM, calf strength. Walking: treadmill or 6MWT (pain-free and maximum distance), claudication scale. Questionnaires: Walking Impairment Questionnaire, VascuQoL.

ICF (W22: diabetic head clerk, chain smoker, pain at 5 min, stops at 10)

  • Body function and structure: calf claudication at 5 min; ↓ walking endurance; calf wasting and weakness; dry thin skin; possible ↓ sensation; ↓ fitness. Reason: narrowed artery cannot meet demand; disuse; starved skin.
  • Activity: walking over ~10 min, stairs, shopping, walking to the bus. Participation: commuting, outings, errands, leisure.
  • Environment: easy cigarettes, fatty diet (barriers); desk job, family, hospital programme (facilitators). Personal: 50, smoker, diabetic.

Goals: short-term (4 weeks): follows the walk–rest programme 3 times a week, daily foot checks, sets a quit date. Long-term (12 weeks+): clearly longer pain-free and maximum walking time, tobacco-free, sugar and BP controlled, commutes without stopping.

Supervised exercise therapy (the core)

30 / 3 / 12

Walk 30–45 min a session, ≥ 3 times a week, for ≥ 12 weeks (ACC/AHA 2024, Class 1A).

  • Warm-up 5–10 min with calf stretches.
  • Treadmill or level ground: walk until pain is moderate (grade 2–3 of 4), rest until it has gone, repeat.
  • Progress speed or slope when he walks 8–10 min before moderate pain. Cool-down 5–10 min.
  • Pain scale: 1 starts, 2 moderate, 3 intense, 4 unbearable.
  • Cannot walk (ulcer, arthritis): arm cranking or cycling. No supervision: home walking with a log book, 30 min, 3–5 times a week.
  • Add heel raises and sit-to-stand (2–3 × 10–15, 2–3 times a week), calf stretches 30 s × 3, active ankle and toe ROM.

Buerger's disease (7m S17, S22) and rest pain

  • Absolutely no tobacco (bidi, chewing, passive). Walking within tolerance; foot and calf exercises.
  • Buerger–Allen (classic): leg up 45–90° until it blanches (1–2 min) → hang down until red (2–5 min) with ankle movements → flat 5 min; repeat several cycles a few times a day. Weak evidence; supervised walking is the main treatment.
  • Keep hands and feet warm with socks and gloves, never direct heat; meticulous foot and hand care.
  • Rest pain: bed head slightly raised or leg slightly down; never elevate an ischaemic leg; pad heels; gentle ankle and toe movement. After bypass or angioplasty: early walking, protect the groin, then the walking programme.

Precautions and stop

No heat on an ischaemic limb (it burns). No compression if ABI is low. Don't elevate in rest pain. Check HR and BP (heart disease is common). Diabetics: sugar and feet before and after. No walking on an open ulcer without offloading. Stop and refer: sudden 6 P's, chest pain or breathlessness, new rest pain, new ulcer, black toe.

Education

Foot care: look daily (mirror), lukewarm water tested with the elbow, dry between toes, cut nails straight, soft footwear, never barefoot (even in temples), no heat near feet, report changes. Lifestyle: stop all tobacco, walk daily with walk–rest, less fat and salt, take medicines, avoid cold.

Prognosis: few lose the leg, but heart attack and stroke risk is high; smoking predicts worsening and amputation in Buerger's.

  4. Management (7–8): ABI and Buerger's test; walking dose; strengthening; foot care; stop smoking; no heat; outcome measures.
