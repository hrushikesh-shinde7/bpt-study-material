Asked: 15-mark case: empyema with a drain for 15 days (S25: ICF 3 + goals 4 + physio 8). 7 marks: pneumothorax with precautions and physio with a drain (W22); drain and underwater seal (W20). 3 marks: X-ray of effusion (S16), drain indications (S25).

Definitions and types

Effusion: fluid in the pleural space. Pneumothorax: air, lung collapses. Hydropneumothorax: both. Empyema: pus. Haemothorax: blood. Pleurisy: inflamed pleura, sharp pain on breathing.

Light's criteria (exudate if any): fluid/serum protein over 0.5; fluid/serum LDH over 0.6; fluid LDH over ⅔ of upper normal serum LDH. Transudate (pressure imbalance): heart failure, cirrhosis, kidney disease, low albumin. Exudate (diseased pleura): pneumonia, TB, cancer, PE.

Pneumothorax: primary spontaneous (tall thin young men, smokers); secondary (COPD bullae, TB, CF, ILD: more dangerous); traumatic, iatrogenic; tension: one-way valve, mediastinum pushed across, venous return stops: emergency.

Pathophysiology and signs

Air or fluid stops the lung expanding → ↓ volume, breathlessness, low O₂. Large collections push the mediastinum away; collapse pulls it towards. Inflamed pleura hurts → shallow breathing. Untreated empyema or haemothorax → thick peel (fibrothorax) → trapped lung, stiff chest wall, scoliosis.

Pleurisy: pain on breathing and coughing, pleural rub. Empyema: swinging fever, sweats. Tension: severe distress, low BP, fast HR, distended neck veins.

X-ray (3m S16) and management

Effusion: blunted costophrenic angle (≥ ~200 mL on PA); homogeneous basal opacity with a curved upper border rising to the axilla (meniscus sign); lost diaphragm outline; large: mediastinum pushed away, white-out. Pneumothorax: thin pleural line, no markings beyond; tension: shift away, flat diaphragm. Hydropneumothorax: straight air–fluid level. Ultrasound, CT, pleural tap.

Effusion: treat cause; aspiration or drain; pleurodesis or indwelling catheter if malignant. Pneumothorax (BTS 2023): guided by symptoms, not just size: observe, aspirate, ambulatory device or drain; VATS pleurodesis for recurrence. Tension: needle decompression then drain. Empyema: antibiotics, drain, decortication if it will not drain. Complications: trapped lung, recurrence, persistent air leak, re-expansion oedema, drain infection.

ICF and goals (S25: 45, driver, tobacco chewer, empyema, drain 15 days)

Impairments: drain-site pain, ↓ expansion (trapped lung risk), sputum with weak painful cough, ↓ tolerance, stiff shoulder and trunk on the drain side, leaning towards it. Activity: bathing, dressing, toileting, walking, stairs, lifting. Participation: driving work, income. Environment: team, family; drain tubing, tobacco. Personal: 45, tobacco chewer.

Short-term (2 weeks): drain pain ≤ 3/10 on breathing and coughing, clears sputum with ACBT, better expansion, independent self-care and ward walking, full shoulder movement. Long-term (6–12 weeks): full expansion and posture without pleural thickening, fit to sit and drive long hours, tobacco-free.

PT management

  • Check the drain (swing, bubbling, fluid), temperature, SpO₂; treat after analgesia.
  • Lie on the unaffected side (affected lung up to expand; healthy lung down for ventilation and perfusion); high sitting; change position.
  • Localised thoracic expansion to the affected side, hands on the lower ribs, 3-s hold, 5–10 every hour.
  • ACBT and huffing; supported cough over the drain site.
  • Walk with the bottle below the chest, daily progress, stairs; shoulder flexion and abduction; side-bending away from the drain; mirror posture.
  • After drain removal: thoracic mobility, strength, aerobic training; return to driving when trunk and shoulder move painlessly; stop tobacco; pre-op physio if decortication.
  • Pleurisy: pain relief; lying on the painful side may splint it; hand pressure for deep breaths and cough; gentle expansion; walk.

Pneumothorax precautions (7m W22)

Undrained pneumothorax: no positive pressure (IPPB, NIV, PEP, manual hyperinflation): can cause tension. Drain rules: swing and bubbling checks, bottle below chest, never clamp a bubbling drain. No vigorous percussion over drains or wounds; no straining or breath-holding until healed; no flying until cleared; no scuba unless surgery done. Stop and call: sudden worse breathlessness, falling BP, tracheal shift; drain out or disconnected; heavy blood. Educate: stop smoking (recurrence), finish TB treatment, gradual return to work, know warning signs.
