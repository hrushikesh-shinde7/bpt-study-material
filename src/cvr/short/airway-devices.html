PEP principle

Breathing out against a resistance, so airway pressure stays above atmospheric through expiration; PEP mask target 10–20 cmH₂O.

  • Splints airways open: back-pressure stops early collapse of floppy small airways (like pursed lips).
  • Air behind mucus: slight volume rise, collateral channels; on expiration that air pushes mucus centrally.
  • Oscillating PEP adds vibration: pressure flutters many times a second, shearing mucus off and making it less sticky.

Then clear with huffs and a cough.

Types (7m S21)

PEP mask (low pressure)One-way valve, fixed resistor, manometer; set by the physio; sitting or drainage positions; nebuliser can be added
FlutterPipe with a steel ball in a cone; breath lifts the ball, it drops back repeatedly; must be held at the right angle (sitting only); small, cheap
AcapellaCounterweighted lever and magnet; dial sets resistance and frequency; any position; suits low flows
OthersRC-Cornet, Aerobika: position-independent
Bottle (bubble) PEPTube into water: depth in cm ≈ cmH₂O (10 cm ≈ 10); bubbles add oscillation; very cheap, child-friendly; change water daily
High-pressure PEPForced expiration through the mask at high pressure: specialist CF use

Technique (PEP mask)

  1. Sit, elbows on a table (or drainage position); mask firmly over nose and mouth.
  2. Breathe in slightly deeper than normal; out actively, not forcefully, manometer 10–20 cmH₂O.
  3. 8–10 breaths, remove the mask, 2–3 huffs and a cough, rest with breathing control.
  4. Repeat ~15–20 min, once or twice a day (more in infection).

Flutter: tilt until vibration is felt most in the chest. Clean after each use; never share (CF cross-infection).

Uses (3m S26): independent, portable clearance (bronchiectasis, CF, COPD); unstable collapsible airways (COPD, tracheomalacia); post-op lung expansion; better aerosol delivery with a nebuliser; play-based practice in children (bubble PEP).

Contraindications: untreated pneumothorax (main); active haemoptysis; raised ICP; haemodynamic instability; recent face, mouth, skull or oesophageal surgery; sinusitis, nosebleed, ear problems or perforated eardrum; exhaustion; bullae (low pressure, care).

Other assistive devices (7m W21)

Incentive spirometerVisual feedback for slow deep breaths with hold: post-op add-on
IPPB (Bird)Positive pressure boosts each patient breath: weak or tired; not with undrained pneumothorax
Oscillation vestRapid chest-wall squeezes: CF, neuromuscular; hands-free, expensive
IPVRapid small bursts with saline: thick secretions
Cough assist (MI-E)Positive breath in, quick switch to negative, ~±40 cmH₂O: weak cough (neuromuscular, SCI)
Manually assisted coughPush in and up on abdomen or lower ribs with the cough: SCI (not over a fresh wound)
SuctionFor those who cannot cough secretions out
