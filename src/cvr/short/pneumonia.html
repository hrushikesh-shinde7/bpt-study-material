Pneumonia

Infection of lung tissue: alveoli fill with fluid and cells (consolidation). Community- or hospital-acquired (≥ 48 h after admission), ventilator-associated, aspiration; lobar, broncho-, interstitial; pneumococcus commonest.

Features: fever, cough, sputum (rusty in pneumococcal), pleuritic pain, fast breathing, confusion in the elderly; dull percussion, bronchial breathing, crackles, ↑ vocal resonance. X-ray: white with air bronchograms, no volume loss.

CURB-65 (1 point each): new Confusion; Urea over 7 mmol/L; RR ≥ 30; BP systolic below 90 or diastolic ≤ 60; age ≥ 65. 0–1 home, 2 hospital, 3–5 consider ICU.

Antibiotics, oxygen (94–98%, or 88–92% if CO₂ risk), fluids. Early consolidation is solid exudate: percussion and drainage don't clear it.

Current (BTS 2009, Cochrane 2022): airway clearance not routine in uncomplicated pneumonia; sit out ≥ 20 min within 24 h, then walk. Write: "early mobilisation and positioning for all; ACBT only for retained sputum or underlying lung disease".

  • Low O₂: in one-sided pneumonia, good lung down.
  • Resolving sputum: ACBT 1–2 times a day, affected lung up, humidification. Pleuritic pain: supported breathing and cough, analgesia first.

Ventilated pneumonia (W24: 35, both lungs, CMV, FiO₂ 0.6, PEEP 10, 3 days)

Read the settings: FiO₂ 0.6 and PEEP 10 are high: stiff, poorly oxygenated lungs, near ARDS; losing PEEP (disconnection) collapses alveoli and drops SpO₂.

ICF: poor oxygenation, stiff low-volume lungs, collapse and VAP risk, ICU-acquired weakness, joint stiffness, pressure and DVT risk, sedation → fully dependent → off work; young and fit, ICU team and family (facilitators); tubes and sedation (barriers).

  • Safety check each session: SpO₂, FiO₂ and PEEP trend, HR, BP, sedation, doctor's plan. At FiO₂ 0.6, PEEP 10: upper limit, so in-bed treatment first.
  • Head up 30–45° (aspiration, VAP); regular side-lying; help with prone if ordered.
  • Little sputum: suction only when needed with a closed in-line system (keeps PEEP); no bagging off the ventilator at this PEEP (ventilator hyperinflation instead).
  • Passive → active-assisted limb movement; ankle positioning against foot drop; pressure care.
  • As FiO₂ falls and sedation lightens: active exercise, edge of bed, bed cycling, standing and walking on the ventilator. After extubation: deep breathing, ACBT, walking, strengthening, IMT.

Precautions: sepsis (low BP, confusion), worsening hypoxia, fatigue; infection control.

Lung abscess

Localised necrosis forming a pus-filled cavity. Usually aspiration (alcohol, seizures, drowsiness, poor teeth, swallowing problems): posterior segment of right upper lobe, superior segment of right lower lobe; also Staph or Klebsiella pneumonia, blocked bronchus. Swinging fever, copious foul sputum, haemoptysis, weight loss, clubbing. X-ray: thick-walled cavity with an air–fluid level. Weeks of antibiotics; drainage or surgery if unresolved.

Physio: once it drains into a bronchus: gravity-assisted drainage of that segment with ACBT, 2–3 times a day; strong huffs and coughs so pus is cleared, not spilled into other lobes; breathing exercises, walking, nutrition, mouth hygiene. Stop with haemoptysis; suction ready in weak or drowsy patients.

Atelectasis (collapse)

Incomplete expansion of part or all of a lung. Types: obstructive or resorption (mucus plug, tumour, foreign body), compressive (effusion, pneumothorax), passive (shallow post-op breathing), adhesive (surfactant loss), cicatrisation (scarring). Small: silent; large: breathless, low SpO₂, ↓ sounds. X-ray: opacity with volume loss: trachea pulled towards, raised hemidiaphragm, shifted fissures, crowded ribs.

Physio: deep breaths with 3-s hold (sustained maximal inspiration), thoracic expansion to the area, 5–10 every hour; affected area uppermost; humidification, ACBT, suction for a plug; early mobilisation (the best deep-breathing exercise); CPAP, manual hyperinflation or IPPB as prescribed in weak or ventilated patients; incentive spirometer as add-on. No positive pressure with an undrained pneumothorax; compressive collapse needs drainage first.
