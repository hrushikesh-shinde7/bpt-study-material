Definitions and types

ILD: inflammation and scarring of the interstitium between the air sacs: stiff small lungs, poor O₂ transfer. Occupational lung disease: from dusts, fibres, fumes or organic material at work.

IdiopathicIPF: commonest fibrosing ILD in older adults

Pathophysiology and features

Repeated injury → inflammation and scar → stiff lungs, ↓ volumes and compliance (restriction) → rapid shallow breathing. Thick alveolar walls → ↓ DLCO; in exercise blood moves faster → marked exertional desaturation. Hypoxia → pulmonary hypertension, right heart failure. Breathlessness → inactivity, weakness; troublesome dry cough.

Features: progressive exertional breathlessness, dry cough, fatigue, weight loss; fine "Velcro" end-inspiratory basal crackles; clubbing (IPF); rapid shallow breathing; ↓ expansion; SpO₂ falls sharply on walking; signs of the cause.

Investigations (3m S19)

Restriction: FVC ↓ (FEV1 ↓ in proportion); FEV1/FVC normal or high (≥ 0.70, often over 0.80); TLC ↓, with low DLCO in ILD. (Restriction lowers the plateau; obstruction slows the rise.)

HRCT: reticular lines, honeycombing, ground glass, traction bronchiectasis. X-ray: small lungs, reticular-nodular shadows; silicosis: upper-zone nodules, eggshell calcified nodes. 6MWT with lowest SpO₂; ABG; echo; autoimmune tests.

Medical: remove exposure (job change, masks, dust control); antifibrotics (nintedanib, pirfenidone); steroids for inflammatory types; long-term or ambulatory oxygen; pulmonary rehab (ATS 2023, strong); vaccines, quitting, transplant, palliative care. Complications: respiratory failure, cor pulmonale, acute exacerbation (often fatal in IPF), pneumothorax, lung cancer, TB with silicosis.

Assessment and ICF (S18: 40, cotton mill 15 years, grade II dyspnoea)

Assess: work history (dust, years, masks), smoking, mMRC, cough; pattern, expansion, crackles, clubbing; SpO₂ at rest and during 6MWT, HR, Borg; strength; ADL and work. Outcomes: 6MWT distance and lowest SpO₂, mMRC, SGRQ-I, King's Brief ILD.

  • Impairments: grade II breathlessness, ↓ volumes, exertional desaturation, dry cough, ↓ tolerance, weakness.
  • Activity: walking fast, stairs, carrying, housework. Participation: mill work, family roles.
  • Environment: continued cotton dust, need for income (barriers); hospital, possible transfer or masks (facilitators). Personal: 40, earning member.

Goals (3m S17): short-term: safe exercise with SpO₂ ~88% or more (oxygen if needed), breathing control and pacing in ADL, cough control. Long-term: better 6MWT and QoL, home programme, energy conservation, no further dust, suitable work.

Pulmonary rehab for ILD (7m S21)

  • Aerobic: walking or cycling 3–5 days a week, 20–30 min, Borg 3–4; interval training if SpO₂ drops quickly.
  • Oxygen during exercise to keep SpO₂ ~88–90% or more.
  • Resistance 2–3 days a week; breathing control and forward-lean recovery; pacing; energy conservation.
  • Dry cough control: sip water, swallow, nose breathing, suppression.
  • Thoracic mobility, posture; IMT may help. Education: oxygen safety (no flames or smoking), when to seek help, workplace protection.

Differs from COPD: airway clearance rarely needed; the limit is O₂ transfer, so monitor SpO₂ continuously, use oxygen and intervals, progress gently (pulmonary hypertension).

Precautions and education

Stop or reduce: SpO₂ below ~85% despite oxygen, chest pain, dizziness, severe breathlessness. Pulmonary hypertension: no sudden heavy effort, breath-holding or heavy lifting; watch for fainting. Steroids: fragile bones. Educate: avoid the causative dust (transfer, fitted respirator), stop smoking, daily activity within safe SpO₂, vaccines, report fever or sudden worse breathlessness; TB screening in silicosis. IPF progresses over years; some types improve if exposure stops early.
