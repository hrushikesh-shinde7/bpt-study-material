Definitions and types of surgery

Mastectomy: removal of the breast, usually for cancer, often with axillary node removal. Lymphoedema: chronic swelling from protein-rich fluid the lymphatics cannot drain (arm, hand, breast, chest wall).

Lymphoedema stages (ISL)

0 latent: no visible swelling, may feel heavy. I: pitting swelling, goes down with elevation. II: elevation rarely helps; pitting, then fibrosis. III: elephantiasis: no pitting, thick warty skin, folds. Diagnosis: ≥ 2 cm larger circumference or ~10% larger volume than the other arm.

Risk factors: axillary clearance, axillary radiotherapy, infection or seroma, obesity, cellulitis, sudden heavy overuse. Breast cancer: female, age, family history and BRCA, early menarche, late menopause, no children or first after 30, obesity, alcohol.

Surgery to impairments (clinical reasoning)

  • Pain and fear → arm held to the chest → stiff shoulder (flexion, abduction, ER); scar and radiotherapy fibrosis tether the chest wall and axilla.
  • Nodes removed → lymph load exceeds capacity → lymphoedema → fibrosis, infection risk.
  • Nerves: long thoracic (serratus anterior → winging), thoracodorsal (latissimus dorsi), intercostobrachial (numb inner arm).
  • Axillary web syndrome: tight cords from the axilla down the arm in weeks 1–8; limit abduction.
  • Raised, protracted shoulder; weight imbalance → neck and back pain. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy: fatigue, low counts, neuropathy, skin and lung changes.

Features: pain and tightness, drains in week 1, ↓ elevation, abduction and ER, arm or hand swelling, numb inner arm, cords, fatigue, low mood, body-image distress. Early lymphoedema clues (W21): heavy arm, tight sleeves or rings: measure both arms. Red, hot arm with fever = cellulitis: medical treatment first.

Investigations and treatment

Mammography, ultrasound, biopsy; staging scans (bone metastases: avoid heavy loading); blood counts in chemotherapy (low counts: reduce or hold exercise); arm circumference and volume; lymphoscintigraphy, bioimpedance.

Surgery ± reconstruction; chemotherapy (before or after); radiotherapy; hormone therapy (aromatase inhibitors: joint pain, bone loss); trastuzumab (heart).

PT assessment

Subjective: surgery, side, nodes removed, drains, reconstruction; chemo and radiotherapy; pain, heaviness, numbness; hand dominance; job and household load (cooking, washing, lifting children); goals.

Objective: wound and drains; posture, shoulder height, winging; shoulder ROM both sides (goniometry); scapular strength and grip; arm circumference every 4 cm from the wrist, volume, pitting, skin texture; inner-arm sensation; palpate cords.

Outcome measures: DASH, shoulder ROM, arm volume, FACT-B, fatigue scale, 6MWT.

ICF (W20: right-handed teacher, right MRM 3 weeks, on chemotherapy, arm swelling)

  • Body function: right shoulder pain; ↓ flexion, abduction, ER; early lymphoedema (stage I); scar and axillary tightness, cords; ↓ strength, chemotherapy fatigue; anxiety, body image.
  • Activity: reaching overhead, writing on the board, combing hair, dressing, lifting, cooking.
  • Participation: teaching, care of two children, social life.
  • Environment: family, hospital, a valued job (facilitators); board work, heavy chores, school bags (barriers). Personal: 40, motivated, worried about cancer and appearance.

Goals: short-term (4 weeks): less pain, flexion and abduction ~150°, smaller arm, knows skin care, walks 20 min. Long-term (3–6 months): full pain-free range, swelling controlled with a sleeve, exercise 2–3 times a week, prosthesis, back to teaching.

PT management: the timeline

  • Before surgery: baseline shoulder range and both arm measurements; teach first-week exercises, breathing and posture.
  • Days 1–7 (drains in): deep breathing, supported cough, early walking; hand pumping, wrist and elbow movement, shoulder shrugs, 10 reps several times a day; shoulder flexion and abduction only to ~90°; arm on a pillow, hand above elbow.
  • Why 90°? Full-range exercise straight after surgery increases drainage and seroma; waiting ~1 week reduces this and the shoulder still recovers in about a month (Cochrane).
  • Drains out: full active and assisted range: wand, finger wall-climb, pulley, hands behind head and back; stretches held 20–30 s, 3–5 reps, 2–3 times a day; scapular setting and posture; serratus work for winging; scar massage once healed; cord stretches; keep stretching through radiotherapy.
  • From ~6 weeks: progressive resistance (start light, supervised), aerobic walking or cycling, work tasks (board writing, cooking, lifting children).
  • Cancer fatigue (ACSM 2019): aerobic 3 a week, 30 min, moderate (RPE ~12); resistance 2 a week, ~60% 1RM, 2 × 12–15; at least 12 weeks; cut the dose on bad days rather than stopping.

Complete decongestive therapy (7m S21)

  • 1. Intensive, daily 2–4 weeks: manual lymph drainage (light, slow, skin-stretching; clear the neck, other axilla and trunk first, then upper arm → forearm → hand); multilayer short-stretch bandaging day and night; exercises in the bandage; skin care.
  • 2. Maintenance, lifelong: compression sleeve (± glove) by day; self lymph drainage; regular exercise including supervised resistance; skin care; weight control.
  • Also: elevation at rest, deep breathing, pneumatic compression only as an add-on; workstation advice (arm supported, breaks, sleeve at work).

Bandages (3m S23): short-stretch (low pressure at rest, high when muscles work, safe at night); multilayer (stockinette, finger gauze, padding, short-stretch layers, firmest at the hand); adjustable Velcro wraps for self-care. Long-stretch elastic bandages are not used (tourniquet at rest).

Current evidence: slowly progressive, supervised resistance training is safe with or at risk of lymphoedema ("start low, go slow"). Write: "avoid sudden heavy loading; build strength gradually; protect the skin; injections in the other arm where possible."

Breast prosthesis (3m S17)

Temporary soft fibre-filled form in a soft bra soon after surgery. Permanent silicone form once healed and swelling settles (~6–8 weeks, or after radiotherapy), matching the other breast's weight, in a pocketed bra. Why: symmetry and balance, stops the shoulder rising and spine tilting, clothes fit, confidence, protects the chest wall.

Precautions (3m S21)

Shoulder to ~90° while drains are in; watch seroma and infection; protect the arm from cuts, burns, bites, tight sleeves and bangles; no heat or vigorous massage on a swollen arm; build lifting gradually; protect flap donor sites; in chemotherapy hold exercise with fever, very low counts or vomiting; no heavy loading of bones with metastases; no electrotherapy over tumour. Refer urgently: red hot painful arm with fever, sudden calf or arm swelling, new bone pain or breathlessness.

Lifelong arm care (S19: why and how)

Why: removed nodes never regrow, so any extra load (infection, burn, injury, heavy unaccustomed work) can start lymphoedema, even years later.

How: clean, moisturised skin; gloves for cooking and washing vessels, oven gloves, long-handled spoons; antiseptic for every cut or bite; no tight bangles or sleeves; build heavy tasks slowly, carry with both hands; stay active, healthy weight; check the arm and report heaviness early; sleeve for travel or heavy work.
