1Neurophysiological facilitation (NPF): what it is

Neurophysiological facilitation of respiration uses external touch and pressure (proprioceptive and tactile stimuli) to produce reflex changes in breathing: deeper breaths, more movement in a chosen area, and changes in rate. It was described by Bethune (1975) for unconscious adults.

  • Who: patients who cannot follow instructions: unconscious or sedated (including ventilated patients with some own breathing), after head injury or stroke, confused patients, young children.
  • Aims: increase depth of breathing and lung expansion, improve basal and segmental ventilation, help secretions move, improve oxygenation.
  • Dose: each technique held through several breaths (often 30 seconds to a minute), repeated as long as a good response is seen; watch breathing, SpO₂ and heart rate.

2NPF techniques (7 marks in W22)

TechniqueHow to applyResponse seen
Perioral pressureFirm, maintained pressure with a finger on the upper lip, between the nose and the lipMore movement of the upper abdomen, deeper breaths, sighing; mouth closure and swallowing (linked to the sucking reflex)
Intercostal stretchPressure on the upper border of a rib, pushing it down to widen the intercostal space above; hold through several breathsMore expansion of the stretched area (intercostal stretch reflex)
Vertebral pressureFirm pressure over the upper thoracic (about T2–T5) or lower thoracic (about T7–T10) spinous processesUpper level: more upper-abdominal movement and deeper breathing. Lower level: more upper-chest (apical) movement
Co-contraction of the abdomenPressure on the lower ribs and the pelvis on one side, drawing them towards each otherMore upper-abdominal movement and posterior basal expansion; firmer abdominal wall
Anterior stretch basal liftPatient supine; the therapist's hands under the lower posterior ribs lift them gently upwards and holdPosterior basal expansion and upper-abdominal movement
Maintained (moderate) manual pressureFirm, steady hand pressure over an area of the chest wallMore movement under the hand during breathing in

Precautions: no pressure techniques over fractured ribs or an unstable spine, osteoporosis, painful wounds or burns; stop if the patient becomes agitated, the heart rate or BP becomes unstable, or SpO₂ falls. In raised intracranial pressure keep handling short and gentle.

Evidence: small studies in ventilated, stroke and paediatric patients show short-lasting improvements in breathing pattern, chest expansion and some gas-exchange measures. The effects last minutes, so NPF is used together with positioning and other techniques.

3PNF for respiration (7 marks in W24 and S25)

Proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation (PNF) applies the same principles used for limbs (manual contacts, stretch, resistance, verbal commands, irradiation from strong patterns) to the breathing muscles, in patients who can co-operate.

TechniqueHow to applyEffect
Repeated quick stretch with resistanceHands on the lower lateral ribs (or upper abdomen for the diaphragm); a quick stretch inwards and downwards at the end of expiration, then light resistance through inspiration; repeat the stretch during the breath in ("breathe in, push my hands out")Stretch reflex recruits the intercostals or diaphragm; more expansion of that area
Upper-limb D2 flexion pattern with inspirationBilateral D2 flexion (arms move up and out, as if reaching overhead) while breathing in; return in D2 extension while breathing out; can be resistedIrradiation from strong arm and scapular muscles; lifts and expands the upper chest
Trunk lift and chop"Lift" pattern (diagonal upward reach with trunk extension and rotation) with inspiration; "chop" pattern (diagonal downward with trunk flexion) with expirationTrunk extension opens the chest for inspiration; flexion helps expiration and coughing
Scapular posterior elevation with inspirationSide-lying; resisted scapular posterior elevation as the patient breathes inUpper-chest and lateral expansion
Rhythmic initiationPassive, then active-assisted, then active and resisted breathing movement in a rhythmTeaches a coordinated breathing pattern in patients who cannot start the movement
Approximation and timing for emphasisCompressing the ribs at end-expiration before the breath in; holding a strong pattern while emphasising the weaker areaFacilitates expiration and directs expansion to the weak area

For "three PNF techniques" (W24): write repeated quick stretch with resistance, bilateral D2 flexion with inspiration, and the trunk lift and chop, each with the method and the effect.

Indications: weak or uncoordinated breathing after stroke, spinal cord injury and neuromuscular disease; post-operative and post-ICU patients with low lung expansion; collapse at a lung base. Precautions: rib fractures, fresh wounds and drains under the hands, pain, unstable cardiovascular status.
