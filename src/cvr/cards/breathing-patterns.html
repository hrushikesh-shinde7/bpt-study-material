1Breathing patterns (3 marks in S16 and S22)

PatternDescriptionCauses
TachypnoeaRate above about 20 a minute in adultsFever, pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, pulmonary embolism, anxiety, pain, ILD
BradypnoeaRate below about 12 a minuteOpioids and sedatives, raised intracranial pressure, hypothermia, severe hypothyroidism
Hyperpnoea and Kussmaul breathingDeep breaths; Kussmaul is deep, fast and laboured, regularMetabolic acidosis: diabetic ketoacidosis, kidney failure (the body blows off CO₂ to compensate)
Cheyne–Stokes respiration (S16)Cycles of breaths that gradually deepen, then gradually become shallow, followed by a pause (apnoea) of seconds; then repeatsHeart failure (slow circulation delays the feedback from chemoreceptors), stroke and other brain injury, high altitude, opioids; can be normal in sleeping infants and the elderly
Biot's (ataxic) breathingGroups of quick, equal-depth breaths with irregular pauses; ataxic breathing is completely irregularDamage to the medulla: meningitis, raised intracranial pressure, brainstem stroke, opioids. A sign of serious brain injury
Apneustic breathingDeep gasping breath in, held, then a short breath outDamage to the pons
Central neurogenic hyperventilationSustained deep, rapid breathingMidbrain and upper pons lesions
Paradoxical breathingPart of the chest or abdomen moves the wrong wayFlail chest (segment moves in on inspiration); diaphragm paralysis or fatigue (abdomen moves in on inspiration); airway obstruction
Prolonged expiration, pursed lipsBreath out much longer than breath inCOPD, asthma
Sighing and erratic upper-chest breathingIrregular, with frequent sighs and yawnsDysfunctional breathing and hyperventilation (see breathing retraining)

Related positional terms: orthopnoea (breathless lying flat), paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea (waking breathless), platypnoea (breathless upright, better lying).

3Exam corner

Past questions (MUHS, 2016–2026)

  • 3-mark questionsCheyne–Stokes respiration (S16). Three abnormal breathing patterns (S22).

Test yourself

3 mDescribe Cheyne–Stokes respiration with two causes.

Cycles of gradually deepening then gradually shallower breaths, followed by a pause, repeating (draw it). Causes: heart failure, stroke or other brain injury, high altitude, opioids.

MCQDeep, rapid, regular breathing in a patient with diabetic ketoacidosis is called:A. Biot's breathingB. Kussmaul breathingC. Apneustic breathingD. Cheyne–Stokes respiration

B. Kussmaul breathing compensates for metabolic acidosis.
