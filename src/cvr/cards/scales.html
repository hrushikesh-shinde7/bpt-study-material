1Borg rating of perceived exertion (6–20)

Rates the overall effort of exercise. The numbers roughly match heart rate ÷ 10 in healthy young adults (13 ≈ 130 beats per minute).

RatingWords
6No exertion at all
7Extremely light
9Very light
11Light
13Somewhat hard
15Hard (heavy)
17Very hard
19Extremely hard
20Maximal exertion

The even numbers in between have no words. Use: Phase I cardiac rehab below 13; Phase II training 11–16 (start 11–13); moderate intensity 12–13.

2Modified Borg and CR10 breathlessness scales (3 marks in S16, S18, S26)

A category–ratio scale from 0 to 10 for breathlessness (or fatigue, pain) during activity. The modified Borg dyspnoea scale below is the version used in the six-minute walk test.

RatingBreathlessness
0Nothing at all
0.5Very, very slight (just noticeable)
1Very slight
2Slight
3Moderate
4Somewhat severe
5Severe
6–
7Very severe
8–
9Very, very severe (almost maximal)
10Maximal
  • Borg's own CR10 uses the same 0–10 frame with slightly different words ("extremely weak" at 0.5, "weak" at 2, "strong" at 5, "very strong" at 7, "extremely strong" at 10), and allows ratings above 10 for an absolute maximum. Either version scores the S16 and S18 question.
  • Why "category–ratio": the numbers have word anchors (categories) and are spaced so that a 4 feels about twice a 2 (ratio), so it can be used to compare intensities.
  • Uses: breathlessness before and after the 6MWT; pulmonary rehab training at 3–5 (moderate to severe); stop criteria (for example above 7); pacing advice.

3mMRC dyspnoea scale

GradeDescription
0Breathless only with strenuous exercise
1Short of breath when hurrying on the level or walking up a slight hill
2Walks slower than people of the same age on the level because of breathlessness, or has to stop for breath when walking at own pace on the level
3Stops for breath after walking about 100 metres or after a few minutes on the level
4Too breathless to leave the house, or breathless when dressing or undressing

Use: grades breathlessness in daily life (not during a test); GOLD uses mMRC 2 or more as "more symptoms". MUHS cases quote it (S21 bronchiectasis mMRC 3; S21 ILD mMRC 2).

4NYHA classes (3 marks in S21) and CCS angina grades

New York Heart Association functional classes (heart failure)
ClassDescription
INo limitation; ordinary activity causes no undue breathlessness, fatigue or palpitations
IISlight limitation; comfortable at rest, ordinary activity causes symptoms
IIIMarked limitation; comfortable at rest, less than ordinary activity causes symptoms
IVUnable to do any activity without discomfort; symptoms may be present at rest
Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) angina grades
GradeDescription
IOrdinary activity (walking, climbing stairs) does not cause angina; angina only with strenuous, rapid or prolonged exertion
IISlight limitation: angina walking or climbing stairs rapidly, uphill, after meals, in cold or wind, under stress; or walking more than two blocks on the level or more than one flight of stairs at a normal pace
IIIMarked limitation: angina walking one or two blocks on the level or one flight of stairs at a normal pace
IVUnable to do any physical activity without discomfort; angina may occur at rest

Generic or disease-specific (syllabus 10a): generic tools compare different conditions but are less sensitive to change in one disease; disease-specific tools detect small changes after rehab. Use one of each when possible, and compare the patient's own rating with your assessment (syllabus 10b).
