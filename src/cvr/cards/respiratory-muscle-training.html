1Why train the breathing muscles

Breathing muscles can be weak (low strength) or overloaded (stiff lungs, airway obstruction, hyperinflation), and both cause breathlessness, poor exercise tolerance, a weak cough and failure to wean from a ventilator. Like limb muscles, they get stronger with progressive overload.

  • Measure: maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP or PImax) and maximal expiratory pressure (MEP) with a mouth pressure meter. A MIP stronger than about −80 cmH₂O usually rules out important inspiratory weakness; in COPD, a MIP weaker than about 60 cmH₂O marks the patients who gain most from training.
  • Who benefits: COPD with inspiratory muscle weakness, heart failure, before high-risk heart, lung or abdominal surgery, weaning failure in ICU, neuromuscular disease and spinal cord injury (early stages), dysfunctional breathing, athletes.

3Dose

PartInspiratory muscle training (threshold)
IntensityStart at about 30% of MIP; progress towards 50–60% or more as tolerated; re-measure MIP every 1–2 weeks
VolumeAbout 30 breaths (or about 15 minutes), twice a day
Frequency and duration5–7 days a week for at least 6–8 weeks, then a maintenance dose
TechniqueSitting upright, nose clip if needed, a quick strong breath in through the device, then a slow relaxed breath out; rest between sets

4Precautions and contraindications

  • Recent or untreated pneumothorax; recent lung or chest-wall surgery not yet healed.
  • Acute exacerbation of COPD or asthma; unstable heart failure; recent heart attack.
  • Perforated eardrum; raised intracranial pressure; active haemoptysis.
  • Watch for fatigue (especially in neuromuscular disease, where overwork can harm weak muscles), dizziness and breathlessness; stop if they appear.

Classic answer vs current evidence: IMT in COPD

Classic

Give inspiratory muscle training to every COPD patient.

Current

On its own, IMT improves inspiratory strength, endurance, breathlessness and walking distance, most in patients with weak inspiratory muscles (meta-analysis, 2011). Added to a full pulmonary rehabilitation programme, it improved muscle strength but not exercise capacity in a 2018 trial. It is an add-on for weak patients, not a replacement for whole-body exercise. In ICU, IMT helps patients who fail weaning (review, 2015).

Write in the exam

"IMT at 30% or more of MIP, about 30 breaths twice a day for at least 8 weeks, for patients with inspiratory muscle weakness, alongside general exercise training."

5Exercise programme to strengthen respiratory muscles (7 marks in W22)

  1. Assess: MIP and MEP, breathing pattern, chest expansion, peak cough flow, breathlessness (Borg, mMRC), posture.
  2. Diaphragmatic breathing with facilitation and manual resistance, progressing from half-lying to sitting and standing.
  3. Inspiratory muscle training with a threshold device at the dose above.
  4. Expiratory muscle training and huffing practice where cough is weak; abdominal muscle strengthening (bracing, curl-ups as allowed).
  5. Accessory and postural muscles: scapular and upper-limb exercise (arm ergometry, resisted arm patterns), thoracic mobility.
  6. Whole-body aerobic training (walking, cycling): the ventilatory load of exercise also trains the breathing muscles.
  7. Endurance: normocapnic hyperpnoea where available; singing or wind-instrument games for children.
  8. Progress and review every 1–2 weeks; re-measure MIP; adjust load.

Test yourself

3 mDifferentiate threshold loading from resistive training.

Threshold: a spring valve opens at a set pressure, so the load is fixed whatever the flow. Resistive: breathing through a small hole, so the load rises with faster flow and the breathing pattern must be controlled.
