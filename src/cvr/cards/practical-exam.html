2Long case: assessment proforma

Subjective

  • Demographics: name, age, sex, occupation (what the job demands), hand dominance, address (stairs, distance).
  • Chief complaints in the patient's words, with duration.
  • History of present illness: breathlessness (onset, triggers, mMRC or NYHA grade, orthopnoea, waking at night), cough and sputum (amount, colour, smell, blood), chest pain (character, radiation, relief), palpitations, fainting, swelling of the feet, calf pain on walking (claudication distance), fever, weight loss.
  • Past history: TB, diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, surgery, admissions, ventilation.
  • Personal history: smoking in pack-years (cigarettes a day ÷ 20 × years smoked), bidis and chewing tobacco, alcohol, diet, sleep, bladder and bowel.
  • Drug history: inhalers and technique, cardiac drugs (beta-blockers change your exercise plan), oxygen at home.
  • Family and social history: who lives at home, support, finances, home layout, work.
  • The patient's goals ("I want to go back to driving").

Objective

  • Vital signs: HR (rate and rhythm), BP, respiratory rate, SpO₂ (on air or oxygen), temperature, pain score.
  • General observation: build and BMI, posture, cyanosis, clubbing, pallor, oedema, lines, drains, oxygen device, scars.
  • Chest: shape, breathing pattern and rate, accessory muscle use, chest movement; palpation (trachea, expansion: tape measurements at the axilla, nipple line and xiphisternum, compared side to side); percussion; auscultation (breath sounds, added sounds); cough strength; sputum.
  • Cardiovascular: pulses (including foot pulses), capillary refill, oedema (pitting grade), postural BP, ABI if vascular.
  • Musculoskeletal and function: shoulder and thoracic range, posture, muscle strength (MRC grade), bed mobility, transfers, gait, balance.
  • Exercise tolerance: 6MWT or shuttle test with SpO₂ and Borg, or a sit-to-stand test.
  • Scales: mMRC, modified Borg, NYHA, CCS, pain scale, quality of life (CAT, SGRQ, MLHFQ).
  • Investigations: X-ray, ABG, PFT, ECG, echo (EF), blood tests.

Then

  1. ICF table and one-line functional diagnosis (see the answer-writing card).
  2. Problem list; short- and long-term goals with time frames.
  3. Plan of care with doses and precautions.
  4. Demonstrate one test (for example chest expansion, 6MWT set-up, ABI, auscultation, postural BP) and one treatment (for example ACBT, drainage position, incentive spirometry, Phase I walking with monitoring).

3Presenting the long case (3–5 minutes)

Template

"Mr X, 55, a bank manager from Pune, is on day 3 after CABG for triple vessel disease. His main complaints are wound pain and breathlessness on walking. He is a smoker of 20 pack-years with hypertension. On examination: HR 92 regular, BP 130/84, SpO₂ 95% on air, reduced breath sounds at the left base, weak cough, walks 40 m with RPE 12. My functional diagnosis is ... . My short-term goals are ... ; long-term goals ... . My plan is ... . I will now demonstrate a 6-minute walk set-up and ACBT."

Likely viva questions: why this goal; what you would do if SpO₂ falls; stop criteria; how you would progress; what the X-ray or ECG shows; why this dose; what the drugs mean for exercise; outcome measures; home programme.

4Short case: demonstrating skills (2 × 10 marks)

Steps for any skill demonstration

  1. Introduce yourself, check the patient's identity, explain and take consent.
  2. Hand hygiene; privacy and draping; mask in suspected TB.
  3. Check vital signs and contraindications for this skill.
  4. Position the patient (and yourself) correctly.
  5. Explain each step in simple words; demonstrate; let the patient do it; correct.
  6. Watch the response (breathing, SpO₂, pain, colour) and stop if needed.
  7. State the dose (repetitions, sets, frequency) and the home programme.
  8. Reassess, thank the patient, and document.

6The journal (5 marks)

Each case written up under the same headings: assessment (subjective, objective), evaluation (investigations, scales), ICF and functional diagnosis, prognosis, goals, intervention with doses and progression, outcome measures, and a SOAP follow-up note. Keep it neat and complete; examiners read the ICF and the doses first. Template on the answer-writing card.

7The affective domain: what examiners watch

  • Greeting, introduction, consent, respectful language, privacy and draping.
  • Hand hygiene; infection control (mask for TB, cleaning shared devices).
  • Safety: checking vital signs, oxygen, lines and drains before moving the patient; stopping when the patient is unwell.
  • Explaining in the patient's language (Marathi or Hindi where needed) and checking understanding.
  • Calm, honest answers in the viva: if you do not know, say so and say how you would find out.
  • Thanking the patient and leaving them comfortable.

Last two weeks

  1. Present three practice long cases aloud (a cardiac, a respiratory and a vascular or surgical case), timed.
  2. Practise each short-case skill on a friend with the 8-step sequence.
  3. Do spot drills: one X-ray, ABG, PFT, ECG and device in 10 minutes, then check.
  4. Finish the journal.
