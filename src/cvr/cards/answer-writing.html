1The paper and a time plan

All 20 papers from 2016 to 2026 used this pattern (no MCQs, although the syllabus prints a different scheme): 80 marks in 3 hours, one answer book.

QuestionChoiceMarksTime to spend
Read and chooseTick your choices; plan the long answers–5 min
Q1 short answersAny 5 of 65 × 3 = 15About 6 min each (30 min)
Q2 short answersAny 5 of 65 × 7 = 35About 14 min each (70 min)
Q3 and Q4 long answersAny 1 of 2 each (patient cases)2 × 15 = 30About 30 min each (60 min)
ReviseUnderline keywords, add missing doses and precautions–15 min
  • About 2 minutes a mark. Never spend 20 minutes on a 3-mark answer.
  • If you write slowly, do the two long answers first, so 30 marks are safe; never leave them for the last 20 minutes.
  • Choose the long-answer option whose condition you know best, not the shorter-looking question.

2Short answers: 3 and 7 marks

3 marks (about half a page)

  1. One-line definition.
  2. Three to five points, a small table, or a labelled sketch (numbers, values, names).
  3. One line of physiotherapy relevance if it fits.
Example: "Ankle–brachial index" (3 marks)

Definition: ratio of ankle to arm systolic pressure, measured with a Doppler. Formula: highest ankle ÷ highest brachial pressure, for each leg. Interpretation: 0.91–1.40 normal, 0.90 or less PAD, below 0.40 severe, above 1.40 non-compressible arteries. Use: diagnosis of PAD and a check before compression bandaging.

7 marks (1.5–2 pages)

  1. Definition or introduction (2 lines).
  2. Principle, classification or "why" (a flowchart is ideal).
  3. The main body as 6–8 headed points or a table: technique with dose, or management by phase.
  4. A labelled diagram where one exists (cycle, positions, curve, timeline).
  5. Precautions and contraindications.
  6. One concluding line (evidence or outcome measure).

Each technique card and condition page has an answer skeleton with marks for the questions actually asked.

3Long answers: the 15-mark case

  1. Read the split (for example 5 + 5 + 5 or 3 + 4 + 8) and give each part space in proportion.
  2. Underline the case clues: age, occupation, earning role, home (stairs, lives alone), day after surgery, drains, settings, test results, what the patient wants.
  3. Functional diagnosis in one sentence, then the ICF table with a "clinical reasoning" column (section 4).
  4. Problem list in order of priority.
  5. Goals: short-term and long-term, SMART, with time frames (section 5).
  6. Management: by phase or by impairment, with technique, dose and reason; precautions and stop criteria; education and home programme.
  7. Outcome measures and discharge or progression criteria in one or two lines.

Write doses with units (repetitions, sets, times a day, minutes, RPE, % HRR); a plan without doses loses marks.

4Applying the ICF model (Ch 5)

The WHO International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) describes a person's health as the interaction of the health condition with body functions and structures, activities and participation, and contextual factors. The model diagram is drawn on the IHD page, section 10; draw it in every long answer.

ComponentMeaningTypical clues in the question
Health conditionThe diagnosis and its stage"CABG 3 days back", "post-Koch's bronchiectasis", "40% burns"
Body functions and structures (impairments)Problems in body parts or functionsPain VAS 6, breathlessness, sputum, reduced expansion, weakness, stiff shoulder, oedema, low SpO₂
Activity limitationsDifficulty doing tasksCannot sit or sleep, cannot climb stairs, cannot walk to the field, difficulty with self-care
Participation restrictionsDifficulty in life rolesCannot work as a driver, teacher or cook; only earning member; misses school; embarrassed to meet people
Environmental factorsFacilitators and barriers around the person2nd floor with no lift, lives alone, family support, cost, dusty job, distance to hospital
Personal factorsThe person's own featuresAge, smoker, motivation, fear, "wants to resume job early", "ignored advice"
Functional diagnosis template

"[Age]-year-old [occupation] with [health condition, day or stage], presenting with [3–4 key impairments], leading to [activity limitations], restricting [participation], with [main facilitator] and [main barrier]."

Common mistakes: listing symptoms as participation; leaving out contextual factors; writing a disease description instead of function; not linking each impairment to a treatment.

5Goals with time frames (Ch 5a, 5b)

Goals should be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant to the patient, Time-bound. Write them as what the patient will do.

TypeTime frameExample
Short-termDays to about 2 weeks (in hospital, or early rehab)"Clears sputum independently with ACBT within 3 days"; "walks 100 m on the ward with RPE below 13 by discharge"
Long-termAbout 6–12 weeks or more (end of the programme)"Returns to driving to work (1 hour) by 6 weeks"; "climbs to the 2nd floor with one rest by 8 weeks"
Patient-specific goals from real MUHS cases
CaseLong-term goal
Bank manager after angioplasty who drives 1 hour to work (S20)Drives to work and completes a full working day without symptoms by about 4 weeks, after clearance
Tiffin cook after mastectomy (S19)Cooks for ten clients twice a day, lifting cooking vessels with the operated arm, by about 8–12 weeks, with lymphoedema precautions
Farmer with COPD (S17)Walks to the field and does light farm work with pacing, without an exacerbation, by 12 weeks
Milkman after below-knee amputation (S19)Delivers door to door with a prosthesis, managing stairs, within the rehabilitation programme
Schoolchild with asthma (S23, W22)Attends school every day and joins outdoor play with a warm-up and reliever plan

Clinical reasoning: link each goal to an impairment and each treatment to a goal ("because...").

Also record consent, precautions, adverse events and outcome measures with dates. The practical-exam journal (5 marks) follows the same headings plus ICF, prognosis and intervention.

7Presentation that earns marks

  • Headings, numbered points and tables; one idea per line.
  • Underline the keywords listed in each exam corner (for example ICF, FITT, RPE, sternal precautions).
  • Labelled diagrams: ICF model, ACBT cycle, drainage positions, CR phases timeline, rule of nines, spirogram.
  • Always include precautions and stop criteria in management answers.
  • Keep pathology short unless it is asked; spend the space on physiotherapy.
  • Leave a few lines after each answer for additions during revision.

Checklist before you hand in

  • Every part of every split answered?
  • Doses with units? Time frames on goals?
  • Precautions written? Diagram labelled?
  • Question numbers written correctly in the margin?
