#Exercise and precautions in valve disease

  • Moderate exercise is safe in mild to moderate lesions.
  • No exercise training in symptomatic severe aortic stenosis until it is treated: risk of fainting and sudden death.
  • Mitral stenosis: fast heart rates shorten filling time, causing breathlessness. Mitral disease often leaves pulmonary hypertension and atrial fibrillation, so progress more slowly.
  • After valve surgery through a sternotomy, follow the CABG sternal precautions.
  • Use RPE and the talk test when atrial fibrillation or beta-blockers make heart rate unreliable.
  • Warfarin: bruising and bleeding are easy, so handle gently, prevent falls, avoid vigorous manual techniques and contact sports, and report any bleeding.
Do not exercise if
  • Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis, uncontrolled arrhythmia, chest pain at rest
  • Signs of decompensated heart failure: breathless at rest, new orthopnoea, rapid weight gain (for example 2 kg in 3 days), increasing oedema

#Education

  • Rheumatic heart disease: never miss penicillin injections; keep teeth healthy.
  • Mechanical valve: take warfarin every day and keep regular INR checks.
  • Infective endocarditis: keep teeth and skin healthy; report fever, night sweats or new breathlessness early.
  • Keep active every day within your plan.

Test yourself

3 mWhich valve lesions cause a systolic murmur, and which a diastolic murmur?

Systolic: aortic stenosis and mitral regurgitation, because in systole the aortic valve should be open and the mitral valve shut. Diastolic: mitral stenosis and aortic regurgitation.

MCQWhich lesion makes exercise training unsafe until it is treated?A. Mild mitral regurgitationB. Symptomatic severe aortic stenosisC. Mild aortic regurgitationD. A well-functioning tissue valve

B. The narrow valve stops output rising with exercise: fainting and sudden death.
