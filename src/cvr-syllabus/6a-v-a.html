#Definitions

Acquired heart disease develops after birth, unlike congenital heart disease. It includes coronary disease (6(a)(ii)), valve disease (6(a)(iii)), heart failure and cardiomyopathy. This page covers heart failure, cardiomyopathy and the devices used in them.

  • Heart failure: a condition in which the heart cannot pump enough blood for the body's needs, or can do so only at high filling pressures, causing breathlessness, fatigue and fluid retention.
  • Cardiomyopathy: disease of the heart muscle itself (dilated, hypertrophic or restrictive).
  • Pacemaker: a device that sends electrical impulses to keep the heart rate from going too low. ICD (implantable cardioverter-defibrillator): a device that detects dangerous fast rhythms and treats them with pacing or a shock.
