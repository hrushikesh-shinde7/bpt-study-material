vPneumothorax

Definition and types

Pneumothorax: air in the pleural space, so the lung collapses partly or fully.

  • Primary spontaneous: no known lung disease; typically tall, thin young men; smoking raises risk.
  • Secondary spontaneous: underlying lung disease (COPD with bullae, TB, cystic fibrosis, ILD); more dangerous.
  • Traumatic (rib fractures, stab wounds) and iatrogenic (after procedures, central lines, ventilation).
  • Tension pneumothorax: a one-way valve lets air in but not out; pressure pushes the mediastinum across and stops venous return. A medical emergency.

Clinical features

Sudden one-sided chest pain and breathlessness. Tension pneumothorax: severe distress, low BP, fast heart rate, tracheal shift away, distended neck veins.

X-ray

  • A thin visible pleural line with no lung markings beyond it.
  • Tension: mediastinal shift away, flattened diaphragm.

Medical and surgical management

  • BTS 2023: management depends mainly on symptoms and stability, not just size: observation for minimal symptoms; needle aspiration, ambulatory device or chest drain for symptomatic patients; surgery (VATS pleurodesis) for recurrence or persistent air leak.
  • Tension pneumothorax: immediate needle decompression, then a chest drain.

Classic answer vs current guideline: pneumothorax size

Classic

A rim of more than 2 cm on X-ray means a chest drain.

Current (BTS 2023)

Decisions are led by symptoms and stability. Many patients with few symptoms can be observed or treated as outpatients, even with a larger rim.

Write in the exam

"Management depends on symptoms: observation, aspiration or intercostal drain."

Physiotherapy, including with an intercostal drain (7 marks in W22)

  • Check the drain before and after each session: swing, bubbling, drainage, no kinks; bottle upright and below chest level; never clamp a bubbling drain (full detail in 6(e) pulmonary surgery).
  • Breathing control; deep breathing exercises and thoracic expansion to help the lung re-expand once drained.
  • Supported cough at the drain site; adequate pain relief.
  • Early mobilisation with the drain; shoulder ROM on the drain side.
  • Posture correction.
Precautions and contraindications
  • Undrained pneumothorax: do not use positive-pressure techniques (IPPB, NIV, PEP, manual hyperinflation) until a drain is in. They can turn it into a tension pneumothorax.
  • Avoid vigorous percussion over the drain and fresh wounds.
  • Avoid heavy straining and breath-holding until healed.
  • No flying until the doctor confirms resolution; no scuba diving unless definitive surgery has been done.
Stop and call for help if
  • Sudden worsening breathlessness, falling BP, tracheal shift (tension)
  • The drain falls out or disconnects
  • Sudden heavy blood in the drain

Prevent recurrence: stop smoking, which is strongly linked to recurrence; know the warning signs (sudden chest pain and breathlessness).

viHydropneumothorax

Definition

Hydropneumothorax: both air and fluid in the pleural space. It brings together the problems of a pneumothorax and a pleural effusion.

X-ray

A straight horizontal air–fluid level: air above, fluid below. A plain effusion has a curved upper border (meniscus) instead.

Management and physiotherapy

  • The pleural space is drained, usually with an intercostal drain, and the cause is treated.
  • Physiotherapy combines both plans: drain care and the positive-pressure precautions of a pneumothorax (v above), with the positioning and localised expansion exercises of an effusion (ix below).
