vOccupational lung diseases

Occupational lung disease is lung disease caused by breathing in dusts, fibres, fumes or organic material at work, usually after years of exposure without masks or ventilation (cotton mills, stone quarries, mines, construction, agriculture).

  • Pneumoconioses (mineral dusts): silicosis (stone cutting, mining, sandblasting, glass and ceramics), asbestosis, coal workers' pneumoconiosis. They scar the lung like other interstitial lung diseases.
  • Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (organic dusts): farmer's lung (mouldy hay or grain), bird fancier's lung.
  • Byssinosis (cotton, flax, jute): chest tightness on the first working day of the week.

Prevention comes first: avoid the dust that caused the disease, ask about a job transfer, wear a fitted respirator mask if exposure continues, and screen for TB in silicosis. Assessment and physiotherapy are the same as for ILD below.

viInterstitial lung disease

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) is a group of disorders that inflame and scar (fibrose) the lung tissue between the air sacs. The lungs become stiff and small, and oxygen passes poorly into the blood, so patients desaturate on exertion.
