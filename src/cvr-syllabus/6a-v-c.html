#Clinical features

  • Breathlessness on exertion, orthopnoea, waking breathless at night; fatigue; ankle swelling; weight gain from fluid.
  • Fast pulse, low BP in advanced disease, raised neck veins, basal crackles, third heart sound.
  • Palpitations from arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia); fainting.

#Investigations

  • Echocardiogram: EF and chamber size (the key test).
  • ECG: rhythm (AF), conduction blocks, ventricular hypertrophy; Holter monitoring.
  • Chest X-ray: large heart, pulmonary congestion, effusions.
  • BNP or NT-proBNP: raised in heart failure.
  • Exercise test or CPET: peak oxygen uptake and a safe exercise range.

#Medical and device management

  • Heart failure with reduced EF: four main drug groups (ACE inhibitor/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, mineralocorticoid antagonist, SGLT2 inhibitor), diuretics for congestion; devices (ICD, cardiac resynchronisation therapy); exercise training.
  • Pacemaker or ICD: implanted under the skin below the collarbone (usually left), with leads passed through a vein into the heart.

#Complications

  • Acute pulmonary oedema, arrhythmias, stroke (AF), sudden death, kidney impairment.
  • Device: lead displacement (early, from arm movement), pocket haematoma or infection, pneumothorax at insertion, inappropriate ICD shocks, anxiety after shocks.
