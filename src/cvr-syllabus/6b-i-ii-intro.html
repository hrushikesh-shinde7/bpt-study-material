iChronic bronchitis

Chronic bronchitis (clinical definition): cough with sputum on most days for at least 3 months a year, for 2 years in a row, with other causes excluded.

  • Main problem: inflammation from smoke and dust narrows the small airways, enlarges the mucus glands and damages the cilia, so there is cough, sputum and repeated infection.
  • Classic picture: marked productive cough, moderate breathlessness; often overweight, cyanosed, with ankle oedema (cor pulmonale); low oxygen and high CO₂ early.
  • X-ray: often near-normal, or more prominent lung markings ("dirty chest") and thickened bronchial walls seen as tram lines.

Physiotherapy puts airway clearance first (4(a)), within the COPD plan below.

iiEmphysema

Emphysema (structural definition): permanent enlargement of the air spaces beyond the terminal bronchioles, with destruction of their walls.

  • Main problem: destroyed alveolar walls lose the elastic recoil that holds the small airways open. Airways collapse during breathing out, air is trapped, and the flattened diaphragm works at a mechanical disadvantage.
  • Classic picture: severe breathlessness early, little sputum; thin, pursed-lip breathing, barrel chest, accessory muscles; oxygen kept near normal until late; diffusing capacity (DLCO) reduced.
  • X-ray (3 marks in W18): hyperinflated, very black lung fields, low flattened diaphragms, a long narrow heart; full list in section 6.
  • Bullous emphysema: avoid high-pressure techniques (high PEP, aggressive manual hyperinflation): risk of pneumothorax.

Physiotherapy puts breathing control, pursed-lip breathing and positions of ease first, with pulmonary rehabilitation for the long term.

Most patients have features of both, so the two are managed together as COPD in sections 1 to 16 below. The classic comparison table is in section 2.
