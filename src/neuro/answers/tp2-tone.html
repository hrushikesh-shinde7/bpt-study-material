Define and classify muscle tone

Muscle tone: the resistance of a relaxed muscle to passive stretch, with a neural part (stretch reflex, descending control) and a non-neural part (muscle and connective tissue stiffness).

  • Hypertonia: spasticity (velocity-dependent, clasp-knife; UMN); rigidity (lead-pipe or cogwheel, any speed; basal ganglia); dystonia.
  • Hypotonia (flaccidity): low resistance, floppy, hypermobile (LMN, cerebellum, spinal shock, Down's).
  • Fluctuating tone: swings low to high (athetoid CP).

Muscle tone; physiotherapy of hypotonia (3 + 4)

Muscle tone (3)

The resistance of a relaxed muscle to passive stretch: neural (stretch reflex, descending control) plus non-neural (tissue stiffness). Normal tone is high enough to hold posture against gravity and low enough for selective movement. Types: spasticity, rigidity, dystonia, hypotonia, fluctuating tone.

Physiotherapy of hypotonia (4)

  • Protect hypermobile joints (shoulder support in early stroke; no overstretching).
  • Positioning against gravity with support: prone over a wedge, supported sitting, standing frame.
  • Facilitation just before active movement: quick stretch, tapping, fast brushing, quick icing, vibration, fast vestibular input.
  • Co-contraction: approximation and weight-bearing in kneeling and standing; rhythmic stabilisation.
  • Strengthening and function: head and trunk control, sit to stand, walking; electrical stimulation of weak muscles.
  • Teach carers handling and safe positions.

Three facilitatory techniques for hypotonia

  1. Quick stretch and tapping over the muscle belly or tendon before movement: excites the muscle spindle.
  2. Approximation (joint compression) in weight-bearing positions: joint receptors facilitate co-contraction.
  3. Fast brushing or quick icing over the skin of the muscle (Rood).

Others: vibration, resistance, bouncing on a ball, fast vestibular input, PNF repeated stretch.

Slow icing

  • Method: an ice pack wrapped in a damp towel, or cold immersion, over the spastic muscle for about 15–20 minutes (prolonged cold).
  • Effect: cooling slows nerve conduction and reduces muscle spindle activity, so tone drops for a short time.
  • Use: the low-tone window for stretching and active practice (spastic hand, calf).
  • Precautions: check sensation and circulation; avoid in cold sensitivity, Raynaud's, numb skin.

Contrast: quick icing (brief strokes) facilitates.

Three methods to reduce tone

  1. Prolonged stretch by positioning, splint, standing frame or serial cast: reduces reflex excitability and keeps length.
  2. Prolonged cold (slow icing) for 15–20 minutes: slows conduction and spindle activity.
  3. Weight-bearing through the extended arm or a plantigrade foot: stretch plus compression.

Others: neutral warmth, slow rhythmic rotation, reflex-inhibiting patterns, electrical stimulation of antagonists.

Tone-inhibiting techniques; physiotherapy of spasticity

Spasticity (Lance 1980): velocity-dependent increase in tonic stretch reflexes with exaggerated tendon jerks, part of the UMN syndrome. Measure with MAS and Tardieu.

First: treat only if it causes a problem (pain, contracture, hygiene, function); look for triggers (bladder, constipation, pressure sore, ingrown toenail, tight clothes, anxiety).
