Types of neurological (pathological) bladder

  • Uninhibited: brain lesion (stroke, PD, MS); urgency, frequency, urge leaks; empties fully.
  • Reflex (automatic, spastic): complete cord lesion above S2; reflex emptying without sensation; dyssynergia.
  • Autonomous (atonic, flaccid): sacral cord or cauda equina; large capacity, overflow dribbling.
  • Sensory paralytic: diabetes, tabes, B12 deficiency; no sense of fullness.
  • Motor paralytic: polio, root injury; sensation present but cannot empty.

Reflex bladder

  • Follows a complete cord lesion above the sacral segments (S2–S4).
  • The sacral reflex arc is intact but cut off from the brain, so the bladder contracts by reflex when it fills, without sensation or voluntary control; small capacity.
  • The sphincter often tightens at the same time (detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia): high pressure, incomplete emptying, risk of reflux, kidney damage and autonomic dysreflexia (T6 and above).
  • Management: clean intermittent catheterisation with drugs that calm the detrusor.

Physiotherapy

  • Both: teach clean intermittent catheterisation 4–6 times a day, volumes under 400–500 mL: hand washing, long sitting or wheelchair position, sitting balance with both hands free, clothing; adapted aids for weak hands.
  • Spastic: plus antimuscarinic drugs; teach autonomic dysreflexia signs and first steps.
  • Flaccid: pelvic floor training if any contraction; protect skin from leaks.
  • Transfers to toilet or commode; bladder diary; fluid plan; watch for infection.

Suprapubic tapping (spastic) and Credé or Valsalva (flaccid) raise bladder pressure: only if urodynamics show safe pressures (EAU 2026).

Classify neurogenic bladder; assessment and physiotherapy management

Classification

  • By lesion level (EAU): above the pons (detrusor overactivity, sphincter normal); spinal cord between pons and sacral cord (overactivity with dyssynergia); sacral cord and below (weak or absent detrusor).
  • Classic (Lapides): uninhibited, reflex, autonomous, sensory paralytic, motor paralytic.

Assessment

  • How the person empties; leaks, urgency, infections, autonomic dysreflexia, bowel pattern.
  • Bladder diary for 3 days; residual urine (scanner); urodynamics by the urologist.
  • Hand function, sitting balance, transfers, dressing, cognition; pelvic floor contraction; home toilet type.
