Three clinical features (symptoms) of multiple sclerosis

  1. Visual: optic neuritis (painful eye movement, blurred central vision, faded colours), double vision, nystagmus.
  2. Sensory: numbness, tingling, band-like feelings; Lhermitte's sign (electric shock down the spine on neck flexion).
  3. Motor: weakness and spasticity of the legs with UMN signs.

Also: fatigue (often the most disabling), cerebellar ataxia and intention tremor, bladder urgency, cognitive and mood changes, heat sensitivity (Uhthoff's).

Uhthoff's phenomenon

  • Definition: a temporary worsening of MS symptoms (often blurred vision or weakness) when body temperature rises: exercise, hot bath, hot weather, fever.
  • Mechanism: heat slows or blocks conduction further in demyelinated fibres; settles on cooling. It is not a relapse.
  • Physiotherapy: pre-cooling (cool drink, cool shower), cool room, fan, cooling vest, breaks; exercise at the coolest time; avoid hot packs. Cool rather than stop exercise.

Charcot's triad in multiple sclerosis

  1. Nystagmus
  2. Intention tremor
  3. Scanning (staccato) speech

These are cerebellar and brainstem signs from plaques in the cerebellum and its connections. Physiotherapy for the ataxia: proximal stability, Frenkel's exercises, slow controlled practice with visual feedback, weighted wrist cuffs for some tasks.

Do not confuse with Charcot's triad of cholangitis (fever, jaundice, right upper quadrant pain).

Visual, cognitive and behavioural disturbances in multiple sclerosis

AreaFeaturesPhysiotherapy approach
VisualOptic neuritis (painful eye movement, blurred central vision, poor colour vision); double vision (internuclear ophthalmoplegia); nystagmus; oscillopsiaGood lighting; high-contrast step edges; uncluttered spaces; refer for eye care; gaze stability exercises if appropriate
CognitiveSlowed processing; poor memory, attention and planning (executive function)Short, simple instructions; written programmes; practise in quiet surroundings; avoid dual tasks at first
Behavioural and emotionalDepression, anxiety, irritability, emotional lability (sudden laughing or crying), euphoria, low motivationAgree goals together; encourage; exercise improves mood; refer for counselling

Fatigue and heat (Uhthoff's) make all three worse, so schedule sessions in the cool part of the day and pace activity.

These "invisible" symptoms often limit work and family life more than weakness; they decide how you teach the home programme.

Relapsing-remitting (85% at onset)Relapses for days to weeks, then full or partial recovery; stable between
Secondary progressiveAfter a relapsing course, steady increase in disability, with or without relapses
Primary progressiveSteady worsening from the start, often walking difficulty

Physiotherapy in relapsing-remitting MS (EDSS 0–6.5)

  • During a relapse: rest the affected function, keep range, position well, gentle activity; avoid overheating.
  • After a relapse: grade back up: transfers, balance, walking, strength.
  • Aerobic: 2–3 times a week, 10–30 minutes, moderate (40–60% HRmax, RPE 11–13).
  • Resistance: 2–3 times a week, 1–3 sets of 8–15, 5–10 exercises.
  • Balance and gait training; AFO for foot drop; stretching for spasticity.
  • Fatigue: energy conservation; heat: pre-cooling. Target 150 minutes a week of activity.

Multiple sclerosis: features and physiotherapy

MS: chronic immune-mediated demyelination of the CNS, lesions scattered in space and time; average onset 32 years; women twice as often.

Features: optic neuritis, double vision; numbness, Lhermitte's sign; spastic weakness of the legs; ataxia, intention tremor; fatigue; bladder urgency; cognitive and mood changes; heat sensitivity (Uhthoff's).

Physiotherapy

  • Principle: exercise is safe and helpful at every level (Kalb 2020); grade to EDSS and fatigue; keep cool.
  • Aerobic (EDSS 0–6.5): 2–3 times a week, 10–30 min, moderate intensity; resistance 2–3 times a week, 1–3 sets of 8–15.
  • Balance and gait: static and dynamic balance, stepping, turning; AFO, walking aids.
  • Spasticity: daily stretching, standing, positioning; check triggers (bladder, skin, infection).
  • Ataxia and tremor: proximal stability, Frenkel's, weighted cuffs.
  • Fatigue: plan, prioritise, pace; rest breaks. Heat: pre-cooling, cooling vest.
  • Severe (EDSS 7–9): short daily sessions, arm exercise, standing frame, transfers, seating, carer training.
  • Bladder: timed voiding, pelvic floor training. Work adjustments.

A new symptom lasting over 24 hours without fever or heat may be a relapse: refer.
