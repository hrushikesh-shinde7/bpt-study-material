Classification of head injury

BasisTypes
Severity (GCS)Mild 13–15; moderate 9–12; severe 3–8
MechanismClosed (blunt) or open (penetrating); acceleration–deceleration; blast
SkullLinear, depressed or basal fracture (CSF leak, raccoon eyes)
Focal lesionsExtradural haematoma, subdural haematoma, intracerebral haemorrhage, contusion
Diffuse lesionsConcussion, diffuse axonal injury, diffuse swelling
TimingPrimary (at impact); secondary (later: hypoxia, hypotension, raised ICP, seizures, infection)

Features (signs) of raised intracranial pressure

  • Symptoms: headache worse on waking, coughing and bending; vomiting (may be projectile); blurred vision.
  • Signs: falling level of consciousness (GCS); papilloedema; a dilating pupil on the side of the mass (third nerve); sixth nerve palsy; seizures; bulging fontanelle in infants.
  • Cushing's triad (late, dangerous): rising BP with wide pulse pressure, bradycardia, irregular breathing.

Normal ICP is about 5–15 mmHg; treat above 22 mmHg; aim for CPP 60–70 mmHg (CPP = MAP − ICP).

Physiotherapy in the acute stage of traumatic brain injury

Principles: protect the injured brain (keep within ICP and CPP limits), prevent secondary complications, match treatment to the level of consciousness, involve the family.

Before each session: GCS, pupils, ICP and CPP, BP, oxygen, sedation, ventilator settings, skull precautions, other fractures.

AreaPhysiotherapy
PositioningHead of bed up about 30°, head in midline; avoid neck flexion and rotation (jugular compression); side lying and supported positions to reduce abnormal postures; turn regularly
Chest carePositioning, gentle manual techniques, short suctioning with pre-oxygenation; no head-down drainage while ICP is raised
Range and toneDaily passive ROM of all joints; splints or serial casting of ankles and wrists when tone rises
ArousalSitting over the bed edge and tilt table once ICP is controlled; short periods of meaningful input (family voices, music, touch); avoid overstimulation
SkinTurning schedule, pressure-relieving mattress
Agitation (Rancho IV)Quiet room, one therapist, short sessions, familiar automatic tasks; low bed and mats instead of restraints
FamilyExplain the stages of recovery; teach positioning and range

Stop if ICP rises and stays above the limit, CPP falls, GCS drops or a pupil dilates. After craniectomy, protect the skull defect.
