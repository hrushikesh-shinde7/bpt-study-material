Diagnostic criteria of motor neuron disease

Gold Coast criteria (2020):

  1. Progressive motor impairment after previously normal function, shown by history or repeated examination.
  2. UMN and LMN dysfunction in at least one body region, or LMN dysfunction in at least two regions.
  3. Investigations exclude other diseases (MRI, blood tests).

Older revised El Escorial criteria graded ALS as definite, probable or possible by the number of regions (bulbar, cervical, thoracic, lumbosacral) with UMN and LMN signs. EMG (fibrillations, fasciculations) shows LMN involvement even in normal-looking muscles; sensory studies are normal.

Pseudobulbar palsy

Pseudobulbar palsy: UMN weakness of muscles supplied by cranial nerves IX–XII, from bilateral corticobulbar tract lesions (bilateral strokes, MND, MS).

Classify motor neuron disease with features

MND: progressive degeneration of upper motor neurons (cortex) and lower motor neurons (brainstem, anterior horn), with sensation, eye movements and bladder spared; onset usually after 50; 10% familial.

1. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, about 70%)

  • UMN + LMN together: wasting, fasciculations, cramps with spasticity, brisk reflexes and Babinski in the same limb.
  • Often starts in one hand ("split hand": thenar wasting more than hypothenar) or foot drop.

2. Progressive bulbar palsy

  • Brainstem motor nuclei: slurred nasal speech, dysphagia, drooling, wasted fasciculating tongue; often pseudobulbar (UMN) features too. Worst outlook (aspiration, malnutrition).

3. Progressive muscular atrophy

  • LMN only: wasting, fasciculations, weakness, lost reflexes; usually starts distally.

4. Primary lateral sclerosis

  • UMN only: slowly progressive spasticity (legs first), brisk reflexes; best outlook.

Course: neck extensor weakness (head drop), breathing weakness (first at night), weight loss; median survival about 3 years. Function scale: ALSFRS-R (0–48).

Physiotherapy in one line: non-fatiguing exercise early; orthoses and aids in the middle stage; chest care, positioning and carer training late.

Physiotherapy for a terminally ill patient with MND

Aims: comfort, dignity, prevent painful complications, ease breathlessness and secretions, support the carers; follow the person's wishes and advance care plan with the palliative team.

  • Comfort and positioning: supported positions with pillows, profiling bed, pressure-relieving mattress; regular turning.
  • Range of motion: gentle passive movements to prevent painful stiff joints, especially shoulders.
  • Breathlessness: upright or forward-lean positions; a hand-held fan; breathing control and relaxation; support NIV use and mask comfort.
  • Secretions: positioning, gentle airway clearance, manually assisted cough or cough-assist machine, suction as needed.
  • Equipment: hoist, slide sheets, tilt-in-space wheelchair, head support and neck collar.
  • Aspiration: upright for meals and 30 minutes after.
  • Carers: safe handling and positioning, pressure care; emotional support for the family.

No strengthening or fatiguing exercise at this stage; treatment is about comfort, not cure.
