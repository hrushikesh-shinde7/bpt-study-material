Ankle-foot orthosis

  • Parts: calf section with strap, ankle section (solid or hinged), footplate; worn in a shoe.
  • Principle: three-point force system controls the ankle (footplate lifts the forefoot, strap over the front of the ankle, calf shell behind); through the ground reaction force it also controls the knee.
  • Uses: foot drop (stroke, peroneal injury, MS), equinus and spasticity, ankle instability, crouch gait, knee hyperextension.
  • Types: posterior leaf spring, solid, hinged with plantarflexion stop, ground-reaction, supramalleolar.

Orthosis used for radial nerve injury

Radial palsy gives wrist, finger and thumb drop, so grip is weak.

  • Static cock-up splint at rest and night: holds the wrist in 20–30° extension, ending at the distal palmar crease so the fingers move freely; prevents overstretching of the extensors.
  • Dynamic cock-up (lively) splint by day: wrist support plus an outrigger with elastic slings under the proximal phalanges and thumb; the patient flexes actively to grip and the elastics re-extend the fingers, so grasp and release are possible.

A forearm splint with the wrist extended and an outrigger over the knuckles with finger slings.

Dynamic cock-up splint: uses and principle

  • Uses: radial nerve palsy (wrist, finger and thumb drop); after extensor tendon repair; after tendon transfers for radial palsy.
  • Principle: the splint replaces the paralysed extensors. A static wrist support keeps the wrist extended so the flexors grip strongly (tenodesis advantage); elastic slings on an outrigger return the fingers and thumb to extension after each active grasp.
  • Benefits: allows function (grasp and release), prevents overstretching of extensors, stops the flexors shortening.

Cock-up splint

  • Description: a forearm-based (volar) static splint holding the wrist in about 20–30° of extension (neutral for carpal tunnel), from two-thirds of the forearm to the distal palmar crease, clear of the thumb, so the fingers and thumb move freely; straps at forearm, wrist and palm.
  • Uses: radial palsy (rest and night); carpal tunnel syndrome (neutral, at night); wrist fractures, arthritis, spastic wrist.
  • Making: paper pattern → low-temperature thermoplastic heated at 65–70 °C → mould over stockinette → trim, pad, straps → check fit.
  • Care: check the skin; keep away from heat.
