Glasgow Coma Scale: components, interpretation and uses

Eye opening (E 1–4)4 spontaneous, 3 to sound, 2 to pressure, 1 none
Verbal (V 1–5)5 oriented, 4 confused, 3 words, 2 sounds, 1 none
Motor (M 1–6)6 obeys, 5 localises, 4 normal flexion, 3 abnormal flexion, 2 extension, 1 none

Total 3–15: severe 3–8 (coma; airway at risk), moderate 9–12, mild 13–15. Report the parts (E2 V2 M4 = 8); the motor score predicts outcome best; "T" if intubated.

Used in: head injury, stroke, meningitis and encephalitis, raised ICP, overdose; serial scores show deterioration.

Modified Ashworth Scale

Grades resistance to passive movement (spasticity) with the patient relaxed, moving the part through full range in about one second.

0No increase in tone
1Slight increase: catch and release, or minimal resistance at the end of range
1+Slight increase: catch, then minimal resistance through less than half the remaining range
2More marked increase through most of the range, part still moves easily
3Considerable increase; passive movement difficult
4Rigid in flexion or extension

Quick and clinical, but does not separate neural from stiffness components (the Tardieu scale does).

ASIA scale

The ASIA Impairment Scale grades completeness of spinal cord injury from the ISNCSCI examination (key muscles and dermatomes, plus S4–S5).

  • A, complete: no sensory or motor function in S4–S5.
  • B, sensory incomplete: sensation but no motor function below the level, including S4–S5.
  • C, motor incomplete: motor function below the level, but less than half the key muscles below the neurological level are grade 3 or more.
  • D, motor incomplete: half or more of those key muscles are grade 3 or more.
  • E, normal: normal sensation and power in a person who had deficits.

Use: classification, prognosis (incomplete injuries recover more), goals.

Hoehn and Yahr scale

Stages the severity of Parkinson's disease by spread of signs and balance.

  1. Stage 1: one side only.
  2. Stage 2: both sides, balance normal.
  3. Stage 3: both sides, some postural instability; physically independent.
  4. Stage 4: severe disability; still walks or stands without help.
  5. Stage 5: wheelchair-bound or bedridden unless helped.

The modified scale adds 1.5 (one side plus trunk) and 2.5 (both sides, recovers on the pull test). Use: describe stage, guide goals (balance and falls work from stage 3).
