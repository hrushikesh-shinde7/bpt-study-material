Gross and fine motor milestones up to 5 years

Principles: sequence is fixed, rate varies; cephalocaudal (head before feet), proximodistal (trunk before fingers), general to specific; use corrected age for preterm babies until 2 years.

Normal ranges are wide: walking alone anywhere between about 8 and 18 months is normal (WHO 2006). Red flags: loss of skills, asymmetry, hand preference before 12 months.

Asymmetrical tonic neck reflex (ATNR)

  • Elicit: child supine, head in midline; turn the head to one side.
  • Response: arm (and leg) on the face side extend, those on the skull side flex: "fencing" posture.
  • Normal: birth to about 6 months; then integrated so hands can come to midline and the child can roll.
  • Abnormal: obligatory at any age or persisting beyond 6 months (CP): cannot bring hands together or to mouth, cannot roll or sit symmetrically, poor hand–eye coordination, asymmetry with risk of scoliosis and hip problems.
  • PT: midline positioning (side lying, symmetrical seating, toys in front).

Any one primitive reflex: the Moro reflex

  • Elicit: let the supported head drop back suddenly a few centimetres.
  • Response: arms abduct and extend with hands opening, then adduct and flex (embrace), often with a cry.
  • Normal: birth to 3–4 months (up to 6).
  • Abnormal: absent (severe brain depression, hypotonia); asymmetrical (brachial plexus palsy, fractured clavicle or humerus, hemiplegia); persisting beyond 6 months (CP).

Cortical reflexes

Highest level of the classic reflex hierarchy (Fiorentino): equilibrium (tilting) reactions, which appear once the child can get into each position and persist for life.

  • Equilibrium reactions in prone and supine (about 6 months).
  • In four-point kneeling and sitting (about 7–8 months).
  • In kneel-standing and standing (second year).
  • Hopping and dorsiflexion reactions in standing.

Response to a tilt: trunk curves towards the higher side; limbs on the higher side abduct and extend. The hierarchy is a teaching model; skills are trained directly.

Parachute reactions

  • Elicit: hold the child prone around the trunk and move suddenly head-first towards a surface.
  • Response: both arms extend and abduct, hands open, to protect the head.
  • Timing: forward at about 6–9 months, then sideways and backwards; persists for life.
  • Abnormal: absent, delayed or asymmetrical (hemiplegic CP, brachial plexus palsy, motor delay): sitting and standing are unsafe.
  • PT: train on a ball, roll or tilt board.
