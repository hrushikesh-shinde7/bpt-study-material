Gowers' sign

The way a child with weak hip and knee extensors rises from the floor:

  1. Turns face down and gets onto hands and knees.
  2. Hands and feet with the hips high.
  3. Walks the hands up the knees and thighs ("climbing up his own legs").
  4. Pushes the trunk upright.

Shows proximal lower-limb weakness: typical of DMD, also other myopathies and SMA.

Gowers' sign and gait deviations in DMD

Gowers' sign

Rising from the floor by turning prone, getting onto hands and feet with hips high, then walking the hands up the thighs to push the trunk upright, because hip and knee extensors are weak.

Gait deviations

  • Waddling (Trendelenburg) gait: pelvis drops and trunk sways over the stance leg (weak hip abductors).
  • Increased lumbar lordosis with shoulders held back: weak hip extensors, so the line of gravity is kept behind the hips.
  • Wide base for balance.
  • Toe walking (equinus): tight calves and weak dorsiflexors; no heel strike.
  • Knee locked back in stance as quadriceps weaken.
  • Slow, short steps; arms behind the trunk; frequent falls; cannot run or jump.

Calves look big but are weak (pseudohypertrophy: fat and fibrous tissue).

Duchenne muscular dystrophy: features, principles and physiotherapy

DMD: X-linked recessive absence of dystrophin; boys; onset 2–5 years: late walking, falls, difficulty running and climbing stairs, toe walking, calf pseudohypertrophy, Gowers' sign, waddling gait, lordosis; loss of walking about 10–14 years; then scoliosis, respiratory failure, cardiomyopathy. CK very high.

Principles

  • Preserve walking and range as long as possible; keep active without overworking fragile muscle; prevent deformity and falls; support breathing; keep independent and included.
  • Avoid eccentric and heavy resistance exercise; stop before fatigue.

By stage

  • Early ambulatory: home stretching 4–6 times a week; night AFOs; submaximal aerobic play (swimming, cycling); buggy for long distances; fall prevention; parent education.
  • Late ambulatory: serial casting; KAFOs with locked knees to prolong standing and walking; manual wheelchair; standing frame.
  • Early non-ambulatory: power wheelchair with custom seating; supported standing; arm stretching; breathing exercises; spine checks.
  • Late non-ambulatory: seating and pressure care; lung volume recruitment and assisted cough; arm supports; hoist transfers.

Dark urine or next-day pain means the activity was too hard. Never stop steroids suddenly.
