Physiotherapy in acute-stage Guillain-Barré syndrome

GBS: acute immune-mediated polyradiculoneuropathy: rapidly ascending, symmetrical, flaccid weakness with areflexia, worst within 4 weeks; breathing, cranial and autonomic nerves may be involved (up to 22% need a ventilator).

Aims in the ICU (progression and plateau)

Protect breathing, prevent the complications of immobility, monitor the autonomic system, relieve pain, reassure.

Do not overstretch flaccid joints; support the shoulders; upright for meals if swallowing is weak; protect the eyes if the face is weak.

Physiotherapy management of Guillain-Barré syndrome

Principles: watch breathing and autonomic system; prevent immobility complications; exercise within the person's strength; never overwork recovering muscles; plan return to home and work.

1. Acute (ICU)

  • Chest care: positioning, deep breathing, airway clearance, assisted cough; monitor VC or single breath count.
  • Passive ROM, resting splints (wrist, ankle), turning, padding of elbows and fibular heads.
  • Tilt table and sitting once stable.

2. Early recovery (ward)

  • Active-assisted then active exercise, gravity eliminated first.
  • Sitting balance, rolling, bridging, sit to stand, standing in parallel bars; short sessions with rests.

3. Rehabilitation

  • Progressive resistance, few repetitions, rests between sets; stop before fatigue.
  • Gait training: parallel bars → walker → stick; AFO for foot drop.
  • Balance with visual feedback when position sense is poor.
  • Endurance: cycling 30 minutes at 70–90% HRmax, 3 times a week for 12 weeks (Garssen 2004); fatigue management (pacing).
  • Hand function; return to school or work in stages.

Overwork rule: if weakness or tiredness is worse the next day, the session was too hard: shorten it or lower the load. About 80% walk independently at 6 months.
