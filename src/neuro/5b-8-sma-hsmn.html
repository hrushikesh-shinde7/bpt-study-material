1Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)

Definition and types

  • SMA is an inherited (autosomal recessive) loss of anterior horn cells, causing symmetrical, proximal, lower motor neuron weakness. About 94% have deletion of both copies of the SMN1 gene (exon 7) on chromosome 5; the number of copies of the back-up gene SMN2 modifies severity.
  • About 1 in 10,000 live births; about 1 in 40–50 people carry it.

Key features

  • Symmetrical weakness and low tone, legs more than arms and proximal more than distal; absent reflexes; tongue fasciculations; tremor of the hands (minipolymyoclonus).
  • Normal sensation and intelligence; the face is usually spared.
  • Weak intercostals with a working diaphragm: bell-shaped chest and belly breathing; weak cough, chest infections.
  • Scoliosis, contractures, hip subluxation, feeding difficulty (type 1).

Medical point that matters for physiotherapy

  • Diagnosis by genetic testing (SMN1 deletion), increasingly through newborn screening.
  • Disease-modifying drugs have transformed SMA: nusinersen (intrathecal, 2016), onasemnogene abeparvovec (one-time gene therapy, 2019) and risdiplam (oral, 2020). Treated babies, especially before symptoms, can reach milestones never seen before, so physiotherapy now aims at gaining skills, not only preventing complications.
  • Cost and access remain major barriers in India.

Physiotherapy by functional level (SMA standards of care, 2018)

LevelFocus
Non-sittersPositioning and supported seating, gentle range of motion, splints, chest physiotherapy and airway clearance, cough assist and NIV as advised, developmental play
SittersSeating and a wheelchair (often powered), spinal support, standing frame, orthoses, active play and arm function, contracture and scoliosis prevention, respiratory care
WalkersStrength, endurance and balance training without exhaustion, aquatic exercise, falls prevention, orthoses, a wheelchair for long distances

Precautions

  • Avoid exhausting exercise; watch breathing (night-time breathing problems, weak cough).
  • Fragile bones: gentle handling.

2Hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease)

Definition and types

  • The commonest inherited neuropathy (about 1 in 2500 people): slowly progressive damage to the longest motor and sensory nerves.
  • CMT1 (HSMN I): demyelinating, with very slow nerve conduction; CMT1A (PMP22 gene duplication) is the commonest type.
  • CMT2 (HSMN II): axonal, with near-normal conduction velocity but small responses.
  • Usually autosomal dominant, starting in childhood or adolescence.

Key features

  • Distal weakness and wasting of the legs ("inverted champagne bottle" calves), then the hands.
  • High-arched feet (pes cavus) with clawed toes; foot drop with a high-stepping gait; ankle sprains and falls.
  • Reduced or absent ankle reflexes; distal sensory loss (glove and stocking); thickened nerves in some.

3 mClassify spinal muscular atrophy.

Type 0 (prenatal, severe); type 1 Werdnig–Hoffmann (onset before 6 months, never sits); type 2 (6–18 months, sits but never walks); type 3 Kugelberg–Welander (after 18 months, walks, may lose walking); type 4 (adult, mild). Caused by SMN1 deletion; SMN2 copies modify severity.

3 mClinical features of Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease.

Slowly progressive distal weakness and wasting of the legs (inverted champagne bottle), pes cavus and clawed toes, foot drop with high-stepping gait, absent ankle reflexes, distal sensory loss, later hand weakness; usually dominant inheritance, onset in childhood or adolescence.

7 mPhysiotherapy for a child with SMA type 2.

A sitter: supported seating and a powered wheelchair; spinal support and scoliosis monitoring; standing frame for bones and hips; orthoses; range of motion and stretching for hip, knee and ankle contractures; active play and arm function; airway clearance, cough assist and NIV as advised; aquatic play; family education; review gains if on disease-modifying drugs.

LAQPractice: a 15-year-old boy with CMT1A trips often, has high-arched feet and weak ankles, and wants to play cricket. Write impairments, goals and physiotherapy. (5 + 3 + 7)

Impairments (5): distal weakness (dorsiflexors, evertors, intrinsics), pes cavus and calf tightness, reduced ankle reflexes and distal sensation, poor balance, mild hand weakness. Goals (3): no trips at school, safe participation in cricket with an orthosis, maintained ankle range. Physiotherapy (7): carbon or leaf-spring AFOs and supportive footwear, calf stretching and night splints, proximal strengthening and moderate distal work, balance training, sport-specific agility with ankle support, foot care, and orthopaedic review for the cavus foot.
