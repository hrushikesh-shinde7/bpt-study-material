Apraxia (7m S23, S24, W24; 3m S22)

Apraxia: inability to carry out learned, purposeful movements on command despite adequate strength, sensation, coordination, comprehension and willingness; usually left parietal or frontal.

IdeomotorKnows what to do, cannot do on command or imitation (left parietal): cannot show combing, combs spontaneously
IdeationalLoses idea or sequence; misuses objects: toothpaste on the comb
Limb-kineticClumsy fine finger movement (premotor): cannot button, pick up a coin
ConstructionalCannot copy or build 2- or 3-dimensional shapes (parietal, either side): cube, blocks
DressingShirt inside out or one arm only (right parietal, with neglect)
BuccofacialCannot blow out a candle on command, does when lit (left frontal, with Broca's)
GaitFeet "glued to the floor"; leg movements normal in lying (frontal, hydrocephalus)

Assess: pantomime ("show me how you brush"), imitate gestures, real objects, multistep task (making tea); note wrong movement, body part as tool, wrong order, perseveration. PT: real tasks with real objects in natural setting; strategy training (talk through steps, step cards); errorless learning, chaining; physical guidance not long instructions; fade cues; gesture training (ideomotor).

Aphasia (3m S23, S25)

Loss of language (speak, understand, read, write), usually left hemisphere; dysarthria = motor speech, language normal.

  • Broca's (expressive, non-fluent): slow, effortful, telegraphic; comprehension fairly good; aware, frustrated; left inferior frontal; right arm weak.
  • Wernicke's (receptive, fluent): fluent but empty, made-up words; poor comprehension; unaware; left posterior superior temporal. Repetition poor in both.
  • Conduction: repetition very poor (arcuate fasciculus); global: all lost (large left MCA); anomic: word-finding; transcortical: repetition kept.

PT: short simple sentences, one instruction, yes or no questions; gestures, demonstration, pictures, guidance; check by action not "yes"; quiet room, time to respond; work with speech therapist.

Unilateral neglect (3m S19)

Failure to report, respond or orient to the side opposite the lesion, not from weakness or sensory loss; commonest after right parietal lesion. Eats half the plate, bumps left door frames, denies the arm. Vs hemianopia: hemianopia is aware and turns the head; neglect is unaware.

Tests: line bisection (shifted to good side); cancellation (star, letter, Albert's: targets missed); clock drawing, copying (half missing); reading (starts mid-line); observing ADL, Catherine Bergego Scale. PT: visual scanning with a coloured anchor on the neglected edge; approach from that side as tolerated; limb activation, trunk rotation to that side; prism adaptation, mirror (limited evidence); check limb in wheels and footplate.

Perception and cognition (3m W18)

Figure-ground: cannot pick an object from its background (white shirt on white bed, wheelchair brake, step edge; right parietal-occipital). Find item in cluttered drawer; reduce clutter, colour contrast tape, scanning, busier settings gradually.

Combined cortical sensations (primary sensation + parietal): stereognosis (coin, key, eyes closed); graphaesthesia (number on skin); two-point discrimination; localisation; barognosis, texture; bilateral simultaneous stimulation (extinction).

Others: body scheme (somatoagnosia, right-left, finger agnosia); spatial (depth, getting lost); agnosias, prosopagnosia; anosognosia. Cognition: MMSE (30; adjust for education), MoCA (30; better for mild, executive); short structured sessions, one-step instructions, errorless learning, memory aids, family.
