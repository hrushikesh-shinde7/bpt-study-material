Asked: not asked on its own. Expect 3 marks on FES, TENS types or the strength-duration curve, or 7 marks on electrotherapy in hemiplegia or nerve injury.

Uses in neurology

Pain: TENS. Muscle activation: NMES, FES. Feedback: EMG biofeedback. Denervated muscle: long-pulse stimulation (limited evidence). Electrodiagnosis: strength-duration curve (largely replaced by EMG and nerve conduction).

Neuro uses: painful hemiplegic shoulder (with positioning and movement), postural musculoskeletal pain, some neuropathic pain. Electrodes over the area or along the nerve; patient must feel the tingling.

NMES and FES

NMES: stimulates an innervated nerve for strength or re-education. FES: stimulation timed to a functional task (dorsiflexors in swing; wrist and finger extensors in grasp and release). Settings: 30–50 Hz; 200–300 µs (longer for big muscles); ramp up and down; on–off 1:3 to 1:5 at first; strong comfortable contraction; 20–30 min. Always with active effort and task practice.

EMG biofeedback

Surface electrodes show muscle activity as sound or light: increase activity in a weak muscle or reduce it in an overactive one. Early re-education after nerve injury or stroke (flicker before visible movement), relaxation, after tendon transfer. Add-on to exercise, not a replacement.

Denervated muscle and strength-duration curve

Denervated muscle responds only to long pulses (tens to hundreds of ms; interrupted direct or triangular); limited evidence; no benefit in Bell's palsy.

  • SD curve: current for a minimal contraction plotted against pulse duration.
  • Rheobase: smallest current that causes a contraction with a long pulse.
  • Chronaxie: pulse duration at twice the rheobase; under about 1 ms normally, much longer when denervated.
  • Denervation shifts the curve up and to the right; a kink = partial denervation; serial curves show reinnervation.

Precautions and contraindications

SCI at or above T6: stimulation below lesion can trigger autonomic dysreflexia: monitor BP, stop at once. Pacemakers, defibrillators (avoid chest); carotid sinus, front of neck, eyes; malignancy, infection, DVT, broken skin; abdomen or low back in pregnancy; head and neck in uncontrolled epilepsy. Numb skin or poor understanding: low intensity, frequent checks. Check skin before and after; explain the sensation.
