Definition and types (3m S21, W24; 7m S23)

MND: progressive degeneration of UMN and LMN (cortex, brainstem, anterior horns): weakness, wasting, spasticity, then breathing failure; sensation spared. 1–3 per 100,000 a year; after 50; ~10% familial (SOD1, C9orf72).

ALS (commonest)UMN + LMN: wasting, fasciculations with spasticity, brisk reflexes, Babinski; one hand or foot first
Progressive bulbar palsyBrainstem nuclei: slurred speech, dysphagia, wasted fasciculating tongue; worse outlook
Progressive muscular atrophyLMN only: wasting, fasciculations, lost reflexes
Primary lateral sclerosisUMN only: slow spasticity; best outlook

Criteria (3m S18): Gold Coast 2020: progressive motor loss after normal function; UMN + LMN in 1 region or LMN in 2; tests exclude others.

Features and pseudobulbar palsy (3m S19)

LMN loss → wasting, fasciculations, cramps; UMN loss → spasticity, brisk reflexes even in wasted limbs. Split hand; foot drop; head drop; night hypoventilation; eyes, sensation, bladder spared.

Investigations, medical care, assessment

EMG (denervation, normal sensory); MRI excludes myelopathy; FVC, night SpO₂. Riluzole (+2–3 months); NIV; gastrostomy; palliative care. PT: MRC, ALSFRS-R (0–48), grip, timed walk, Berg, FVC sitting and lying, cough peak flow.

ICF (W16: 29, teacher, sole earner, ALS 4 months)

  • Structure/function: shoulder, hand and leg weakness; fatigue (fewer motor units overworked); cramps, spasticity; check breathing.
  • Activity: writing on the board, standing through a lesson, long walks, buttoning.
  • Participation: full teaching days; family income at risk.
  • Environment: family, school may adapt (facilitators); blackboard, stairs, cost (barriers). Personal: sole earner; coping with diagnosis.

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): energy conservation, rests between lessons; lower whiteboard or projector, sits to teach; no falls. Long-term (3–6 months): keeps teaching with adaptations; self-care as long as possible; NIV and equipment in place before urgent. S16 dentist (split hand): thumb and wrist splints, thicker instrument handles.

PT by stage

  • Early: moderate, non-fatiguing strengthening of muscles grade 3 or more (few reps, rests); walking, cycling, swimming; daily ROM; pacing; work ergonomics.
  • Middle: AFO, wrist and thumb splints, neck collar; aids, then wheelchair; transfers; breathing, airway clearance; stretch for cramps.
  • Late: positioning, pressure care, passive ROM, assisted cough, NIV support, hoist, tilt-in-space chair, carer training.

Terminally ill (7m W25): comfort positions, profiling bed, turning; gentle passive ROM (shoulders); breathlessness: forward lean, hand-held fan, breathing control, NIV mask comfort; secretions: assisted cough, cough machine, suction; hoist, head support; respect wishes and advance care plan; palliative team.

Precautions and prognosis

Never to exhaustion (Cochrane 2013: moderate exercise no harm); no heavy eccentric work or strengthening below grade 3; stop if cramps or weakness rise next day; refer orthopnoea, morning headache; upright for meals + 30 min. Median survival ~3 years; bulbar onset faster, PLS slowest.
