Definition and causes

PD: slowly progressive loss of dopamine neurons of the substantia nigra; basal ganglia cannot control movement. Diagnosis: bradykinesia + rest tremor or rigidity (MDS 2015), plus non-motor problems. About 1% over 60. Parkinsonism is the picture; PD its commonest cause.

Other parkinsonism: drug-induced (haloperidol, metoclopramide); vascular (legs, little tremor); Parkinson-plus (PSP: early falls, poor vertical gaze; MSA: autonomic failure, ataxia), poor levodopa response. Risk: age; genes 5–10%; pesticides, rural living. Subtypes: tremor-dominant; PIGD: more falls, faster progress.

Hoehn and Yahr (3m W20)

1One side only; little or no disability (1.5: plus trunk)
2Both sides, balance normal (2.5: recovers on pull test)
3Both sides, mild–moderate; some postural instability; physically independent
4Severe disability; can still walk or stand unaided
5Wheelchair-bound or bedridden unless helped

Stage III = turning point: falls begin, still independent.

Basal ganglia (3m W21) and pathophysiology

  • Functions: select and start wanted movement, suppress unwanted; scale size, speed, force; run learned sequences automatically; motor learning, habits.
  • Nigral neurons die, Lewy bodies → ↓ striatal dopamine → thalamus and cortex over-inhibited → slow, small movements.
  • Automatic control lost → walking needs attention; worse with a second task; freezing. External cues supply the timing.
  • Non-motor signs may come years before motor signs.

Cardinal features (3m S25), rigidity (3m W19, S24)

TRAP

Tremor at rest, 4–6 per second, pill-rolling, absent in sleep. Rigidity whole range, flexors and extensors. Akinesia/bradykinesia: slow, hard to start, movements shrink; mask face, soft voice, small handwriting. Postural instability: later; falls back on pull test.

Lead-pipe: smooth, even, any speed. Cogwheel: ratchet (tremor on lead-pipe). Decorticate: arms flexed, legs extended; above red nucleus. Decerebrate: all extended, arms IR; brainstem (worse).

Gait (7m S16, W17) and other features

Flexed posture (line of gravity in front of feet); short shuffling steps, no heel strike; festination (shorter, faster steps chasing the centre of gravity; propulsion, retropulsion); ↓ arm swing and trunk rotation; en bloc turns; start hesitation and freezing (doorways, turns, talking); worse with dual task and "off" period.

Non-motor: loss of smell, constipation, acting out dreams, depression, dizziness on standing, urgency, later dementia; dysphagia.

Investigations and medicines

Clinical diagnosis; levodopa response supports; DaTscan if uncertain; MRI excludes vascular, NPH. Levodopa + carbidopa (most effective; later "off" periods, dyskinesia); agonists, MAO-B, COMT, amantadine; anticholinergics avoided in elderly; DBS. Physio: train in the "on" period; note last dose time.

PT assessment

Medicine timing, falls, freezing, ADL, home. H&Y; MDS-UPDRS III. Kyphosis, contractures, chest expansion, Five Times Sit to Stand. Mini-BESTest or Berg; pull test. 10 m walk, TUG ± dual task, 6MWT, freezing questionnaire. Lying and standing BP; PDQ-39.

ICF (W21: 65, retired teacher, akinesia, tremor, slow gait)

  • Structure/function: PD (H&Y II–III); akinesia, bradykinesia, rigidity, rest tremor, flexed posture, ↓ trunk rotation, balance (stage III), ↓ fitness.
  • Activity: slow short-stepped gait; chair rise, turning in bed, dressing, buttoning, writing, eating.
  • Participation: social, religious, community outings; tutoring.
  • Environment: family, medicines, exercise groups (facilitators); rugs, poor light, low seats, Indian toilet (barriers). Personal: educated, follows strategies.

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): chair rise in one attempt; longer steps with cue; more trunk rotation; better Mini-BESTest; exercises 3 days a week. Long-term (3–6 months): daily tasks without help; outdoors without cues; no falls; community class, keeps roles.

Principles and stages (stage III: 7m S26)

Exercise early and for life; big, fast, purposeful; cues and strategies bypass lost automatic control; real tasks; prevent falls; "on" period. APTA 2022 strong: aerobic, resistance, balance, gait, cueing, community exercise; weak: flexibility. Stages 1–2: fitness, community classes; 2–4: add cued gait, strategies, falls, posture, home; 5: positioning, pressure care, breathing, carer.

Aerobic≥3 days a week, 30–40 min, 60–75% to 75–85% max HR; use RPE
Resistance1–2 days a week; 80% 1RM strength, 40% 1RM fast for power
BalanceReactive steps, turning, dual task: 2–3 times a week, 16–30 h over 5–10 weeks
PostureTrunk extension and rotation, chest expansion; prone lying

Classic vs current: not gentle ROM only; moderate–high intensity is safe (SPARX 2018).

Gait training (7m W16, S17), bradykinesia and rigidity (7m S22)

  • Cues: floor stripes at step length; metronome, music; "big step". Attention to long steps, heel strike.
  • Arm swing; treadmill; Nordic poles; wide-arc turns; doorways; stops, starts. Festination: stop, stand tall, long step.
  • Dose: 20–60 min, 3–5 days a week, 4–12 weeks; cueing 2–5 times a week.
  • Freezing: stop, stand tall, shift weight; step over a line; count or march; target beyond doorway.
  • Cognitive strategies: chair rise in steps: front of seat, feet back, "nose over toes", push, stand tall.
  • Bradykinesia: LSVT BIG (16 one-hour sessions, 4 days a week, 4 weeks); speed, power.
  • Rigidity: slow rhythmic rotation; rhythmic initiation (passive → active); stretch pectorals, hip flexors, hamstrings; relaxation.

Precautions and fall prevention (4m S22)

Complications: falls (~60% yearly, 39% repeatedly), kyphosis, contractures, deconditioning, orthostatic hypotension, aspiration pneumonia, pressure sores (stage 5). Take care: train in "on" period; gait belt; lying and standing BP; RPE as HR may be blunted; dual tasks only when safe; no reclined exercise after meals. Falls: find risks; balance + leg strength; wide turns, freezing cues; home safety, footwear; stand slowly; teach floor rise.

Home management (5m S19) and prognosis

Remove rugs and clutter; lighting, switch at bed; grab bars; raised western toilet or commode; firm chairs with armrests; wide turning paths; floor stripes at doorways. Demanding tasks in "on" period; sit to dress; Velcro, elastic laces; weighted cutlery, non-slip mat; bed rope or rail. Daily programme + community class; medicines on time; fluids, fibre. Prognosis: slow; PIGD faster; falls, dysphagia, dementia mark late stages (death often pneumonia). PT in blocks with review.
