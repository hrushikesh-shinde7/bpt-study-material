Asked: 6 of 20 papers. 7 marks: gross and fine motor milestones to 5 years (W19), to 3 years (S21); gross motor to 5 years (S23). 3 marks: ATNR (S20, S21), any one primitive reflex (W20), cortical reflexes (W20), parachute reactions (W25).

Principles

Sequence same in all children, rate varies; continuous; cephalocaudal (head → sitting → standing); proximodistal (trunk → fingers); general to specific; primitive reflexes fade so milestones appear. Born before 37 weeks: corrected age (age minus weeks early) until about 2 years.

WHO (2006): walking alone is normal anywhere from about 8 to 18 months (median 12).

Primitive reflexes (3m W20: any one; ATNR 3m S20, S21)

Brainstem and spinal; integrated mostly by 4–6 m. Absent, asymmetrical, obligatory or persisting = brain damage (CP). Rooting, sucking to 3–4 m; palmar grasp ~6 m; plantar grasp ~15 m; stepping 2–3 m; placing ~1 y; Galant 2–4 m; STNR gone 9–12 m; Babinski normal to 1–2 y.

Moro: sudden drop of supported head → arms abduct, extend, hands open, then adduct and flex, often a cry; to 3–4 m (normal to 6). Absent: brain depression, hypotonia; asymmetrical: brachial plexus palsy, fractured clavicle or humerus, hemiplegia; after 6 m: CP.

ATNR: supine, head midline, turn head → face-side arm and leg extend, skull-side flex ("fencing"); integrated by ~6 m. Obligatory at any age or after 6 m = CP. Persistence: no hands to midline or mouth, cannot roll or sit symmetrically, poor hand–eye, scoliosis and hip risk, cannot cross midline. PT: midline positioning, toys in front.

Reactions: parachute (3m W25), cortical (3m W20)

Righting: head upright, body aligned (neck, body, labyrinthine, optical); Landau 3 m–2 y. Parachute: held prone, moved suddenly head-first → both arms extend and abduct, hands open; forward 6–9 m, for life, then sideways, backward. Absent or asymmetrical: hemiplegic CP, brachial plexus palsy, delay. Train on ball, roll, tilt board.

Levels (Fiorentino): spinal (withdrawal, extensor thrust, crossed extension) → brainstem (ATNR, STNR, tonic labyrinthine, supporting reactions) → midbrain (righting, protective) → cortical: equilibrium reactions in prone, supine, four-point, sitting, kneel-standing, standing + hopping, dorsiflexion; for life. Current: a teaching model; train skills directly.

Red flags and screening

Not by 6 m: rolls, pushes up on straight arms; 9 m: sits alone; 12 m: pulls to stand, pincer; 18 m: walks alone; 2 y: runs, kicks. Loss of skills; asymmetry; hand preference before 12–18 m; very low or high tone. Tools: Trivandrum chart, Denver II, AIMS (0–18 m), Bayley; Hammersmith, General Movements (early CP).

Exam plan

7 marks: principles (1) → gross motor table to the age asked (3) → fine motor (3) + one line of red flags. 3 marks: elicit, response, timing, abnormal. Draw: WHO windows chart; ATNR "fencing" figure. Underline: cephalocaudal, sequence constant, corrected age, neck holding 3 months, sits alone 8 months, walks alone 12–15 months, pincer, obligatory ATNR, equilibrium reactions.
