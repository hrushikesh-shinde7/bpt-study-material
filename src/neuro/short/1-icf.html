The ICF model

ICF (International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health; WHO, 2001): health and disability as an interaction between a health condition and contextual factors. Biopsychosocial: joins the medical model (disease → disability) and the social model (disability created by society and environment). Functioning (body functions, activities, participation) vs disability (impairments, activity limitations, participation restrictions). Principles: universality, parity, neutrality, environmental influence.

Components and domains (7m S19)

Body functions (b)Mental; sensory, pain; voice, speech; cardiovascular, haematological, immunological, respiratory; digestive, metabolic, endocrine; genitourinary, reproductive; neuromusculoskeletal and movement; skin (power b730, tone b735, gait pattern b770)
Body structures (s)Matching 8: nervous system; eye, ear; voice, speech; cardiovascular, respiratory; digestive; genitourinary; structures related to movement; skin (brain, cord, nerve)
Activities and participation (d)Learning; general tasks; communication; mobility; self-care; domestic life; relationships; major life areas (work); community life (transferring d420, walking d450)
Environmental (e)Products, technology; natural and built; support, relationships; attitudes; services, policies (aids e120, family e310)
PersonalNot classified: age, sex, education, coping, habits, motivation

Qualifiers: 0 no, 1 mild, 2 moderate, 3 severe, 4 complete (8 not specified, 9 not applicable); b730.3 = severe weakness; e120.2 moderate barrier, e120+2 moderate facilitator.

Relationships, factors, capacity

Two-way: spasticity limits walking; not walking → weakness, contracture (secondary impairments); lost job → low mood → fatigue; ramp helps, stairs hinder; motivation changes practice. Use: treat at several levels at once; predict secondary problems.

Facilitators: walking aid, orthosis, rails, western commode; ground floor, lift, ramps; joint family, supportive employer; schemes, disability certificate; young, motivated. Barriers: Indian-style toilet; stairs, uneven roads; living alone; overprotection, stigma; cost, distance, lost income; older, depressed.

Capacity: best in a standard environment, no help (clinic). Performance: what is actually done at home with real aids. Capacity above performance → environment is a barrier. Walks 50 m in clinic, never leaves first-floor flat → stair training and a rail.

Goals and documentation

From what matters to patient and family; SMART; short-term (2–6 weeks): impairments, basic activities; long-term (months): activities, participation; ICF reason for each. "Improve balance" → Berg 38 to 46 in 6 weeks to cook safely; "improve walking" → 200 m outdoors with a stick in under 4 min within 8 weeks to reach the bus stop. Document: each level with measures, functional diagnosis, goals, dates; ICF Core Sets (stroke, SCI, MS, CP).

Red flags: stop, refer

Face droop, speech loss → stroke; vomiting, falling GCS, unequal pupils → raised ICP; saddle numbness, new bladder loss → cauda equina; spreading weakness, weak cough → GBS, myasthenic crisis; headache, high BP in SCI T6 or above → autonomic dysreflexia; calf swelling → DVT; neck stiffness, fever → meningitis; night pain, weight loss → tumour.
