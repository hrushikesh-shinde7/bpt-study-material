Horner's syndrome (3m S23)

Definition: loss of sympathetic supply to one side of the face and eye. Same side: mild ptosis, miosis (clearest in dim light; still reacts to light), anhidrosis of the face, apparent enophthalmos.

NeuronCourse; causes
1st (central)Hypothalamus → brainstem → cord to T1; lateral medullary stroke, syringomyelia, cervical SCI, MS
2nd (preganglionic)T1 root over lung apex → superior cervical ganglion; T1 avulsion (Klumpke's), Pancoast tumour, neck surgery
3rd (postganglionic)Along internal carotid to eye; carotid dissection, cluster headache, cavernous sinus

Physio link: Horner's in a plexus injury = T1 root torn from the cord → poor recovery of the hand (brachial plexus).

Orthostatic hypotension

Definition (2011): sustained fall of ≥20 mmHg systolic or ≥10 mmHg diastolic within 3 minutes of standing or head-up tilt. Causes: SCI at or above T6, PD and MSA, diabetic and autonomic neuropathies, GBS, bed rest, drugs (BP tablets, levodopa, diuretics). Symptoms: dizziness, greying vision, yawning, nausea, fainting; tetraplegia: few symptoms despite big falls.

PT for orthostatic hypotension

  • Measure BP lying and after sitting or standing; record symptoms.
  • Gradual uprighting: head of bed up → tilt table or sitting, raise angle in small steps as BP allows.
  • Reduce pooling: abdominal binder, compression stockings, ankle pumps before rising.
  • Slow changes; sit on bed edge first; fluids as advised; no heat or big meals before exercise.
  • Counter-manoeuvres: cross legs, tense leg and buttock muscles, squat.
  • Symptoms: stop, recline or lower tilt, raise legs, check BP.

Dysreflexia and other problems

Autonomic dysreflexia: SCI at or above T6; stimulus below lesion (most often full bladder) → sudden high BP, pounding headache, flushing and sweating above lesion, slow pulse. Sit up, loosen clothing, check bladder and bowel, monitor BP.

  • Poikilothermia (high SCI): no sweating or shivering below lesion; cool room, avoid heat and cold.
  • GBS dysautonomia: rhythm and BP swings. Brain injury: paroxysmal sympathetic hyperactivity (fast HR, high BP, fever, sweating, posturing): calm handling.
  • Bladder and bowel: bladder and bowel; CRPS: neuropathies.

C6 tetraplegia (7m practice): binder, stockings, gradual tilt; dysreflexia steps, no tight straps, check catheter; cool room; slow HR with suction; bladder and bowel routine first; teach carers.
