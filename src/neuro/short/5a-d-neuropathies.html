Diabetic neuropathy: types (7m W17, W19, W22, S23)

India: about 101 million adults with diabetes (11.4%). Long poor control damages nerves and their blood supply; longest nerves fail first, so it starts in the toes and rises.

Group (ADA 2017)Types
DiffuseDistal symmetric polyneuropathy (commonest, glove and stocking; small, large-fibre or mixed); autonomic (HR, BP, gut, bladder, sexual, sweating)
MononeuropathyOne cranial or limb nerve (third cranial, median, ulnar, peroneal); mononeuritis multiplex
RadiculopathyLumbosacral radiculoplexus (diabetic amyotrophy: painful thigh weakness, wasting); thoracic

Diabetic: features and the diabetic foot

  • Sensory: numbness, tingling, burning, "walking on cotton wool"; loss of protective sensation; poor position sense, balance worse in the dark.
  • Motor: weak foot intrinsics → toes claw, slippers slip off, pressure under metatarsal heads.
  • Autonomic: dry, cracked skin; orthostatic hypotension; resting tachycardia.
  • Diabetic foot: neuropathy + PAD + deformity → ulcer, infection, amputation. Hot, swollen, painless foot = Charcot foot.

Investigations (3m in S16) and risk

Blood sugar, HbA1c; 10 g monofilament, 128 Hz tuning fork; NCS; foot pulses, ankle-brachial index; B12 (low on metformin), kidney, thyroid. Wagner ulcer grades 0–5 (0 at-risk, 1 superficial, 2 deep, 3 abscess or bone, 4 part gangrene, 5 whole foot). Control sugar, BP, lipids; stop smoking; plantar ulcer: total contact cast or non-removable walker.

ICF (S16: 50, diabetic 8 years, numb feet, slippers slip off)

  • Structure/function: LOPS and lost position sense in both feet; weak intrinsics and toes; possible clawing, high-pressure areas; poor balance.
  • Activity: unsteady walking, worse in the dark and on uneven ground; footwear falls off; fall risk.
  • Participation: housework, market, religious outings, family roles.
  • Environment: loose slippers, barefoot at home (barriers); family, diabetes clinic (facilitators).

Goals: short-term: daily foot-care routine, proper footwear, safer walking, no new skin damage. Long-term: no ulcers or falls, walks daily, good sugar control.

Diabetic foot PT

  • Ulcer risk: daily inspection with a mirror; wash and dry between toes, moisturise, nail and callus care; protective footwear with a back strap; no barefoot walking, no socks-only or thin slippers; screen by risk category.
  • Foot-ankle exercises (toe flexion and extension, ankle range and strength), preferably supervised: may lower plantar pressure.
  • Balance: static → dynamic, with vision → varied surfaces; good home lighting.
  • Activity: walking in proper footwear, building up to an extra 1000 steps a day; cycling or swimming if an ulcer is present. Foot risk is not a barrier if footwear is right (IWGDF 2023).
  • Ulcer: offloading (cast, walker), crutches or wheelchair; keep the rest of the body active. Pain: graded activity, TENS trial.

Precautions: neuropathic feet

No heat, ice or electrical treatment on numb feet unchecked; check feet after every session, stop weight-bearing for a new red area or blister; check sugar before exercise, carry sugar; watch orthostatic hypotension; hot swollen foot → refer the same day (Charcot or infection).

Leprosy (W25 case; 7m W22)

Mycobacterium leprae infection of skin and peripheral nerves. India: about 59% of world new cases (2023). WHO: paucibacillary (1–5 lesions, smear negative); multibacillary (more than 5, nerve involvement or smear positive). Ridley–Jopling: tuberculoid → borderline → lepromatous.

  • Features: hypopigmented or reddish anaesthetic patches; thickened nerves (ulnar at elbow, common peroneal at fibular neck, posterior tibial, median, facial, great auricular). Claw hand, foot drop, clawed toes, plantar ulcers, lagophthalmos.
  • Lepromatous (W22): many symmetrical lesions and nodules, leonine face, lost eyebrows, collapsed nose, glove-and-stocking anaesthesia, symmetrical nerve thickening, eye damage; smears strongly positive.
  • Leprous neuritis (W22): painful, tender, swollen nerve with new sensory or motor loss, often in a reaction (type 1 reversal; type 2 erythema nodosum leprosum); silent neuritis = loss without pain.
  • Disability grade: 0 none; 1 anaesthesia only; 2 visible deformity.
  • Medical (WHO 2018): rifampicin, dapsone, clofazimine for 6 months (PB) or 12 months (MB); steroids and rest for neuritis; tendon transfers once controlled.

Leprosy: W25 reasoning and PT

W25 (42, labourer, sole earner, slum): leprosy, probably multibacillary, with ulnar and median neuropathy: patches + sensory loss + weak numb hands; check nerve thickening at elbows and wrists; confirm by smear; rule out diabetic, hereditary, cervical root. Impairments: intrinsic weakness, early claw, dry numb skin; activity: grip, tools, burns; participation: work, income, stigma; free MDT (facilitator), slum, hot or sharp tools (barriers).

  • Monitor: voluntary muscle and monofilament testing of hands, feet, eyes every visit (catch new damage for steroids).
  • Acute neuritis: rest in a splint, no exercise of the inflamed nerve; refer for steroids; then gentle range.
  • Claw hand: knuckle-bender, finger stretch, intrinsics; before and after tendon transfer.
  • Self-care: soak, scrape, oil; daily inspection; padded handles, gloves. Foot: AFO, microcellular rubber footwear, short walks, ulcer care.
  • Work and stigma: tool changes, vocational training, family counselling; not spread by touch, treated patients soon non-infectious.

Alcoholic neuropathy (7m S25)

Length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy from heavy drinking + poor nutrition (thiamine). Burning feet, glove and stocking numbness, lost ankle jerks, sensory ataxia (worse in the dark), distal weakness, foot drop. Others: B12, kidney, hypothyroid, isoniazid, chemotherapy, HIV, CMT. Medical: stop alcohol, thiamine, diet; slow, partial recovery.

PT: balance with visual feedback, stick, lighting, Frenkel's exercises; progressive ankle and hand strengthening, AFO; graded activity, TENS; foot inspection and footwear; fitness, support to stay off alcohol. Watch withdrawal, low sugar, liver disease.

Neuropathic pain (7m W21) and CRPS (7m S21)

Neuropathic pain (IASP): pain from a lesion or disease of the somatosensory system: burning, shooting, allodynia, hyperalgesia (diabetes, post-herpetic, SCI, stroke, phantom). Drugs: gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, amitriptyline. PT: explain pain (hurt is not harm); graded activity with pacing (no boom-and-bust); desensitisation; sensory discrimination, graded motor imagery, mirror; TENS; relaxation, sleep.

CRPS (RSD): pain out of proportion after injury or immobilisation (wrist fracture); type I no nerve injury, type II (causalgia) with one. Budapest: symptoms in 3 of 4, signs in 2 of 4 groups: sensory, vasomotor, sudomotor and oedema, motor and trophic. PT: early gentle use; graded motor imagery (left/right → imagined → mirror); desensitisation; oedema (elevation, pumping, retrograde massage); graded exercise, return to work; team. No forced painful stretching; no long splinting.
