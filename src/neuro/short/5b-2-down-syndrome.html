Definition and types

Trisomy 21: extra chromosome 21; typical features, intellectual disability (usually moderate, IQ 35–50), low tone, delay, medical problems; commonest chromosomal cause of intellectual disability. India about 1 in 1000 births. Risk: maternal age over 35, previous child, parent with balanced translocation.

Non-disjunctionAbout 96%; fails to separate, usually in the egg; maternal age
Translocation3–4%; parent may carry it, higher recurrence
Mosaicism1–2%; some cells only, often milder

Why they move late

Smaller brain, cerebellum and brainstem → low tone, poor postural control, slow reactions and motor learning. Collagen → lax ligaments: hypermobility, flat feet, unstable patellae and hips, atlantoaxial instability. Child widens the base and locks joints. Heart defects, sleep apnoea, chest infections ↓ endurance; obesity.

Traits and impairments (7m S23)

Physical traits: brachycephaly; flat face and nasal bridge; upslanting eyes, epicanthic folds; Brushfield spots; small mouth, protruding tongue; small low-set ears; excess neck skin; short broad hands, single palmar crease, clinodactyly; sandal gap, flat feet; short stature; lax joints.

Neurological: hypotonia, weakness; intellectual disability, speech delay; milestones: sits ~10 months, crawls ~14, walks ~26 months (slowest ~40); poor balance, slow reactions, poor motor planning; gait wide base, out-turned feet, knees locked, little heel strike; hearing loss (75%), vision (60–80%), seizures (1–13%), autism (7–19%), early Alzheimer's.

Medical: heart disease 40–50% (AV septal defect); sleep apnoea 50–79%; thyroid up to 50%; hip 2–8%; symptomatic atlantoaxial instability 1–2%; gut atresias 12%.

Investigations and assessment

Karyotype; echo; hearing, vision, thyroid. Neck X-ray (neutral) only if symptoms: no routine screening (AAP 2022). PT: heart status, neck symptoms; milestones, AIMS, GMFM, Peabody; tone, Beighton, strength; balance, gait; feet, hips; endurance.

PT management (7m S20, W25)

  • Developmental sequence: head and trunk control → sitting → crawling → pull to stand → cruising → walking. Infancy: tummy time, midline, weight on arms; avoid frog position.
  • Treadmill stepping (parent supports) from sitting alone: 8 min a day, 5 days a week; walked about 101 days earlier (Ulrich 2001).
  • Strength: squat to stand, step-ups, climbing daily; adolescents progressive resistance twice a week for 10 weeks.
  • Balance on varied surfaces, ball play; fitness: swimming, cycling, dance most days; weight control.
  • Lax joints: no end-range stretching; supramalleolar orthoses or insoles for flat feet.
  • Short, frequent, play-based sessions; demonstration, simple words, praise; parent-run home programme.

Classic vs current: not tapping and approximation while waiting for "readiness"; practise the skills (treadmill, strength); facilitation only an add-on.

Precautions

Atlantoaxial instability, stop and refer: neck pain, head tilt, new weakness, change in walking, brisk reflexes, bladder or bowel change. No trampolines; care with contact sports, gymnastics; support the neck in handling. Heart: stop for breathlessness, blue lips, unusual tiredness. Face the child (hearing). Prognosis: almost all walk, later (~26 months).
