Asked: not on its own in 2016–2026; transfers and gait training fill 4–7 management marks in most long cases ("trunk control and ambulation"). Named gaits are asked on condition pages: hemiplegic, parkinsonian, ataxic, DMD, L3 fracture ambulation.

Bed mobility, transfers, posture

  • Rolling: head and arm lead, knees bent; hemiplegia: both ways (to the strong side trains the weak side). Supine to sit: side lying, legs over the edge, push up.
  • Sit to stand: slide forward, feet back, trunk forward ("nose over toes"), push through both legs; sit down with control. Raise the seat to ease, lower to progress.
  • Transfers: stand-pivot (hemiplegia: usually towards the stronger side, guard the weak knee); sliding board or depression lift (paraplegia); car, toilet, bath; up from the floor after a fall. Teach the family safe help and equipment.
  • Re-education: whole tasks in real settings; part practice only for missing parts; many repetitions, progressively harder.
  • Posture: pelvis upright, weight even; correct flexed (PD) or asymmetrical (stroke) posture with mirror, verbal cues, extensor and trunk strengthening.

Normal gait cycle and measures

Stance about 60%: loading response 0–10%, midstance 10–30%, terminal stance 30–50%, pre-swing 50–60%. Swing about 40%: initial 60–73%, mid 73–87%, terminal 87–100%. Double support 0–10% and 50–60% (about 10% each); slower walking lengthens stance and double support.

Measures: 10 m walk (speed), 6MWT (endurance), step and stride length, cadence, symmetry. Stroke speed (Perry 1995): below ~0.4 m/s household; 0.4–0.8 limited community; above ~0.8 m/s community walker.

Pathological neurological gaits

HemiplegicShort weak-side stance, stiff knee, foot drop, circumduction or hip hiking (stroke)
Scissoring, crouchThighs cross; hips and knees bent, toe walking (diplegic CP)
FestinatingFlexed, short steps that speed up, little arm swing, freezing, turns en bloc (PD)
Cerebellar ataxicWide-based, staggering; no better with eyes open (cerebellum, MS)
Sensory ataxicHigh steps, heels stamp, eyes on feet; worse in the dark; Romberg + (neuropathy, dorsal column)
High-steppingHip and knee high to clear a dropped foot; foot slaps (peroneal palsy, CMT, polio)
WaddlingTrunk sways, wide base, lordosis (DMD, myopathies)
TrendelenburgPelvis drops on swing side (weak abductors); hand-on-thigh: weak quads (polio)

Principles of gait training

  • Walking practice improves walking: moderate to high intensity in chronic stroke, incomplete SCI, brain injury (Hornby 2020).
  • Overground with variety (turns, slopes, obstacles); treadmill ± body-weight support; dual task; cueing in PD.
  • Prerequisites: standing balance, weight shift, strength (hip extensors, quads, plantar- and dorsiflexors), range (hip extension, DF), endurance.
  • AFO for foot drop (orthoses); the least aid that keeps walking safe.
  • Progression: parallel bars → walker → crutches or stick → no aid; level → slopes, stairs, outdoors; short → long; supervised → independent; re-measure speed and distance.

Aids, crutch gaits, stairs

Height: handgrip at the wrist crease (or greater trochanter), elbow 20–30° bent; stick in the hand opposite the weak leg. Four-point: crutch, opposite foot, other crutch, other foot (most stable). Two-point: crutch and opposite foot together (faster). Three-point: both crutches with the affected leg, then the good leg (non- or partial weight bearing). Swing-to, swing-through: paraplegia with KAFOs. Stairs: "up with the good, down with the bad" (stick leads down).

Exam plan (gait training in a long case)

  1. Gait analysis: deviations in each phase and their causes.
  2. Prerequisites: balance, weight shift, strength, range.
  3. Walking practice with intensity and variety; treadmill; orthosis and aid.
  4. Progression (aids, surfaces, distance, stairs) and outcome measures (speed, distance).
