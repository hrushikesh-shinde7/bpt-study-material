Definition, level, ASIA scale (3m S19)

Tetraplegia: cervical segments, arms affected; paraplegia: thoracic, lumbar, sacral, cauda equina, arms spared. Key muscles (0–5): C5 elbow flexors, C6 wrist extensors, C7 elbow extensors, C8 finger flexors, T1 little-finger abductor; L2 hip flexors, L3 knee extensors, L4 DF, L5 long toe extensors, S1 PF. 28 sensory points (light touch, pinprick 0/1/2); anal VAC, DAP. Motor level: lowest key muscle grade 3+ with those above 5. NLI: the higher of motor and sensory levels.

BSensation but no motor below the level, incl. S4–S5
CMotor: less than half the key muscles below NLI grade 3+
DMotor: half or more grade 3+
ENormal now, had deficits

Sacral sparing (3m S16); complete vs incomplete (3m W25): any S4–S5 sensation, deep anal pressure or anal contraction → incomplete (sacral fibres lie on the outer edge of the tracts); none → AIS A. S16: triceps 4/5, nothing below; sensation to T4; anal sensation intact → NLI C7, AIS B.

Paraplegia in extension vs flexion (3m W22): extension: pyramidal only (vestibulospinal works), legs extended, adducted; incomplete, better. Flexion: pyramidal + extrapyramidal, hips and knees drawn up, spasms, mass reflex; poorer.

Causes, pathophysiology, spinal shock (3m S17, W20)

Causes: falls from height (India 53%, road accidents 28%), diving; Pott's spine, transverse myelitis, tumour, myelopathy, anterior spinal artery infarct.

  • Primary injury → secondary over hours–days (swelling, poor flow).
  • Cord → UMN once shock ends; cauda equina → LMN. T6 and above: low BP, OH, AD, poor temperature control.
  • Vertebra → segment: cervical +1; T1–T6 +2; T7–T9 +3; T10 → L1–L2; T12 → L5; L1 → conus.

Spinal shock: flaccid, all reflexes lost incl. bulbocavernosus, retention, ileus. Bulbocavernosus returns days 1–3 → hyperreflexia 1–4 weeks → spasticity 1–12 months. Not neurogenic shock (low BP, slow HR). Transverse myelitis (S17, S21): legs, sensory level, bladder over hours–days; steroids; a third each recover well, partly, poorly. Pott's (W22): gibbus, evening fever; anti-tubercular drugs, brace.

Medical: ISNCSCI within 72 h; early stabilisation; baclofen. Classic vs current: not 6–12 weeks' bed rest; mobilise early within precautions.

Autonomic dysreflexia (7m W18, W24; 3m S17, W19, S21, W21, S22, S24)

Systolic BP more than 20 mmHg above usual (15 in children), SCI at or above T6; emergency. Mechanism: stimulus below → sympathetic discharge → splanchnic vessels (T6–L2) narrow → BP up → vagus slows heart, flushing above; calming signals cannot pass down. Causes: full bladder (blocked catheter), bowel, pressure injury, ingrown toenail, tight straps. Signs: pounding headache, slow pulse, flushing, sweating above, blocked nose.

  1. Sit upright, lower the legs; loosen clothes, straps, binder.
  2. BP and pulse every 1–2 min.
  3. Bladder first: catheterise, or unkink, flush, change.
  4. Systolic still 150 or more → doctor's short-acting drug (nitrate paste, nifedipine).
  5. Bowel (anaesthetic gel) → other triggers; monitor 2 h; teach; wallet card.

Prevent in PT: ask about the bladder each session; know usual BP (tetraplegia 90–110, so 130 may be high); stop exercise or stimulation if headache starts.

Other complications

Orthostatic hypotension (fall of 20 systolic or 10 diastolic): tilt table in small steps, binder, stockings. Pressure injury: turns every 2–6 h; relief every 15–30 min for 30–90 s; mirror skin checks twice a day. DVT (painless swollen leg): stop leg exercise, report. Heterotopic ossification (hip): no forceful stretch. Shoulder pain: strengthen, level transfers.

ICF (S22: 42, photographer, complete T9–T10, 1 month)

Assess: ISNCSCI, VC, cough, MAS, skin, sitting, transfers, SCIM III, WISCI II, BP lying and sitting.

  • Structure/function: paralysis and sensory loss below (AIS A; UMN, shock over); spasticity; reflex bladder (sacral centre intact, cut off); weak lower abdominals; skin, DVT risk; below T6, so AD unlikely.
  • Activity: cannot stand, walk, climb stairs; already sits unsupported and transfers.
  • Participation: cannot reach his first-floor studio; outdoor work.
  • Environment: good finances, studio at home, family (facilitators); stairs, Indian toilet (barriers). Personal: self-employed, seated job.

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): own pressure relief; push-ups clear of seat; stands in KAFOs in bars; self-catheterises, volumes under 400–500 mL. Long-term (3–6 months): floor transfer; kerbs; household walking, KAFOs, swing-through; back at work, hand controls.

Expected outcome and PT management

Expected (complete): C1–C4 power chair (C1–C3 ventilator); C5 power chair, feeds with set-up; C6 tenodesis, manual chair on level; C7–C8 manual chair, independent; T1–T12 household walking with KAFOs; L1–S1 KAFO or AFO and crutches.

  • Acute: deep breathing, assisted cough, positioning; turns; daily ROM within precautions; ankle splints at 90°; strengthen working muscles; sit up in stages watching BP.
  • Rehab: tilt table → rolling with momentum → long sitting (SLR about 100–110°, do not overstretch) → sitting balance → push-ups on blocks → sliding board → lifting → floor transfers; wheelchair skills.
  • Dose (Martin Ginis 2018): arm cycling, wheeling 20 min moderate–vigorous 2 times a week (30 min 3 times for heart health); resistance 3 sets per group 2 times a week; daily stretch, prone lying. AIS C, D: treadmill ± body-weight support.
  • Tetraplegia: assisted cough (push in and up under ribs); tenodesis (C6–C7): stretch finger flexors only with wrist flexed.

Ambulation (S22, S26; L3 fracture 7m S18)

  • Before: strong shoulder depressors, latissimus, triceps; full hip extension, DF to neutral; standing without dizziness.
  • KAFOs, drop-lock knees, ankles slight DF; higher thoracic: HKAFO or RGO.
  • Bars: sit to stand knees locked; hips forward (Y-ligaments), shoulders behind; swing-to → swing-through; walker → elbow crutches; floor rise, kerbs, stairs. KAFO walking costs far more energy: chair stays main mobility.
  • L3 fracture: below the cord → cauda equina, LMN, often incomplete, uneven: strengthen hip extensors, abductors, quads; AFOs (KAFO only if quads weak); crutches four-point → two-point → canes; stairs, kerbs, slopes; expect community walking; protect insensate feet.

Education, prognosis, bladder (S26)

Skin checks, AD card; ramps, commode, ground floor or lift. Prognosis: most recovery in 6–9 months; 20–30% of AIS A improve; walking likelier with AIS D, youth, strong quads and calves, preserved pinprick.

S26 bladder: T9–T10 is above the sacral centre (S2–S4 at L1 vertebra) → reflex (UMN) bladder; clean intermittent catheterisation 4–6 times a day. See bladder and bowel.
