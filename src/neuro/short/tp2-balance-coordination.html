Asked: 7 marks: training static and dynamic standing balance (S16), evaluating sensory and motor strategies of balance (W18). 3 marks: three strategies to improve dynamic balance (S17), three motor strategies of balance (S18).

What balance needs

Balance: keeping the centre of mass over the base of support: static, dynamic, reactive.

  • Sensory: vision, vestibular, somatosensation; brain reweights them (on foam: more vestibular and vision).
  • Central: cerebellum, basal ganglia, brainstem, cortex.
  • Motor: strength, range (ankle), postural strategies; anticipatory (before movement) and reactive (after disturbance).
  • Cognition: worse with dual tasks.

Motor strategies (3m S18)

AnkleSmall, slow disturbance, firm wide surface; sways as one piece about ankles; distal → proximal
HipLarger, faster, or narrow or soft surface; quick hip flexion or extension; proximal → distal
Stepping (reaching)Centre of mass outside base: rapid step or grab makes a new base

Sometimes added: suspensory (bend knees, lower centre of mass).

Evaluating strategies (7m W18)

Sensory: mCTSIB: 30 s each in four conditions: firm eyes open, firm eyes closed, foam eyes open, foam eyes closed. Falls firm eyes closed = somatosensory loss; foam eyes closed = vestibular loss or poor reweighting. Romberg, sharpened Romberg (sensory ataxia); Sensory Organisation Test (six conditions).

Motor: push and release or nudge each direction: ankle, hip or step, quick and effective?; pull test (PD). Functional reach along a ruler at shoulder height (less than ~15 cm = high fall risk); multidirectional reach; rising on toes. BESTest / Mini-BESTest (biomechanics, stability limits, anticipatory, reactive, sensory orientation, gait). Berg (0–56), TUG, DGI or FGA, dual-task walking.

Static and dynamic training (7m S16)

ChangeEasier → harder
BaseFeet apart → together, semi-tandem, tandem, single leg
SurfaceFirm → foam, wobble board, uneven
VisionEyes open → closed, moving scenes
SupportHands on bars → no hands → holding an object
Head; attentionStill → turning, nodding; single → talking, counting back, tray

Static: hold (e.g. 30 s) before progressing; even weight with mirror or scale (stroke).

Dynamic (3m S17):

  • Reaching beyond arm's length, to floor and overhead.
  • Weight shifts and stepping all directions, onto a step, over obstacles; turning.
  • Perturbation training: expected, then unexpected pushes (ankle, hip, step).
  • Walking: speed, direction, narrow paths, head turns, carrying, dual task; tai chi, dance, ball games.

Dose: challenging balance more than 3 hours a week = largest effect, 39% fewer falls (Sherrington 2017); near limit of ability, safely.

Coordination

Tests (eyes open and closed: cerebellar vs sensory): finger-nose, finger-finger, heel-shin, dysdiadochokinesia, rebound, foot circle, tapping. Frenkel's: slow, precise, repeated movements of rising difficulty, lying → sitting → standing → walking, watching the limb, to counts or floor marks; vision replaces position sense: best for sensory ataxia. Also PNF rhythmic stabilisation, stabilising and slow reversals, approximation, light wrist or ankle weights, task practice (ataxia).

Precautions

Gait belt, bars or harness; stand on weaker side; clear obstacles; BP before standing in orthostatic hypotension; progress one variable at a time; challenging but safe.

Exam plan (S16, 7 marks)

Assessment: systems, Berg, mCTSIB (1) → static progression: base, surface, vision, support, head, attention (2) → dynamic: reaching, stepping, perturbation, walking variations, functional (3) → dose over 3 h a week, guarding (1). Draw: stick figures of ankle, hip, stepping strategies. Underline: centre of mass, base of support, reweighting, anticipatory and reactive, mCTSIB, functional reach, perturbation, dual task.
