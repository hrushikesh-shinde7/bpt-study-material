Definition and types

DMD: X-linked recessive; dystrophin absent, so fibres are damaged by their own contractions and replaced by fat and fibrous tissue; progressive, mainly proximal weakness from early childhood, later breathing and heart. Boys, about 16–20 per 100,000 male births; signs at 2–5 years. Exon deletion; carrier mother → half of sons affected, half of daughters carriers; some new mutations. Maternal cousin or uncle (W20) is a clue.

Stages (2018): diagnosis → early ambulatory (Gowers', waddling, toe walking; climbs stairs) → late ambulatory (loses stairs, floor rise) → early non-ambulatory (self-propels; scoliosis starts) → late non-ambulatory (arm, breathing, heart).

Pathophysiology to impairments

  • No dystrophin → membrane tears in contraction, especially eccentric → calcium floods in → fibre dies → fat and fibrosis.
  • Pseudohypertrophy: calves big but weak (fat and fibrous tissue).
  • Proximal first (hip extensors, quadriceps, shoulder girdle) → floor rise, stairs, running fail.
  • Contractures: plantarflexors, iliotibial band, hip flexors first; later knees, elbows, wrists.
  • After walking is lost: scoliosis, restrictive lung disease; cardiomyopathy.

Why no eccentric or heavy resistance: lengthening and high loads stress the fragile membrane most, causing more damage than repair; swimming, cycling keep fitness safely.

Gowers' sign (3m S16, S24) and gait (7m S26)

Features: late walking, falls, cannot run, jump, climb stairs; toe walking; big calves; learning or speech delay in some.

Gowers' sign: turns face down → hands and knees → hands and feet, hips high → walks hands up the knees and thighs → pushes trunk upright. Weak hip and knee extensors: proximal lower-limb weakness; typical of DMD, also other myopathies and SMA.

  • Waddling (Trendelenburg): pelvis drops, trunk sways over stance leg (weak abductors).
  • Lumbar lordosis, shoulders back: keeps line of gravity behind weak hip extensors.
  • Wide base; toe walking, no heel strike (tight calves, weak DF); knee locked back; arms behind trunk; slow short steps; falls.

Investigations (S16) and PT assessment

CK very high from early life; genetic testing (deletion and duplication first, then sequencing); muscle biopsy (absent dystrophin) only if unclear; EMG myopathic; ECG, echo; FVC, peak cough flow, mouth pressures; spine X-ray, bone density.

PT: maternal family history, steroids, home (floor living, stairs, toilet); Gowers' time, 10 m walk or run, four-stair climb, 6MWT; North Star Ambulatory Assessment, Performance of the Upper Limb; MMT; ROM (DF knee straight, Ober, Thomas, hamstrings); lordosis, scoliosis; FVC, cough. Repeat every 6 months to stage.

Medical management

Steroids before major decline: prednisone or prednisolone 0.75 mg/kg a day or deflazacort 0.9 mg/kg a day; mean age at losing walking 10.0 → 11.2 (prednisone) → 13.9 years (deflazacort). Never stop suddenly (adrenal crisis); watch weight, bones, behaviour, growth. Exon skipping, gene therapy in a few countries. Achilles lengthening in selected walkers; posterior spinal fusion after walking is lost.

ICF (S20: 5-year-old, falls while playing, difficulty rising, big calves)

  • Structure/function: proximal weakness (hip extensors, abductors, quads, shoulder girdle); calf pseudohypertrophy, tight calves and iliotibial bands; lordosis, waddling, toe walking; poor balance, endurance.
  • Activity: falls; Gowers' to rise; cannot run, jump, climb stairs like peers.
  • Participation: left out of active play; slower at school.
  • Environment: concerned parents who came early, school (facilitators); stairs, floor sitting, little knowledge (barriers). Personal: 5-year-old who wants to play. Early ambulatory stage.

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): parents stretch 4–6 times a week, night AFOs worn; swims or cycles without next-day exhaustion; home and school safer; walks school with rests; parents understand. Long-term (6–12 months): DF at least neutral; keeps up in short play; fewer falls, no fractures; walking kept as long as possible; genetic counselling done.

Principles and PT by stage (7m S16, W22, S24, S25)

Principles: preserve walking and range; active without overworking muscle; prevent deformity, falls; support breathing; inclusion. Classic vs current: no progressive resistance; submaximal aerobic (swimming, cycling) with rests; stop before fatigue.

  • Early ambulatory: home stretching, night AFOs; swimming, cycling; buggy or wheelchair for long distances; fall prevention; parent education.
  • Late ambulatory: + serial casting; KAFOs with locked knees to prolong standing and walking; manual wheelchair; standing frame.
  • Early non-ambulatory: power wheelchair, custom seating; supported standing; daytime AFOs; arm stretching; breathing exercises; spine checks.
  • Late non-ambulatory: seating, pressure care; assisted cough; arm supports; hoist.

Wheelchair prescription (S26: 8-year-old, rural, walks only with support)

Assess: stage, arm and trunk strength, contractures, spine, breathing; home, school, rough roads, no ramps; who pushes; budget, repairs. Choose: light manual chair (family pushes) while he walks indoors → power chair when walking is lost (tilt, recline, elevating leg rests, standing support); rural: sturdy frame, large pneumatic tyres repairable locally. Seating: solid seat and back, lateral supports, hip guides, pommel, headrest, pressure cushion, lap belt.

Measure: seat width hip + 5 cm; depth buttock-to-knee − 5 cm; height so feet clear with footplate; armrest 2.5 cm above elbow; back high enough for the trunk. Train family; review fit every few months.

Complications (7m S18, W24) and precautions

  • Contractures; scoliosis (common after walking is lost: seating, spine checks, refer early, fusion).
  • Fractures: 20–60% limb, up to 30% vertebral; can end walking: safe home, vitamin D, calcium, get him up quickly.
  • Respiratory failure: weak cough, night hypoventilation (morning headache): thresholds above. Cardiomyopathy: often silent; regular review.
  • Obesity on steroids; pain, low mood.

Avoid eccentric work (jumping down, downhill running, lowering loads) and heavy resistance. Too hard: pain or weakness into the next day, dark urine. No forceful stretching; never stop steroids suddenly; watch breathlessness and colour.

Parent education and home care (3m W16; S20)

Explain honestly; not their fault. Daily stretches, night splints, skin checks; swimming, cycling; no jumping games or long walks; buggy for outings. Clear floors, stair rails, western toilet or commode, chairs he can rise from. School: ground floor, rests, adapted sport. Diet and weight on steroids. Genetic counselling for mother and sisters; disability certificate; support groups. Prognosis: walking lost ~10 years without steroids; many now live into adulthood.
