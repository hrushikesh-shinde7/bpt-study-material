Order of assessment

  1. History: chief complaints; onset (sudden or gradual), duration, progression; past, family, personal, social; drugs; red flags.
  2. General: vitals, GCS, built, posture, limb attitude, deformities, skin and pressure areas, aids.
  3. Higher functions: orientation, memory, speech, MMSE or MoCA, perception. Cranial nerves.
  4. Motor: bulk (girth), tone, power, reflexes, coordination, involuntary movements. Sensory: superficial, deep, combined; dermatomes.
  5. Balance and gait; function: bed mobility, lying to sitting, sit to stand, walking, reaching, ADL (Barthel). Outcome measures, investigations.
  6. Sum up: problem list, ICF and functional diagnosis, goals, plan (ICF).

UMN vs LMN (3m S16, S20, W21)

UMN: motor cortex → corticospinal tract → anterior horn (or cranial nerve nuclei). LMN: anterior horn cell (or nucleus) → root → plexus → peripheral nerve → muscle. The anterior horn cell is where they meet.

UMN goes up, LMN goes down

UMN: tone up, reflexes up, toes up. LMN: tone down, reflexes down, bulk down.

Features of a UMN lesion (3m W16, W22, S24)

  • Negative (lost): weakness in a pyramidal pattern; loss of fine and skilled movement; slowness, early fatigue.
  • Positive (released): spasticity; brisk reflexes with spread and clonus; Babinski and Hoffmann's signs; flexor or extensor spasms; associated reactions, mass synergies.
  • Abdominal and cremasteric reflexes lost; little wasting, no fasciculation.
  • Exception: acute lesion (cerebral or spinal shock) → flaccid, reflexes absent; spasticity appears over days to weeks.

Why it matters: UMN: manage tone, train movement control and tasks, prevent contracture, strengthen (does not increase spasticity). LMN: strengthen each muscle by its grade, splint to protect denervated muscle from overstretch, care for numb skin, watch for reinnervation.

Motor examination

Bulk: wasting, fasciculation; girth at fixed distances from a bony point, both sides. Tone: passive movement slow and fast; spasticity (velocity-dependent, clasp-knife), rigidity (lead-pipe, cogwheel), hypotonia; MAS (tone). Power: MRC for LMN and muscle disease; in UMN record voluntary control and synergy (Brunnstrom, Fugl-Meyer). Coordination: finger–nose, heel–shin, rapid alternating, rebound. Involuntary: tremor (rest in PD; intention in cerebellar), chorea, athetosis, dystonia, ballismus, myoclonus, tics.

MRC: 0 no contraction; 1 flicker; 2 movement with gravity eliminated; 3 against gravity; 4 against gravity and resistance; 5 normal.

Grading: 0 absent; 1+ reduced; 2+ normal; 3+ brisk; 4+ with clonus. Compare sides; Jendrassik's manoeuvre before calling one absent. Superficial: abdominal (T8–T12), cremasteric (L1–L2), plantar (S1), anal (S2–S4, important in SCI). Pathological: Babinski (big toe up, toes fan; normal in infants); Hoffmann's (flick middle fingernail → thumb and index flex); sustained clonus = UMN; grasp, sucking, palmomental in adults = frontal lobe damage.

Sensory examination

Eyes closed, compare sides, numb → normal skin. Light touch (cotton; dorsal columns and spinothalamic); pain (pin; spinothalamic, crosses in cord); temperature (warm and cold tubes); joint position (hold digit's sides; dorsal columns, cross in medulla); vibration (128 Hz fork, distal first); combined (parietal): two-point discrimination, stereognosis, graphaesthesia, localisation, extinction.

Patterns: glove and stocking → polyneuropathy; one dermatome → root; nerve territory → nerve injury; level on trunk → cord; half body → brain; pain and temperature lost, touch kept → syringomyelia, anterior cord; Brown-Séquard; saddle anaesthesia + bladder change → cauda equina (emergency).

Key points: C5 outer elbow crease; C6 thumb; C7 middle finger; C8 little finger; T1 inner elbow; T4 nipple; T6 xiphisternum; T10 umbilicus; T12 mid inguinal ligament; L2 mid front thigh; L3 inner knee; L4 medial malleolus; L5 dorsum at third toe joint; S1 outer heel; S2 back of knee; S3 ischial tuberosity; S4–5 perianal.
