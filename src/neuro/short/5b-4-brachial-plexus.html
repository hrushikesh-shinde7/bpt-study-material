Asked: 1 long case (S19, 5 + 10): 6-month-old cannot move the right arm since a forceps delivery, other milestones normal: differential diagnosis, short- and long-term goals, PT. Adult plexus questions are on nerve injuries.

Definition, groups, causes

Obstetric brachial plexus palsy: infant arm weakness from stretching or tearing of C5–T1 at birth; about 0.4–5 per 1000 births. Causes: shoulder dystocia (traction on the head stretches upper roots); large baby, maternal diabetes, prolonged labour, forceps or vacuum, breech; some after normal or caesarean delivery.

Narakas I: C5–C6 (Erb's)No shoulder abduction or elbow flexion; hand works; commonest, best outlook
II: C5–C7+ weak wrist and finger extension (dropped wrist)
III: C5–T1Flail arm, no Horner's
IV: C5–T1 + Horner'sT1 avulsion; worst outlook

Pathophysiology and features

Deltoid, spinati, biceps paralysed; pectoralis major, subscapularis work → internal rotation pull on a forming joint → glenohumeral dysplasia, posterior subluxation. Non-use → developmental disregard; shorter arm.

Erb's: arm by side, IR, elbow extended, pronated ("waiter's tip"). Moro absent on that side; grasp present in Erb's, absent in total. Phrenic (C3–C5): breathing difficulty. Later: IR and elbow flexion contracture, winging, trumpet sign.

Tests and differential diagnosis (S19, 5 marks)

X-ray clavicle, humerus; shoulder ultrasound (dysplasia); MRI for avulsion. Active Movement Scale (0–7); Mallet score from ~3 years. Assess passive ER at side, Moro, grasp, sensation, Horner's, milestones.

Fracture clavicle or humerusPain, swelling, crepitus; X-ray; moves again in 2–3 weeks
Septic shoulderFever, swelling, pain; raised markers; emergency
Hemiplegic CPUMN signs, fisting, leg involved, other delays
Cervical cord injuryBoth arms or legs; breathing, bladder
ArthrogryposisStiff joints in several limbs

Medical and surgical

Refer by 1 month, start before 3 months. Nerve surgery if no biceps by ~3 months (Toronto under 3.5) or fails cookie test by ~9 months; total + Horner's explored early. Later: internal rotator release, tendon transfer, humeral osteotomy; botulinum + casting.

ICF and goals (S19 case)

Structure/function: weak shoulder and elbow; IR posture, dysplasia risk; sensory loss, disregard. Activity: reach, hand to mouth, prop and crawl. Participation: two-handed play with family. Environment: parents for home programme; clinic distance, guilt. Personal: 6 months, otherwise normal; past the 3-month point: refer now.

Short-term (4–6 weeks): full passive ER and abduction; hand towards mouth gravity eliminated; holds toys at midline; parents do the programme. Long-term (6–12 months): hand to mouth and overhead reach; both hands in play, crawls; no deformity.

PT management

  • First 1–2 weeks: gentle handling; never pull; dress affected arm first; no rigid splint.
  • Home ROM several times a day (each nappy change): ER with scapula held, abduction, flexion, elbow extension, supination, wrist and finger extension; touch, textures.
  • Active play: gravity eliminated (side lying, water) → against gravity; toys on the affected side; hand to mouth; prone on elbows, four-point, crawling.
  • Later: short constraint of good arm for disregard; resting wrist splint at night (group II); elbow splint or serial casts.

Precautions and prognosis

Never force ER or abduction; do not lift by the arms; stop and refer for clunk, lost range, swelling or pain; protect a numb hand. Good: Erb's only, antigravity biceps by ~3 months. Poor: total palsy, Horner's, no biceps by 3–6 months. ER and abduction often stay limited: review until growth ends.

Exam plan (S19 long case)

DD (5) → goals (3–4) → management (6–7): refer, handling, home ROM, play, constraint and splints later, precautions, parents. Draw: plexus, upper roots marked; ICF. Underline: shoulder dystocia, Narakas, waiter's tip, refer by 1 month, biceps by 3 months, external rotation, dysplasia.
