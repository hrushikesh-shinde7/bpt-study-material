Definition and types (3m S25)

Neural tube defect: tube fails to close in the first 4 weeks of pregnancy (anencephaly, encephalocele at the head; spina bifida along the spine). Spina bifida: incomplete closure of the vertebral arches, with or without a sac of meninges and cord.

  • Occulta: bony gap only, skin closed (hair tuft, dimple, fatty lump); usually no deficit.
  • Meningocele: skin-covered sac of meninges and CSF; cord in the canal; few deficits.
  • Myelomeningocele: cord and roots in the sac, often open; paralysis, sensory loss, bladder below the level; commonest serious type.
  • Others: lipomyelomeningocele (fatty mass tethers the cord); myeloschisis (open flat cord plate).

Causes and prevention

Multifactorial: low folate, previous affected pregnancy, maternal diabetes, obesity, valproate, high fever in early pregnancy, genes. India about 4.5 per 1000 births. Folic acid 400 micrograms daily from at least a month before conception to 12 weeks; 4–5 mg on medical advice after an affected pregnancy; food fortification.

Hydrocephalus: types (3m W19)

Too much CSF in the ventricles, usually under raised pressure. Non-communicating (obstructive): blocked inside the ventricles (aqueduct stenosis, Chiari II, tumour). Communicating: CSF leaves the ventricles but is not absorbed (bleed in a premature baby, meningitis). Congenital or acquired; normal-pressure in older adults.

Pathophysiology to impairments

  • Exposed cord damaged → flaccid paralysis and numb skin below the level; some spasticity.
  • Unopposed muscles above the level → deformity: L3–L4 hip flexors and adductors → flexion–adduction, dislocation; tibialis anterior with paralysed calf → calcaneus.
  • S2–S4 in almost all → neurogenic bladder and bowel.
  • Chiari II → hydrocephalus; about 8 in 10 repaired after birth need a shunt in year 1.
  • Numb skin breaks down; thin bones fracture (after casts); tethered cord stretches with growth → new loss.

Hydrocephalus features (3m W19, S20, W20)

Infants: head crossing centiles; bulging fontanelle; widened sutures; scalp veins; setting-sun eyes; irritability, vomiting, poor feeding, drowsiness; high-pitched cry; stiff legs; delay; seizures. Older: morning headache, vomiting, drowsiness, papilloedema, diplopia, poor balance. Normal-pressure: walking difficulty, memory loss, incontinence.

Tethered cord signs: back pain with activity, new weakness or loss of walking, changing foot shape, worsening scoliosis, new bladder or bowel problems: report at once.

Investigations and PT assessment

Before birth: maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein, anomaly scan. After: MRI brain and spine, cranial ultrasound, serial head circumference, spine and hip X-rays; kidney ultrasound, urodynamics. PT: muscle chart and sensory testing (touch, pin) for the level, repeated to catch change; tone; hips, knees, feet, spine, limb lengths; skin; shunt signs; development and mobility; arm strength; cognition.

Level, not the hip, decides walking (Swaroop and Dias 2009).

Medical and surgical

Back closure in the first days; prenatal repair (MOMS: shunt need 82% → 40%, better walking at 30 months, more premature births). VP shunt or endoscopic third ventriculostomy (shunts block, infect, over-drain). Clean intermittent catheterisation, bowel routine. Clubfoot casting; releases; untethering; spinal fusion.

ICF (practice: 4-year-old, L4 myelomeningocele, VP shunt)

  • Structure/function: glutei and calves paralysed; no sensation below knees; hip flexion–adduction and calcaneus risk; heel pressure mark; neurogenic bladder.
  • Activity: walks short distances with AFOs and walker; floor to stand, stairs, toileting need help.
  • Participation: keeping up at nursery and play; toileting at school.
  • Environment: AFOs, walker, parents, nursery (facilitators); distance, rough ground, latex, no wheelchair (barriers). Personal: 4, keen to play.

Goals: short-term (6–8 weeks): walks at home, starts forearm crutches; full hip extension in prone; heel healed, skin checks twice daily. Long-term (6–12 months): AFOs + crutches at nursery, light wheelchair for distance; level pelvis, plantigrade feet; no sores; independent transfers.

PT for lumbar myelomeningocele (7m W16, S17, W21, W22)

  • Before closure: prone or side lying, never press the sac; record moving muscles. Infancy: positioning, gentle ROM, handling, head control, rolling, sitting, arm strength.
  • Strengthen working muscles (hip flexors, adductors, quads, trunk) and arms and shoulders (push-ups in sitting, crutch drills) daily.
  • Prevent deformity: prone lying daily; stretch hip flexors, adductors, hamstrings, calf; abduction positioning; night splints; serial casts (knee flexion over 20° hinders orthoses); assist clubfoot casting.
  • Standing frame from about 12 months; parapodium or RGO from about 2 years (high lesions).
  • Orthoses by level: no quads → RGO or HKAFO + walker + wheelchair; weak quads → KAFO; strong quads (L4) → AFOs + crutches; sacral → AFOs or none.
  • Gait: standing balance → walker → crutches (swing-through or four-point) → stairs, falls; wheelchair skills for distance.
  • Skin: checks twice daily (mirror), padded shoes, no heat; bladder; latex-free; shunt and tethered cord signs.

Classic vs current: not surgical reduction of every dislocated hip; aim for a level pelvis and free movement.

PT for hydrocephalus (7m W21)

Support the heavy head when lifting and carrying; change head position every few hours (scalp sores, flattening), no long pressure on the shunt valve; side lying, prone on wedge; head control, rolling, sitting, reaching, standing frame in sequence; stretch stiff legs; sensory and visual play; seating with head support; teach shunt warning signs.

Precautions and complications

Latex-free gloves, balls, bands from birth. Stop and refer: shunt signs (vomiting, drowsiness, irritability, headache, bulging fontanelle, fever, fast head growth); new weakness, pain or bladder change (tethered cord). Thin bones: gentle ROM, no forceful stretch; avoid crawling for 3–4 weeks after immobilisation. No heat on numb skin; check skin after orthoses. Complications: pressure sores, fractures, contractures, obesity.

Education and prognosis

Daily prone lying, stretches, standing; skin and orthosis care; bladder routine; latex; warning signs; folic acid in future pregnancy; accessible school, toilet, wheelchair. Walking depends on level; most survive into adulthood.
