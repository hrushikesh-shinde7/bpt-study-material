Definition and types

GBS: acute, immune-mediated polyradiculoneuropathy: rapidly progressive, usually symmetrical limb weakness with absent or reduced reflexes, worst within 4 weeks. LMN: brain and cord normal. 1–2 per 100,000 a year; two-thirds have an infection in the 6 weeks before (diarrhoea, chest).

Hughes disability scale: 0 healthy; 1 can run; 2 walks 10 m unaided; 3 walks 10 m with help; 4 bed or chair; 5 ventilated; 6 dead.

Pathophysiology and features

  • Infection (Campylobacter, cytomegalovirus, hepatitis E, Mycoplasma, Epstein–Barr, Zika) → molecular mimicry → attack on myelin (slows, blocks) or axon (stops).
  • Ascending flaccid weakness, legs → arms, trunk, breathing, face; proximal as weak as distal; reflexes lost.
  • Phrenic and intercostals → up to 22% ventilated in week 1; cranial → bilateral facial, swallowing; autonomic → BP and HR swings, arrhythmia, retention; sensory → tingling, lost position sense, back and leg pain; fatigue.
  • Recovery: remyelination (weeks), axon regrowth (months), so axonal forms are slower. Weakest within 2 weeks; recovery can go on over 5 years.

Diagnosis: required: progressive weakness of arms and legs + reduced reflexes. Supports: progression days to 4 weeks, symmetry, mild sensory signs, bilateral facial, autonomic, pain. Doubt: sensory level, early bladder loss, clear asymmetry, CSF over 50 cells (cord or infection).

Investigations (NCV, EMG 3m W17)

CSF: high protein, normal cells (albuminocytological dissociation); normal in 30–50% in week 1. Breathing: VC, maximum inspiratory and expiratory pressure, single breath count, ABG. ECG; stool; MRI spine if cord possible.

Medical management

If unable to walk unaided: IVIg 0.4 g/kg a day for 5 days within 2 weeks, or plasma exchange 12–15 L over 4–5 exchanges within 4 weeks; equal; not one after the other; no steroids, no second IVIg. ICU, intubate by the 20/30/40 rule: VC under 20 mL/kg, maximum inspiratory pressure under 30, expiratory under 40 cmH₂O; single breath count 19 or less. DVT prophylaxis, ECG, nutrition, eye care, nerve-pain drugs.

PT assessment

Preceding infection, onset, speed, immunotherapy, ventilation, swallowing; HR, rhythm, BP lying and sitting, SpO₂; VC, single breath count, cough, auscultation; MMT, MRC sum score (0–60), facial and bulbar; position sense, pain; ROM, skin; Hughes scale, bed mobility, sitting, transfers, 10 m walk, 6MWT; Fatigue Severity Scale.

ICF (S25: 45, man, viral fever, 10 days, breathless, ICU)

  • Structure/function: breathing first: low VC, weak cough (phrenic, intercostals); symmetrical flaccid weakness of limbs and trunk, areflexia; facial, swallowing; tingling, pain; unstable BP, HR; chest, DVT, pressure, contracture, pressure-palsy risk; anxiety.
  • Activity: cannot turn, sit, stand, walk; dependent for feeding, washing, toilet; cannot clear secretions.
  • Participation: off work; family roles.
  • Environment: ICU, immunotherapy, family (facilitators); cost, noisy ICU (barriers). Personal: 45, likely earner; fear of ventilator.

Goals: short-term (1–3 weeks): chest clear, VC monitored and stable; full range, no ulcers or pressure palsies; sits out with support, tolerates tilt table; feeds with set-up; short sessions without next-day exhaustion. Long-term (3–6 months): normal breathing and cough; walks indoors and outdoors (AFO if foot drop); independent ADL; endurance for work.

Acute stage: ICU (7m W21)

  • Chest: positioning (side lying, upright), deep breathing, incentive spirometry, airway clearance, suctioning, assisted cough; VC or single breath count each shift, report VC under 20 mL/kg. Ventilated: secretion clearance, support weaning.
  • Limbs: neutral positioning; resting splints (wrist, hand) and AFO or foot support; gentle passive ROM once or twice a day; pad elbows and fibular heads; regular turns.
  • DVT: passive and active leg movements, stockings as prescribed. Pain, fear: gentle handling, explain before touching, reassure; teach the family.
  • Sit up and tilt table once breathing and BP are stable; watch postural hypotension.

Recovery (7m S19)

  • Early (ward): active-assisted → active, gravity eliminated first; sitting balance; rolling, bridging, sit to stand; standing in bars; short sessions with rests.
  • Rehabilitation: progressive resistance, few repetitions, rests, stop before fatigue; gait: bars → walker → stick, AFO for foot drop; balance with vision (mirror, then eyes closed); hand function; pacing; return to school, work.
  • Fitness (Garssen 2004): cycling 30 min at 70–90% max HR, 3 times a week for 12 weeks, 5 min warm-up and cool-down.
  • Avoid overwork: recovering nerves supply fewer fibres; weaker or more tired next day → shorten or lighten.
  • Child (W25, cycles to school): spirometry and bubble games; strengthening through play; putty, blocks, writing; static cycling before road cycling; pacing at school; parents' home programme.

Precautions and complications

Stop and report: falling VC or breath count, rising RR, weaker cough; HR, rhythm, BP swings on turning, suctioning (arrhythmia), sitting up (tilt back). No overstretching of flaccid joints; support shoulders; upright for meals if swallowing weak; protect eyes. Complications: respiratory failure, aspiration pneumonia, DVT, sacral and heel ulcers, pressure palsies (ulnar at elbow, peroneal at fibular head), contractures (ankle PF, shoulder, fingers), pain, hallucinations, depression.

Education and prognosis

Not contagious; most recover, slowly; family: positioning, ROM, transfers; graded home programme; staged return; seek help if weakness returns (fluctuations 6–10%, recurrence 2–5%). About 80% walk at 6 months; death 3–10%; poorer: older, diarrhoea before, severe (modified Erasmus GBS outcome score); pain in a third and fatigue in 60–80% at 1 year. Worsening after 8 weeks or 3+ relapses → acute-onset CIDP (chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy).
