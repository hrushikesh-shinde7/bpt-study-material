Asked: an MS long case in 6 papers: the 29-year-old executive after a second relapse (W19, S24, S25: ICF, goals, PT); medical representative with visual loss and imbalance (S23); student with poor fine motor control (S18: goals, PT); teacher with ataxia and diplopia (W17: functional diagnosis). 7 marks: visual, cognitive and behavioural disturbances (W18), PT for MS (S19), stages + PT for one stage (S20), features + PT (W20). 3 marks: clinical features (W16, S22), Uhthoff's phenomenon (W22), Charcot's triad (W25).

Definition and causes

MS: chronic immune-mediated CNS disease; inflammation strips myelin in brain, cord and optic nerves → plaques, later axonal loss; lesions scattered in space and time. 2.8 million worldwide; diagnosis at about 32; women twice as often; less common in India. Risks: Epstein–Barr virus (strongest), low vitamin D and sunlight, smoking, teenage obesity, genes (HLA-DRB1*15:01). Relapses after infection, after childbirth.

EDSS: 0 normal; 1–3.5 walks fully; 4–5.5 walks unaided, about 500 m at 4.0, 100 m at 5.5; 6.0 stick for 100 m; 6.5 support both sides for 20 m; 7–7.5 wheelchair; 8–9.5 bed or chair; 10 death. Disability stages: mild 0–4, moderate 4.5–6.5, severe 7+.

Pathophysiology and features (3m W16, S22; 7m W20)

  • Immune cells cross the blood–brain barrier → plaques near ventricles, brainstem, cerebellum, cord, optic nerves → slow or blocked conduction; symptoms by site.
  • Inflammation settles, some remyelination → remission; axon loss → progression.

Features: numbness, band feelings, Lhermitte's sign (shock down spine on neck flexion); optic neuritis (painful eye movement, blurred central vision, faded colours), diplopia, nystagmus; leg weakness, spasticity, UMN signs; ataxia, intention tremor, dysarthria; fatigue (commonest, most disabling); bladder urgency, constipation, sexual; slowed thinking, depression, lability; pain, trigeminal neuralgia; heat sensitivity.

Uhthoff's phenomenon (3m W22): temporary worsening (blurred vision, weakness) when body temperature rises (exercise, hot bath, weather, fever): heat slows conduction in demyelinated fibres; settles on cooling; not a relapse. Manage: pre-cooling, cool room, breaks. Charcot's triad (3m W25): nystagmus, intention tremor, scanning (staccato) speech: cerebellar and brainstem.

Visual, cognitive, behavioural (7m W18)

VisualOptic neuritis, diplopia, nystagmus, jumping vision, poor colour → good lighting, high-contrast step edges, uncluttered space, eye referral, gaze exercises
CognitiveSlow processing, memory, attention, planning → short simple instructions, written programme, quiet room, no dual tasks at first
BehaviouralDepression, anxiety, irritability, sudden laughing or crying, low motivation → agree goals, encourage, exercise lifts mood, counselling

Investigations and medical

MRI brain and spine; McDonald 2024: lesions in 2 of 5 regions (periventricular, cortical, brainstem-cerebellum, cord, now optic nerve); new lesions over time not always needed; CSF oligoclonal bands or kappa free light chain index; visual evoked potentials; bloods for mimics (B12, neuromyelitis optica antibodies). Relapse: methylprednisolone 3–5 days (speeds recovery, not the course). Disease-modifying: interferon beta, glatiramer, fingolimod, natalizumab, ocrelizumab (also primary progressive). Baclofen, tizanidine, fampridine, botulinum toxin.

PT assessment

Course, last relapse, fatigue pattern, heat sensitivity, bladder, falls, work; EDSS; strength, MAS, range, finger-nose, heel-shin; sensory, vision, cognition; Berg, Timed 25-Foot Walk, TUG, 6MWT, MS Walking Scale-12; Modified Fatigue Impact Scale or Fatigue Severity Scale; Nine-Hole Peg.

ICF (W19, S24, S25: 29, unmarried executive, 2nd relapse, discharged 1 week, moderately dependent)

  • Structure/function: relapsing-remitting, recovering; leg weakness, spasticity; poor balance, possible ataxia; fatigue, heat sensitivity (Uhthoff's); possible sensory, visual, bladder; low endurance after hospital; possible low mood, slow thinking.
  • Activity: moderate help for transfers, bathing, toileting; short walking distance; stairs, travel.
  • Participation: off work; social life; worry about career and marriage.
  • Environment: family, large employer (flexible or home working), medicines (facilitators); commuting, hot weather, long hours (barriers). Personal: young, educated: can self-manage.

Goals: short-term (2–4 weeks): independent bed-chair and toilet transfers, bathes with shower chair; stronger hips and knees, full calf and hamstring length; walks indoors with a stick; pacing without next-day exhaustion; plans phased return. Long-term (2–3 months): independent self-care; stairs; outdoor walking without aid, no falls; full working day; back at work, 150 min a week.

PT by phase (7m S19, S20, W20)

  • Relapse: rest the affected function; keep range, positioning, gentle activity; avoid overheating.
  • Recovery: grade back up: transfers, balance, walking, strength, endurance; short sessions with rests.
  • EDSS 0–6.5: aerobic and resistance doses below; balance and gait; fatigue; work.
  • EDSS 7–9: short daily sessions; upper-body exercise; standing frame; transfers; seating and pressure care; breathing; carer training.

Everyone: 150 min a week of exercise or activity, built gradually. Classic vs current: not "avoid exertion and heat": exercise is safe at every level; cool them rather than stop.

Other techniques

  • Spasticity: daily stretching, standing, positioning; check triggers (bladder, skin, infection).
  • Balance and gait: static → dynamic, stepping, turning, dual task when safe; AFO for foot drop; aids.
  • Ataxia, intention tremor: proximal stability, Frenkel's, slow practice with visual feedback; weighted wrist cuffs.
  • Fine motor (S18): grip and pinch; writing, buttoning, eating with thick pens, adapted cutlery.
  • Fatigue: energy conservation (plan, prioritise, pace, sit for tasks), rests, exercise at the coolest time.
  • Heat: pre-cooling (cool drink, shower, fan), cooling vest, cool water swimming; no hot packs or hot baths.
  • Bladder: timed voiding, pelvic floor (see bladder).

Precautions and complications

Avoid overheating; stop before exhaustion, worse next day → reduce dose; new symptom over 24 h without fever or heat = possible relapse: refer; fever or UTI → pseudo-relapse; guard balance work; after steroids: weaker bones, sugar, mood. Complications: falls, deconditioning, contractures (calf, hamstrings, hip flexors, adductors), pressure sores, osteoporosis.

Education and prognosis

Not contagious; most keep working; home exercise towards 150 min; energy conservation; keep cool; workplace changes (flexible hours, home working, seat near toilet, cool office, less travel); stop smoking; take disease-modifying medicines. Better: female, young, sensory or optic onset, good first recovery. Poorer: older, motor or cerebellar onset, frequent early relapses, progressive from start. PT in blocks after relapses.
