Definition and classification (7m S24; 3m S26)

CP: permanent disorders of movement and posture, causing activity limitation, from a non-progressive disturbance in the developing brain; with sensory, cognitive, communication problems, epilepsy, secondary musculoskeletal problems (Rosenbaum 2007). Lesion static, picture changes with growth. India about 3 per 1000.

Spastic (commonest)Hemiplegia (arm more than leg; stroke or bleed); diplegia (legs much more; periventricular leukomalacia, PVL, in preterm); quadriplegia (four limbs and trunk)
DyskineticAthetosis, chorea, dystonia; fluctuating tone; basal ganglia (asphyxia at term, kernicterus)
AtaxicLow tone, wide base, intention tremor; cerebellum
Hypotonic; mixedFloppy, often later dyskinetic or ataxic; mixed usually spastic + dyskinetic

GMFCS: I walks without limits; II with limits, rail on stairs; III hand-held device; IV powered mobility; V transported. MACS hand, CFCS communication.

Causes (3m W16, W17, S18, W19, W22)

Prenatal: maternal infection (rubella, cytomegalovirus, toxoplasmosis, Zika); multiple pregnancy; placental insufficiency; pre-eclampsia, thyroid; malformations; Rh incompatibility. Perinatal: prematurity (before 32 weeks), low birth weight; birth asphyxia ("delayed cry"); prolonged labour; kernicterus; neonatal infection. Postnatal (to 2 years): meningitis, encephalitis, head injury, near drowning, hypoglycaemia.

Pathophysiology to impairments

Primary: abnormal tone, weakness, poor selective control and balance, sensory loss. Secondary: muscle lags bone growth → contractures (calf, hamstrings, hip adductors, flexors); bony torsion; hip migration; scoliosis. Muscle grows only under stretch in daily use, so active use, standing, casting, orthoses through childhood. Tertiary: toe walking, crouch, W-sitting. Associated: epilepsy, intellectual disability, vision, hearing, swallowing, drooling, low bone density.

Features by type (7m S26; 3m W25, S26)

  • Diplegia: hips flexed, adducted, IR; knees flexed; equinus; sacral or W-sitting; scissoring, toe walking, crouch, in-toeing, arms in high guard; intelligence often normal.
  • Athetoid: writhing movements ↑ with effort and excitement; fluctuating tone; poor head and midline control, persistent ATNR; poor proximal stability, cannot hold or grade; extensor thrusts; drooling, unclear speech; hearing loss; late wide-based walking.
  • Hemiplegia: fisted hand, thumb in palm; equinovarus; hand preference before 12 months.
  • Spinal deviations, quadriplegia (W25): neuromuscular C-curve scoliosis, pelvic obliquity, kyphosis, ↑ lordosis with hip flexion contracture, windswept hips.
  • Hypotonic (S26): weak antigravity muscles; poor head and trunk control; hypermobile joints; frog-leg posture; very delayed milestones; weak cough; feeding difficulty.

Investigations and PT assessment

Diagnosis before 6 months: MRI + General Movements (absent fidgety) under 5 months; MRI + Hammersmith examination after. Hip X-ray migration percentage; EEG, hearing, vision; exclude progressive disease (loss of skills). Assess: birth history, milestones, family goals; GMFCS, GMFM; MAS, Modified Tardieu; reflexes; Thomas test, popliteal angle, DF knee straight and bent; gait; MACS.

Medical and surgical

Botulinum toxin + stretch, casting, orthoses, practice; baclofen (oral, intrathecal); selective dorsal rhizotomy (diplegia). Tendon lengthening, multilevel surgery, hip surgery, spinal fusion.

ICF (W22: 5-year-old athetoid girl, sits alone, cannot stand, at school)

  • Structure/function: basal ganglia lesion; involuntary movement; fluctuating tone; poor co-contraction; poor head and midline control, ATNR; poor standing balance; drooling; hip and spine risk.
  • Activity: cannot stand or walk; transfers, reaching, writing, feeding, toileting.
  • Participation: play with children, classroom and playground, outings.
  • Environment: motivated, well-off parents, school (facilitators); stairs, toilets, no adapted seat (barriers). Personal: 5, likely normal intelligence. Near GMFCS III–IV.

Goals: short-term (6–8 weeks): head midline while reaching; standing frame 60 min a day; sit to stand with help; grasp-release at midline. Long-term (6–12 months): bench sitting hands free 15 min; stands holding 1 min; walker in class; adapted spoon; no hip displacement.

Management (7m S19)

Family-centred, goal-directed, active task practice through play; home programme; prevent deformity. Effective (Novak 2019): CIMT, bimanual, goal-directed, treadmill, strength, fitness, casting, hippotherapy, home programmes, botulinum + therapy, hip surveillance. Not effective: passive NDT, sensory integration. Classic vs current: not inhibit-then-facilitate; handling only to set up active movement.

  • Diplegia gait (S18): stretch and cast calves, hamstrings; strengthen hip extensors, abductors, quads, calf → AFO; abduction in standing → weight shift, wide-base stepping → bars → posterior walker → treadmill ± body-weight support → outdoors.
  • Athetoid: proximal stability (prone on elbows, four-point, kneeling, standing frame, approximation, holding against resistance); midline play; slow small-range movement; seat with pelvic belt, 90–90–90.
  • Hypotonic (S22): prone over wedge; sitting with less support; approximation, tapping, bouncing on ball; standing box → cruising → walker; rolled towel, corner seat.
  • Hemiplegia: CIMT or bimanual, about 40 hours. Quadriplegia: seating, standing, hip and spine watch, chest care.
  • Standing: 60–90 min a day, 5 days a week (bone); 60 min in 30–60° abduction (hips); 45–60 min (range); 30–45 min (spasticity). Activity: 60 min moderate-to-vigorous, 5 days a week. Strength: 2–4 times a week, 1–3 × 6–15 at 50–85% 1RM, 12–16 weeks. Fitness: 20 min, 2–3 times a week, above 60% peak HR.

Complications and precautions

Hip displacement (GMFCS IV–V): surveillance from about age 2; refer if migration above 30% or abduction below 30°. Contractures (passive stretch alone not enough); scoliosis (seating); fractures; aspiration. Take care: seizure plan; no forceful range in non-walkers; do not force abduction; feed upright; skin under casts daily; after botulinum more practice.

Home care (W17) and prognosis

Side lying, prone over wedge, supported sitting; avoid W-sitting; feed upright; dress stiffer limb first; daily play; no baby walkers; picture home programme; school. Prognosis: ~90% of motor potential by 5 years; sitting by 2 years → walks (Molnar 1976).
