Asked: not on its own in 2016–2026; inside long cases as home-care advice: 2-year-old with spastic diplegia (5 marks, W17; see CP), 5-year-old with DMD (W16; see DMD). Possible 3 marks: energy conservation.

Ergonomic advice for patients

  • Posture: pelvis upright, feet and back supported; change position often; no long sustained postures.
  • Workstation: screen at eye level, elbows about 90°, forearms supported, chair and desk heights adjusted; wheelchair users: knee clearance, items within reach.
  • Energy conservation (MS, MND, myasthenia, post-polio, PD): plan, prioritise, pace, sit to work, rest before fatigue, heavy tasks when strongest; items within reach, trolleys; avoid heat (MS).
  • Joint protection: large joints and both hands, no sustained grips, built-up handles, trolleys; protect the hemiplegic shoulder.
  • Home layout: frequent items between hip and shoulder height; clear walkways, good light, rails, non-slip surfaces (adaptive devices).
  • Return to work: graded hours, task changes, rest breaks, travel plan, workplace visit.

Educating parents and caregivers

  • Carrying matched to tone: spastic: break the stiff pattern, hips bent and apart; athetoid: limbs together and bent, close to the carer.
  • Positioning through the day: side lying, prone on a wedge, supported sitting; no W-sitting, no long periods supine.
  • Feeding: upright, head slightly forward, trunk supported; coughing or chest infection → swallow assessment.
  • Dressing: stiffer or weaker limb first in, last out.
  • Adults: slide sheets, boards, hoists; never lift under the arms.
  • Home programme: short, written, illustrated, built into routines (bath, play, meals); demonstrate, then teach-back (carer shows it back).
  • Warning signs: shunt problems, autonomic dysreflexia, breathing trouble, skin redness.

Protecting the carer

Equipment rather than lifting; bed and chairs at working height; load close; no twisting. Lifting-technique training alone does not prevent back pain (Cochrane 2011): equipment and planning matter more. Carer strain: tiredness, low mood, back pain → involve family, respite, support groups.

Exam plan

Home-care advice (5 marks):

  1. Positioning and handling, day and night.
  2. Daily activities: feeding, dressing, bathing, toileting.
  3. Home programme built into routines; play.
  4. Equipment and home changes.
  5. Warning signs, follow-up; carer's own health.

CP parents (7m practice): side lying, wedge, supported sitting, no W-sitting; carrying by tone; feeding upright; stiffer limb first; play for head control, sitting, reaching; standing frame time; splint and skin care; no baby walkers; written programme shown back; warning signs.
