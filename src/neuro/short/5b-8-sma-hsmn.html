Asked: not asked on its own in 2016–2026. Expect 3 marks (types of SMA; features of CMT) or 7 marks (PT for either). See also anterior horn cell and neuropathies.

SMA: definition and types

Autosomal recessive loss of anterior horn cells → symmetrical, proximal LMN weakness. About 94%: deletion of both SMN1 copies (exon 7, chromosome 5); SMN2 copies modify severity. About 1 in 10,000 births; 1 in 40–50 carry it.

SMA: features

Symmetrical weakness, low tone, legs more than arms, proximal more than distal; absent reflexes; tongue fasciculations; hand tremor (minipolymyoclonus). Normal sensation and intelligence; face spared. Weak intercostals, working diaphragm → bell-shaped chest, belly breathing, weak cough, chest infections. Scoliosis, contractures, hip subluxation, feeding difficulty (type 1).

SMA: medical

Genetic test (SMN1 deletion), newborn screening. Disease-modifying drugs: nusinersen (intrathecal, 2016), onasemnogene abeparvovec (one-time gene therapy, 2019), risdiplam (oral, 2020). Treated babies reach new milestones, so PT now aims at gaining skills, not only preventing complications. Cost and access are barriers in India.

SMA: PT by functional level (2018 standards)

Non-sittersPositioning, supported seating, gentle ROM, splints, chest physiotherapy, airway clearance, cough assist and non-invasive ventilation as advised, developmental play
Sitters (type 2)Seating, powered wheelchair, spinal support, scoliosis watch, standing frame, orthoses, stretching hip, knee, ankle, arm function, respiratory care, aquatic play, review gains on drugs
WalkersStrength, endurance, balance without exhaustion, aquatic exercise, falls prevention, orthoses, wheelchair for distance

Precautions: no exhausting exercise; watch night-time breathing and weak cough; fragile bones, gentle handling.

HSMN (Charcot–Marie–Tooth): definition and types

Commonest inherited neuropathy (about 1 in 2500): slowly progressive damage to the longest motor and sensory nerves; usually autosomal dominant, onset in childhood or adolescence. CMT1 (HSMN I): demyelinating, very slow conduction; CMT1A (PMP22 duplication) commonest. CMT2 (HSMN II): axonal, near-normal velocity, small responses.

CMT: features

Distal leg weakness and wasting ("inverted champagne bottle"), later hands; pes cavus, clawed toes; foot drop, high-stepping gait; ankle sprains, falls; reduced or absent ankle reflexes; glove and stocking sensory loss; thickened nerves in some.

CMT: PT by problem

  • Foot drop, ankle instability: AFO (posterior leaf spring or carbon fibre); high-top supportive footwear.
  • Calf and foot tightness, cavus: daily calf and plantar fascia stretching; night splints; surgery if severe.
  • Weakness: moderate strengthening of hip, knee, proximal muscles; do not overwork very weak distal muscles.
  • Balance: training with vision and a stick; home safety. Hands: exercises, splints, built-up handles, button hooks. Numb feet: daily inspection, footwear, callus care.
