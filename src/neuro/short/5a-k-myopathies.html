Myasthenia gravis: definition and types

MG: autoimmune disease of the neuromuscular junction: antibodies block and destroy ACh receptors, so muscles weaken with use and recover with rest. AChR antibodies in ~85% of generalised MG; MuSK in some. Ocular or generalised (ocular usually generalises within 2 years). Peaks in younger women, older men; 10–15% thymoma.

MGFA: I eyes only; II mild; III moderate; IV severe (IVb feeding tube); V intubated. II–IV: a limb and trunk, b bulbar and breathing.

Features (3m W24)

  • Fatigable weakness: worse with repetition and by evening, better with rest.
  • Ptosis and diplopia (commonest first symptom).
  • Bulbar: nasal speech, chewing and swallowing difficulty, "snarling" smile.
  • Neck (head drop), proximal limbs; breathlessness lying flat; may fail suddenly (crisis).
  • Reflexes, sensation, bladder normal; wasting unusual. Triggers: infection, overexertion, heat, stress, surgery, some drugs.

Diagnosis, medicines, crisis

Antibodies; repetitive nerve stimulation (falling responses), single-fibre EMG; ice-pack test; chest CT. Pyridostigmine peaks 1–2 h after a dose; steroids, immunosuppressants; thymectomy (sternal precautions); IVIG or plasma exchange in crisis.

PT for MG (7m W19)

Assess strength before and after repetition, VC, cough, swallowing. ICF: fatigable weakness, ptosis → stairs, chair rise, reading → full work day; heat a barrier.

  • Exercise: moderate resistance and aerobic in stable MG, start low, short sets with rests; aim 150 min a week (Gilhus 2021).
  • Timing: 1–2 h after pyridostigmine, earlier in day, cool room.
  • IMT + breathing retraining: 3 times a week, 8 weeks, 20% → 60% MIP (Fregonezi 2005).
  • Airway clearance: huffing, assisted cough, positioning.
  • Energy conservation: plan, prioritise, pace, sit; rest before tired.
  • Soft collar; eyelid support or alternating eye patch; fall-proof home. After thymectomy: breathing, early walking, sternal precautions.

Classic vs current: not "rest and avoid exercise"; graded training is safe when stable.

Precautions (MG)

No exercise in flare, infection or crisis; stop if breathing, speech or swallowing worsen; avoid heat (hot packs, pools) and exhaustion; watch orthopnoea, weak cough, choking, falling VC; tell team before botulinum toxin.

Other muscle diseases

  • Lambert–Eaton: calcium-channel antibodies, ↓ ACh release; half small-cell lung cancer; proximal leg weakness, dry mouth; strength and reflexes improve briefly with effort.
  • Myositis: dermatomyositis (rash, screen for cancer); polymyositis (high CK); inclusion body (over 50, quads, finger flexors, falls). Active: ROM, isometrics, breathing; stable: progressive resistance, aerobic, functional.
  • Myotonic dystrophy 1: distal weakness, ptosis, myotonia (slow grip release; warm-up eases), cataracts, heart conduction: ECG follow-up, AFO.
  • FSHD: face (cannot whistle), winged scapulae. Limb-girdle: hip and shoulder girdles. Becker: milder dystrophin, calf enlargement, cardiac (DMD).

Rules: moderate aerobic and strength training safe (Cochrane 2019, low quality); no eccentric, high-intensity or exhausting work; review if pain, cramps or weakness last to next day or dark urine; screen heart, VC sitting and lying; orthoses early.
