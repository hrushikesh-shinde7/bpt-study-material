System and lesion signs (7m S17)

3 canals (rotation), utricle and saccule (linear, gravity), nerve, nuclei, cerebellum. VOR: eyes move opposite to the head, vision stays clear. Balance = vision + somatosensation + vestibular.

Signs: vertigo; nystagmus (peripheral: horizontal-torsional, beats away from the bad side, suppressed by fixation); imbalance, veers towards the bad side; oscillopsia (bilateral); nausea; hearing loss, tinnitus (Ménière's, labyrinthitis); anxiety, avoids head movement. Central (stroke, MS): direction-changing or vertical nystagmus, normal head impulse, cannot stand, diplopia, dysarthria. HINTS red flag: normal head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus or skew deviation → emergency referral.

Others: neuritis (days of vertigo, no hearing loss: early gaze and balance); labyrinthitis (+ hearing loss); Ménière's (hours, fluctuating hearing, tinnitus); bilateral loss (gentamicin: substitution); persistent postural-perceptual dizziness (habituation).

Tests (7m W24)

  • Head impulse: fix on your nose, quick 10–20° turns; catch-up saccade = weak VOR on that side.
  • Dix-Hallpike: sit, head 45° to one side, lie back, neck 20° below horizontal; torsional upbeating nystagmus after a delay, under a minute = posterior canal BPPV (lower ear).
  • Supine roll (head up 30°, turn 90°): horizontal canal. Dynamic visual acuity (head moved 2 per second): loss of more than 2 lines. Head-shaking 20 s. Fukuda 50 steps, turn more than 45°. Romberg, mCTSIB (4 conditions, 30 s, foam).
  • Dizziness Handicap Inventory; Motion Sensitivity Quotient (MSQ); Dynamic Gait Index; TUG.

BPPV (7m S20, W21, S24, S26)

Loose otoconia from the utricle into a canal, mostly posterior; brief vertigo (under a minute) on lying down, turning in bed, looking up; no hearing loss. Diagnose with Dix-Hallpike; if negative, other side; roll test if horizontal.

Epley (right): head 45° right → lie back, neck extended 20°, wait ~30 s → head 90° left → roll onto left side, nose down, ~30 s → sit up, chin down → repeat Dix-Hallpike. Semont: head 45° away → quickly onto affected side ~1 min → swing through to other side nose down ~1 min → sit slowly. Horizontal canal: barbecue (Lempert) roll, Gufoni. Brandt–Daroff: home, residual symptoms.

Classic vs current: no sleeping upright 48 h or collar: no postural restrictions (2017); no routine suppressants or imaging; reassess within 1 month.

Vestibular rehabilitation (7m S22, S24, S17, W25)

Start early (within 2 weeks of acute loss); mild symptoms in exercise are expected; stop suppressants early; supervised + home, progressed; add balance and gait.

AdaptationRetinal slip drives cerebellum to raise VOR gain. VOR ×1: fixed target, head moves; VOR ×2: target opposite to head. Progress speed, background, sitting → walking. One-sided loss
HabituationRepeated exposure to provoking movements (from the MSQ) a few times, twice a day; Brandt–Daroff. Motion sensitivity
SubstitutionVision and touch; look at target then turn head; stick, night lights. Bilateral loss

Dose (Hall 2022): acute: gaze 3 times a day, 12 min total; chronic: 3–5 times, 20 min daily, 4–6 weeks; bilateral: 20–40 min, 5–7 weeks, balance 6–9 weeks. Classic vs current: no eye movements with head still (Cawthorne–Cooksey start); use head movement. Balance: narrow base, foam, eyes closed; head turns while walking; dual task; crowds, stairs.

Precautions

Refer if headache, diplopia, slurred speech, weakness or central HINTS; before Dix-Hallpike screen neck and vertebral artery (side-lying test if unsafe); guard, vomit bowl; no driving until settled. Ménière's: not during attacks.
