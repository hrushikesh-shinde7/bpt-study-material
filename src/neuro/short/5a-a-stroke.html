Definition and types

Stroke (WHO): rapidly developing focal (or global) loss of brain function lasting more than 24 hours or causing death, of vascular origin. TIA: brief focal deficit without infarction on imaging: a warning, highest stroke risk in the next days. Hemiplegia is on the side opposite the lesion.

About 85% ischaemic in the West; a fifth to a third haemorrhagic in India. Phases: hyperacute 0–24 h, acute 1–7 days, early subacute to 3 months, late subacute 3–6 months, chronic after 6 months.

Circle of Willis (7m S20): ACAs + ACoA in front; ICA → ACA + MCA; PCoA joins ICA to PCA; vertebrals → basilar → PCAs. A safety link right–left and front–back; aneurysms at its junctions (ACoA).

Risk factors and pathophysiology

INTERSTROKE: 10 factors ≈ 90% of risk: hypertension (top), smoking, diabetes, inactivity, diet, stress, abdominal obesity, alcohol, AF, lipids. Young: RHD, venous thrombosis after childbirth, TB meningitis, dissection. India: 1 in 7 under 45.

  • Core dies in minutes; penumbra salvageable for hours ("time is brain").
  • UMN syndrome: negative (weakness, poor dexterity, fatigue); positive (spasticity, brisk reflexes, clonus, Babinski, associated reactions, synergies); adaptive (contracture, learned non-use, deconditioning).
  • Recovery: use-dependent plasticity; most spontaneous recovery in the first 3 months.

Features, synergies, pusher

BE-FAST: Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time. Flaccid stage first, then spasticity in antigravity muscles; sensory loss, hemianopia, aphasia, dysphagia, neglect, apraxia, shoulder pain, depression.

Pusher syndrome (3m S20, W24): pushes with the strong side towards the weak side and resists correction (right hemisphere, thalamus). Treat with visual vertical (mirror, door frame), reaching to the strong side, strong side against a wall.

Investigations and scales

CT first (excludes bleeding); MRI diffusion (early infarct); angiography, carotid Doppler; ECG, echo (AF); sugar, lipids, clotting. NIHSS 0–42; GCS.

Brunnstrom stages: 1 flaccid; 2 synergies begin; 3 voluntary synergy, spasticity peaks; 4 some movement out of synergy; 5 complex movement; 6 isolated, near normal. Scales: MAS, Fugl-Meyer (arm 66, leg 34), Berg (out of 56, below ~45 = fall risk), TUG, 10 m walk, 6MWT, Barthel, FIM.

Medical management

Stroke unit for all. Clot-busting (tenecteplase or alteplase) within 4.5 h (selected to 9 h); thrombectomy for large vessels within 6 h (selected to 24 h). Bleed: BP control, reverse anticoagulants, surgery. Nothing by mouth until a swallow screen. Craniectomy: no pressure on the bone flap side; helmet.

ICF (S23 worked case: 55, auto driver, left MCA, 3 months, diabetic)

  • Structure/function: left fronto-parietal infarct; right arm weakness (Brunnstrom 3–4), flexor spasticity MAS 1+–2, hand sensory loss, balance and gait deficit, possible aphasia, shoulder pain risk.
  • Activity: self-care with the right hand, community walking, climbing into the auto.
  • Participation: cannot drive his auto, income lost.
  • Environment: family, stick, rehab (facilitators); income loss, stairs, Indian toilet (barriers). Personal: sole earner; motivated by work.

Goals: short-term (4 weeks): cup to mouth; keep wrist and finger extension range; Berg +5; walks 200 m with stick. Long-term (3 months): bimanual self-care; community walking without stick; driving assessment.

Acute stage (7m S17)

  • Start when stable, short frequent sessions (AVERT: very early high-dose mobilisation in 24 h worsened outcome).
  • Positioning every 2 h: on the affected side (shoulder forward), on the unaffected side (arm on pillow), supine (pillow under shoulder and hip); head up 30° if swallow or chest risk.
  • Chest care; shoulder support, never pull the arm; daily pain-free ROM; rolling, sitting, sit to stand, standing; DVT: early mobility, IPC (stockings alone do not work).

Subacute and chronic

  • Principles: task-specific, repetitive, intensive, progressive, feedback, practice outside therapy; NICE: 3 h a day, 5 days a week if able.
  • Balance: reaching beyond arm's length, weight shift, stepping, perturbation; falls programme.
  • Gait: overground and treadmill (± body-weight support); AFO for foot drop.
  • Arm: task practice; CIMT if ≥20° wrist and ≥10° finger extension (EXCITE: mitt 90% of waking hours, 6 h practice, 2 weeks); mirror therapy; FES; bilateral training.
  • Spasticity: positioning, stretch, active use; botulinum toxin + therapy (Class 1).
  • Exercise dose (AHA 2014): aerobic 40–70% HRR (RPE 11–14), 3–5 days, 20–60 min; strength 1–3 × 10–15, 8–10 exercises, 2–3 days; flexibility 10–30 s holds.

Gait, shoulder, hand (the 7-mark trio)

Hemiplegic gait: stance: short, knee snaps back or stays bent, no hip extension, weak push-off; swing: stiff knee, foot drop and inversion, circumduction or hip hiking. Train: treat the cause (strengthen hip extensors, quads, DF; stretch calf) → part practice → whole walking overground and treadmill → AFO or peroneal FES → outdoors. Phases: parallel bars with knee support → break extensor synergy → community.

Shoulder: subluxation, pain, IR/add spasticity, shoulder-hand syndrome. Arm trough, scapula forward, never pull; pain-free ROM with scapula mobilised first; FES to supraspinatus and deltoid; botulinum toxin for spastic painful shoulder; no overhead pulleys (harmful, AHA/ASA 2026).

Spastic hand: slow stretch, weight-bearing on the open hand, prolonged icing, grasp-release practice, FES to extensors, mirror, CIMT, botulinum toxin; resting splints do not prevent spasticity. Flaccid arm: protect shoulder; Rood facilitation; weight-bearing; placing and holding; FES; mirror; bilateral. MRP arm (W18): analyse → practise missing parts → whole task → transfer.

Precautions and stop

Unit BP limits (avoid exercise if resting BP above ~200/110); swallow screen before food; protect the shoulder; craniectomy helmet; neglect and sensory loss: check limb position; anticoagulants: bruising; diabetes: sugar and feet. Stop: new weakness, speech or vision change, sudden headache, falling GCS, chest pain, seizure, DVT signs.

Complications: aspiration pneumonia, DVT, shoulder pain, contracture, pressure injury, falls, depression, CRPS, learned non-use.

Education and prognosis

Secondary prevention: daily BP, sugar, lipid and antiplatelet medicines; stop all tobacco (bidi, gutka); less salt; exercise; BE-FAST. Carer: transfers, shoulder support; commode or raised seat for an Indian toilet; picture home programme. EPOS: finger extension + shoulder abduction at 72 h → 98% regain some dexterity (25% without). Poor outcome: older, severe, early coma, neglect, poor sitting balance, incontinence.
