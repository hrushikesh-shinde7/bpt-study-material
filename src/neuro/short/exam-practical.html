Practical exam: 80 marks + 20 internal assessment. Long case 45, short case 2 × 10, spots 5 × 2, journal 5; marked in cognitive, psychomotor and affective domains. (Terminal and prelim: spots 5 × 3 = 15, journal not marked.)

Marking scheme

Long case 45(a) subjective and physical examination 10; (b) evaluation and PT diagnosis (ICF) 10; (c) plan of care and goals 10; (d) demonstrate one test and one treatment on the patient 15
Short case 20Two PT intervention skills, 10 each
Spots 105 × 2, 3 min each: EMG and nerve conduction, orthoses, prostheses, assessment tools and scales
Journal 5Assessment, evaluation, ICF diagnosis, prognosis, intervention, outcome

Long case proforma

Subjective: demographics, dominance, occupation, family, home (stairs, toilet type), referral; chief complaints in the patient's words; HPI (onset, progression, investigations, treatment); past, personal, family, social history; drugs; red flags; the patient's own goals.

Objective: vitals, GCS, posture, deformity, skin, aids; higher functions, cranial nerves, sensation, tone (MAS), power (MRC or voluntary control), reflexes, coordination, balance (Berg, TUG), gait, function (Barthel, FIM). Condition-specific: ISNCSCI (SCI), Hoehn and Yahr (PD), GMFCS (CP), muscle chart (LMN). Then: investigations → ICF table and functional diagnosis → problems → goals → plan → outcome measures.

Presenting (3–5 min)

  1. One sentence: "Mr A, 54, bank manager, right MCA infarct 2 weeks ago, now..."
  2. Key positive findings in ICF order: body functions and structures, activities, participation, environmental and personal factors.
  3. Functional diagnosis in one line; 2–3 priority problems with short- and long-term goals.
  4. Plan → demonstrate the test and treatment (part d, 15 marks): explain, position, perform, check the response.

Match them to the main problem: MAS + sit-to-stand training (stroke); ISNCSCI key muscles + sliding-board transfer (paraplegia).

Short case: 2 skills × 10

Skills: sensory testing and re-education; muscle testing or voluntary control and re-education; electrical modalities (electrotherapy); tone management, facilitation and inhibition (tone); posture; transfers; functional re-education; gait (gait); coordination and balance (balance); fitness; outcome measures, QoL questionnaires.

Steps: introduce, explain, consent; check precautions (BP, skin, pain) → position patient and self; drape; guard → clear commands, feedback, show progression → re-check response; state dose (sets, repetitions, frequency) and why.

Spots: what to say

EMG, nerve conductionNormal or not; neurogenic or myopathic; demyelinating or axonal; likely condition (slow conduction, absent F-waves = GBS); PT meaning
OrthosisName (AFO type, KAFO, cock-up, knuckle-bender), parts, principle, indication, one precaution (orthoses)
ProsthesisLevel; socket, suspension, pylon, foot; indication (diabetic foot); one gait problem
ToolReflex hammer, 128 Hz tuning fork, two-point discriminator, goniometer: what it tests, how
ScaleName, measures, scoring, interpretation (GCS, MAS, ASIA, Berg, Hoehn and Yahr, Barthel)

Journal, affective domain, last two weeks

Journal: 8 cases with ICF: 4 UMN (stroke, SCI, brain injury, degenerative or demyelinating), 2 LMN (nerve or plexus, GBS), 2 paediatric (CP, myopathy, meningocele); measures at start and end; neat, signed, up to date (any case may be asked).

Affective domain: introduction, consent, dignity, draping (caring); safety: guarding, gait belt, brakes on, BP and skin, stop for warning signs; hand hygiene; tidy equipment; thank the patient; "I would check with the team" rather than guessing.

Last two weeks: proforma on classmates in under 20 min; present stroke, SCI, CP aloud in ICF order; practise each skill with commands and doses aloud; spots with real reports, orthoses, scales; finish the journal.
