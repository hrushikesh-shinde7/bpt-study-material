Asked: not asked on its own. Expect a 3-mark question on Hoover's sign, features of functional paralysis or functional weakness vs malingering, or a 7-mark question on physiotherapy for functional paralysis.

Definition and types

Functional neurological disorder (FND) (older: conversion disorder, hysteria, psychosomatic paralysis): a problem of how the nervous system works, not its structure; real, involuntary weakness, abnormal movements, seizures or numbness without structural damage. Common; often after a trigger (injury, illness, pain, panic, stress). Can coexist with other disease (functional seizures with epilepsy).

Not malingering (deliberate faking for gain: money, leave) nor factitious disorder (producing symptoms to be a patient). People with FND are not pretending.

Types: functional weakness (one limb, one side, both legs); movement disorders (tremor, fixed dystonia: clenched fist, inverted ankle; jerks; gait); dissociative seizures; sensory, dizziness, cognitive; with fatigue and pain.

Positive signs (diagnosis is positive, not only exclusion)

Hoover's signHip extension of the weak leg weak on direct test, returns to normal when the other hip flexes against resistance (automatic); heel presses into examiner's hand
Hip abductor sign"Weak" leg abducts strongly when the other leg abducts
Give-way weaknessNormal power, then sudden collapse
Drift without pronationArm drifts down without pronation (pyramidal drift pronates)
Dragging gaitLeg dragged behind, hip turned in or out; not circumducted
Entrainment (tremor)Tremor takes up the rhythm copied by the other hand, pauses or turns irregular
DistractibilityLess when attention is elsewhere

Reflexes, tone and plantars normal; no early wasting.

PT for functional motor disorders (Nielsen 2015)

  • Explain positively: symptoms real, nervous system not damaged, can relearn; show the positive sign (normal movement in Hoover's).
  • Set expectations; agree goals.
  • Retrain with attention directed away from the limb: automatic movements, rhythm, dual tasks, music, imagery; start with what works (walking backwards, sideways, treadmill) → normal patterns.
  • Minimal aids, braces and hands-on support; withdraw gradually.
  • Self-management: daily practice, pacing, setback plan, return to work.
  • Team: psychology (CBT), psychiatry for anxiety, depression, trauma.

Classic vs current: not "nothing is wrong, refer to psychiatry"; physio is core. Physio4FMD 2024: no better 12-month function score than community physio, but more felt improved; likely cost-effective.

Practice case (28, clerk, right leg after a fall, stick): short-term walks indoors without stick; long-term normal outdoor walking, back to work; treadmill, rhythm, backwards, dual tasks; stick withdrawn; graded return to work.

Chronic pain with psychological factors

Central sensitisation; worse with fear, low mood, poor sleep, inactivity; pain is real. Pain education, graded activity and graded exposure, exercise, pacing, sleep and stress strategies, cognitive-behavioural approach; fewer passive treatments. Screen red flags first; refer if distress high.

Exam plan (7 marks)

Define FND vs malingering (1) → positive signs (1–2) → explanation, goals, retraining with redirected attention, minimal aids, graded progression, self-management, team (4). Draw: Hoover's test, hand under the heel of the "weak" leg while the other leg lifts. Underline: real and involuntary, not malingering, positive signs, Hoover's, entrainment, redirected attention, self-management.
