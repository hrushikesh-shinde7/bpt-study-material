Definition and stages (3m S24)

Polio: poliovirus destroys anterior horn cells → sudden, asymmetrical flaccid paralysis, normal sensation. PPRP: permanent weakness, wasting, deformity once recovery stops, about 2 years after the illness. Not post-polio syndrome (new problems after at least 15 stable years). Faecal–oral, children under 5; 1 in 200 infections paralyse. India polio-free since 2014.

Acute (weeks)Fever, painful spasm, paralysis peaks in days → neutral positioning, no frog position, splints, warmth, gentle passive movement, chest care
Recovery (to 2 years)Neurons sprout, most in 6 months → muscle chart, graded re-education, orthoses, stretching
Residual (after 2 years)No recovery; imbalance and growth → deformity, shortening

Pathophysiology and features

Patchy: tibialis anterior, quadriceps, deltoid, opponens hit hardest. Sprouted large motor units may fail later. Strong vs weak muscle + gravity → contracture; flail joints; short, thin, cold bluish limb. Legs more than arms; reflexes lost; bladder normal. Gaits: hand-on-thigh (quads), Trendelenburg, backward lurch, high-stepping, short-limb.

Deformities (7m W22: any three)

Hip flexion, abduction, ERTight iliotibial band (ITB) (frog position); Thomas, Ober +; pelvic obliquity, lumbar scoliosis → prone lying, stretch, release
Knee flexion; valgusTight hamstrings, ITB; crouch → serial casts, release; valgus + tibial ER → Yount's release
Genu recurvatumWeak quads, knee locked back (+ equinus); leave mild; over about 15° brace or osteotomy
EquinusWeak tibialis anterior, working calf; drop, tripping → stretch, AFO, transfer
CalcaneusWeak calf; heel walking → AFO, transfer or fusion
Scoliosis; armWeak trunk, obliquity; flail shoulder, no opposition

Assessment and surgery

Muscle chart (MRC 0–5, both sides, dated); Thomas, Ober's, popliteal angle, recurvatum angle; true and apparent length; scoliosis; gait, 6MWT. Surgery after 2 years: release; tendon transfer only if donor grade 4–5 (loses about one grade); osteotomy, arthrodesis; shoe raise (ignore under 2 cm); spinal fusion.

PT for PPRP (7m W18)

  • Grade 3+: progressive resisted, 8–12 reps, 2–3 days a week, short of fatigue. Grade 1–2: assisted, gravity-eliminated, hydrotherapy.
  • Stretch ITB, hip flexors, hamstrings, calf; daily prone lying; serial casts, night splints.
  • Orthoses: AFO (leaf spring) for drop; floor-reaction orthosis if quads grade 3 or less, no deformity; KAFO, drop-lock if hip weak too; shoe raise over 2 cm; crutches.
  • Gait, stairs, transfers; tendon transfer: pre-strengthen donor, then ask for its old action, biofeedback; AFO about 6 months after foot transfers.
  • Current: leave mild recurvatum or equinus that stabilises a weak knee.

Complications (7m S25) and precautions

Contractures (stretch, casts, orthoses); instability (strengthen, floor-reaction or KAFO); length difference (shoe raise); scoliosis, obliquity (trunk work, seating, breathing); osteoporosis, femur fractures (weight bearing, falls prevention, gentle force); overuse pain, arthritis (joint protection, crutch height, pacing); cold limb (warmth).

Post-polio syndrome (Halstead): confirmed paralytic polio; recovery; 15+ stable years; 2+ new problems (new weakness, fatigue, pain, wasting, cold intolerance); no other cause. EFNS: supervised aerobic and isometric training safe (Level B); warm water. Take care: no exercise to exhaustion (reduce if fatigue lasts to next day); skin under orthoses; refer new weakness, breathing or swallowing problems.
