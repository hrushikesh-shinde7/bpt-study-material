Asked: not asked on its own (2016–2026). It sits inside motor relearning questions (S18, W18), long cases ("management with emphasis on trunk control and ambulation"), hemiplegic gait and the spastic hand. Expect 3 marks on the components of sit to stand or compensations in reaching.

How movement fails

Impairments: weakness, slowness, loss of dexterity and coordination, altered tone, sensory loss, shortened soft tissue, poor balance. Adaptations (compensations): uses the stronger side, the arms, a wider base or a stiff trunk; some are useful, others block recovery (learned non-use, all weight on the strong leg).

Training rule: analyse the task → find missing components → practise them → whole task many times in varied real settings → measure (MRP, motor learning).

Bed, sitting up, standing up

  • Bed mobility: head turn, arm across, top hip and knee flex, trunk rotation; bridging to move up and onto a bedpan. Problems: weak limbs not brought across; pulls on the rail; weak hip extensors. Train: roll both ways with weak limbs placed to help; bridge with the weak foot loaded; less rail.
  • Lying to sitting: roll to side lying, legs over the edge, push up through the lower arm while side-flexing neck and trunk up. Problems: pulls straight up with the strong arm; legs stay on the bed; poor sitting balance. Train: each part, then whole; vary bed height and side; lie down with control.
  • Sit to stand: feet back behind the knees; trunk forward by hip flexion, straight spine; knees forward over the feet; hips and knees extend together; sit down reverses with control. Problems: weight on the strong leg, weak foot forward, too little lean, arm pushing, drops into the chair. Train: weak foot back; higher seat, then lower; repetitions in sets; step-ups; vary chair, bed, toilet, car; five times sit-to-stand test.

Walking and balance

  • Walking: stance: hip extension, small knee bend at loading then extension, roll over the ankle, push-off; swing: knee bend, hip flexion, dorsiflexion to clear; small pelvic shift. Problems: short stance on the weak leg, knee hyperextension or buckling, little push-off, stiff knee, foot drop, circumduction, slow. Train: intensive varied walking, treadmill, stepping drills, strengthening, AFO when needed; speed and distance.
  • Balance: centre of mass over base, still and moving; anticipatory adjustments; reactive steps; vision, vestibular, body sensation. Problems: wide base, weight on the strong side, stiffening, holding on, fear of falling. Train: reaching beyond arm's length sitting and standing, stepping all directions, varied surfaces, perturbations, dual tasks; Berg, TUG, Mini-BESTest.

Reaching and manipulation

  • Reaching: shoulder flexion with scapular upward rotation and protraction, elbow extension, wrist ready; trunk only beyond arm's length. Problems: shoulder hitch, elbow stays bent, trunk leans forward, uses the other hand. Train: targets at varied heights and distances; trunk restraint (Michaelsen 2006); strengthen shoulder and elbow; real objects.
  • Manipulation: wrist extension during grasp; hand opens to the object; thumb opposition; grip force scaled; release; in-hand movement. Problems: weak wrist extensors → weak grip; hand will not open; poor opposition, force, release. Train: graded objects (size, weight, texture), wrist extension in function, CIMT or intensive bimanual practice, splints only for a stated aim.

Exam plan (training any task)

Essential components → what is missing and how the patient compensates → practise missing parts, then whole task with repetition and variety, feedback → measure. 7m sit to stand in hemiplegia: analyse, problems, training list above, timed test.
