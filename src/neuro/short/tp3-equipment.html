Asked: not asked on its own in 2016–2026. Expect a practical demonstration or a 3-mark note on the uses of one piece of equipment (ball, wedge); a 7-mark "equipment to train balance" is possible.

The named equipment: what each trains

Therapy (vestibular, Swiss) ballPostural, equilibrium and protective reactions, trunk control, vestibular input; relaxation by slow rocking
Tilt and wobble boardsEquilibrium (tilting) reactions, ankle and hip strategies, sitting and standing balance
Bolsters and rollsArm weight bearing, hip abduction (straddle sitting), positioning, trunk control
WedgesHead and upper-trunk control in prone; positioning
Graded benches and stepsSit to stand, step-ups, stairs, seated balance at different heights
Therapeutic matsBed and floor mobility: rolling, crawling, kneeling, getting up from the floor

Activities and progression

  • Ball: child prone over the ball (head and arm weight bearing); sitting with weight shifts; tip slowly → protective arm extension. Progress: larger, faster tilts, less support; peanut ball for more stability.
  • Boards: rocker tilts one direction, wobble all directions. Sit, kneel or stand on it. Progress: sitting → standing, eyes open → closed, holding on → free hands, add reaching or catching.
  • Bolster: prone over a roll for elbow and hand weight bearing; straddle sitting reduces adductor tone (spastic diplegia) and trains sitting balance; side-to-side rolling for reactions.
  • Wedge: prone with forearm support, toys in front (head lifting); supported half-lying for feeding or chest care. Progress: lower wedge → flat prone.
  • Bench: sit to stand from a higher bench, lowered stepwise; step-ups on graded steps; stable seat for reaching.
  • Mat programme: rolling → prone on elbows → four-point kneeling → kneeling → half-kneeling → floor to stand; push-ups in long sitting for SCI.

Other common equipment

Parallel bars: early standing, walking, weight shift; move to a walker or stick as soon as possible. Tilt table: gradual uprighting for orthostatic hypotension, early weight bearing, calf stretch. Standing frame, prone stander: supported standing. Treadmill (± body-weight support): repetitive walking, endurance. Mirror: posture and symmetry feedback; mirror therapy after stroke. Foam pads, beams, cones, obstacles: unstable, narrow-base and obstacle walking. Weights, bands, pulleys, static cycles: strength and fitness. Hand boards, pegboards, putty: grasp, release, dexterity.

Safety and good practice

  • Guard on unstable equipment; gait belt; clear space; mats under balls and boards.
  • Ball properly inflated and right size: hips and knees about 90° when sitting.
  • No fast spinning or rocking if prone to seizures or autonomic problems; watch dizziness and BP.
  • Down's syndrome with atlantoaxial instability: no forced neck flexion or trampoline-type bouncing.
  • Clean between patients; latex-free balls and bands where latex allergy is possible (spina bifida).
  • Equipment supports the task; the goal is a real-life skill without it.

Exam plan

3 marks (uses of one piece): description → what it trains with two activities and a progression → one precaution.

7 marks (balance equipment): balls (sitting reactions); rocker and wobble boards; foam and beams; parallel bars; steps and benches; cones; mirror → progress support, base, eyes, surface, speed, dual task → safety: guarding, gait belt, mats.

Practice LAQ (diplegic child, 4 years, sits but cannot stand): roll with forearm weight bearing 5 min → bolster straddle 10 → ball tilts 10 → mat kneeling and half-kneeling 10 → graded bench sit to stand 10 → standing frame, then rocker board with hands held 10 → stretches and parent teaching 5.

Spots: identify and demonstrate ball, peanut ball, rocker and wobble boards, bolster, wedge, steps, mat, standing frame, tilt table. Underline: equilibrium reactions, protective reactions, weight bearing, graded progression, mat programme, guarding, task transfer.
