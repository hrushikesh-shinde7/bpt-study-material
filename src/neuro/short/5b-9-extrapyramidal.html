Types of involuntary movement

DystoniaSustained or intermittent contractions → twisting, repetitive movements or abnormal postures; worse on trying to move
AthetosisSlow, continuous, writhing, mostly hands and feet
ChoreaBrief, irregular, flowing jerks moving between body parts
BallismusLarge flinging of a whole limb
TremorRhythmic oscillation at rest, in posture or on action
TicsSudden repetitive movements or sounds, can be held back for a while
MyoclonusSudden shock-like jerks

All from disordered basal ganglia circuits (with cortex, cerebellum); usually gone in sleep, worse with effort, emotion, excitement.

Dyskinetic CP (W25; 7m S26)

Commonest childhood extrapyramidal disorder: dystonic or choreo-athetoid movements, fluctuating tone, poor head and trunk control, drooling, unclear speech, often normal intelligence. Linked to severe birth asphyxia (deep grey matter) and kernicterus.

Other childhood disorders

  • Kernicterus: very high unconjugated bilirubin damages basal ganglia, brainstem nuclei, hearing pathways → athetosis, limited upward gaze, sensorineural hearing loss, enamel defects; intelligence often spared. Prevent: early jaundice detection, phototherapy, exchange transfusion. PT as dyskinetic CP; check hearing early; vision and gesture.
  • Wilson's disease (hepatolenticular degeneration): autosomal recessive copper build-up in liver and basal ganglia; tremor ("wing-beating"), dystonia, slurred speech, drooling, parkinsonism, poor handwriting, mood change; Kayser–Fleischer rings; liver disease. Lifelong copper-removing drugs or zinc; transplant; early treatment improves much. PT: stable positions, weighted or built-up utensils, task practice, balance and gait, speech and swallow referral.
  • Sydenham's chorea: rheumatic fever, weeks to months after a streptococcal throat infection; school age; chorea (often one side worse), low tone, "milkmaid's" grip, emotional lability; settles over weeks to months; long-term antibiotics. PT: safety, rest, graded return, handwriting support, check heart before strenuous exercise.
  • Early-onset generalised dystonia: starts in a leg or foot, spreads; deep brain stimulation of globus pallidus. Dopa-responsive dystonia: leg dystonia worse in the evening, better after sleep; dramatic response to low-dose levodopa (trial in every unexplained dystonia). Tourette: multiple motor tics + one vocal tic over a year; PT advice only.

PT principles for involuntary movements

  • Stable base first: seating with pelvic belt, foot and trunk supports; standing frame; work where steady.
  • Midline and symmetry: hands together, head midline, toys in front.
  • Graded sustained holding, weight bearing through arms and legs → proximal stability.
  • Task practice in stable positions; less effort and excitement (overflow); calm surroundings.
  • Wrist and thumb splints, weighted or built-up utensils, switches, communication aids.
  • Team: anticholinergics, baclofen, botulinum toxin (focal dystonia); speech and feeding; family.
