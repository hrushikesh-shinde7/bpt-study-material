Asked: 9 of 20 papers. 7 marks: types of bladder and PT (W25, S26), classify and manage one (S21), spastic vs flaccid with PT (W16, W17). 3 marks: three types (S17), pathological bladders (W20, S23), reflex bladder (W22). Long case: bladder type after T9–T10 fracture (S26).

Definition and control

Neurogenic bladder: abnormal storage or emptying from a brain, cord or nerve lesion (SCI, MS, stroke, PD, spina bifida, cauda equina, diabetes). Risks: infection, stones, dysreflexia, kidney damage from high pressure. Sympathetic (T10–L2) stores; parasympathetic (pelvic, S2–S4) contracts the detrusor; pudendal (S2–S4) holds the external sphincter; pontine centre co-ordinates emptying.

Types (3m S17, W20, W22, S23)

Lesion level (EAU 2026): brain → overactivity, little residual; cord → overactivity + detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia (DSD); sacral and below → weak detrusor, retention.

UninhibitedBrain: sensation present; small; urgency, urge leaks; empties fully
ReflexComplete cord lesion above S2: no sensation; reflex emptying; DSD
AutonomousSacral cord, cauda equina, spinal shock: large; overflow
Sensory paralyticDiabetes, tabes, B12: no fullness sense; large residual
Motor paralyticPolio, root injury: feels full, cannot empty

Reflex bladder (W22): sacral arc intact, cut off from the brain → reflex contraction against a tight sphincter → high pressure; CIC + detrusor-calming drugs. S26 case: reflex (UMN) bladder; fullness sense = spared sensory fibres.

Assessment and PT (7m S21, W25, S26)

  • Assess: bladder diary ≥ 3 days; residual urine; urodynamics; hand function and sitting balance for self-catheterisation; toilet transfers; home (Indian squat toilet not possible for most paraplegics).
  • CIC (preferred after SCI): 4–6 times a day, volume under 400–500 mL; plan fluids; 12–16 Fr, hydrophilic. Teach hygiene, long sitting or wheelchair position, balance with both hands free, recording; C6–C7: adapted aids.
  • Reflex: CIC + antimuscarinics (botulinum toxin if needed); sheath only if pressure is safe. Areflexic: CIC; indwelling or suprapubic only if CIC impossible. Sensory paralytic: void by the clock.
  • Uninhibited: timed voiding by the clock; urge suppression (still, quick pelvic floor squeezes, walk calmly); PFMT supervised ≥ 3 months (MS, stroke, incomplete SCI); tibial nerve stimulation may help; faster transfers, bedside commode, easy clothing; less tea and coffee late.
  • Current: tapping, Credé, Valsalva raise pressure: only if urodynamics show it is safe.

Bowel programme

Reflexic (UMN, above conus): tight sphincter, constipation → daily or ≥ 3 times a week; suppository, digital rectal stimulation, upright on a commode. Areflexic (LMN): slack sphincter, leaks → daily; manual removal, firmer stool. Same time daily after a meal (gastrocolic reflex); Bristol 3–4; under 30 min, never over 1 h. Physio: sitting balance, commode transfers, aids for weak hands. Current: no abdominal massage or Valsalva (2020).

Precautions

SCI T6 and above: full bladder or bowel → autonomic dysreflexia; empty before exercise. Infection: fever, cloudy or smelly urine, more spasms or leaks. Keep skin dry; never clamp or block tubing on the tilt table or in transfers.
