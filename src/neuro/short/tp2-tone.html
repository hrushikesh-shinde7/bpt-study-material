Tone: definition and types (3m S20)

Tone: resistance of a relaxed muscle to passive stretch; neural (stretch reflex, descending control) + non-neural (muscle, connective tissue stiffness). Normal: holds posture, allows selective movement.

SpasticityVelocity-dependent, clasp-knife, brisk reflexes, clonus; UMN (stroke, SCI, MS, CP)
RigiditySame at any speed, lead-pipe or cogwheel; basal ganglia (PD)
DystoniaContractions, twisting postures; dyskinetic CP
HypotoniaFloppy, hypermobile, ↓ reflexes; LMN, cerebellum, early stroke, spinal shock, Down's
FluctuatingLow to high; athetoid CP

Spasticity (Lance 1980): velocity-dependent ↑ tonic stretch reflexes with exaggerated jerks, part of the UMN syndrome; negative features (weakness, poor dexterity) limit function more.

Modified Ashworth (3m S19, W21) and Tardieu

0 no increase; 1 catch and release or minimal resistance at end of range; 1+ catch then minimal resistance through less than half the remaining range; 2 marked through most of range, moves easily; 3 considerable, passive movement difficult; 4 rigid. Test relaxed, full range in ~1 second, max 3 times, grade the first. Tardieu: R2 slow full range, R1 fast catch; large gap = dynamic (stretch, cast, toxin); small gap = contracture.

Reducing tone (7m S19, W20; 3m S23, W18)

Treat when a problem (pain, hygiene, contracture, seating); extensor tone may help standing. Triggers first: pain, full bladder, UTI, constipation, sores, ingrown toenails, tight clothes.

  • Positioning opposite the spastic pattern.
  • Prolonged stretch held minutes: positioning, splint, standing frame, cast.
  • Weight-bearing through extended arm; plantigrade standing (stretch + compression).
  • Slow icing (W18): wrapped ice pack or immersion 15–20 min; slows conduction, calms spindle; use window to stretch and practise (quick icing facilitates).
  • Neutral warmth, slow stroking; rhythmic rotation; serial casts, resting splint, AFO.
  • Electrical stimulation of antagonist (reciprocal inhibition); active task practice, strengthen antagonists.

Three (S23): prolonged stretch, slow icing, weight-bearing. Team: baclofen, tizanidine, dantrolene; botulinum toxin + stretch, splint, task practice (AHA/ASA 2026); intrathecal baclofen. Classic vs current: stretch under 7 months: no important effect on mobility, little on spasticity (Cochrane).

Facilitation and hypotonia (3m W16; 7m W17, W24)

  • Quick stretch, tapping before movement (spindle).
  • Approximation in weight-bearing (co-contraction); light graded resistance (irradiation).
  • Fast brushing, quick icing (Rood); high-frequency vibration (tonic vibration reflex).
  • Weight-bearing, bouncing on a ball; fast vestibular input; PNF repeated stretch, rhythmic stabilisation; electrical stimulation.

Hypotonia PT: protect hypermobile joints (shoulder support, no overstretch); supported anti-gravity positions (prone over wedge, sitting, standing frame); facilitation just before active movement; co-contraction in kneeling and standing; head and trunk control, sit-to-stand, walking; carer handling.

Precautions

Cold: check sensation and circulation; avoid in Raynaud's. Never force a spastic joint. Facilitation can raise tone elsewhere: stop. Fast vestibular: not in seizures, raised ICP, nausea. Splints and casts: check skin daily.
