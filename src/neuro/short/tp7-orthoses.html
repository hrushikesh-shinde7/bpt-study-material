Asked: 5 of 20 papers. 7 marks: foot orthoses for CP children (S22). 3 marks: AFO (W21), orthosis for radial nerve injury (W17), principle and uses of dynamic cock-up (S25), cock-up splint (W25).

Definition, aims, principles

Orthosis: external device that supports, aligns, protects or improves function of a body part. Static holds still; dynamic allows or assists. Aims: function, stabilise, prevent deformity, protect, position.

  • Three-point force system: two forces one side, one opposite between. Foot-drop AFO: footplate lifts forefoot, strap at front of ankle, calf shell at back of upper calf.
  • Pressure = force ÷ area: wide contact protects numb skin. Ground reaction force vs knee decides bend or straighten.

Prescription and check-out

Assess ROM (fixed or flexible), tone, strength, sensation, gait → aim in ICF terms → design with the orthotist → check fit, pressure, shoe → train donning, gradual wearing schedule → review skin, growth; measure gait speed, falls.

AFO (3m W21) and choosing one

Parts: calf section and strap, ankle (solid or hinged), footplate, in a shoe. Uses: foot drop (stroke, peroneal injury, MS, CMT), spastic equinus, ankle instability, crouch, knee hyperextension. AFOs raise stroke walking speed; peroneal FES as good as an AFO (Bethoux 2014).

Foot orthoses in CP (7m S22)

Solid AFO (poor control, non-walkers, night); hinged with plantarflexion stop (walkers, enough calf length); ground-reaction (crouch, good passive range); leaf spring (mild foot drop); supramalleolar (flat valgus); dynamic thin AFO (mild tone). Aims: stable base, control equinus, gait, range. Review with growth; add stretching, strengthening. See CP.

Making a temporary splint

Position → paper pattern (cock-up: two-thirds of forearm to palmar crease) → cut low-temperature thermoplastic → heat in water at 65–70 °C (1–2 min) → test on own skin, mould over stockinette till set → trim, flare, pad, straps → check red areas; spot-heat → teach schedule, keep from heat. Serial casting: changed every few days.

Precautions and current evidence

Skin check after every use (lasting redness, blisters, numbness → adjust); allow for fixed deformity; lightest that works; review children every few months. Current: sustained stretch by splints or casts has no important effect on joint mobility (Cochrane 2017): prescribe for a stated functional aim; active use and positioning prevent contracture.
