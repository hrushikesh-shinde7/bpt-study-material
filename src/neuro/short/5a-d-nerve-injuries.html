Definition and classification (3m S21, W21, S22, S23, S25; 7m S16)

Peripheral nerve injury: LMN loss in its area: weakness, wasting, lost reflexes, sensory loss, lost sweating; can regrow, slowly, from the injury site down. Plexus injury: C5–T1 roots, trunks or cords, usually traction.

Sunderland (Seddon)Damage; recovery
I (neurapraxia)Myelin, conduction block, axon intact, no Wallerian degeneration; full in days to ~3 months
II (axonotmesis)Axon cut, endoneurium intact; full at 1 mm a day
III+ endoneurium; perineurium intact; incomplete (misdirection)
IVOnly epineurium intact (neuroma in continuity); needs surgery
V (neurotmesis)Nerve divided; none without repair

Causes: radial: shaft of humerus, Saturday night palsy, crutch; ulnar: elbow fracture, leprosy; median: wrist cuts, carpal tunnel; axillary: shoulder dislocation; peroneal: fibular neck, tight plaster, squatting; sciatic: gluteal injection.

Pathophysiology

  • Axon cut → Wallerian degeneration below → flaccid, wasted muscle; dry, shiny, numb skin.
  • Regrows 1 mm a day (2.5 cm a month): proximal muscles first; Tinel's sign moves down the limb.
  • Long delay → end-plates degenerate, so proximal injuries recover less; misdirected axons → poor localisation.

Claw hand (7m W19): MCP hyperextension + IP flexion: lumbricals and interossei paralysed, long extensors and flexors unopposed. Ulnar: ring and little; median + ulnar: all four. Also Klumpke's, leprosy, Volkmann's.

Brachial plexus (Erb's 7m S17, S21, W21, W24; Klumpke's 7m S25)

  • Erb's (C5–C6): head and shoulder forced apart (two-wheeler fall). Lost: abduction (deltoid, supraspinatus), ER (infraspinatus), elbow flexion (biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis), supination. "Waiter's tip": adducted, IR, elbow extended, pronated; outer arm numb; biceps jerk lost.
  • Klumpke's (C8–T1): arm pulled overhead (grabbing a branch). Lost: intrinsics, finger and wrist flexors; claw hand; inner forearm numb; Horner's if T1 avulsed.
  • Root avulsion: Horner's, winged scapula, raised hemidiaphragm, burning pain; SNAP preserved in the numb area (dorsal root ganglion still joined); no recovery without nerve transfer. Postganglionic: Tinel's in the neck, SNAP absent, may recover.

S22 level (2m): no fist (C8), elbow extension (C7) or thumb (C8–T1); inner arm and forearm numb → lower plexus, C7–T1; check Horner's.

Investigations and medical management

NCS and EMG: fibrillations from 2–3 weeks; repeat to show reinnervation. MRI or CT myelography for avulsion. Degree I–II: splints, exercise, watch. Clean cut: early repair; gap: sural graft. Plexus: nerve transfer best within 6 months (about 3 usual); late: tendon transfers.

Assessment and ICF (W16: 35, shopkeeper, right Erb's, 5 months)

Assess: dominant hand, job; wasting, skin; MMT proximal to distal, trick movements; sensory map, Tinel's level; Froment's, Phalen's; grip, Nine-Hole Peg, DASH.

  • Structure/function: weak deltoid, spinati, biceps, brachialis, supinator; waiter's tip, subluxation risk; tight IR and adductors; outer arm numb.
  • Activity: cannot lift to shelves, carry goods, comb, eat with right hand; the hand works.
  • Participation: running his shop; family income at risk.
  • Environment: family, own shop he can rearrange (facilitators); high shelves, surgery cost (barriers). Personal: sole earner, young (favours recovery).

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): full passive shoulder range, supported arm; flicker → gravity-eliminated movement; protects numb skin; refer to a plexus surgeon now if no recovery. Long-term (6–12 months): biceps and deltoid grade 4; lifts goods to chest height; runs the shop.

PT management by phase

  • Denervation: functional splint; gentle daily passive ROM; prevent overstretch; oedema and skin care; strengthen working muscles.
  • Reinnervation (Tinel's advancing, flicker): one muscle at a time, EMG biofeedback or stimulation; gravity-eliminated → active → resisted; few repetitions, short sessions; stop trick movements.
  • Functional: grip and pinch, task practice, work simulation. After nerve transfer: contract the donor to fire the target (squeeze fingers to bend elbow).
  • Sensory re-education: protective (look at the hand) → desensitisation (cotton → towel → rough) → discriminative (eyes open then closed, objects).

Splints (3m W17, S25, W25)

Dynamic cock-up principle (S25): replaces paralysed extensors; wrist held up so flexors grip strongly; elastic re-extends fingers and thumb after each grasp. Uses: radial palsy, extensor tendon repair, tendon transfers. Prevents extensor overstretch and flexor shortening.

Specific lesions

  • Radial (7m S18, W18): static cock-up at night, dynamic by day; ROM; re-educate brachioradialis and wrist extensors first, then fingers and thumb.
  • Claw (7m W19): knuckle-bender; stretch MCPs into flexion; intrinsics (finger spreading, table-top); tendon transfer if no recovery.
  • Foot drop (3m S16), peroneal (7m W25): AFO; daily calf stretch; DF and evertor re-education, FES; gait training; protect numb foot.
  • Erb's: support arm; full range esp. ER, abduction, supination; re-educate deltoid, spinati, biceps gravity-eliminated (powder board, sling suspension), biofeedback; refer for transfer if no recovery by 3–6 months.
  • Klumpke's (S25): anti-claw splint; finger and wrist range; intrinsic re-education; inner-hand sensory care.
  • Carpal tunnel (7m S22, APTA 2019): neutral night splint (moderate evidence); education; manual therapy, heat (weak). Do not use laser, iontophoresis, magnets, thermal ultrasound. Refer if thenar wasting.

Complications and precautions

Contracture (thumb web, equinus) → splints, daily range; overstretch of paralysed muscles → cock-up, AFO, sling; burns on numb skin; oedema; CRPS; learned non-use. No heat or stimulation over numb skin unchecked; protect repairs; check splint pressure; no traction on a flail arm.

Education and prognosis

Regrowth 2.5 cm a month: keep exercising for months; splint care; gloves, test water. Better: low degree, young, distal, early repair. Worse: traction, avulsion, old age, delay, proximal. Upper plexus recovers better than lower.

Exam plan (long case)

  1. Restate: age, job, dominant side, mechanism, time; nerve or plexus level and likely degree.
  2. ICF (5–7) with reasoning; one-line functional diagnosis.
  3. Goals (3–6): range, protection, splints, first movement; strength, work; refer for surgery on time.
  4. Management (7–8): splints; passive range; re-education with biofeedback; strengthening; sensory re-education; work; precautions.
