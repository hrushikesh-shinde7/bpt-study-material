Asked: long case W19 (23-year-old teacher with Bell's palsy: ICF, goals). 7 marks: Bell's palsy PT with education (W16, W17), features and management (S24), trigeminal neuralgia (S20, W22). 3 marks: facial nerve assessment (S16), trigeminal assessment, synkinesis, Bell's phenomenon (W18), signs of Bell's palsy (W24).

Definition, causes, grading

Bell's palsy: acute, one-sided, LMN facial palsy, no cause found, over less than 72 h; viral swelling in the bony canal → mostly neurapraxia. Other LMN: Ramsay Hunt (zoster, ear vesicles, worse), trauma, ear, parotid, GBS (both sides), leprosy. UMN (stroke): forehead spared (bilateral cortical supply). House-Brackmann: I normal; II mild; III eye closes with effort; IV eye does not close; V barely moves; VI total.

Features (3m W24; 7m S24), Bell's phenomenon, synkinesis (3m W18)

Whole half face over hours to 3 days; pain behind ear; cannot raise eyebrow; eye does not close; flat nasolabial fold; mouth droops, pulls to the normal side; food in cheek, drinks dribble; unclear p, b, m; watery or dry eye; taste lost front two-thirds; loud sounds (stapedius).

Bell's phenomenon: on trying to close the eyes, the eyeball on the weak side rolls up and out; visible because the lid does not close. Synkinesis: involuntary movement of one part during voluntary movement of another (eye narrows on smiling) from misdirected regrowth, months after severe palsy.

Assessment (3m S16, W18) and medical

Facial nerve: raise eyebrows (frontalis); close eyes against resistance (orbicularis oculi); smile, show teeth; puff cheeks, whistle (buccinator, orbicularis oris); tense neck (platysma); taste, Schirmer's tears, sound. Trigeminal: touch and pinprick V1 forehead, V2 cheek, V3 jaw; clench (masseter, temporalis); jaw deviates to weak side on opening; corneal reflex, jaw jerk.

Medical: oral steroids within 72 h (complete recovery 64% → 83% at 3 months); antivirals not alone; eye drops, night ointment and taping, glasses; refer if incomplete at 3 months.

ICF (W19: 23, teacher, right Bell's palsy)

  • Structure/function: all right facial muscles weak; eye does not close (cornea at risk); mouth droops; later synkinesis.
  • Activity: lip sounds, eating, drinking, expression. Participation: teaching, being seen by students; social outings.
  • Environment: early care, family, school (facilitators); dust, wind, stigma (barriers). Personal: young (good outlook), anxious about appearance.

Goals: short-term (2–4 weeks): no corneal damage; flicker → visible movement, symmetry at rest; lip seal. Long-term (2–3 months): full eye closure; House-Brackmann I–II; normal speech for a teaching day; no troublesome synkinesis.

PT by stage (7m W16, W17, S24)

  • Acute: reassurance; eye care; gentle massage (upward strokes to eye and ear); moist warmth with eye protected; cheek taping; chew on strong side.
  • Recovering: mirror-guided active-assisted → active: eyebrows, eye closure, smile, purse, puff, flare nostrils; small, slow, symmetrical; hold the strong side back; a few repetitions, several times a day.
  • Late: neuromuscular retraining with mirror or EMG biofeedback, relax unwanted muscle; stretch, massage.
  • Advice (W16): most recover; eye drops, night taping, glasses; eat on strong side, clean cheek, straw; no forceful grimacing or gum chewing.

Classic vs current: no faradic or galvanic stimulation: no better than placebo (Cochrane 2011).

Trigeminal neuralgia (7m S20, W22)

Sudden electric shock-like one-sided pain, V2 or V3, seconds to ~2 min; trigger zones (touch, talking, chewing, brushing, cold wind); normal between attacks. Usually a vessel on the root; MS or tumour if young. Carbamazepine; microvascular decompression. PT: avoid triggers (scarf, lukewarm water, soft brush); relaxation; TENS away from trigger zones; never stimulate trigger zones.

Precautions and prognosis

No heat with the eye open; refer if weakness spreads, other cranial nerves, ear vesicles, no recovery by 3 months. Worse: complete palsy, older, Ramsay Hunt, diabetes, no recovery by 3 weeks.
