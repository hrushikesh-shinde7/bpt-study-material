Definition and classification (3m S24)

Acquired brain injury: damage after birth, not hereditary, congenital or degenerative (trauma, stroke, tumour, infection, anoxia). TBI: altered brain function or pathology from an external force (blow, acceleration–deceleration, penetration, blast). SOL: a mass in the skull (tumour, abscess, haematoma, cyst) that compresses the brain and raises ICP.

Severity (GCS)Mild 13–15; moderate 9–12; severe 3–8
MechanismClosed or open (penetrating); acceleration–deceleration; blast
SkullLinear, depressed, basal (CSF leak, raccoon eyes)
FocalExtradural, subdural, intracerebral haemorrhage, contusion
DiffuseConcussion; diffuse axonal injury; diffuse swelling
TimingPrimary at impact; secondary after: low O₂, low BP, raised ICP, seizures, infection

Extradural: arterial (middle meningeal, temporal fracture), stops at sutures → lens-shaped; lucid interval. Subdural: torn bridging veins → crescent; acute after major trauma, chronic in older people after a minor fall. Causes: road accidents (two-wheelers, no helmet), falls, assault; SOL: glioma, meningioma, metastases, abscess, tuberculoma.

GCS (3m W16, S19, S20, S26)

E 1–4: spontaneous, to sound, to pressure, none. V 1–5: oriented, confused, words, sounds, none. M 1–6: obeys, localises, normal flexion (withdraws), abnormal flexion (decorticate), extension (decerebrate), none. Total 3–15; 8 or less = coma, airway needs protection.

Interpreting (S26): report the parts ("E2 V2 M4 = 8"); motor predicts outcome best; "T" for verbal when intubated; best response, better side for motor. Used in (W16): head injury, stroke, meningitis, encephalitis, overdose, any reduced consciousness.

Pathophysiology and raised ICP (3m W22, S25)

  • Primary: coup and contrecoup contusions, haematomas; diffuse axonal injury (rotation shears axons): coma from impact, near-normal CT, MRI lesions in corpus callosum, internal capsule, brainstem.
  • Secondary: swelling, bleeding → ICP rises; skull cannot expand → CPP = MAP − ICP falls → more ischaemia; low O₂ and BP worsen it. Herniation: uncus on III → fixed dilated pupil → brainstem failure.
  • Raised ICP symptoms: headache worse on waking, coughing, bending; vomiting (projectile); blurred vision. Signs: falling consciousness, papilloedema, dilating pupil on the mass side, VI palsy, seizures, bulging fontanelle (infants). Cushing's triad (late): rising BP with wide pulse pressure, slow HR, irregular breathing.
  • Numbers: normal ICP 5–15 mmHg; treat above 22 mmHg; CPP target 60–70 mmHg (Brain Trauma Foundation 2016).

Features: coma → vegetative or minimally conscious; post-traumatic amnesia; hemiparesis (often both sides), spasticity, ataxia; attention, memory, executive loss, agitation. Frontal (S23, S24): poor planning, judgement, safety, disinhibition or apathy. Vision loss (S24), vertigo, swallow, seizures. SOL: slow headache, seizures, focal signs, personality change.

Investigations and medical management

CT first (haematoma, midline shift); MRI (diffuse axonal injury, tumour); ICP monitoring, EEG. Prevent secondary injury: airway, O₂, BP; head up, sedation, osmotic therapy, CSF drainage. Surgery: evacuate haematoma, elevate depressed fracture, decompressive craniectomy (flap out: protect soft area), burr holes (chronic subdural); later botulinum toxin, baclofen.

ICF (W24 worked case: 30, skull fractures, ICU, 2 weeks, Rancho IV)

  • Structure/function: severe TBI; agitation, poor attention, no short-term memory, poor safety; weakness, altered tone, poor balance; secretions, swallow; contracture, sore, ossification risk; protect skull.
  • Activity: dependent for bed mobility, sitting, transfers, walking, feeding, hygiene; cannot follow complex instructions (cognition limits, not only strength).
  • Participation: off work; family roles suspended.
  • Environment: ICU care, family visits (facilitators); noise, alarms, many staff, restraints (raise agitation) (barriers). Personal: young, likely earner.

Goals: short-term (2–4 weeks): calm in short sessions, no falls; clear chest, no sores; full range; sits out, stands with help; washes face. Long-term (3–6 months): follows routines, safe at home; walks indoors and outdoors; independent self-care; planned return to work.

Acute stage in ICU (7m S18, S20)

  • Assess first: GCS, pupils, ICP, CPP, BP, O₂, ventilator, craniectomy, other fractures; Rancho, post-traumatic amnesia; chest, MAS.
  • Positioning: head of bed up about 30°, head midline; no neck flexion or rotation (jugular compression); side lying against abnormal postures; turn regularly.
  • Chest: positioning, gentle manual techniques, short pre-oxygenated suction; no head-down drainage while ICP raised.
  • Range: daily passive range all joints; splints or serial casts for ankles, wrists when tone rises.
  • Early sitting, tilt table once ICP controlled. Structured sensory input (family voices, music, touch) in short periods; "coma stimulation" evidence weak: avoid overstimulation. Monitor ICP, CPP, BP, O₂ throughout.

By level of cognition

  • Unresponsive (GCS 3–8, Rancho I–II; S26): positioning, turning, chest care, range, splints, casting, tilt table and sitting for arousal, sensory input, teach family. Goals: tolerates sitting, more responses → sits supported, follows commands.
  • Agitated (IV; W24): quiet room, one therapist, short sessions; familiar automatic tasks (walking, sit to stand, washing face); low bed, mats, no restraint; do not argue or test memory; stop and retry later.
  • Confused, non-agitated (V–VI): same time daily, one-step instructions, errorless learning, memory aids, repeated functional practice.
  • VII–VIII: balance, dual tasks, community walking, fitness, work and driving assessment. Graded aerobic exercise with pacing once stable.

Precautions, complications

Stop: ICP stays above limit, CPP falls, GCS drops; report a new dilated pupil at once. No head-down or tight collars; short suctioning. Craniectomy helmet, no pressure on the soft area; clear fractures and spine first; seizure plan; tumours: fatigue, steroid effects.

Complications: aspiration; contractures (ankle, elbow, wrist); heterotopic ossification (warm swollen joint losing range: gentle range, report); sores (sacrum, heels, occiput); falls in agitation.

Education and prognosis

Agitation is a stage that passes; positioning, range, transfers, calm routine, memory aids, no alcohol; helmet; driving only after clearance (vision, cognition, seizures); graded return to work. Worse: older, low GCS motor, non-reacting pupils, low O₂ or BP, large lesions. Longer amnesia = more severe; cognition and behaviour limit return to work more than physical problems.
