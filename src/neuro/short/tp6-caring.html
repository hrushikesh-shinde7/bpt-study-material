Asked: not on its own in 2016–2026. Shows as patient and parent education in long cases and as the affective domain in the practical. Expect 3 marks on principles of family-centred care, or 7 on a philosophy of caring.

Person-centred care

  • Start from the person's life, values and goals (work, family roles, faith, hobbies), not only the diagnosis.
  • Set goals together, written in the person's words (ICF and goals).
  • Autonomy: explain options, risks, benefits; accept informed choices, including no.
  • Build self-management and self-efficacy so progress continues after discharge.

Family-centred care in children (likely 3m)

  • Parents are partners and main therapists: they decide priorities with you.
  • Clear, honest information in plain language; teach handling, positioning and a short home programme that fits the family's day.
  • Build on family strengths; respect culture, beliefs, resources; support siblings and grandparents in joint families.
  • Co-ordinate with school and services; plan transitions (preschool, school, adulthood).

Communication and the relationship

  • Introduce yourself, explain what and why, consent every session before touching.
  • Listen actively; teach-back (ask them to explain or show it back); their language, no jargon.
  • Aphasia, hearing loss, neglect, cognition: short sentences, gestures, pictures, demonstration, quiet room.
  • Empathy and realistic hope; praise effort and progress.
  • Dignity and privacy: drape, curtains, chaperone where appropriate, respect modesty and cultural norms.

Ethics in everyday practice

AutonomyInformed consent; their choices; involve them even with reduced capacity
BeneficenceEvidence-based treatment aimed at their goals
Non-maleficenceSafe handling, precautions, no unnecessary or ineffective treatment
JusticeFair care regardless of income, caste, religion, gender, disability; help with schemes
ConfidentialityShare only with consent and the care team

Carers, teamwork, difficult situations

  • Carer burden: ask how they cope; safe handling; respite, support groups, schemes.
  • Teamwork: doctors, nurses, occupational and speech therapists, psychologist, social worker, orthotist, teachers; one shared plan.
  • Progressive and terminal disease (MND, advanced PD, DMD): honest, compassionate talks; plan ahead; comfort, function, quality of life; respect advance wishes.
  • Reflective practice: what went well, what to change; seek supervision.

Exam plan

3 marks (family-centred care): parents as partners setting priorities; honest information; handling and a home programme fitting the routine; family strengths, culture, resources; co-ordinate with school.

7 marks (philosophy of caring): person- and family-centred goals → autonomy and consent → adapted communication → empathy and realistic hope → dignity, privacy, culture → ethics table → carers → teamwork → honesty in progressive disease → reflection.

Practical: introduce, consent, drape, guard, explain each step: affective-domain marks (practical exam). Underline: person-centred, family-centred, shared decision-making, informed consent, dignity, realistic hope, carer burden, teamwork.
