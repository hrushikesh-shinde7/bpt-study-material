Other laws

  • Rehabilitation Council of India Act, 1992: regulates training and registration of rehabilitation professionals (special educators, audiologists, prosthetists, clinical psychologists).
  • National Trust Act, 1999: welfare of persons with autism, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability and multiple disabilities; legal guardianship; schemes such as Niramaya health insurance.
  • Mental Healthcare Act, 2017: rights of persons with mental illness.
  • National Policy for Persons with Disabilities, 2006: prevention, rehabilitation, education, employment, barrier-free environment.

4Difficulties and architectural barriers

Difficulties faced by a community-dwelling person with disability (W17, 3 marks); architectural barriers and their modifications (S18, 7 marks).

Difficulties in the community

  • Physical: steps, narrow doors, inaccessible toilets, uneven roads, no ramps or lifts.
  • Transport: buses and autos hard to board; cost of private transport.
  • Attitudes: stigma, pity, discrimination, overprotection; difficulty in marriage.
  • Economic: unemployment, poverty, cost of aids and treatment.
  • Services: distant health and rehabilitation centres; lack of information about schemes; paperwork for certificates.
  • Education and communication: inaccessible schools; communication barriers for deaf and blind persons.
  • Social: isolation, dependence on family, risk of abuse.

Architectural barriers and modifications

BarrierModification
Steps at the entranceRamp with a gradient no steeper than 1:12, clear width at least 1,200 mm, handrails on both sides (about 760–900 mm high) extending beyond the ends, landing at top and bottom
Narrow doorsClear width of about 900 mm; lever handles; no high thresholds
No space to turn a wheelchairA clear turning circle of 1,500 mm in rooms, toilets and corridors
Inaccessible toiletWestern toilet at wheelchair seat height, grab bars, washbasin without pedestal, outward-opening door, non-slip floor
Upper floorsLift with wide doors and low buttons; ground-floor rooms
High counters and switchesLowered counters, switches and handles within reach from a wheelchair
Pavements and roadsKerb cuts, even paths, tactile paving for blind persons, audible signals
Signs and informationLarge clear signs with contrast, Braille, sign-language information

Measurements from the Harmonised Guidelines and Standards for Universal Accessibility in India (2021) and standard wheelchair space needs.

5Worked long case: a farmer with paraplegia going home

From a real paper (S18, 5 + 5 + 5)

"A 35 years old male, farmer, diagnosed as a case of T12-L1 injury is discharged from the hospital. i) Mention the functional diagnosis (5) ii) Discuss the modes of transport for him in the community iii) Explain the concessions offered to him (5)"

Spot the clues before you write

T12–L1 injury: paraplegia with conus or cauda equina involvement, so bladder and bowel problems; upper limbs and trunk strong. farmer: rural home, uneven fields, needs a new way to earn. discharged: community phase; transport and benefits now matter.

Functional diagnosis (5 marks)

A 35-year-old farmer with paraplegia after a T12–L1 spinal cord injury (classify with the ASIA scale), independent in upper limbs and trunk, with neurogenic bladder and bowel, at risk of pressure sores; he can be independent in wheelchair mobility and transfers and may walk short distances with orthoses; his rural home, fields and roads limit mobility, and he cannot continue his usual farm work (see spinal cord injury).

Modes of transport (5 marks)

ModeUse and training
Manual wheelchairHome and village; rigid or folding, with pneumatic tyres and a pressure-relief cushion for rough roads; wheelie skills for kerbs
Hand-propelled tricycleLonger distances on village roads and to the fields; cheap and available through ADIP camps
Motorised tricycle or scooter with side wheelsTravel to the market, PHC or town; hand controls; driving licence for an adapted vehicle
Car with hand controlsIf affordable; transfer and wheelchair loading training
Public transportLow-floor buses where available; train travel with concession, station wheelchairs and help; autorickshaw transfers practised
Walking with orthosesKnee–ankle–foot orthoses and crutches or a walker for short distances and standing, not for daily long distances

Concessions (5 marks)

  • Disability certificate and UDID card (benchmark 40% or more).
  • Free or subsidised wheelchair, tricycle, motorised tricycle, orthoses and cushions under ADIP.
  • Railway concession (75% in second class and sleeper with the same for his escort) and state bus concessions.
  • Disability pension if eligible; income tax deduction; concessional loans from NDFDC for self-employment (dairy, poultry, shop).
  • Vocational training and 4% job reservation; priority in housing and accessible toilet schemes; panchayat help to build a ramp and toilet.

3 mPersons with Disability Act 1995. (S23, S24)

Full name; 7 disabilities (blindness, low vision, leprosy-cured, hearing impairment, locomotor, mental retardation, mental illness); 40% benchmark; 3% job reservation; free education to 18; prevention and early detection; non-discrimination in transport and buildings; aids and unemployment allowance; Chief and State Commissioners. Replaced by the RPWD Act 2016 (21 disabilities, 4%).

3 mThree national programmes for prevention of disability. (S20, W21, W24)

Universal Immunisation Programme (1985) with Pulse Polio (1995): prevents polio and other vaccine-preventable disability. National Programme for Control of Blindness and Visual Impairment (1976): cataract surgery, spectacles. National Programme for Prevention and Control of Deafness (2006): early detection, hearing aids. (Also NLEP, NIDDCP, RBSK.)

7 mArchitectural barriers faced by persons with disability and their modifications. (S18)

Barriers: steps, narrow doors, no turning space, inaccessible toilets, upper floors without lifts, high counters, uneven roads without kerb cuts, poor signs. Modifications: ramps (1:12, 1,200 mm wide, handrails both sides), 900 mm doors with lever handles, 1,500 mm turning circle, accessible toilets with grab bars, lifts, lowered counters, kerb cuts and tactile paving, clear and Braille signage. Mention the RPWD Act duty and the Accessible India Campaign.
