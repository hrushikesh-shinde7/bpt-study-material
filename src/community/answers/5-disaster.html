Types of disaster and principles of disaster management

Disaster (Disaster Management Act, 2005): a catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence, from natural or man-made causes, or by accident or negligence, that causes substantial loss of life, human suffering, or damage to property or the environment, and is beyond the coping capacity of the affected community.

A disaster happens when a hazard (the threat) meets a vulnerable community: risk = hazard × vulnerability ÷ capacity.

Types

TypeExamples (India)
Natural: geophysicalEarthquakes (Latur 1993, Bhuj 2001), tsunami (2004), landslides
Natural: hydrological and weatherFloods (Kerala 2018), cyclones (Odisha 1999), cloudbursts, droughts, heat and cold waves
Natural: biologicalEpidemics and pandemics (COVID-19)
Man-made: technologicalIndustrial and chemical accidents (Bhopal gas leak 1984), fires, building collapse, nuclear accidents
Man-made: transport and crowdsRail and road accidents, stampedes
Man-made: conflictTerrorism, bomb blasts, war, riots

Also by onset (sudden: earthquake; slow: drought) and by scale (local, state, national).

Principles of disaster management

  1. Comprehensive: all hazards, all phases, all people affected.
  2. Progressive: anticipate and act before disasters (preparedness, mitigation).
  3. Risk-driven: priorities set by hazard and vulnerability assessment.
  4. Integrated and coordinated: all levels of government, agencies, NGOs and communities under one plan and command.
  5. Collaborative: trust and partnership among organisations and the community.
  6. Flexible: adapt to each situation.
  7. Professional: trained people, standards, evidence.
  8. Community participation and inclusion: women, older people and persons with disability in planning; "build back better" in recovery (Sendai Framework).

In India: DM Act 2005 set up the NDMA (chaired by the Prime Minister), State and District Authorities, the NDRF and the National Institute of Disaster Management; National Disaster Management Plan (2016) aligned with the Sendai Framework (2015–2030).

Link the principles to the cycle: mitigation → preparedness → response → recovery.
