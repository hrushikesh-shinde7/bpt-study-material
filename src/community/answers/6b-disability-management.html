Job simulation

Job simulation: reproducing the real tasks of a job, with the same tools, weights, heights, postures and pace, in the clinic or at a mock workstation.

  • To train: builds job-specific tolerance in work hardening.
  • To test: shows whether the worker can meet the job demands in a functional capacity evaluation.
  • Tasks come from the job analysis and are graded in weight, height, distance, repetitions and time.
  • Examples: work simulators with interchangeable tool handles; work sample batteries (such as Valpar work samples); mock construction, assembly or office stations; a mock truck bed for a loader.

It leads to a safe, graded return to the real job.

Functional capacity evaluation (assessment)

Functional capacity evaluation (FCE): a standardised set of tests that measures a person's safe maximum ability to perform work-related physical tasks, and compares it with the demands of a job or a physical demand level. It is a step of disability management.

Uses

Return-to-work decisions and job matching; planning work conditioning and hardening; deciding modified duties; disability rating and compensation; pre-placement screening; measuring progress.

Components

PartWhat is tested
IntakeHistory, job description, symptoms, self-rated abilities, medical clearance, consent
Musculoskeletal screenPosture, ROM, strength, sensation, HR and BP
Material handlingLifting floor to waist, waist to shoulder, overhead; carrying; pushing and pulling, weights increased to a safe maximum
Positional tolerancesSitting, standing, walking, stooping, kneeling, crouching, reaching, climbing stairs and ladders, balance
Hand functionGrip dynamometer, pinch, dexterity tests
Aerobic capacityStep or treadmill test
Effort and consistencyRepeated trials, HR response, observed body mechanics, consistency between tests
ReportSafe abilities, physical demand level, comparison with the job, projection to an 8-hour day, recommendations

Approaches

  • Kinesiophysical (Isernhagen Work System, 28 work-related tests): the evaluator watches body mechanics to decide the safe maximum.
  • Psychophysical: uses the worker's own judgement of an acceptable load.
  • Duration: usually several hours, often over 1–2 days.

Limits

Affected by pain, fear and motivation; predicts return to work only modestly; needs a trained evaluator and a standard protocol.

Report in functional terms, for example "medium work: up to 50 lb (23 kg) occasionally, 25 lb (11 kg) frequently".

Work conditioning

Work conditioning: an intensive, work-related, goal-oriented conditioning programme to restore systemic, neuromusculoskeletal and cardiopulmonary function (strength, endurance, mobility, motor control, aerobic capacity) so the person can return to work.

  • Usually one discipline (physiotherapist); part of a day, several days a week; starts after acute care when there is a physical deficit.
  • Aerobic training (treadmill, cycle, walking) 20–40 minutes.
  • Progressive strengthening of the injured area and the whole body (trunk, legs, grip).
  • Flexibility, mobility, motor control and core stability.
  • Basic body mechanics and lifting practice; education.

It is followed by work hardening (interdisciplinary, job-specific) when physical capacity is nearly enough.

Define work hardening and work conditioning

Work conditioning: an intensive, work-related, goal-oriented conditioning programme to restore systemic, neuromusculoskeletal and cardiopulmonary function (strength, endurance, mobility, motor control, aerobic capacity) so the person can return to work.

Work hardening: a highly structured, goal-oriented, individualised, interdisciplinary programme using real or simulated work tasks to restore physical, behavioural and vocational functions for return to a specific job.

Work hardening, with two examples

Work hardening: a highly structured, goal-oriented, individualised, interdisciplinary programme using real or simulated work tasks to restore physical, behavioural and vocational functions for return to a specific job.

Features

  • Team: physiotherapist, occupational therapist, psychologist, vocational counsellor, case manager.
  • Focus: job-specific tasks, productivity, safety, work tolerance, work behaviours (punctuality, pacing, attitude), pain coping.
  • Hours: build up towards a full working day, several days a week.
  • Entry: when physical capacity is nearly enough but job tolerance, confidence or behaviour limit return; usually after work conditioning.

Content

  • Job simulation: real or simulated tasks from the job analysis, graded in weight, height, distance, repetitions and time.
  • Physical conditioning continued alongside.
  • Body mechanics and pacing during the tasks; ergonomic changes for the real job.
  • Pain management, stress and fear-of-movement counselling; work habits (timekeeping, following instructions).
  • Regular re-measurement against the job demands; plan for graded return.

Two examples

  1. Warehouse loader after low back strain: lifting crates floor → waist → shoulder with gradually heavier loads (for example 10 kg to 25 kg); carrying 20 m; pushing a loaded trolley; loading a mock truck bed; building up to the full shift's number of lifts with correct technique.
  2. Data-entry clerk after carpal tunnel release or wrist fracture: graded typing and mouse sessions from 15 to 60 minutes; filing and lifting files; dexterity tasks; workstation set-up and micro-breaks; building up to a full working day.

Work conditioning restores general physical capacity (one discipline); work hardening is job-specific and interdisciplinary.

Define functional capacity assessment

Functional capacity assessment (evaluation): a standardised set of tests that measures a person's safe maximum ability to perform work-related physical tasks, and compares it with the demands of a job or a physical demand level.

  • Components: intake; musculoskeletal screen; material handling (lifting floor to waist, waist to shoulder, overhead; carrying; pushing and pulling); positional tolerances; hand function (grip, pinch, dexterity); aerobic test; effort and consistency; report with an 8-hour projection.
  • Uses: return-to-work decisions and job matching; planning work conditioning and hardening; modified duties; disability rating and compensation; pre-placement.
  • Example: Isernhagen Work System (kinesiophysical, 28 tests).
