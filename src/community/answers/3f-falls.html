Risk factors for falls in the elderly

Fall (WHO): an event which results in a person coming to rest inadvertently on the ground, floor or other lower level. About 28–35% of people aged 65 and over fall each year; a previous fall is the strongest predictor.

Causes of falls in the elderly; environmental modifications for prevention

Fall (WHO): an event which results in a person coming to rest inadvertently on the ground or floor or other lower level. About 28–35% of people over 65 fall each year (32–42% over 70); results include fractures, head injury, a long lie, fear of falling and loss of independence.

Causes

  • Intrinsic: leg weakness, arthritis, foot problems; poor balance and slow gait; poor vision, neuropathy, vestibular loss; postural hypotension, stroke, Parkinson's, dementia, depression, diabetes, urinary urgency and nocturia, acute illness; fear of falling, cognitive impairment.
  • Extrinsic: environmental hazards (below); medicines (four or more drugs, sedatives, antidepressants, antipsychotics, BP drugs, diuretics); poor footwear and faulty walking aids.

Environmental modifications

AreaModification
LightingBright, even light; switches at room entrances and by the bed; night lights to the toilet
FloorsRemove loose rugs, cords, clutter; non-slip mats; wipe spills; contrasting colour on step edges
StairsRails on both sides; good light at top and bottom; nothing left on steps
Bathroom and toiletGrab bars; western toilet or raised commode over the Indian toilet; non-slip floor; bathing stool; hand shower
BedroomBed and chair at knee height; firm chair with armrests; urinal or commode at night
Kitchen and storageCommon items between waist and shoulder height; no climbing on stools
OutdoorsEven paths, rails at entrances; avoid wet or dark roads

Evidence: home safety assessment and modification reduced the rate of falls (rate ratio 0.81), more in people at high risk (Gillespie et al., 2012).

Add footwear (firm, well-fitting, thin non-slip soles), a phone or alarm within reach, and balance and strength exercise, the most effective single measure.

A safe bathroom: grab bars, raised seat over the toilet, non-slip floor, bathing stool.

Extrinsic risk factors for falls in the elderly

Extrinsic factors are outside the person: the environment, medicines, footwear and aids. They are the easiest to change.

  • Lighting: poor or dim light, no night light.
  • Floors: slippery or wet floors, loose rugs, clutter, cords.
  • Stairs without rails; low beds and chairs.
  • Indian (squat) toilets, bathrooms without grab bars.
  • Outdoors: uneven roads, animals.
  • Medicines: four or more drugs; sedatives, sleeping pills, antidepressants, antipsychotics, BP drugs, diuretics.
  • Footwear and aids: loose slippers, high heels, worn soles; wrong-height or faulty walking aids.

Remedy: home safety check, medication review by the doctor, well-fitting shoes, aids checked and fitted.
