Strategies of health promotion for rural women

Health promotion for rural women works through village workers and the places where women already gather. Their main problems are anaemia (57% of women aged 15–49, NFHS-5), back pain, pelvic floor problems, pregnancy care, chulha smoke and diabetes, with little time and limited mobility.

1. Know the community

  • Meet the sarpanch, ASHA, ANM, anganwadi worker and self-help group leaders; learn the main problems and the women's daily timetable.

2. Use existing platforms

  • Anganwadis, Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Days, self-help group meetings, mahila mandals, PHC and Ayushman Arogya Mandir clinics, temples and schools.

3. Choose the message

  • Nutrition and iron; antenatal and postnatal exercise; pelvic floor exercise.
  • Back care: safe ways to lift water pots, squat and carry; osteoporosis prevention.
  • Physical activity (walking groups, yoga); clean cooking fuel; toilet use; menstrual hygiene; screening for BP, diabetes and cancer.

4. Use the right methods

  • Local language, pictures, demonstrations and practice, songs and role plays; short sessions at convenient times; women educators; home visits for those who cannot come.

5. Involve the family

  • Men, mothers-in-law and elders, so women get permission and time.

6. Link to schemes and services

  • JSY, PMMVY, PMSMA, Ujjwala, pensions, PHC referral.

7. Train local women

  • ASHAs and self-help group leaders continue the programme (sustainability).

8. Evaluate

  • Attendance; knowledge before and after; behaviour change (exercise, iron tablets, institutional delivery); feedback.

Example: hold antenatal exercise sessions at the PMSMA clinic on the 9th of the month, where pregnant women already gather.

Social issues affecting the physical function of women in India

Women's health in India is shaped by social conditions: 57% of women aged 15–49 are anaemic (NFHS-5); about 1 in 4 women aged 20–24 married before 18; 29% of ever-married women reported spousal violence; female literacy 65% against 82% for men (Census 2011).

Role of the physiotherapist

  • Education in local language at anganwadis and self-help groups: back care, safe lifting and carrying, posture.
  • Pelvic floor training; antenatal and postnatal exercise; walking groups and yoga for bone health.
  • Home programmes and home visits for women who cannot travel; women therapists.
  • Link to schemes: JSY, JSSK, PMMVY, Ujjwala (LPG), Swachh Bharat (toilets), Anaemia Mukt Bharat.

Flow: no toilet → holding urine → UTI and pelvic floor problems → pelvic floor training and education.
