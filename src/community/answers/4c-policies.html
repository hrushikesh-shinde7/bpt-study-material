Difficulties faced by a community-dwelling person with disability

  • Physical: steps, narrow doors, inaccessible toilets, uneven roads, no ramps or lifts.
  • Transport: buses and autos hard to board; cost of private transport.
  • Attitudes: stigma, pity, discrimination, overprotection; difficulty in marriage.
  • Economic: unemployment, poverty, cost of aids and treatment.
  • Services: distant health and rehabilitation centres; lack of information about schemes; paperwork for certificates.
  • Education and communication: inaccessible schools; communication barriers for deaf and blind persons.
  • Social: isolation, dependence on family, risk of abuse.

In the social model these barriers, not the impairment, turn a disability into a handicap.

Persons with Disabilities Act, 1995

Full name: Persons with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection of Rights and Full Participation) Act, 1995.

  • 7 disabilities: blindness, low vision, leprosy-cured, hearing impairment, locomotor disability, mental retardation, mental illness.
  • Benchmark: 40% or more.
  • 3% of government jobs reserved.
  • Free education up to 18 years; 3% seats in government-aided institutions.
  • Prevention and early detection; non-discrimination in transport and buildings; aids; unemployment allowance; Chief and State Commissioners.

Replaced by the RPWD Act 2016: 21 specified disabilities, 4% job reservation, at least 5% seats in higher education, in line with the UN Convention (2006).

Architectural barriers faced by persons with disability and their modifications

Architectural barriers are features of buildings, roads and transport that stop a person with disability from entering, moving about or using a place. In the social model they are a main cause of handicap. The RPWD Act 2016 gives a right to accessibility (buildings, transport, ICT), and the Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan, 2015) promotes accessible buildings, transport and websites. Measurements follow the Harmonised Guidelines (2021).

Role of the physiotherapist

  • Accessibility audit of the home, school, workplace and public buildings.
  • Advice on ramps, rails, toilet changes and furniture height; training in wheelchair skills for the barriers that remain.
  • Linking the family to the panchayat or schemes for a ramp and toilet.

A ramp with a 1:12 gradient, handrails and landings; an accessible toilet with grab bars and a 1,500 mm turning circle.
