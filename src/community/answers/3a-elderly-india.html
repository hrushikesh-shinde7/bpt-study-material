National policies that facilitate the elderly

A senior citizen in India is a person aged 60 or over (Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007). India had 104 million elderly in the 2011 Census (8.6%), projected to reach about 227 million (14.9%) by 2036. Most live in villages, many are widows and poor, and most worked without pensions, so state support is essential.

Policy or schemeMain points
National Policy on Older Persons (1999)State support for financial and food security, health care and nutrition, shelter, education, welfare, and protection against abuse and exploitation
Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act (2007)Children and heirs must maintain parents; maintenance tribunals (allowance up to ₹10,000 a month); old age homes for indigent elders in districts; medical care; abandoning a senior citizen is punishable

Role of the physiotherapist

  • Rehabilitation and physiotherapy in NPHCE units at CHCs and district hospitals; home-based care.
  • Assess and prescribe aids at Vayoshri camps; link elders to pensions, PM-JAY and NGOs.

Start with the definition and numbers, then write the policy, the Act and NPHCE first: these carry the marks.

Policies that facilitate an elderly person

A senior citizen in India is a person aged 60 or over.

  • National Policy on Older Persons (1999): financial and food security, health care, shelter, welfare, protection from abuse.
  • Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act (2007): children must maintain parents; tribunals (up to ₹10,000 a month); abandonment punishable.
  • NPHCE (2010): geriatric clinics and wards; rehabilitation and physiotherapy at CHCs; home-based care.
  • AB PM-JAY for 70 and over (2024): ₹5 lakh a year hospital cover.
  • IGNOAPS pension for elders below the poverty line; Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana free assistive devices; Elderline 14567.

Role of NGOs in elderly care in India

Examples: HelpAge India, Dignity Foundation, Agewell Foundation, Nightingales Medical Trust, Silver Innings, local religious and community trusts.

  • Health: mobile medical units and camps (eye, hearing, physiotherapy).
  • Care: old age homes, day care centres, senior citizens' clubs.
  • Protection: helplines; protection from abuse.
  • Money: livelihood and pension help; sponsorship of destitute elders.
  • Advocacy: awareness and policy; caregiver training; disaster relief.
  • Linking elders to government schemes (NGOs get grants under Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana).

Communication barriers in the elderly (geriatric population)

Communication barriers are factors in the older person, the professional or the setting that stop a message being given or understood. Good communication matters for safety, adherence to the exercise programme and dignity.

BarrierExamplesWhat to do
HearingPresbycusis (high tones lost first), difficulty in noiseFace the person; quiet room; speak slowly, clearly, in a lower pitch; do not shout; hearing aid in and working

When explaining a home programme, demonstrate it, give a large-print handout and check with teach-back.
