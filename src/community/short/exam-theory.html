Short answers: 3 and 7 marks

3 marks (half a page): definition → 4–6 points or small table → one example or number; quick diagram only (ICIDH chain, T-score scale). "Enumerate" = list; "define" = standard definition word for word (WHO health, ICIDH, rehabilitation, ergonomics, CBR).

7 marks (1.5–2 pages): definition (1–2 lines) → table or numbered points with headings echoing the question → diagram → PT role or prevention → one line of evidence ("exercise reduces falls by 23%").

Long answers: five templates

  • Worker in pain: job and task analysis; ICF, functional diagnosis; goals; treatment with doses; ergonomic changes; prevention; return to work.
  • Woman: stage and body changes; ICF; goals; exercise and pelvic floor; posture and daily-task ergonomics; warning signs; education.
  • Older person: assessment with scales; ICF; goals; strength, balance, gait; falls; home; institution or home; social support, schemes.
  • Weight and fitness: BMI, waist, body fat, fitness tests with working; ICF; goals; FITT with heart-rate target; diet; precautions; review.
  • Disability and CBR: ICF functional diagnosis; local resources; plan by CBR matrix; aids, transport, concessions; referral, follow-up.

Always: ICF (six boxes), one-line functional diagnosis, short- and long-term goals with numbers, doses, a community element (ergonomics, home, schemes, prevention). Theory long answers: break-up as headings, diagram, table, PT role.

Diagrams and common mistakes

Draw: ICF for the case; workstation, hierarchy of controls, worker care spectrum; pelvic floor sling, diastasis test, stages of labour; fun-to-failure, balance system, ageing-theories tree; rehab team, CBR matrix, ICIDH chain; three tiers of care, disaster cycle, START; apple and pear, energy systems, circuit. Pencil, labelled, titled, referred to in the text.

Mistakes: no community part; "strengthening" without exercise, dose, progression; ignoring the break-up; missing measurements (BMI, waist, TUG, PERFECT, REBA); old facts (PWD 1995 replaced by RPWD 2016: 21 disabilities, 4% jobs; Asian Indian BMI cut-offs); no red flags (pre-eclampsia, osteoporotic fracture, postural hypotension, hypoglycaemia); too long on 3-mark answers.
