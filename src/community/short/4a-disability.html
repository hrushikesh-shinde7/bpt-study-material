ICIDH terms (3m W16, W17, W20, W25)

ICIDH: International Classification of Impairments, Disabilities and Handicaps, WHO 1980; replaced by the ICF 2001. Chain: disease → impairment → disability → handicap.

  • Impairment (organ): any loss or abnormality of psychological, physiological or anatomical structure or function.
  • Disability (person): any restriction or lack, from an impairment, of ability to perform an activity in the manner or range considered normal.
  • Handicap (society): a disadvantage, from an impairment or disability, that limits or prevents a role normal for that individual (age, sex, social, cultural factors).

Polio → paralysed leg → cannot walk far → cannot farm. ICF: two-way, adds environmental and personal factors; disability = umbrella term for impairments, activity limitations, participation restrictions.

Models (3m W17) and types (3m S19, W20)

Medical: problem of the person → treat the impairment. Social: created by barriers (steps, attitudes, laws) → remove them. Biopsychosocial (ICF): interaction → treat person, change environment. Rights-based: UN Convention 2006, RPWD Act 2016. Charity: pity, dependence. Nagi: pathology → impairment → functional limitation → disability.

RPWD Act 2016: 21 specified disabilities. Locomotor (locomotor, leprosy-cured, CP, dwarfism, muscular dystrophy, acid attack); sensory (blindness, low vision, deaf and hard of hearing, speech and language); intellectual (intellectual disability, specific learning disabilities, autism); mental illness; chronic neurological (MS, Parkinson's); blood (haemophilia, thalassaemia, sickle cell); multiple incl. deaf-blindness. Benchmark: 40% or more, certified; needed for benefits. Also congenital or acquired, temporary or permanent, visible or invisible, mild to profound.

Primary and secondary disability (3m S16)

Primary: caused directly by the disease or injury (paraplegia after SCI). Secondary: later, from the primary disability, inactivity or poor care: contractures, pressure sores, obesity, osteoporosis, pain, UTI, depression, deconditioning; largely preventable by PT (positioning, exercise, education, early mobility).

Upper-limb permanent impairment (7m S23)

At maximum improvement; measured, not opinion. Arm 90%: ROM (shoulder, elbow, wrist 30% each, mean % loss × 0.3); strength (MRC → % loss × 0.3); coordination (10 activities, 9% each). Hand 90%: prehension 30% (opposition 8%, lateral pinch 5%, cylindrical 6%, spherical 6%, hook 5%); sensation 30% (thumb ray 9%, index 6%, others 5%); strength 30% (grip 20%, pinch 10%).

Combining formula: a + b(90 − a) ÷ 90 (a = higher). Arm values combined, hand values added, then arm and hand combined: arm 27%, hand 64% → 71.8%. Extra up to 10% (pain, deformity, dominant limb 4%, shortening 2% per inch beyond the first); never over 100%.

Levels of prevention (7m W20, S26; 3m W16, W22, W25)

WHO 1981; each level breaks one link of the chain.

Primary: no impairmentImmunisation (polio, measles, rubella); iodine, vitamin A, folic acid, iron; antenatal care; genetic counselling; helmets, seat belts; health education
Secondary: impairment not → disabilityScreening (RBSK, hearing, vision, diabetic foot); early treatment; early PT, splinting; early surgery (clubfoot, cataract)
Tertiary: disability not → handicapRehabilitation; aids; prevent secondary conditions; inclusive education; access; jobs; rights; CBR

PT: education and exercise; early detection and therapy; rehab and inclusion. Polio: vaccine → early PT, calliper → rehab, school access.
