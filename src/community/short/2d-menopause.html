Menopause and hormones (W24; 7m S23)

Permanent end of menstruation from loss of follicle activity, diagnosed after 12 months without a period; India ~46 years (West ~51). Premature: before 40; surgical: both ovaries removed (sudden). Oestrogen and progesterone fall; inhibin falls → FSH rises markedly, LH rises; androgens relatively higher.

Effects of low oestrogen

Vasomotor: hot flushes, night sweats, palpitations, poor sleep. Mind: mood swings, anxiety. Bone: rapid loss in early years → wrist, spine, hip fractures. Muscle, joints: less mass and strength, aches. Genitourinary: dryness, urgency, infections, stress incontinence, prolapse. Heart, metabolism: worse cholesterol, abdominal fat, BP. Skin: thin, weight gain. Medical: hormone therapy, bone drugs.

Importance of PT after menopause (7m S17, S22)

Bone: progressive resistance + impact, back extensors. Falls: balance, home safety, footwear. Pelvic floor: pelvic floor training, bladder training. Weight, heart: aerobic 150 min/week + resistance. Pain: exercise, posture. Flushes, sleep, mood: exercise, relaxation, paced breathing, yoga; cool room. Education: calcium, vitamin D, sunlight, no smoking or alcohol, screening.

Osteoporosis: definition and types (3m S23)

WHO 1994: systemic skeletal disease with low bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration → fragility and fracture. DEXA hip and spine, T-score: normal −1 or above; osteopenia −1 to −2.5; osteoporosis −2.5 or below; + fragility fracture = established.

Type I (postmenopausal)Women 50–70; trabecular; vertebral crush, Colles'
Type II (senile)Both sexes over 70; trabecular + cortical; hip, vertebra
OthersIdiopathic juvenile; secondary (steroids, thyroid, parathyroid, rheumatoid arthritis, alcohol, immobility); regional (disuse, complex regional pain)

Risk factors (3m W22, S26), prevention (3m W18)

Fixed: female, age, early menopause, family hip fracture, thin build, previous fragility fracture. Changeable: inactivity, low weight, low calcium and vitamin D, smoking, alcohol, caffeine. Disease, drugs: steroids, rheumatoid arthritis, thyroid, diabetes. Prevent: peak bone mass in youth (jumping); lifelong impact, resistance, balance; calcium (milk, ragi, sesame, greens), sunlight; no smoking; falls prevention; DEXA, drugs if high risk.

Exercise for osteoporosis (S25)

Multicomponent; aerobic alone is not enough (Too Fit to Fracture 2014). Resistance 2–3 days, 8–12 reps, progress: sit-to-stand, step-ups, rows, wall push-ups, back extensors. Impact (no fracture): heel drops, stamping, stairs. Balance most days: tandem, single-leg, tai chi. Posture: chin tucks, thoracic extension. Spine-sparing: no loaded flexion or twist (toe touches, sit-ups); hip hinge; log-roll out of bed. S25: TUG, chair stand; higher chair, low step, rail.
