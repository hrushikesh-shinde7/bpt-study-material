Asked: 7 marks in 7 papers: define ageing, types, genetic theories (W16); theories of ageing (S17, S18, W19, S24); enumerate and explain any three (W20); enumerate and describe one (S21). 3 marks: enumerate theories (S22); three theories (W22).

Ageing and its types (7m W16)

Ageing: progressive, time-related decline in structure and function that reduces the ability to adapt to stress and raises the risk of disease and death. Universal, progressive, intrinsic, harmful.

Types: chronological (years); biological (age of the systems; two 70-year-olds differ); psychological (memory, learning, coping); social (retirement, widowhood); functional (what the person can do vs peers); primary (age itself) vs secondary (disease, inactivity, lifestyle).

Successful ageing (Rowe and Kahn): low disease and disability, high physical and cognitive function, active engagement with life: the PT aim.

Programmed (genetic) theories (W16)

Genetic clockGenes switch on and off in sequence; species maximum lifespan; long-lived families; progeria
Hayflick limitNormal cells divide a limited number of times, then senesce: fibroblasts stop after 40–60 divisions (Hayflick and Moorhead 1961)
TelomereChromosome end caps shorten at each division → cell stops; explains Hayflick; telomerase restores them in germ and cancer cells
EndocrineHypothalamic-pituitary clock: ↓ growth hormone, sex hormones, melatonin; menopause; insulin and IGF-1
ImmunologicalThymus shrinks (immunosenescence): more pneumonia, shingles, cancer, autoimmunity; poor vaccine response

Damage (stochastic) theories

Free radical (Harman 1956)Reactive oxygen species from metabolism damage DNA, proteins, membranes; mitochondrial damage makes more; lipofuscin builds up
Wear and tear (Weismann)Tissues wear out with use (cartilage in OA); body also repairs, so only part of the story
Cross-linking (Bjorksten 1942)Collagen and elastin cross-link (glucose: glycation end products) → stiff skin, arteries, tendons, lens; worse in diabetes
DNA damageUnrepaired somatic mutations; cancer rises with age
Error catastrophe (Orgel 1963)Protein-making errors accumulate; largely not supported
Rate of living (Pearl 1928)Faster metabolism, shorter life; birds an exception

Psychosocial theories

Disengagement (Cumming and Henry 1961)Elder and society withdraw by mutual agreement; criticised: not inevitable, harms health
Activity (Havighurst 1961)Staying active, replacing lost roles → successful ageing; basis of exercise groups, day centres
Continuity (Atchley 1989)Keep earlier habits and interests; plan around the past (gardening, temple, walking group)
Erikson's 8th stageEgo integrity vs despair; reminiscence, respect, purposeful roles

What they mean for PT

No single theory explains ageing; much decline is secondary and PT can change it. Free radical: moderate exercise raises own antioxidant defences; no smoking. Cross-linking: daily stretching and ROM; diabetes control. Wear and tear: joint protection, weight control, strengthening; disuse is worse than use. Immune, endocrine: exercise improves immunity and insulin sensitivity; vaccination. Activity, continuity: groups, walking clubs, roles. Disengagement: withdrawal is a risk, not normal; encourage participation.
