Asked: 7 marks in 7 papers: define ageing, types, genetic theories (W16); theories of ageing (S17, S18, W19, S24); enumerate and explain any three (W20); enumerate and describe one (S21). 3 marks: enumerate theories (S22); three theories (W22).

Ageing and its types (7m W16)

Ageing: progressive, time-related decline in structure and function that reduces the ability to adapt to stress and raises the risk of disease and death. Universal, progressive, intrinsic, harmful. Types: chronological; biological (two 70-year-olds differ); psychological; social (retirement, widowhood); functional; primary (age itself) vs secondary (disease, inactivity). Successful ageing (Rowe and Kahn): low disease and disability, high function, active engagement.

Programmed (genetic) theories (W16)

Genetic clockGenes switch on and off in sequence; species lifespan; progeria
Hayflick limitFetal fibroblasts stop after ~50 doublings (40–60) (Hayflick and Moorhead 1961); senescent cells build up
TelomereTTAGGG caps shorten each division (end-replication problem) → senescence; ~9.5 kb at birth; telomerase rebuilds (germ, cancer cells); Nobel 2009. Against: mice long telomeres, short life; cancer risk
Endocrine (Dilman)Hypothalamic clock: ↓ growth hormone, sex hormones, melatonin; menopause
Immunological (Walford)Thymus shrinks (immunosenescence): more pneumonia, shingles, cancer; "inflammaging"; poor vaccine response

Damage (stochastic) theories

Wear and tear (Weismann 1882)Tissues wear out with use (cartilage in OA); but body repairs and use strengthens
Cross-linking (Bjorksten 1942)Collagen cross-links, glycation → stiff skin, arteries, tendons, lens
DNA damage; error catastrophe (Orgel 1963)Unrepaired mutations (cancer rises with age); protein errors (largely not supported)
Rate of living (Pearl 1928)Faster metabolism, shorter life; birds an exception

Free radical theory in full (7m S21)

Harman 1956; mitochondrial version 1972. Radical: unpaired electron. ROS: superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl, from normal metabolism (electron leak in mitochondria). Damage: membrane lipid peroxidation, oxidised proteins, DNA mutations; damaged mitochondria leak more (vicious circle). Defences: superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase; vitamins C, E. Lipofuscin builds up. Against: antioxidant pills do not prolong life (68 trials); naked mole-rat lives > 28 years despite high damage. PT: regular exercise raises own antioxidant enzymes (hormesis); pills blunt gains.

Psychosocial theories

Disengagement (Cumming and Henry 1961): elder and society withdraw by mutual agreement; criticised: not inevitable, harms health. Activity (Havighurst 1961): staying active, new roles → successful ageing; basis of exercise groups. Continuity (Atchley 1989): keep earlier habits (temple, walking group). Erikson's 8th stage: ego integrity vs despair.

What they mean for PT

No single theory explains ageing: today seen as interacting hallmarks of ageing (12 in 2023). Much decline is secondary. Cross-linking: daily stretching; diabetes control. Wear and tear: joint protection, weight control; disuse is worse than use. Telomere: activity, no smoking, weight and stress control. Immune, endocrine: exercise improves immunity, insulin sensitivity; vaccination. Activity, continuity: groups, walking clubs, roles. Disengagement: withdrawal is a risk, not normal.
