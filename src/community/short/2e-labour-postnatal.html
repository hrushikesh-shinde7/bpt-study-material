Stages and changes of labour (S18)

First: cervix to 10 cm (active from 5 cm; up to 12 h first labour, 10 h later, WHO 2018): walking, upright, breathing, relaxation, massage, TENS. Second: full dilatation → birth: her position, push with the urge. Third: baby → placenta. Fourth and puerperium: first hours, then 6 weeks: breathing, ankle pumps, pelvic floor, feeding posture.

Changes: stronger, more frequent contractions; effacement, dilatation, descent, rotation; each contraction returns blood → output and BP rise; pain → over-breathing; slow stomach, fatigue; bladder pressed; perineum stretches greatly in the second stage.

PT in labour and positions (7m S23; 3m W19; S24)

Aims: less fear and pain, control, mobility, progress, save energy, protect perineum; taught in antenatal classes.

First stage: walking, standing leaning forward, birthing ball, kneeling over a ball, all-fours (backache), side-lying for rest or epidural. Upright and mobile: first stage ~1 h 22 min shorter, fewer caesareans and epidurals (Lawrence 2013). Second stage: position of her choice (kneeling, supported squat, all-fours, side-lying); avoid lying flat (great vessels, narrow outlet; WHO 2018).

Breathing in labour (7m S19)

  • Early: slow relaxed breathing: sigh out at start, in by nose, out by mouth, sigh at end.
  • Strong contractions: lighter, slightly faster at the peak, back to slow as it fades.
  • Transition: "pant-pant-blow" to avoid pushing before full dilatation.
  • Second stage: push with the urge, breathing out or short holds, not long forced breath-holding; pant as the head crowns.
  • Over-breathing (tingling, dizzy): slow down, breathe into cupped hands. Why: focus, relaxation, oxygen for both, no exhausting holds.

Pain relief in labour (7m S17)

  • Relaxation: Mitchell's or progressive, practised before; music. Massage, warmth: firm sacral pressure, warm packs (both WHO-recommended if she wants).
  • TENS: two pairs on the back, T10–L1 and S2–S4; low background + boost button in contractions; start early; not in water. Little effect on pain scores but most would use again, no harms (Dowswell 2009).
  • Warm shower or bath; continuous companion (less fear, fewer drugs); medical: epidural, Entonox, opioids → PT adjusts positions.

Puerperium (S18) and goals (3m W25)

6 weeks. Involution: fundus at umbilicus → down a finger a day → not felt above pubis by ~2 weeks → near normal by 6; after-pains with feeding. Lochia: red → pink-brown → yellow-white. Perineum stretched or torn, episiotomy heals 1–2 weeks, early leakage; lax abdomen, diastasis; lax ligaments for months; DVT risk raised 6 weeks; milk comes in day 2–4; constipation; baby blues.

Goals: prevent complications (chest, DVT, retention, constipation); relieve pain; restore pelvic floor and abdominal wall; posture for feeding, lifting, carrying; emotional support; gradual return to fitness and work.

Postnatal programme (S16, W20)

  • Week 1: deep breathing; ankle pumps; pelvic floor from day 1 (eases swelling); transversus, pelvic tilts; roll to get up; walking; check diastasis; feeding posture.
  • Weeks 2–6: longer holds, fast contractions; bridging, side-lying leg lifts, four-point arm and leg lifts; head lifts only if no doming; daily walks.
  • 6–12 weeks: after the 6-week check: brisk walking, cycling, swimming (bleeding stopped), resistance.
  • After 12 weeks: running only when load and impact tests pass without leak, heaviness or pain (Goom 2019); 150 min/week.

Perineum: wrapped ice 10–15 min in first 24–48 h; pelvic floor pumping; side-lying; soft cushion, squeeze before sitting; pad on stitches when opening bowels; no straining; report pain, smell, discharge.

Caesarean complications (3m S17)

Bleeding; wound or uterine infection; DVT, PE; chest infection, atelectasis; retention, bladder injury; ileus, wind pain; wound breakdown, hernia, adhesions; spinal headache; delayed mobility and feeding; later scar rupture.

After caesarean (S17 elective, cephalopelvic disproportion)

Before: breathing, supported huff and cough, bed mobility, leg exercises. Day 0–1: deep breathing; huff with a pillow over the wound; ankle pumps, static quads; roll and push up; early upright walking; gentle transversus, pelvic floor. Days 2–7: walking; feeding off the wound (side-lying, football hold, lap pillow); walking, pelvic tilts, abdominal massage for wind. Weeks 2–6: progress abdominal and pelvic floor; scar massage once healed; lift nothing heavier than the baby ~6 weeks; drive when an emergency stop is painless. Home: pelvic floor, transversus 3 times a day; warning signs: calf pain, fever, discharge, breathlessness.

Postnatal complications (LAQ S23, 7 + 8)

Perineal pain: ice, pelvic floor, positioning. Retention, leakage: walking, privacy; pelvic floor training. Constipation, haemorrhoids, anal leakage: fibre, footstool, no straining; sphincter training. Diastasis: transversus, no sit-ups while doming. Back, pelvic girdle, coccyx pain: posture, core, belt, cushion. DVT: walking, leg exercise; calf pain = urgent review. Chest: breathing, huffing. Mastitis: fever, red hot area → refer. De Quervain's, carpal tunnel: neutral wrist, splint. Blues, depression: screen, refer.

Back pain after delivery (W18; W21)

Differential: mechanical (feeding, lifting, weak core); pelvic girdle (sacroiliac, symphysis); disc with root pain; coccydynia; dragging pain from pelvic floor weakness or prolapse (W21 forceps: PERFECT, heaviness, pelvic floor training); rarely epidural-site infection. Red flags: fever, night pain, leg weakness, bladder or bowel change, saddle numbness → refer. Assess: delivery (instruments, epidural, baby weight), habits, posture, active straight leg raise, posterior pelvic pain provocation, neuro screen, diastasis, pelvic floor, mood.

Engorgement (3m S24), blues (3m S22), feeding ergonomics (3m S21)

Engorgement: painful hard breasts 2–5 days after birth (milk stasis + swelling). Feed on demand 8–12 times a day, correct latch; hand-express to soften areola; cold packs between feeds; gentle lymphatic strokes to the armpit, no deep massage; supportive bra; fever, red hot area → refer (breastfeeding protocol 2022).

Blues: tearful, mood swings, poor sleep in about half or more; starts day 2–3, settles within 2 weeks (hormone fall, sleep loss). Reassure, rest, family help. Beyond 2 weeks or severe: postnatal depression → Edinburgh scale, refer; confusion or hallucinations = psychosis, emergency.

Feeding: baby to breast, pillow on lap; back-supported chair, feet on stool, arm supported; look up between sucks; vary holds (cradle, cross-cradle, football after caesarean, side-lying at night); straight wrists; nappies at waist height; carrier high and close.

ICF (S26: 33, first baby, 15 days after normal delivery)

  • Structure/function: dull back pain 5/10; heavy tender breasts; weak transversus, pelvic floor; possible diastasis; forward head; fatigue.
  • Activity: long feeds, lifting, bathing, rising from floor or low bed. Participation: baby care, self-care, rest.
  • Environment: family, ASHA lactation help (facilitators); floor sitting, low bed, no feeding pillow (barriers). Personal: anxious, little feeding knowledge.

Goals: short-term (1–2 weeks): engorgement settled, correct latch; pain 5 → 2/10; correct pelvic floor and transversus; daily walks. Long-term (6–12 weeks): pain-free baby care; good pelvic floor; functional gap; 150 min/week. Doses: heat 10–15 min; transversus 10 × 10 s, bridging 10, cat–camel, pelvic tilts 2–3 times a day; pelvic floor ≥ 8 contractions 3 times a day; lift close with bent knees.
