Ageing in general

Ageing: gradual, progressive decline in reserve and ability to adapt to stress, raising risk of disease and death. Primary = change in everyone; secondary = added by disease, inactivity, smoking, poor nutrition: much "ageing" is secondary, so exercise helps. At rest may look normal; loss shows under stress (illness, surgery, heat, exercise, a fall). People and systems age at different rates.

Level (Guccione)What the person can do
FunWork, home and leisure without restriction
FunctionMost tasks, done differently; leisure limited
FrailtyBasic ADL use most of the reserve; needs help outside or at home
FailureNeeds help with basic and instrumental ADL; may be bedbound

PT keeps people on the higher steps and helps them climb back after illness.

Musculoskeletal (3m W16, S25; W22)

MuscleSarcopenia: loss of mass, esp. type II (fast) fibres; fewer motor units; fat infiltration; strength and power fall faster than mass → slow sit-to-stand, poor trip recovery, falls
BoneLoss of mass and quality, fast after menopause; thin cortex, fewer trabeculae → osteoporosis, hip, wrist, spine fractures, kyphosis
CartilageLess water and proteoglycan, thinner, stiffer → OA, pain, stiffness
Tendon, ligament, capsuleMore collagen cross-links, less elastic, slow healing → ↓ ROM, tendon injury
DiscsDehydrate, lose height → stiff spine, spondylosis, stenosis
Posture, gaitForward head, thoracic kyphosis, flexed hips and knees; slower, shorter steps, wider base, more double support → falls, restricted breathing

Nervous system (7m S17, S26, W25)

  • Brain: volume falls (frontal, hippocampus); fewer synapses and dendrites; white matter change; ↓ dopamine (slow movement), ↓ acetylcholine (memory).
  • Cognition: slower processing and reaction time; weaker working memory, divided attention; knowledge, vocabulary, judgement kept.
  • Motor control: slower, less smooth, poor fine coordination, slower anticipatory postural adjustments. Sleep: lighter, broken, early waking.
  • Peripheral: fewer motor and sensory fibres; slower conduction; ankle jerks reduced or absent.
  • Autonomic: weak baroreflex (postural hypotension); poor temperature control; urgency, nocturia.

Senses (visual 3m S16) and sensorimotor (7m S23)

VisionPresbyopia; small pupil, yellow lens; poor dark adaptation, glare tolerance, contrast, depth; narrow field; cataract, glaucoma, macular degeneration → misjudges steps, night falls: lighting, contrast step edges, eye checks
HearingPresbycusis: high tones lost first; speech in noise → face them, speak slowly
Touch, proprioceptionFewer receptors; ↓ position and vibration sense, most in feet → poor balance on uneven ground, in dark
VestibularHair cell and fibre loss → dizziness on turning
Taste, smellReduced → poor appetite

Sensorimotor summary: slower sensing (fewer receptors, fibres) + slower processing (central) + slower, weaker response (fewer motor units, sarcopenia) → late, weak balance reactions → falls (falls).

Cardiovascular and respiratory (3m S19, S20, W24, S26; 7m S22)

  • Heart, vessels: stiff arteries → ↑ systolic BP, pulse pressure; thick LV, slow relaxation → filling depends on atrium (AF poorly tolerated); max HR falls (~220 − age); less adrenaline response; resting HR little change.
  • VO₂max and max output fall: 3–6% per decade in 20s–30s, over 20% per decade from the 70s (Fleg 2005). Varicose veins, swelling.
  • Chest wall stiffer (cartilage calcification, kyphosis); weaker respiratory muscles. Lungs lose elastic recoil; small airways close earlier.
  • FEV₁, VC fall; residual volume rises; TLC little change; more V/Q mismatch → resting PaO₂ falls slowly.
  • Weaker cough, slower mucociliary clearance, blunted response to low O₂ and high CO₂ → less reserve in pneumonia, surgery, bed rest: breathing exercises, early mobilisation, airway clearance.

Metabolic and other (W20)

Less muscle and water, more (abdominal) fat; BMR falls (mostly from muscle loss); insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes; ↓ growth hormone, sex hormones, skin vitamin D; less sweating, shivering, thirst (heat stroke, cold, dehydration); ↓ kidney filtration, slow drug clearance; thin fragile skin (pressure sores; care with heat and electrotherapy); weaker immunity, poorer vaccine response.

Effects of exercise (W21, W22, 7m W25)

MuscleResistance training gains strength and power at any age: frail ~90-year-olds +174% strength, +9% thigh area, tandem gait +48% in 8 weeks (Fiatarone 1990); early gains neural, later size
Nervous systemFaster reaction time, better coordination and balance reactions; aerobic: brain blood flow, memory, mood, less cognitive decline
Balance, fallsExercise cut community falls rate by 23%; balance + functional work best (Sherrington 2019)
Bone, jointsWeight-bearing and resistance slow bone loss; less OA pain, stiffness; stretching improves ROM
Heart, lungs↑ VO₂max; ↓ resting HR and BP; better tolerance
MetabolismBetter insulin sensitivity, less abdominal fat, better sleep
Function, mindFaster walking, easier stairs and sit-to-stand, independence; less depression, more social contact

WHO 2020 (65 and over)

  • 150–300 min a week moderate aerobic or 75–150 min vigorous.
  • Muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days a week.
  • Multicomponent balance and strength on 3 or more days a week (function, falls).
  • Some is better than none; reduce sitting.

Precautions

Screen heart disease, uncontrolled BP, diabetes, osteoporosis, falls risk; beta-blockers blunt HR: use Borg. Longer warm-up and cool-down; no breath-holding; rise slowly (postural hypotension); hydrate, avoid heat. Start low, progress slowly; supervise balance work; good footwear, safe floor.
