Definitions and components

Physical activity: any movement by skeletal muscles that uses energy. Exercise: planned, structured, repeated, to improve or keep fitness. Physical fitness: attributes people have or achieve that relate to the ability to perform physical activity (Caspersen 1985).

Health-related (5): cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, body composition. Skill-related (6): agility, balance, coordination, speed, power, reaction time.

Fitness tests (3m S26) and field tests (7m S16)

Cardiorespiratory: YMCA 3-min step, Rockport 1-mile walk, Cooper 12-min run, 6MWT, submaximal cycle. Strength: 1RM, handgrip. Endurance: push-up, curl-up. Flexibility: sit-and-reach, back scratch. Body composition: BMI, waist, skinfolds, bioimpedance. Agility: Illinois, T-test, 4 × 10 m shuttle. Balance: stork stand, Y-balance. Coordination: wall toss. Speed: 30–50 m sprint. Power: vertical and broad jump, medicine-ball throw. Reaction: ruler-drop.

Field tests in athletes: cheap, quick, own setting, sport-specific, many at once; track training and return to play. Examples: 20 m shuttle (beep), Yo-Yo intermittent recovery, Cooper run, sprints, jumps, Illinois, push-ups, 1RM. Rules: standard warm-up; same surface, time, order (least tiring first, endurance last); trained tester; record conditions; repeat at intervals.

Energy systems (7m W16) and the MET

  • ATP is the only direct energy for contraction; stored for seconds.
  • ATP–PC: creatine phosphate rebuilds ATP without oxygen; maximal efforts up to ~10 s (100 m sprint, jump).
  • Anaerobic glycolysis: glucose → lactate; ~10 s to 2 min (400 m run).
  • Aerobic: full oxidation of fat and glucose in mitochondria; beyond ~2 min and at rest (walking, marathon).
  • All overlap; intensity and length decide the lead. Intervals train anaerobic, continuous aerobic.

MET: energy used sitting quietly, 3.5 ml O₂/kg/min (~1 kcal/kg/h). Light below 3; moderate 3–5.9; vigorous 6 or more. Brisk walking ~4; running ~8–10.

Principles of training (3m S19; 7m S16)

  • Overload: adapts only when stressed beyond usual.
  • Progression: duration → frequency → intensity; ~10% a week.
  • Specificity: aerobic training builds endurance, not strength. Reversibility: "use it or lose it".
  • Individuality: age, fitness, disease, goals. Recovery: adapts in rest; too little → overtraining.

Prescription steps (7m W25) and phases (7m S16)

  1. Screen: history, risk factors, medicines, red flags; medical clearance if needed.
  2. Test fitness; set goals with the person.
  3. Write FITT-VP: aerobic, resistance, flexibility, balance.
  4. Teach warm-up, cool-down, talk test, RPE, warning signs.
  5. Progress; retest every 4–6 weeks; adjust for adherence and enjoyment.

Phases: warm-up 5–10 min (light aerobic, dynamic; less injury and heart strain) → conditioning 20–60 min → cool-down 5–10 min (prevents pooling, dizziness) → stretching ≥ 10 min while warm. Stages: initial (weeks) → improvement (months) → maintenance.

Circuit training (7m S25)

Stations one after another with little rest, different muscle groups plus aerobic stations; set repeated. Method: 8–12 stations; 30–60 s or 10–15 reps, light to moderate load; 15–30 s to move on; 2–3 rounds; warm-up, cool-down. Types: resistance, aerobic, sport-specific. Benefits: endurance + strength + some aerobic fitness in one short session; groups (schools, workplaces, cardiac rehab phase III, older adults on chairs). Precautions: technique; no breath-holding; adapt for joints; RPE in patients.

Sedentary lifestyle (7m W18)

Not active enough → 20–30% higher risk of death (WHO). Heart, metabolism: CHD, stroke, hypertension, type 2 diabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome. Cancer: colon, breast, endometrial. Musculoskeletal: back and neck pain, osteoporosis, sarcopenia, falls. Mind: depression, anxiety, poor sleep. Prevent: 150–300 min/week moderate + strength twice a week; break up long sitting (WHO 2020).

Bed rest deconditioning (7m S16; 3m W16)

Heart: ↑ resting HR, ↓ stroke volume, ↓ VO₂max, postural hypotension (plasma falls), DVT. Lungs: ↓ volumes, basal collapse, secretions, pneumonia. Muscle: antigravity wasting, ~1–1.5% strength a day (classic estimate). Bone, joints: bone loss, urine calcium, contractures. Skin: sores (sacrum, heels, hips). Metabolic: insulin resistance, protein loss. Kidney, gut: stones; constipation. Mind: poor balance, confusion, low mood. Prevent: sit out and walk early; breathing; ankle pumps; resisted exercise; turning; early weight-bearing.

Exercise in hypertension (7m W25)

Acute: BP lower for up to 22 h after one session (post-exercise hypotension). Chronic: resting BP down ~5–7 mmHg, most in hypertensives; resistance and isometric also help. How: ↓ sympathetic activity and peripheral resistance; better endothelium; weight loss; insulin sensitivity. Dose: moderate aerobic ≥ 30 min most days; resistance 2–3 days moderate load.

Precautions: diabetes (3m S18), hypertension

Diabetes dose (ADA 2016): ≥ 150 min/week over ≥ 3 days, no more than 2 days in a row off; resistance 2–3 non-consecutive days.

  • Hypoglycaemia: glucose before and after if on insulin or sulphonylureas; carry fast sugar; no insulin into the exercising limb; lows 6–15 h later, risk to 48 h. No exercise with high glucose + ketones.
  • Feet: daily check, footwear; cycling or swimming if ulcer. Retinopathy: no vigorous or heavy lifting, jarring, head-down, breath-holding. Autonomic neuropathy: RPE not HR; slow position change; fluids. Exercise 1–2 h after a meal, with a partner.
  • Hypertension: no start if resting BP ~200/110 or more; no breath-holding or heavy isometric straining; long warm-up and cool-down (vasodilators, alpha-blockers); RPE with beta-blockers; diuretics: dehydration, low potassium.
