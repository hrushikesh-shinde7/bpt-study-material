Disability management and acute care

Disability management: the "after injury" half of the worker care spectrum: a planned process by employer, worker and health team to limit the effect of an injury on work and bring the worker back safely and early.

Steps: early reporting → acute care → case management, contact with employer → FCE → work conditioning → work hardening → modified or transitional duties → full return → prevent recurrence.

Acute care: diagnosis and treatment; pain and swelling control (rest from aggravating tasks, ice, elevation, medication, electrotherapy); early graded movement, no long bed rest; education, reassurance, expected return date set early; employer contact on light duties, keep the worker connected; screen yellow flags (fear of movement, low mood, job dissatisfaction).

Functional capacity evaluation (7m W16, S17, S19; 3m W22, W25)

Definition: a standardised set of tests measuring a person's safe maximum ability to perform work-related physical tasks, compared with the demands of a job or a physical demand level. Uses: return-to-work decisions, job matching; planning conditioning and hardening; modified duties; disability rating, compensation; pre-placement; progress.

IntakeHistory, job description, symptoms, self-rated abilities, medical clearance, consent
Musculoskeletal screenPosture, ROM, strength, sensation, HR and BP
Material handlingLift floor to waist, waist to shoulder, overhead; carry; push, pull; weights raised to a safe maximum
Positional toleranceSit, stand, walk, stoop, kneel, crouch, reach, stairs and ladders, balance
Hand functionGrip (dynamometer), pinch, dexterity
AerobicStep or treadmill test
Effort, consistencyRepeated trials, HR response, observed body mechanics
ReportSafe abilities, demand level, comparison with job, projection to an 8-hour day, recommendations

Approaches: kinesiophysical (Isernhagen Work System, 28 tests; body mechanics decide the safe maximum); psychophysical (worker judges an acceptable load). Several hours, often over 1–2 days.

Content, examples (7m W18), job simulation (3m S16)

Work conditioning: aerobic (treadmill, cycle, walking) 20–40 min; progressive strengthening of the injured area and whole body (trunk, legs, grip); flexibility, motor control, core stability; basic body mechanics and lifting; education.

Work hardening: job simulation from the job analysis, graded in weight, height, distance, repetitions, time; conditioning continued; body mechanics and pacing; ergonomic changes for the real job; pain, stress and fear-of-movement counselling; work habits (timekeeping, instructions); re-measure against job demands; plan graded return.

  • Warehouse loader, low back strain: lift crates floor → waist → shoulder, 10 kg to 25 kg; carry 20 m; push a loaded trolley; load a mock truck bed; build to the full shift's lifts.
  • Data-entry clerk after carpal tunnel release or wrist fracture: typing and mouse 15 to 60 min; filing, lifting files; dexterity; workstation set-up, micro-breaks; build to a full day.

Job simulation: reproducing the real tasks (same tools, weights, heights, postures, pace) in the clinic or a mock workstation, to train (work hardening) and test readiness (FCE). Examples: work simulators with interchangeable handles, Valpar work samples, mock construction, assembly or office stations.

Return to work: same job full duty → same job with changes → modified or light (transitional) duty → different job, same employer → retraining. Graded: part days, lighter tasks, increase weekly. Prevent recurrence: ergonomics, home exercise, fitness, early reporting.

Long case W18: job task analysis (sugarcane worker, 32, 65 kg)

Cutting caneStooping, squatting for hours, forceful sickle strokes, heat → sustained neck flexion, repetitive shoulder and elbow load
Bundling, carryingHeavy bundles on head or shoulder, uneven fields → axial cervical compression, one-sided load
LoadingLifting above shoulder, twisting, climbing → overhead lifting with neck extension
Mill workFeeding crushers, sugar bags, vibration, noise, heat, shifts
OrganisationPiece rate, long harvest days, few breaks → fatigue

Assess: neck range, neuro screen (myotomes, reflexes, sensation, Spurling's, upper limb tension tests), deep neck flexor, scapular and grip strength, pain scale, Neck Disability Index; FCE: floor-to-waist and waist-to-shoulder lifts, carrying, overhead and repetitive arm work, stooping; compare with job (very heavy work).

ICF and plan (W18)

  • Structure/function: 6 months mechanical neck pain with arm referral (rule out radiculopathy); reduced neck mobility; weak deep neck and scapular muscles; low work tolerance.
  • Activity: head and shoulder loading, stooped cutting, overhead loading. Participation: cannot manage full harvesting or loading work.
  • Environment: head-loading, piece rate, heat, few breaks. Personal: young, light build carrying heavy loads; deconditioning and fear likely.

Work conditioning (weeks 1–3): neck and thoracic mobility, nerve gliding, 10 reps twice a day (heat or TENS first if needed); deep neck flexors, isometrics, scapular retraction and lower trapezius with bands 3 × 10 → resisted shoulder work; aerobic 20–30 min, trunk, hip, leg, grip strength; hip-hinge and squat lifting with light loads.

Work hardening (weeks 3–8): stooped cutting with a weighted handle; carry bundles 10 kg rising to job load; load onto a raised platform; longer towards a full shift. Avoid head-loading: padded yoke on both shoulders, cart or wheelbarrow, share loads, keep close. Micro-breaks every 30–60 min, water in heat; pain education, graded exposure, full-day attendance. Return: cutting without carrying first, then full work; talk to the contractor on load size and carts.

Practice case W20 (supervisor, 54, after fracture fixation)

FCE: intake, medical clearance, fracture healing limits, ROM, strength, walking and stairs, standing and sitting tolerance, lifting and carrying within limits, hand function, aerobic test, report. Work conditioning: strength, mobility, gait, balance, aerobic. Work hardening: simulated floor rounds, stairs with files, desk work, building to full days; modified duties first.

Limits and cautions

FCE is affected by pain, fear and motivation, predicts return only modestly, needs a trained evaluator and standard protocol. Avoid long bed rest. Yellow flags delay recovery. Test within fracture healing limits; safe stopping criteria in lift tests.
