Nerve: pudendal (S2–S4) + direct S3–S4. Functions: support; continence; relax to void; sexual; controls abdominal pressure with diaphragm and transversus.

Micturition (W19, 5 of 15)

Sympathetic (hypogastric, T10–L2): relaxes detrusor, closes bladder neck (storage). Parasympathetic (pelvic, S2–S4): contracts detrusor (emptying). Pudendal (S2–S4): external sphincter, voluntary holding. Capacity 400–600 ml; cortex inhibits the pontine micturition centre until convenient → sphincters relax, detrusor contracts.

Incontinence types (7m S19)

Stress (SUI)Leaks on effort, cough, sneeze: weak floor (childbirth, menopause, cough, obesity, lifting)
Urgency (UUI)With or just after urgency: overactive detrusor, caffeine, infection
Mixed; othersTreat the worse first; overflow, fistula, functional

Stamey grades: 1 cough, sneeze, lift; 2 walking, standing up, stairs; 3 regardless of activity. Mechanism: urethral hypermobility or sphincter deficiency. PT: PFMT for all; knack for grade 1; biofeedback or ES if very weak; grade 3 → urogynaecologist.

Assessment and tests (W19, 3m S25)

History: when it leaks, how much, pads; urgency; bowel; births (big baby, forceps, tears), menopause, cough, weight, diuretics. Refer: blood in urine, recurrent infection, pain, palpable bladder, neuro signs, large prolapse.

  • Bladder diary ≥ 3 days; ICIQ-UI short form.
  • Pad test: 1-hour gain ≥ 1 g = positive. Cough stress test with a comfortably full bladder.
  • Q-tip test: swings > 30° on straining = hypermobility.
  • Digital vaginal exam (consent): PERFECT; perineometer. Urine test, residual, urodynamics before surgery.

PERFECT (3m W20; Laycock and Jerwood 2001)

P-E-R-F-ECT

Power: Modified Oxford 0–5 (0 none, 1 flicker, 2 weak, 3 moderate with lift, 4 good, 5 strong). Endurance: seconds held (up to 10). Repetitions of that hold (up to 10). Fast: 1-second contractions (up to 10). ECT: every contraction timed → becomes her programme.

PFMT: Kegel's (3m S24)

  • Supervised ≥ 3 months = first-line for stress and mixed (NICE); ~8 times more likely cured (Dumoulin 2018).
  • "Squeeze and lift as if stopping wind and urine"; no breath-holding or buttock squeeze; check by palpation; stopping the stream only to find the muscle.
  • Dose: holds up to 10 s × 10 + up to 10 fast; at least 8 contractions 3 times a day, set from PERFECT.
  • Lying → sitting → standing → functional (lift, stairs, jump). The knack: strong contraction just before and during cough, sneeze, lift.

Bladder training, deferment (3m W19), adjuncts (IFT 3m W18)

Bladder training ≥ 6 weeks: void by the clock, lengthen gap by ~15 min towards 3–4 h. Deferment: stop, stand still or sit (perineal pressure); quick strong squeezes; slow breathing; distract; curl toes; walk calmly to the toilet.

Continence-promoting advice (3m S18)

Drink normally (1.5–2 L); less caffeine, fizzy, alcohol; avoid constipation (fibre, footstool); weight loss if BMI over 30; stop smoking, treat cough; sit fully; knack before lifting; PFMT for life.

Prolapse (7m S20, S21, W22, W25; 3m W21)

Definition: descent of a vaginal wall, uterus or vault. Anterior: cystocele, urethrocele. Middle: uterine, vault. Posterior: rectocele, enterocele.

1st degreeCervix down, inside vagina (POP-Q I: > 1 cm above hymen)
2nd degreeCervix out through opening on straining (II: within 1 cm of hymen)
3rd (procidentia)Whole uterus outside (III: > 1 cm below; IV: complete eversion)

Causes: childbirth, low oestrogen, chronic pressure (cough, constipation, lifting, obesity), ageing. Symptoms: something coming down, dragging worse by evening, backache, leaks.

PT for prolapse; effects of exercise (W25)

  • Supervised PFMT ≥ 16 weeks first option for stage 1–2 (NICE).
  • How it works: knack narrows the levator hiatus during cough or lift; strength training → thicker, stiffer muscle, bladder and rectum higher at rest (ultrasound, Brækken 2010).
  • Effects: one POP-Q stage better in 19% vs 8% (Brækken); fewer symptoms (POPPY); Indian trial: one-to-one training beat a manual (Kashyap 2013). Reviews: first-line for stage I–III; long-term effect uncertain (Bø 2022). Dose: 3 × 8–12 near-max, 3–4 times a week, ≥ 4 months. Modify, not stop, straining tasks.
  • Weight, constipation, cough, knack; pessary if unfit or unwilling for surgery; surgery (vaginal hysterectomy, colporrhaphy) with PT before and after.

Bowel in pregnancy and after birth (S18)

Reasons: constipation (progesterone, iron, uterus pressure); haemorrhoids; painful defecation (stitches); anal incontinence (3rd–4th degree tear, pudendal stretch). Assess: diary, Bristol scale, leakage, tear. Manage: footstool, knees above hips, lean forward, breathe out, no strain, pad on stitches; fibre, fluids; gentle PFMT and sphincter exercises early.

Hysterectomy (3m S23), PCOD (S26)

Indications: fibroids (commonest), uncontrolled bleeding, adenomyosis, endometriosis, prolapse, cancer, chronic PID pain, postpartum haemorrhage. PT: breathing, supported cough, leg exercises, early walking, gentle PFMT, no heavy lifting ~6 weeks. PCOD: 2 of 3 (anovulation, androgen signs, cystic ovaries); aerobic 150–300 min a week + strength 2 days; 5–10% weight loss over 6 months; core, posture.

ICF (W22, S25: 38, two children, leaks on cough, housework, lifting)

  • Structure/function: weak pelvic floor (Oxford 2, endurance 4 s, 5 fast), no pre-contraction.
  • Activity: coughing, sneezing, lifting, housework, exercise.
  • Participation: avoids outings, classes, social events; pads.
  • Environment: can afford PT, family (facilitators); distant toilets, pad cost (barriers). Personal: 38, embarrassment, maybe overweight.

Goals: short-term (4–6 weeks): correct contraction; Oxford 2 → 3; 6-s holds; knack; leaks halved. Long-term (3–6 months): Oxford 4; 10-s holds × 10; 10 fast; dry on cough, lift, housework; no pads. Low-impact aerobic 150 min a week; review every 4–6 weeks; refer if no gain by 3 months.
