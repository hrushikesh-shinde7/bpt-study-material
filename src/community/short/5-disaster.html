Definition and types (7m W19, S24)

Disaster (Disaster Management Act 2005): a catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence, natural or man-made, or by accident or negligence, causing substantial loss of life, human suffering, or damage to property or environment, beyond the coping capacity of the affected community. Hazard = the threat; vulnerability = how easily people are harmed (weak buildings, poverty, disability); risk = hazard × vulnerability ÷ capacity.

NaturalGeophysical: earthquakes (Latur 1993, Bhuj 2001), tsunami 2004, landslides. Weather: floods (Kerala 2018), cyclones (Odisha 1999), drought, heat waves. Biological: epidemics (COVID-19)
Man-madeTechnological: Bhopal gas leak 1984, fires, building collapse, nuclear. Transport and crowds: rail, road, stampedes. Conflict: terrorism, war, riots

Also by onset (sudden or slow) and scale (local, state, national).

Principles (7m W19, S24) and India

Comprehensive (all hazards, phases, people); progressive (act before); risk-driven; integrated and coordinated (one plan and command); collaborative; flexible; professional; community participation and inclusion (women, elderly, disabled in planning); build back better (Sendai).

DM Act 2005: NDMA (chaired by the Prime Minister), State and District authorities, NDRF, National Institute of Disaster Management. National plan 2016; Sendai Framework 2015–2030.

Disaster management cycle (S26)

Before: mitigation (earthquake-resistant buildings, flood walls, land-use rules, industrial safety, accessible design, risk maps) → preparedness (plans, early warning, stockpiles, first aid and triage training, mock drills, lists of elderly and disabled and their evacuation). After: response (search and rescue, triage, first aid, evacuation, field hospitals, shelter, food, water, sanitation, epidemic prevention, psychological first aid) → recovery (rehabilitation, reconstruction, livelihoods, mental health, long-term disability support) → feeds back into mitigation.

START triage (S26)

Sorting casualties by urgency so most people survive; under a minute per adult; colour tags. Children: JumpSTART.

Walks → green. Not breathing after airway opened → black; breathes only after opening → red. RR over 30 → red. Refill over 2 s or no radial pulse → red. Cannot obey commands → red; obeys → yellow.

30-2-can do

Red (immediate): airway, severe bleeding, shock, tension pneumothorax. Yellow (delayed): closed fractures, stable spinal injury, moderate burns. Green (minor): sprains, cuts. Black: dead or unsurvivable with resources.

Role of the physiotherapist (S26)

Injuries: fractures, SCI (many after earthquakes), traumatic amputation, crush, nerve and head injury, burns; later chronic pain, disability, trauma.

  • Preparedness: in hospital and community plans; first aid, triage, safe lifting, spinal handling; stock crutches, walkers, wheelchairs, splints; evacuation plans for disabled and elderly; teach safe evacuation.
  • Response: triage, casualty handling; spinal transfer and positioning; fracture splinting; chest PT, breathing exercises; early mobilisation; pressure-sore prevention; amputation care; burns positioning; aids at discharge to free beds; psychological first aid. WHO: at least 1 rehab professional per 20 beds in field hospitals.
  • Recovery: rehab of SCI, amputation, fractures, nerve and brain injury; prostheses, orthoses, wheelchairs; shelter access; CBR; vocational rehab; follow-up.
  • Throughout: records, team coordination, rescuers' health, advocacy for inclusive plans.
