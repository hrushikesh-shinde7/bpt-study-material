Definition and types (3m S20; S23)

Rehabilitation (WHO): "a set of interventions designed to optimize functioning and reduce disability in individuals with health conditions in interaction with their environment". Older WHO wording: all measures that reduce the impact of disabling and handicapping conditions and enable disabled people to achieve social integration.

Aim: highest possible independence. Kinds: medical, vocational, social, educational, psychological.

By place: IBR: hospitals, centres, special schools. Outreach: staff go out (camps, mobile vans, home visits). CBR: in the person's own community with family and local workers, local resources, referral when needed.

IBR vs CBR (7m W16, W21, W24, S24, S25; 10m S23)

CBR (ILO, UNESCO, WHO 2004): "a strategy within general community development for the rehabilitation, equalization of opportunities, poverty reduction and social inclusion of all people with disabilities", through the combined efforts of disabled people, families, communities and government and non-government services.

Rehabilitation team (S23, S26)

Person and family at the centre, setting goals. Physiatrist: diagnosis, medical care, prognosis, certification. Physiotherapist: assessment, exercise, gait, pain, chest care, aids, home programme, prevention. Speech therapist, audiologist: speech, swallowing, hearing aids. Prosthetist and orthotist: limbs, callipers, splints. Psychologist: IQ tests, counselling. Vocational counsellor: work ability, placement. Nurse: skin, bladder, bowel, teaching family. Plus special teacher; in CBR, the CBR worker.

Multidisciplinary: each works separately. Interdisciplinary: share findings, common goals. Transdisciplinary: one key worker (CBR worker or family) delivers, trained by the others; usual in CBR.

Team effort in CBR (3m S23): needs span health, education, work, social life; shared goals avoid conflicting advice; few professionals train many local workers; two-way referral, continuity; community ownership → cheap, sustainable; family empowered.

Occupational therapist (3m S26) and social worker (3m W20, S26; 7m S21)

  • OT: ADL assessment and training (feeding, dressing, bathing, toileting, cooking); hand function, splints, sensory re-education; adaptive devices (built-up spoon, button hook, reacher, sock aid); home, school, work modification; cognitive and perceptual retraining; pre-vocational skills, seating; sensory integration in children.
  • Medical social worker: psychosocial assessment (family, income, housing, work); counselling to accept disability; linking to benefits: certificate and UDID card, pensions, concessions, free aids (ADIP), scholarships, loans; discharge planning, return to school or work; advocacy.
  • In CBR: survey to find persons with disability; awareness; self-help groups, CBR committees; linking to schemes, livelihood, education.

Vocational rehabilitation (3m W19, S21; 7m W17, S23)

Purpose (ILO Convention 159, 1983): "to enable a disabled person to secure, retain and advance in suitable employment" and so further integration into society.

  1. Evaluation: physical capacity (FCE), skills, interests, aptitude, work history, job market.
  2. Guidance and counselling: realistic goal matching ability and interest.
  3. Training: ITIs, Vocational Rehabilitation Centres for Handicapped, NGOs; work conditioning, work hardening.
  4. Placement: old job with changes; open employment (4% of government posts, RPWD Act 2016); sheltered workshop; supported employment; self-employment with loans (National Divyangjan Finance and Development Corporation).
  5. Follow-up to keep the job.

Significance of guidance: matches job to remaining ability (no wasted training); motivation, acceptance; earning, independence; social role, dignity; guides family and employer on adaptations; informs about schemes, reservation, loans.

ADL training and the disabled hand (7m W17 each)

Basic ADL: feeding, grooming, bathing, dressing, toileting, transfers, mobility. Instrumental: cooking, shopping, money, phone, transport. Steps: assess (Barthel, FIM) → goals with the person → task analysis → teach step by step (forward or backward chaining) in the real setting at the usual time → easiest method first, fade help → aids and environment → train family → review. Energy conservation and joint protection.

Examples: hemiplegia: affected arm in first, out last; paraplegia: sliding-board transfer, long-handled mirror; RA: built-up handles, lever taps; low vision: tactile marks on the stove.

Disabled hand (restore, protect, adapt): assess cause (nerve injury, leprosy, burns, stroke, RA), deformity, oedema (volumeter), goniometry, grip dynamometer (sitting, elbow 90°, mean of 3) and pinch gauge, monofilaments, two-point discrimination, Nine-Hole Peg, Jebsen–Taylor, DASH. Grips (Napier): power (cylindrical, spherical, hook), precision (tip, three-jaw, key pinch); train with graded objects. Treat oedema, mobilise, strengthen; splints (static or dynamic; bamboo or tubing): cock-up radial palsy, knuckle-bender mobile claw, web spacer median palsy, safe position for burns (wrist 20–30° ext, MCP 70–90° flex, IP straight). Sensory re-education: localisation then textures and objects, eyes closed, check eyes open. Anaesthetic hand (NLEP): inspect daily, soak ~½ h, scrape, oil while wet, protect from heat and friction. Adapt: built-up handles, universal cuff, one-handed use. Work: job analysis, work simulation, insulated tools, job change; tendon transfer for mobile claw.

NGOs (3m S16, W17, S23, S25; 7m S19), SSI (S18), Red Cross (S20, W21)

NGO: voluntary, non-profit, independent of government, a registered society or trust: ADAPT, Indian Red Cross, HelpAge India, Jaipur foot (BMVSS), Mobility India. Role: surveys, early identification; awareness; services where government does not reach (therapy, camps, aids, schools); training CBR workers and parents; vocational training, self-help groups; advocacy, linking to schemes; pilot programmes.

Spastics Society of India (now ADAPT): 2 October 1972, Mumbai, Dr Mithu Alur, for children with CP; Able Disabled All People Together. Inclusive education; therapies; teacher training; vocational training; early intervention; advocacy; outreach.

Red Cross: Henry Dunant (Solferino); ICRC Geneva 1863; Indian Red Cross by an Act of 1920, President of India is president. Seven principles: humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence, voluntary service, unity, universality. Functions: disaster relief; blood banks; first aid training; ambulances; maternal and child welfare; war victims; Junior Red Cross.
