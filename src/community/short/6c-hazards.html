Types of occupational hazards (3m W25)

Physical (heat, cold, light, noise, vibration, radiation, air pressure); chemical (dusts, fumes, solvents, metals, pesticides); biological (TB, hepatitis B, anthrax, leptospirosis); mechanical and ergonomic (machines, falls, lifting, posture: mechanical stress); psychosocial (job stress, shifts, monotony, harassment).

Chemical agents (3m S19) and pneumoconioses (S18, W18)

Three routes: local action on skin, eyes (dermatitis, burns, ulcers, cancer); inhalation (pneumoconiosis, asthma, CO, lead, solvents); ingestion from hands, food, smoking at work (lead, mercury). Pneumoconiosis: lung fibrosis from inhaled mineral or organic dust.

SilicosisSilica: quarrying, mining, sand industry, glass, pottery, slate-pencil, agate; upper-lobe nodular fibrosis, progressive massive fibrosis (PMF); silico-tuberculosis; no cure
Coal workers'Underground mining; nodules → PMF; chronic bronchitis; Caplan's (with rheumatoid arthritis)
Asbestosis (W18)Mining, cement sheets, insulation, brake linings, ship-breaking; lower-lobe fibrosis, pleural plaques, clubbing; lung cancer (more with smoking), mesothelioma decades later
Byssinosis (S18)Cotton, flax, hemp: carding and blowing rooms; "Monday chest tightness" on the first day back, later chronic obstruction

Schilling grades: ½ occasional tightness on the first working day; 1 every first day; 2 first and other days; 3 grade 2 with permanent incapacity.

Sequelae of pneumoconiosis (3m W20)

Progressive fibrosis, restrictive disease, PMF; tuberculosis (silica); chronic bronchitis, obstruction; pulmonary hypertension, cor pulmonale, respiratory failure; lung cancer, mesothelioma, pneumothorax; disability, loss of work and income, early death.

PT and prevention for dust disease

PT: breathing control, pursed-lip breathing; ACBT if sputum; thoracic mobility; graded exercise (pulmonary rehabilitation); energy conservation; oxygen as prescribed; quit smoking; vaccines.

Prevention: substitute (asbestos bans); wet methods, enclosure, exhaust ventilation; fitted respirators (N95 or better); pre-placement and periodic chest X-ray (ILO) and spirometry; TB screening in silica workers; remove from exposure; notifiable (Factories Act); compensation.

CTD (3m W16; 7m W22, S24) and carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS)

CTD: injury of muscles, tendons, nerves, joints from repeated small strains without enough recovery. Work factors: repetition, force, awkward or static posture, vibration, contact pressure, cold, no breaks, piece rate. Personal: age, female, pregnancy, menopause, diabetes, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis, obesity, previous injury, low fitness, smoking, stress. Examples: CTS, de Quervain's, trigger finger, lateral epicondylitis, rotator cuff, tension neck, hand-arm vibration.

Stage 1Ache during work, settles overnight; work normal; reversible
Stage 2Pain early in shift, lasts overnight; sleep disturbed; capacity reduced
Stage 3Pain at rest and with light tasks; cannot do light duties; months to years

CTS: median nerve under the flexor retinaculum; tingling thumb, index, middle, half ring, worse at night, shaking helps; later thenar wasting (abductor pollicis brevis). Tests: Phalen's (wrist flexion 60 s), Tinel's, carpal compression; nerve conduction. Treat: night splint in neutral; nerve and tendon gliding; ergonomics, breaks; injection; surgery if wasting, constant numbness or failed care.

Hand-arm vibration syndrome (HAVS)

Grinders, drills, riveters. Vascular: cold-triggered white finger, then blue, red. Sensorineural: tingling, numbness, poor dexterity, often CTS. Musculoskeletal: weak grip, pain. Stockholm scale: vascular 0–4 (4 = skin changes at tips); sensorineural 0SN–3SN (3SN = reduced discrimination or dexterity).

Gear (3m of S16): low-vibration tools, anti-vibration handles; tool balancers, suspension; maintenance; warm, dry gloves (anti-vibration gloves cut little); vibration meters, time limits; warm workplace; no smoking.

Industrial injury (7m S22) and risk assessment (7m S18)

Factors: worker (fatigue, no training, haste, alcohol, no protection); machine (unguarded, faulty); environment (light, noise, heat, slippery floors, height); organisation (long hours, night shifts, pressure, no rules or PPE); ergonomic (heavy lifting, posture, repetition).

Risk assessment: identify hazards → who is harmed → evaluate (likelihood × severity) → record and act → review. Tools: RULA 1–7 (7 change immediately); REBA 1–15 (11+ very high); NIOSH lifting equation (23 kg ideal limit, lifting index); Strain Index, OCRA; OWAS, QEC; Nordic Questionnaire.

Hierarchy of controls: elimination → substitution → engineering (guards, wet methods, hoists) → administrative (rotation, breaks, training) → PPE last.

ICF (S16 case: 45, automobile worker, vibration, no protection)

  • Structure/function: HAVS ± bilateral CTS; tingling, numbness, pain, poor two-point discrimination, weak grip and pinch, possible blanching; Phalen's, Tinel's positive.
  • Activity: holding tools, fine tasks, carrying. Participation: full factory work; job and income at risk.
  • Environment: vibrating tools, no PPE, cold workshop (barriers); safety officer, ESI (facilitators). Personal: earning member, possibly smoker.

Short-term goals (4–6 weeks): less pain and tingling, no blanching at work; better grip, pinch, dexterity; exposure below the action value; knows warm-hands routine and warning signs.

  • Remove exposure (main treatment): reduce tool work, rotate; refer for nerve conduction, vascular tests.
  • Nerve gliding (median, ulnar) 5–10 reps, 3 times a day; night splint in neutral.
  • Pain: warm (not hot) soaks, TENS. Grip: putty, gripper, opposition, pegboard, coins 10–15 min twice a day.
  • Blanching: keep warm, gloves, no smoking, warm-up exercises. Numb skin: protect, check daily; sensory re-education.

Practice cases (S16 sand, S21 coal, W17 clerk)

Sand worker: silicosis, rule out TB; low exercise tolerance; dust PT and prevention. Coal miner: task analysis: stooping, kneeling, crawling; drilling (dust, vibration); shovelling; heat, poor light, noise, shifts; roof falls, gas → coal workers' pneumoconiosis with chronic bronchitis → wet drilling, sprays, ventilation, respirators, periodic X-ray and spirometry, rotation, quit smoking, Mines Act. Clerk with thenar wasting: refer for nerve conduction, surgical opinion; breaks, nerve gliding; keyboard at elbow height, neutral wrists, mouse close; cock-up splint, wrist neutral, night and flares.
