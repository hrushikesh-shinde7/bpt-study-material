Definition and types (3m W16, S25)

Obesity (WHO): abnormal or excessive fat accumulation that may impair health; screen by BMI and waist, confirm by body fat. BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²: 68 ÷ 1.60² = 68 ÷ 2.56 = 26.6.

  • Distribution: android (apple, central; men; more diabetes and heart disease) vs gynoid (pear, hips and thighs; women).
  • Fat cells: hyperplastic (more cells; childhood; harder to reverse) vs hypertrophic (bigger cells; adult onset).
  • Cause: primary (intake more than use) vs secondary (hypothyroidism, Cushing's, PCOS, steroids, Prader–Willi). Severity: BMI grade.

Indian 2025: any BMI over 23 = obesity (no overweight step). Stage 1: no organ or activity effect. Stage 2: + waist ≥ 90 cm men, ≥ 80 cm women or waist-to-height over 0.5, + activity limit or related disease. Grades I 23–24.9, II 25–27.5, III 27.6–32.4, IV 32.5+. Body fat obesity: over 25.5% men, 38% women.

Significance: risk rises each grade; Asian Indians have more (abdominal) fat at the same BMI → lower cut-offs; grade guides lifestyle → drugs → surgery; waist catches risk at normal BMI. BMI uses: cheap screening, tracking, treatment decisions, surveys. Limits: fat vs muscle (athlete), no distribution, age charts in children.

Body composition (7m S16, W16; 3m S24), anthropometry (7m S26)

Fat mass: essential (nerves, marrow, organs; more in women) + storage fat. Fat-free mass: muscle, bone, organs, water (~three-quarters water); sets resting metabolic rate → keep it (resistance work, protein, no crash diets). Body fat % = fat mass ÷ weight × 100.

Causes, set point (7m S20), adult effects (7m W20)

Causes: high-calorie food, sugary drinks; sedentary work, screens; poor sleep, stress; genes, family habits; after pregnancy or caesarean; menopause; steroids; hypothyroidism, PCOS; stopping sport or smoking; ageing.

Set-point theory: hypothalamus (with leptin from fat) defends a "set" weight like a thermostat; dieting → hunger up, metabolism down → regain after crash diets. Slow loss with exercise and lasting habits may settle a lower set point.

Effects: metabolic (type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, fatty liver, gallstones, gout); heart (hypertension, CHD, failure, stroke, DVT); lungs (effort breathlessness, sleep apnoea, hypoventilation); joints (knee and hip OA, back pain, flat feet, heel pain); women (PCOS, infertility, gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, stress incontinence); cancer (breast, colon, endometrial); mind (depression, stigma, shorter life).

Childhood obesity (7m S20, W21, W24; LAQ W20, W21)

Measure: BMI-for-age on IAP 2015 charts (5–18 y): overweight from the adult-equivalent 23 line, obesity from 27; WHO standards under 5. Causes: fast food, sugary drinks, portions; little play; screen time; short sleep; obese parents, family habits; no safe play space; academic pressure; emotional eating; rarely endocrine or genetic; birth weight, mother's diabetes, formula. Ill effects: early type 2 diabetes, BP, fatty liver, sleep apnoea; flat feet, knock knees, slipped capital femoral epiphysis; early puberty, PCOS; teasing; persists into adult life.

School screening and promotion (W20)

Khelo India (9–18 y): BMI (+ waist); 600 m run or walk; 50 m dash; partial curl-ups; push-ups (modified for girls); sit and reach. Consent → start-of-year test by teachers, PTs → report card → refer obesity, high BP, low fitness → retest at year end.

Promotion: ≥ 60 min/day moderate to vigorous, muscle-bone work 3 days a week (WHO); daily PE, active travel; no junk food or sugary drinks in canteens, home tiffin, water; limit screens; sleep; whole family changes; no blame, praise effort not weight; fun non-competitive games.

ICF (S25 worked case: 27, 5 ft 3 in, 68 kg, marriage in 3 months)

Status: BMI 26.6 (WHO overweight; Asian obese; 2025 grade II); waist 99 cm, waist-to-height 0.62; fat 50%; sit and reach −3 cm poor; 12 modified push-ups fair (good 15–20); 34 squats baseline; test aerobic (6MWT, YMCA step).

  • Structure/function: central fat, poor flexibility, fair endurance; crash-diet fatigue; rule out thyroid, PCOS.
  • Activity: low exercise tolerance; tires in busy wedding days.
  • Participation: anxious about appearance; social events.
  • Environment: wedding food, social pressure, little time (barriers); clinic, family (facilitators). Personal: motivated, short deadline, believes in crash dieting.

Goals: short-term (4 weeks): stops crash diet, dietitian plan (~500 kcal deficit); 0.5 kg a week; 150 min/week; sit and reach 0 cm. Long-term (3 months): ~5 kg (7%), waist −5 cm; over 250 min/week; 15–20 push-ups; healthy eating after marriage.

Weight management: FITT and lifestyle

  • Aerobic: ≥ 5 days; 40–59% HRR (talk, not sing) → vigorous, intervals; 30 → 60 min/day; 150/week, then over 250 for real loss and to keep it off (ACSM 2009); walking, cycling, water exercise, dance.
  • Resistance: 2–3 non-consecutive days; 8–10 exercises, 2–3 × 8–12; squats, lunges, bridges, modified push-ups, band rows, planks; keeps fat-free mass. Flexibility daily: 30 s × 2–3. Warm-up and cool-down 5–10 min.
  • Karvonen: age 27, resting HR 80 → max 193, reserve 113 → 40–59% ≈ 125–147 beats/min.
  • Diet (dietitian): 500 kcal/day deficit ≈ 0.5 kg/week; less refined carbs, sugar, fried food; more vegetables, pulses, protein. Target 5–10% in 3–6 months, then maintenance.
  • Lifestyle: rising step target, stairs, less sitting, sleep 7–8 h, stress, food diary, family; review every 2–4 weeks.
  • Other cases: housewife: home exercise around housework; banker (BMI 40): low impact, diastasis, desk set-up, breaks every 30–60 min; lecturer: sugar, BP, feet; boy: IAP charts, family.

Crash dieting and precautions

Crash diet fails: loses water and muscle, not fat; metabolism slows (set point); hunger rises; fatigue, dizziness, hair loss, irregular periods, gallstones, deficiency, disordered eating; yo-yo regain with more fat. Precautions: screen heart, diabetes, BP; low impact for painful knees; avoid heat; footwear; blood sugar in diabetes.

Obesity with knee pain (LAQ S16, W16)

Extra load, weak quads, inactivity, fat-tissue inflammation → early OA. Exercise: static quads, straight-leg raise, terminal extension, mini-squats in pain limits; hip abductors, extensors; hamstring, calf stretch; static cycling, water walking → walking. Loss of over 5% in ~20 weeks improved disability (Christensen 2007). Joint protection: no floor squatting or cross-legged sitting in flares; higher chair, western toilet; rail, up with good leg, down with painful; stick in opposite hand; cushioned shoes. Heat, ice, TENS as a bridge.
