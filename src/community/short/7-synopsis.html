Asked: not set in theory papers. The synopsis carries 25 marks in the university practical (with long case 50, journal 5) and 30 in the preliminary; expect a presentation and viva on question, design, sample size, outcomes, statistics and ethics.

What it is and what examiners want

Synopsis: a short, complete plan of a study (what you will ask, why, exactly how), carried out in internship; any area of physiotherapy. Examiners look for: clear answerable question, fitting design, defined variables, feasible sample and setting, valid outcomes, sensible analysis, ethics, and that you can explain every choice.

Choosing the question

FINER: Feasible (participants, internship time, skills, money); Interesting; Novel (new population, Indian or rural setting, outcome); Ethical (consent, approvable); Relevant (practice, policy). PICO: Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome: desk workers with neck pain; deep neck flexor training vs stretching; Neck Disability Index at 4 weeks.

Good topics: prevalence surveys (back pain in bus conductors, postnatal incontinence, falls risk in a seniors' club); correlation (BMI and knee pain); small comparisons of two techniques. Avoid: rare conditions, long follow-up, costly equipment, vague titles.

Sections of a synopsis

Title (design, intervention, population, outcome) → introduction (problem with numbers, impact, gap) → need for study → review of literature (10–20 recent studies by theme, ending with the gap) → question and hypotheses (null: no difference; alternative) → aim and objectives (one aim; measurable objectives) → methodology → ethics (ethics committee, written consent in local language, confidentiality, right to withdraw; CTRI (Clinical Trials Registry–India) for a trial) → timeline (Gantt) and budget → references (one style, usually Vancouver) → annexures (consent, information sheet, proforma, questionnaires, protocol).

Methodology

Designs: cross-sectional (prevalence: neck pain in IT workers); correlational; case–control (past exposures); cohort (follow forward); quasi-experimental pre–post; RCT (random allocation, strongest for cause and effect).

  • Population: where and who; inclusion and exclusion criteria, measurable, with reasons.
  • Sampling: probability (simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster) or non-probability (convenience, purposive, snowball); allocation by sealed envelopes or computer list.
  • Sample size: from a previous study (prevalence or mean difference and SD), 5% significance, 80% power, + 10–20% drop-outs; state the source.
  • Variables: independent (intervention, exposure), dependent (outcomes); control confounders.
  • Outcomes valid and reliable: VAS, NPRS, goniometer, Neck Disability Index, Oswestry, Berg, TUG, 6MWT, ICIQ, PERFECT.
  • Procedure repeatable: dose (frequency, intensity, time, type, duration), when and who measures (blinding).

Presentation and viva

8–10 min: title, guide → problem with one number → gap (3–4 studies) → aim, objectives, hypotheses → design, setting, sample size, criteria → outcomes and study flowchart → analysis, ethics, timeline → expected use.

Asked: why this topic, what is new; why this design, limits; sample size; dependent vs independent; validity and reliability of outcomes; bias (randomisation, blinding); null hypothesis and test; consent, harm, drop-outs; feasibility in internship.

Tips: know every reference; admit limits; carry consent form, proforma, questionnaire; simple slides; write it yourself (copying fails). Draw: study flowchart (eligible → excluded → randomised → two groups → outcomes → analysis); Gantt chart. Underline: FINER, PICO, null hypothesis, power 80%, valid and reliable, informed consent, CTRI.
