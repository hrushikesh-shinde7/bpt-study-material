Asked: long answers: 88-year-old after hip replacement, home or old age home, prevention (W17); balance pathomechanics + prevention programme (S20, W24, 7 + 8); causes + rehab, 72-year-old with fear of falling (S23); intrinsic and extrinsic, two measures each (S24). 7 marks: causes + environmental modifications (W22). 3 marks: risk factors (W20); extrinsic factors (W25).

Definition and burden

Fall (WHO): event in which a person comes to rest inadvertently on the ground, floor or other lower level. 28–35% of 65+ fall each year, 32–42% over 70; a previous fall is the strongest predictor. Fractures, head injury, long lie, fear, lost independence, death.

Risk factors (3m W20, W25; W22, S23, S24)

IntrinsicLeg weakness (quads, hip abductors, dorsiflexors), arthritis; poor balance, shuffling gait, previous falls; vision (cataract, bifocals), foot neuropathy, vestibular loss; postural hypotension, stroke, PD, dementia, depression, nocturia; fear
ExtrinsicPoor light, wet floors, loose rugs, clutter, cords, no stair rails, low beds, Indian toilets, no grab bars, uneven roads; 4 or more drugs, sedatives, antidepressants, antipsychotics, BP drugs, diuretics; loose slippers, worn soles, faulty aids

Two measures each (S24): strength, footwear; balance, aid; eye check, single-vision glasses; medical review, rise slowly; home check; drug review.

Pathomechanics of balance (7m S20, W24)

  • Sensory: vision (contrast, glare), vestibular (hair cells), feet less sensitive; more reliance on vision, so dim light falls.
  • Central: slower reaction; dual-tasking worsens gait.
  • Motor: type II fibre and power loss, step too slow or short; ↓ ankle range → ankle to hip strategy, late stepping.
  • Flexed posture, smaller limits of stability; late anticipatory adjustments; postural hypotension, fear, drugs → late, weak response: fall.

Assessing risk

Three questions: fallen in the past year? unsteady? worried? Each fall: where, how, dizziness, injury, time on floor. TUG ≥ 13.5 s, Berg, gait speed, chair stand, postural BP, vision, feet, cognition, FES-I; drugs; home visit. Low risk: education + exercise; high risk (injury, repeated falls, cannot get up, poor gait): multifactorial plan (World Falls Guidelines 2022).

Prevention programme (8m S20, W24; W17)

  • Exercise cut falls 23%; balance and functional 24%; with resistance about a third (Sherrington 2019); best with over 3 h a week of challenging balance (2017).
  • Otago: home leg strengthening (ankle weights) + balance ~3 times a week + walking; falls down about a third.
  • Balance: narrow base (tandem, single leg), less hand support, reaching, stepping, turns, tai chi; add dual tasks.
  • Strength: sit-to-stand, mini-squats, heel and toe raises, hip abduction, knee extension; 2–3 sets of 8–12, 2–3 times a week.
  • Drug review; cataract surgery; footwear; rise in stages; vitamin D, hip protectors in care homes.

Environment (7m W22): bright light, night lights to toilet; no rugs or cords, mark step edges; rails both sides; grab bars, raised commode over Indian toilet, bathing stool; bed and chair at knee height; items waist to shoulder height. Home modification: rate ratio 0.81 (Gillespie 2012). Getting up: check injury → roll to side → hands and knees → crawl to chair → half-kneel, push up.

ICF (S23: 72, male, two falls in a year, fear of falling)

  • Structure/function: poor balance, weak legs, fear. Activity: walking, stairs, going out.
  • Participation: social life, independence. Environment: home hazards, drugs. Personal: 72, lost confidence; check BP drugs, nocturia.

Rehab: balance 3 times a week supervised + daily home, to over 3 h a week; Otago strength 2–3 × 8–12; gait with turns, obstacles, dual tasks; fear: small goals, graded exposure, floor recovery, groups; home and drug review; alarm in reach. Review every 4–6 weeks (TUG, Berg, FES-I); continue long term.
