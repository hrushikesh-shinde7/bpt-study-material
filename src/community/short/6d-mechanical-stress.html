How work causes pain

Cumulative microtrauma, not one injury (CTD). Physical: awkward or static posture held long, repetition, force, vibration, contact pressure, cold, too little rest. Personal: low fitness, obesity, previous injury, smoking, age. Psychosocial: high demand, little control, deadlines, monotony, poor support: more muscle tension, pain persists.

PT method: job and task analysis → risk factors → treat impairments → change the workstation or way of working → self-management, fitness.

Desk and computer workers (long cases)

Problems: neck and shoulder pain with forward head, rounded shoulders; thoracic stiffness; low back pain from slumping; wrist symptoms (carpal tunnel); eye strain; weight gain.

  • Screen: in front, arm's length (50–100 cm), top line at or slightly below eye level; laptop on a stand, separate keyboard and mouse.
  • Chair: feet flat, thighs level; lumbar support; armrests let shoulders relax. Keyboard, mouse: close, elbow height, wrists straight.
  • Document holder beside the screen; headset for calls. Movement break every 30–60 min; look into the distance.
  • PT: deep neck flexor, scapular retractor, lower trapezius strengthening + general fitness (reduced pain; ergonomics alone weaker: Chen 2018); thoracic mobility, pectoral stretch; workstation exercises; heat, TENS only as a bridge to exercise; weight control, flare-up plan.

Prolonged standing (7m S21; watchman S23, traffic police S24)

Effects (Waters and Dick 2015): low back pain, leg pain, swelling, fatigue, varicose veins, heel pain. Why: static load, little calf pumping, hard floors, poor shoes.

Prevention: change position and walk (most effective); sit–stand or leaning stool; anti-fatigue mats; cushioned shoes, insoles; footrest to alternate feet; compression stockings if swelling; calf raises, ankle pumps in the shift; breaks.

Surgeons (7m S26): table so hands are near elbow height, elbows about 90° (lower for laparoscopy); monitor top at eye level; neutral neck with loupes; mat, shoes; stretch micro-breaks every 30–60 min (American College of Surgeons); back and neck strengthening.

Manual handling, low back pain in industry (7m W22, W25)

Factors: heavy, frequent, awkward lifting (bend, twist), far from body or from the floor; whole-body vibration; long sitting or standing; slips; low fitness, smoking, obesity; dissatisfaction, stress.

Revised NIOSH equation: 23 kg limit in ideal conditions, reduced for distance, height, travel, twist, frequency, poor grip; a 50 kg bag to 3 feet is far beyond it, so the task must change. Prevention: redesign first (lighter bags, trolleys, hoists, conveyors, waist-height loads, team lifts) → training, fitness. Current: lifting-technique training alone did not prevent back pain (Verbeek 2011).

W22 labourer (27, 50 kg bags, leg pain, bidis): mechanical low back pain, referred; education, graded activity, core and hip strength, hamstring flexibility, aerobic, quit smoking; 25 kg bags or two-person lifts, trolley or ramp, waist-height loading, rotation. Squatting work (S19): low stools, kneeling pads, rotate; quads and hips; stretch calves, hamstrings.

Job stress (7m W17, S18, W18) and shift work (3m W21)

Causes: demand–control (high demand, little control); effort–reward imbalance; monotony, dissatisfaction; deadline anxiety; long hours, shifts; poor relationships, harassment, insecurity. Effects: muscle tension, pain, headache, fatigue, poor sleep, high BP, anxiety, depression, errors, absence. Prevent: fair workload, control, variety, breaks, support, clear roles.

Shift work: disturbed body clock: poor sleep, fatigue, accidents; digestive, weight, metabolic and heart risk; night shifts "probably carcinogenic" (IARC 2019); family strain. Advice: forward-rotating shifts, dark quiet sleep room, regular meals, exercise, short runs of nights.

PT in stress management (7m S20, W21, W24)

  • Relaxation: Jacobson progressive (tense then release each group); Laura Mitchell physiological (move into the opposite of the stress posture); diaphragmatic breathing.
  • Exercise: regular aerobic, workplace stretching, yoga. Ergonomic and task changes; education on posture, sleep, pacing.
  • Refer anxiety or depression; evaluate (pain, absence, stress questionnaires).

ICF (S21 case: 36, computer operator, mother of a 1-year-old)

  • Structure/function: neck and upper trapezius pain, less range, forward head, rounded shoulders, weak deep neck flexors and scapular muscles, tight pectorals, poor sleep.
  • Activity: long typing, looking down, lifting and feeding the child. Participation: full working day, childcare, leisure.
  • Environment: unadjusted workstation, long hours, no breaks (barriers); family, employer willing to adjust (facilitators). Personal: working mother, little time: short home exercises.

Short-term (2–4 weeks): pain 6 → 3/10 at work; corrected sitting, better neck flexor hold; workstation adjusted, breaks every 30–60 min; safe lifting and feeding. Long-term (2–3 months): pain-free working day; posture held; self-checks set-up; exercise 3 times a week; no recurrence.

Plan: heat, TENS, mobilisation first 2 weeks → chin nods, band retraction, lower trapezius, pectoral stretch → resisted work, fitness; workstation, headset, micro-breaks; child close, knees bent; supported feeding; carrier; relaxation, walking or yoga.

Cautions

Neck pain: rule out radiculopathy. Tailor (S25) who cannot walk over 15 min: examine for spinal stenosis or vascular claudication (lumbar extension tests, foot pulses, ankle–brachial index, neurological exam).

  1. Task analysis or diagnosis (5): posture, load, hours, tools, environment; ICF; one line.
  2. Goals (5): short and long term, measurable, linked to work and home.
  3. Management and prevention (5): pain relief, exercise with doses, workstation, breaks, health promotion, return to work.
