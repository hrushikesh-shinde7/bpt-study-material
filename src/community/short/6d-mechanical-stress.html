How work causes pain

Cumulative microtrauma, not one injury (CTD). Physical: awkward or static posture held long, repetition, force, vibration, contact pressure, cold, too little rest. Personal: low fitness, obesity, previous injury, smoking, age. Psychosocial: high demand, little control, deadlines, monotony, poor support: more muscle tension, pain persists.

PT method: job and task analysis → risk factors → treat impairments → change the workstation or way of working → self-management, fitness.

Desk and computer workers (long cases)

Problems: neck and shoulder pain with forward head, rounded shoulders; thoracic stiffness; low back pain from slumping; wrist symptoms (carpal tunnel); eye strain; weight gain.

  • Screen: in front, arm's length (50–100 cm), top line at or slightly below eye level; laptop on a stand, separate keyboard and mouse.
  • Chair: feet flat, thighs level; lumbar support. Keyboard, mouse: close, elbow height, wrists straight.
  • Headset for calls; movement break every 30–60 min.
  • PT: deep neck flexor, scapular retractor, lower trapezius strengthening + general fitness (beats ergonomics alone: Chen 2018); thoracic mobility, pectoral stretch; workstation exercises; heat, TENS only as a bridge to exercise; weight control.

Prolonged standing (7m S21; watchman S23, traffic police S24)

Effects (Waters and Dick 2015): low back pain, leg pain, swelling, fatigue, varicose veins, heel pain. Why: static load, little calf pumping, hard floors, poor shoes.

Prevention: change position and walk (most effective); sit–stand or leaning stool; anti-fatigue mats; cushioned shoes, insoles; footrest to alternate feet; compression stockings if swelling; calf raises, ankle pumps in the shift.

Surgeons (7m S26): Evaluate: Nordic questionnaire → video → measure set-up → RULA, REBA → change → re-evaluate. Open: hands near elbow height, elbows 90°; light loupes angled down. Laparoscopic: table 0.7–0.8 × elbow height; monitor straight ahead, top at eye level; handles for a straight wrist. Microsurgery: sit, forearms supported, eyepieces for a neutral neck. Micro-breaks 1.5–2 min every 20–40 min; mat; ratchet instruments.

Manual handling, low back pain in industry (7m W22, W25)

Factors: heavy, frequent, awkward lifting (bend, twist), far from body or from the floor; whole-body vibration; long sitting or standing; slips; low fitness, smoking, obesity; dissatisfaction, stress.

Revised NIOSH equation: 23 kg limit in ideal conditions, reduced for distance, height, travel, twist, frequency, grip; 50 kg bags far exceed it, so the task must change. Prevention: redesign first (lighter bags, trolleys, hoists, conveyors, waist-height loads, team lifts) → training, fitness. Current: lifting-technique training alone did not prevent back pain (Verbeek 2011).

W22 labourer (50 kg bags, bidis): education, graded activity, core, hips, hamstrings, quit smoking; 25 kg bags or two-person lifts, trolley, waist-height loading. Squatting (S19): low stools, kneeling pads, rotate; quads, hips.

Other occupations: housemaid (7m S16), physiotherapists (7m S19), air hostess (3m W18)

Goldsmith (S20, W24): neck flexion over fine work, pinch, poor light → raise work, magnifier, light, micro-breaks, nerve gliding. Tailor (S25): bent sitting, pedal → back support, machine at elbow height, walk breaks. Teacher (S19, S24): arms raised at board → write at chest to eye height, projector, alternate sit and stand.

Housemaid: pain knees 38.2%, low back 27.5% (Bengaluru); low back risk: over 10 years in job, distress. Short jhadu → long-handled broom; hand mopping → long mop, knee pad; squat washing → low stool (paat), raised platform; water → half-filled buckets, two small loads close, turn with feet. PT: stay active; bracing, bridging, sit-to-stand (8–12 reps, 2–3 days a week); stretches; weight control. Current (NICE): no belts, TENS, ultrasound, IFT.

Physiotherapists: lifetime 55–91%, low back commonest; top cause transfers; thumbs with manual therapy. Patient limit about 16 kg (35 lb) → hoists, slide sheets; adjustable plinth 5–10 cm below elbow light work, 20–40 cm heavy; straight wrists; breaks, rotation, fitness; body mechanics alone does not prevent injury.

Air hostess: back injuries mostly from turbulence, overhead bins, trolleys → no heavy overhead lifts, bags under seat; push trolleys with both hands, close; sit belted in turbulence; shoes, calf raises, sleep plan.

Job stress (7m W17, S18, W18) and shift work (3m W21)

Causes: demand–control (high demand, little control); effort–reward imbalance; monotony, dissatisfaction; deadline anxiety; long hours, shifts; poor relationships, harassment, insecurity. Effects: muscle tension, pain, headache, fatigue, poor sleep, high BP, anxiety, depression, errors, absence. Prevent: fair workload, control, variety, breaks, support, clear roles.

Shift work: disturbed body clock: poor sleep, fatigue, accidents; digestive, weight, metabolic and heart risk; night shifts "probably carcinogenic" (IARC 2019); family strain. Advice: forward-rotating shifts, dark quiet sleep room, regular meals, exercise, short runs of nights.

PT in stress management (7m S20, W21, W24)

Relaxation: Jacobson progressive (tense then release each group); Laura Mitchell physiological (move into the opposite of the stress posture); diaphragmatic breathing. Exercise: aerobic, workplace stretching, yoga. Ergonomic and task changes; posture, sleep, pacing. Refer anxiety or depression; evaluate (pain, absence, stress questionnaires).

ICF (S21 case: 36, computer operator, mother of a 1-year-old)

  • Structure/function: neck and upper trapezius pain, less range, forward head, weak deep neck flexors and scapular muscles, tight pectorals, poor sleep.
  • Activity: long typing, looking down, lifting and feeding the child. Participation: full working day, childcare, leisure.
  • Environment: unadjusted workstation, long hours, no breaks (barriers); family, employer willing to adjust (facilitators). Personal: working mother, little time: short home exercises.

Short-term (2–4 weeks): pain 6 → 3/10 at work; corrected sitting; workstation adjusted, breaks every 30–60 min; safe lifting and feeding. Long-term (2–3 months): pain-free working day; posture held; self-checks set-up; exercise 3 times a week; no recurrence.

Plan: desk-worker PT and workstation (above); child close, supported feeding; relaxation.

Cautions

Neck pain: rule out radiculopathy. Tailor (S25) who cannot walk over 15 min: examine for spinal stenosis or vascular claudication (lumbar extension tests, foot pulses, ankle–brachial index, neurological exam).

  2. Goals (5): short and long term, measurable, linked to work and home.
