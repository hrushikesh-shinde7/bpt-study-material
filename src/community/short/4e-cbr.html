Definition and features (3m S16, W24; 7m W20)

CBR (ILO, UNESCO, WHO 2004): "a strategy within general community development for the rehabilitation, equalization of opportunities, poverty reduction and social inclusion of all people with disabilities", carried out by disabled people, families and communities with health, education, vocational and social services.

History: WHO promoted CBR after Alma-Ata (1978); joint papers 1994, 2004 widened it to rights and inclusion; WHO CBR guidelines 2010 gave the matrix. Compare with IBR.

  • In the person's own village or town; local resources, simple technology.
  • Person and family are active partners.
  • Community workers (CBR workers, anganwadi workers, ASHAs, teachers) do most work, trained by professionals.
  • Health, education, livelihood, social, empowerment, not only therapy.
  • Works with PHCs, schools, panchayats; refers; low cost, wide coverage.

CBR matrix (WHO 2010)

Five components × five elements; a programme picks what it needs. Draw as five columns.

  • Health: promotion, prevention, medical care, rehabilitation, assistive devices.
  • Education: early childhood, primary, secondary and higher, non-formal, lifelong.
  • Livelihood: skills, self-employment, wage jobs, financial services, social protection.
  • Social: personal assistance, relationships, marriage and family, culture and arts, recreation and sport, justice.
  • Empowerment: advocacy, community mobilisation, political participation, self-help groups, disabled people's organisations.

Team (7m S17) and teamwork benefits (7m S20)

Village: person, family, CBR worker, anganwadi worker, ASHA, teacher, self-help group, gram panchayat, craftsmen. Block: PHC and CHC (doctor, ANM), multipurpose rehabilitation worker, block resource centre, RBSK teams. District: district hospital, DEIC (0–6), DDRC, PT, OT, prosthetist, speech therapist, psychologist, social worker, NGOs. State and national: national institutes, composite regional centres.

Benefits: disability touches health, school, work, social life; few professionals train many local workers (transdisciplinary); shared goals → one plan; early identification, quick referral; follow-up near home; pooled resources → low cost, lasting; attitudes change.

Strategies (7m W19) and approaches (7m S26)

Steps: situation analysis (leaders, resource map, household survey) → plan with community (CBR committee) → train workers and families → implement (home visits, camps, school inclusion, aids, SHGs) → monitor, evaluate, hand over.

  • Urban: urban health and community centres (clinics, camps); clubs, mahila mandals, social centres (awareness, volunteers, funds); schools (inclusion, screening); industries (training, jobs); sports centres; NGOs, CSR funds.
  • Rural: PHC, rural and district hospitals (screening, treatment, referral); anganwadi workers and ASHAs (identification, home programmes); local-material aids by craftsmen (bamboo crutches, wooden walkers, parallel bars, standing frames, CP chairs); panchayat; SHGs; camps.

Approaches (S26). By control: top-down (government-led, set packages; Iran, Viet Nam, DDRC camps): easy to run widely, more money and reach; weak local commitment; bottom-up (community, NGO or disabled people's groups; PROJIMO, Mobility India): flexible, owned; fewer resources. Best: government support + local sensitivity (WHO 2010).

By place: home-based (local supervisor teaches family; WHO 1989): reaches homes, cheap; no back-up for aids, referrals fail; centre-based (village centre, anganwadi, early intervention centre): back-up, local aids; travel; outreach (camps, mobile teams): specialists near home; periodic; PHC-integrated (ASHA, anganwadi, RBSK): wide, cheap; stays medical; community development and rights-based (matrix, SHGs, disabled people's groups, twin-track): tackles poverty and inclusion; complex, little evidence.

Anganwadi worker (3m S17, S22; 7m S26)

ICDS (1975) village worker; centre for ~400–800 people (300–800 tribal, hilly) with one helper; serves children under 6, pregnant and nursing mothers.

  • Identification: house-to-house survey, growth monitoring, milestone checks.
  • Referral: PHC, RBSK team, District Early Intervention Centre; certificate and UDID help.
  • Prevention: immunisation, nutrition, vitamin A, iodised salt, anaemia, antenatal care, birth spacing.
  • Home programme taught by the physiotherapist; checks aids. Pre-school inclusion.
  • Counsels mothers, reduces stigma; registers; links to schemes, ASHA, ANM, panchayat.

Physiotherapist in CBR and in a special school (7m W22)

CBR: trainer of workers, teachers, families (assessment, positioning, exercise, transfers, when to refer); assessment at camps, picture home programmes; low-cost local aids; prevention; referral and follow-up; advocacy, supervision.

School for physically handicapped children: screen each child (GMFCS, tone, ROM, posture, gait, hand, self-care), goals in the individual education plan; group and individual play-based therapy, home programme; seating (pelvis level, feet supported), standing frame 30–60 min a day; orthoses, walkers, wheelchairs; access (ramps 1:12, rails, toilets); train teachers and parents; adapted PE: WHO 2020, ages 5–17 incl. disability, 60 min a day, vigorous and strengthening 3 days a week, as able; prevent contractures, scoliosis, obesity, sores; transition to neighbourhood school, later vocational.

Law and schemes: RPWD Act s16 schools admit without discrimination, access, reasonable accommodation, transport; s17 survey every 5 years, free assistive devices up to 18; s31 free education 6–18 in neighbourhood or special school. Samagra Shiksha (pre-school to XII): assessment camps, aids, escort allowance, girls' stipend, block-level therapy, ramps and toilets, home-based education. UDID, ADIP, RBSK school screening.
