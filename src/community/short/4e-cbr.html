Definition and features (3m S16, W24; 7m W20)

CBR (ILO, UNESCO, WHO 2004): "a strategy within general community development for the rehabilitation, equalization of opportunities, poverty reduction and social inclusion of all people with disabilities", carried out by disabled people, families and communities with health, education, vocational and social services.

History: WHO promoted CBR after Alma-Ata (1978) as most disabled people in low-income countries had no access to institutions; joint papers 1994, 2004 widened it to rights and inclusion; WHO CBR guidelines 2010 gave the matrix. Compare with IBR.

  • In the person's own home, village or town; local resources, simple technology.
  • Person and family are active partners and decide goals.
  • Community workers (CBR workers, anganwadi workers, ASHAs, teachers, volunteers) do most work, trained by professionals.
  • Covers health, education, livelihood, social life, empowerment, not only therapy.
  • Works with PHCs, schools, panchayats, schemes; refers to institutions.
  • Low cost, wide coverage, community-sustained.

CBR matrix (WHO 2010)

Five components × five elements; a programme picks what its community needs, not all 25. Draw as five columns; empowerment runs across all.

  • Health: promotion, prevention, medical care, rehabilitation, assistive devices.
  • Education: early childhood, primary, secondary and higher, non-formal, lifelong.
  • Livelihood: skills, self-employment, wage jobs, financial services, social protection.
  • Social: personal assistance, relationships, marriage and family, culture and arts, recreation and sport, justice.
  • Empowerment: advocacy, community mobilisation, political participation, self-help groups, disabled people's organisations.

Team (7m S17) and teamwork benefits (7m S20)

Village: person, family, CBR worker, anganwadi worker, ASHA, teacher, self-help group, gram panchayat, craftsmen. Block: PHC and CHC (doctor, ANM), multipurpose rehabilitation worker, block resource centre, RBSK mobile teams. District: district hospital, District Early Intervention Centre (DEIC, 0–6), DDRC, PT, OT, prosthetist and orthotist, speech therapist, psychologist, social worker, NGOs. State and national: national institutes, composite regional centres.

Benefits: disability touches health, school, work and social life; few professionals train many local workers (transdisciplinary); shared goals → one consistent plan, no repeated assessments; early identification and quick referral; follow-up near home; pooled resources → low cost, lasting; community attitudes change.

Strategies (7m W19) and approaches (7m S26)

Steps: situation analysis (leaders, resource map, household survey) → plan with community (CBR committee, matrix priorities) → train workers and families → implement (home visits, camps, school inclusion, aids, livelihood, self-help groups) → monitor, evaluate, hand over.

  • Urban: urban health and community centres (clinics, camps); clubs, mahila mandals, social centres (awareness, volunteers, funds); ordinary schools (inclusion, screening); industries (training, jobs, workplace adaptation); sports centres; NGOs, corporate social responsibility funds.
  • Rural: PHC, rural and district hospitals (screening, treatment, referral); anganwadi workers and ASHAs (identification, home programmes); local-material aids by village craftsmen (bamboo crutches, wooden walkers, parallel bars, standing frames, CP chairs); panchayat; self-help groups; camps for certificates and aids.

Approaches: home-based (worker teaches family); centre-based in village or ward (CBR centre, anganwadi); outreach (camps, mobile teams from hospitals, DDRCs, NGOs); health-system integrated (PHC, RBSK, anganwadis); community development and rights-based (disabled people's organisations and self-help groups lead; twin-track: mainstream inclusion + specific needs).

Anganwadi worker (3m S17, S22; 7m S26)

ICDS (1975) village worker; centre for ~400–800 people (300–800 tribal, hilly) with one helper; serves children under 6, pregnant and nursing mothers.

  • Identification: house-to-house survey, growth monitoring, milestone checks.
  • Referral: PHC, RBSK team, District Early Intervention Centre; certificate and UDID help.
  • Prevention: immunisation, nutrition, vitamin A, iodised salt, anaemia, antenatal care, birth spacing.
  • Home programme taught by the physiotherapist; checks aids. Pre-school inclusion.
  • Counsels mothers, reduces stigma; registers; links to schemes, ASHA, ANM, panchayat.

Physiotherapist in CBR and in a special school (7m W22)

CBR: trainer of workers, teachers, families (assessment, positioning, exercise, transfers, when to refer); assessment at camps, picture home programmes; low-cost local aids; prevention (ergonomics, falls, chronic disease, antenatal advice); referral and follow-up; advocacy, supervision, evaluation.

School for physically handicapped children: screen each child (GMFCS, ROM, strength, posture, gait, function), goals in the individual education plan; individual and group therapy, play-based; seating, standing frames, orthoses, wheelchair skills; train teachers in lifting and transfers, parents in home programmes; access (ramps, rails, toilets), adapted physical education and sport; prevent contractures, scoliosis, obesity, sores; plan transition to ordinary school and vocational training.
