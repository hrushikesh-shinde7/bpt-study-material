Dysfunctions, discomforts and PT (3m S21; S22)

  • Low back pain (posture, weight, laxity, weak abdominals): body mechanics, pelvic tilts, cat–camel in four-point, back and hip strengthening, heat not on abdomen, side-lying with pillow between knees, belt.
  • Pelvic girdle pain (sacroiliac, symphysis): pelvic belt, knees together turning in bed and out of a car, no standing on one leg (sit to dress), short steps, stabilising exercise, crutches if severe.
  • Carpal tunnel: night splint in neutral, elevation, nerve and tendon gliding, hand pumping. Cramps: calf stretch before bed, ankle pumps; pull toes up in a cramp.
  • Varicose veins, swelling: elevation, ankle pumps, stockings, no long standing, walking, swimming. Heel, foot, knee: footwear, insoles, calf and plantar fascia stretch, strengthening, ice.
  • Breathlessness, rib pain: upright, side-flexion stretch, lateral costal breathing. Frequency, leakage: daily pelvic floor training, the knack. Constipation, heartburn: walking, fibre, footstool; small meals, head-up sleep.

Hyperemesis gravidarum (3m W19)

Severe nausea and vomiting with dehydration, weight loss, electrolyte imbalance, often admitted; fluids, anti-emetics. PT: relaxation, slow breathing; P6 acupressure bands (some find helpful); bed and chair exercise, ankle pumps; early walking; energy conservation; support.

Diastasis recti: definition and test (3m S17, W24)

Separation of the two recti along the linea alba from stretching in pregnancy. First-time mothers: 33% at 21 weeks, 60% at 6 weeks after birth, 45% at 6 months, 33% at 12 months (Sperstad 2016). Risks listed: multiple or large babies, many pregnancies, weak abdominals, obesity, lifting (study found none clear). Effects: doming, poor trunk support, back and pelvic pain, cosmetic worry; rarely hernia.

Test: crook lying, slow head and shoulder lift till scapulae clear the bed; fingers across midline at the umbilicus, 4.5 cm above and 4.5 cm below; 2 finger-widths or more = diastasis (or calipers, ultrasound); note depth, tension, doming; pain, pelvic floor, function.

Diastasis recti: management (7m W16, S22, S24)

Pregnancy: roll to the side to get up; no straining or breath-holding; transversus + pelvic floor activation; posture; avoid doming exercises. After birth: transversus drawing-in with pelvic floor, pelvic tilts, side-lying and four-point → head lifts and curl-ups only when the abdomen stays flat → loaded functional training. Splint with crossed hands or binder for coughing and getting up; correct lifting, constipation care, weight; surgery only if large gap with hernia persists after long rehab.

Exercise guidelines (7m W18)

  • Benefits: less gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, excess weight; better mood, sleep, back; not linked with miscarriage or preterm birth (Canadian 2018).
  • Dose: ≥ 150 min/week moderate over ≥ 3 days, ideally daily, aerobic + resistance (Canadian, ACOG); active women continue usual activity. Intensity: talk test (talk, not sing) or Borg 12–14.
  • Choose: walking, stationary cycling, swimming, water exercise, low-impact aerobics, prenatal yoga and Pilates (not hot), light resistance, daily pelvic floor.
  • Precautions: warm-up, cool-down; fluids; no heat; no long supine after first trimester; no breath-holding, straining, end-range or bouncing stretch; eat enough.

Contraindications and stop (3m S17, S22)

Avoid (ACOG 2020): contact sports (boxing, hockey, kabaddi); high fall risk (riding, skiing, gymnastics, off-road cycling); scuba, sky diving; hot yoga; unaccustomed altitude. Indian texts add: double leg raises, full sit-ups, long supine work, deep squats with pelvic pain, ballistic stretch, heavy lifting with breath-holding, jumping late.

Absolute: significant heart disease; restrictive lung disease; incompetent cervix or cerclage; multiple pregnancy at preterm risk; persistent 2nd or 3rd trimester bleeding; placenta praevia after 26 weeks; preterm labour; ruptured membranes; pre-eclampsia; severe anaemia. Stop: bleeding, painful contractions, leaking fluid, breathless before starting, dizziness, headache, chest pain, weakness, calf pain or swelling.

Obstetric PT: aims and principles (7m W21, W24)

Aims: adapt to changes, prevent and treat discomforts; safe fitness, weight control; protect back, pelvis, abdomen, pelvic floor; prepare for labour (knowledge, positions, relaxation, breathing, less fear); recovery after birth; prevent incontinence, prolapse, back pain, diastasis. Principles: assess, screen red flags, work with the obstetrician; moderate regular exercise by trimester; self-management; family, group support; individualise high-risk pregnancies.

Early bird (3m W18) and antenatal classes (7m S25)

Early bird (first trimester): changes, nutrition, back care, safe exercise, pelvic floor, warning signs, early discomforts; healthy habits early.

Antenatal course: from ~28 weeks, weekly, 6–8 sessions, groups of 8–12, partner at some. Goals: knowledge, fitness, discomfort relief, pelvic floor, labour relaxation, breathing, positions, postnatal care, confidence. Session (~90 min): welcome and questions 10 → talk 20 → exercise 30 (warm-up, low-impact aerobic, strength, pelvic floor, stretch) → relaxation and breathing 20 → summary, handout 10. Topics: posture; pelvic floor; signs and stages of labour; positions and breathing (partner); caesarean; feeding, baby care.

ICF (W17: 25, nurse, third trimester, +20 kg, heel and knee pain)

Reasoning (5): extra weight loads heels and knees; relaxin → arches drop, feet roll in → plantar fascia strain, knees turn in; centre of gravity forward → leans back, locks knees; swelling; long standing on hard floors, lifting patients. 20 kg is above the 11.5–16 advised.

  • Structure/function: heel pain on first steps, knee pain, flat pronated feet, tight calves, swelling, weak hip and thigh.
  • Activity: long standing and walking, stairs, turning patients, rising from low seats.
  • Participation: full shifts till her due date; housework.
  • Environment: hard floors, long shifts (barriers); lighter duties, hoists (facilitators). Personal: motivated, health knowledge.

Management: cushioned arch-support shoes or insoles; calf and plantar fascia stretch 30 s × 3, 3 times a day; cold-bottle rolling; low-dye taping; static quads, straight-leg raise, side-lying hip abduction 10 × 2–3, no deep squats; ankle pumps 20 every hour, side-lying elevation, stockings; work: sitting duties, stool, anti-fatigue mat, hourly breaks, no manual patient lifting; water walking, cycling; dietitian (no dieting); pelvic floor, breathing, labour positions. Report: sudden face or hand swelling, headache, blurred vision (pre-eclampsia), bleeding, leaking, fewer baby movements, calf pain.
