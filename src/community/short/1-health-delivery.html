Health and prevention (3m S19)

WHO (1948): "Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." Dimensions: physical, mental, social (+ spiritual). Disease: disturbance of structure or function of body or mind, with signs and symptoms. Health is a continuum: positive health → ill-health → death. Determinants: genes; lifestyle; environment; socioeconomic; health services.

Primary health care (3m S17)

Alma-Ata 1978: essential care, universally accessible through full community participation, at affordable cost; the first level of contact, close to where people live and work. Principles: equity; community participation; intersectoral coordination; appropriate technology; prevention and promotion. Eight elements: health education; food and nutrition; safe water and sanitation; mother and child health, family planning; immunisation; local endemic disease control; treatment of common illness and injury; essential drugs.

Functions of the PHC (7m S22)

Medical care and referral; mother and child (antenatal, delivery, postnatal, newborn) and family planning; immunisation; TB, malaria, leprosy and non-communicable disease control; national programmes; health education and nutrition; water and sanitation; vital statistics; laboratory and essential drugs; training ANMs, ASHAs; school health; Arogya Mandir packages.

Role of PT by level (3m S24)

Primary: fitness, ergonomics, antenatal classes, screening, training ASHAs, home programmes, CBR. Secondary: CHC and district outpatients and wards (fractures, stroke, chest, post-op). Tertiary: ICU, cardiac, neuro, paediatric, sports.

Socioeconomic barriers (3m S20, W24)

Out-of-pocket cost, lost wages; PTs in cities and private clinics; distance; low awareness ("rest cures"), healers; gender (permission); floor-level homes, Indian toilets; PM-JAY mainly hospital care. Solutions: CBR, camps, tele-rehab, posts at CHCs and Arogya Mandirs.

Community PT: principles and scope (7m W25)

PT for the whole community, preventive and promotive, where people live and work. Principles: prevention first; equity; participation; ICF; teamwork, intersectoral; low-cost technology; self-management; evidence, sustainability. Scope: fitness; women's health; geriatrics; industrial health; CBR; non-communicable diseases; disaster; school, palliative care.
