Investigations: chest X-ray, PFT, ECG and bedside tests

Investigations are asked as 3-mark questions (X-ray of pleural effusion, emphysema, COPD and tetralogy of Fallot; PFT in obstructive and restrictive disease; ECG criteria for MI; ABI) and they are the spots in the practical exam (chest X-ray, ABG, PFT, ECG). Learn a fixed order for reading each one and the physiotherapy meaning of each finding.

My revision

1Chest X-ray

A fixed order for reading

  1. Details and quality: name, date, PA or AP (AP films, taken in bed, make the heart look bigger); rotation (the spinous processes sit midway between the clavicle ends); inspiration (about 6 anterior or 10 posterior ribs above the right diaphragm); exposure.
  2. A, Airway: trachea central or shifted.
  3. B, Breathing (lungs and pleura): compare left and right zone by zone; lung markings reaching the edge; pleural lines; costophrenic angles sharp.
  4. C, Cardiac: heart size (cardiothoracic ratio below 0.5 on a PA film), borders, mediastinum.
  5. D, Diaphragm: the right is usually slightly higher; flattened in hyperinflation; free air under it.
  6. E, Everything else: bones (fractures, sternal wires), soft tissues (surgical emphysema), lines and tubes.

Silhouette sign: when a white area touches a border and blurs it, it lies next to that border. Loss of the right heart border means right middle lobe disease; loss of a hemidiaphragm means lower lobe disease.

  • Bronchodilator response: traditionally an FEV1 or FVC rise of at least 12% and 200 mL after salbutamol (ATS/ERS 2005); the 2022 ERS/ATS standard uses a rise of more than 10% of the predicted value. Large responses suggest asthma.
  • Flow–volume loop: a "scooped" (concave) expiratory curve in obstruction; a small, narrow loop in restriction; flattening in upper airway obstruction.
  • Peak flow variability through the day supports asthma; a personal best guides the action plan.
  • Read the S25 case: FVC 60%, FEV1 82%, FEV1/FVC 94% predicted: both volumes reduced with a high ratio, so restrictive (old TB scarring).

3ECG

Basics

  • Paper speed 25 mm a second: one small square = 0.04 s, one large square = 0.2 s; 10 mm = 1 mV.
  • P wave = atria depolarise; QRS = ventricles depolarise; T wave = ventricles repolarise.
  • Normal intervals: PR 0.12–0.20 s (3–5 small squares); QRS below 0.12 s (under 3 small squares); QTc below about 0.44 s in men and 0.46 s in women.
  • Heart rate: 300 ÷ number of large squares between two R waves (regular rhythm), or the number of QRS complexes in a 10-second strip × 6 (irregular rhythm).
  • Order: rate, rhythm (regular?), P before every QRS?, PR, QRS width, ST and T changes.
Rhythm or findingKey featuresPhysiotherapy action
Sinus tachycardiaNormal beats above 100Look for a cause (pain, fever, anaemia, low fluid, anxiety); resting rate above 120: postpone exercise
Sinus bradycardiaNormal beats below 60Common with beta-blockers and in fit people; stop if dizzy or the BP falls
Atrial fibrillationNo P waves; irregularly irregularExercise if the rate is controlled; use RPE, not heart rate; anticoagulants mean bleeding risk
Ectopic beatsEarly extra beats; ventricular ones are wideOccasional ones are harmless; frequent, multiform or in runs during exercise: stop
Ventricular tachycardiaFast, wide, regular complexesEmergency: check pulse, call for help; if no pulse, start CPR
Ventricular fibrillationChaotic line, no QRSCardiac arrest: CPR and defibrillation (see the BLS card)
Heart blockFirst degree: PR above 0.20 s. Second degree: some P waves not followed by QRS (Mobitz I: PR lengthens then a beat drops; Mobitz II: beats drop suddenly). Third degree: P and QRS unrelatedFirst degree and Mobitz I: usually safe. Mobitz II and third degree: no exercise until treated (pacemaker)

Test yourself

3 mGive three X-ray signs of a left lower lobe collapse.

A triangular white shadow behind the heart (the "sail sign"); loss of the medial part of the left hemidiaphragm outline (silhouette sign); raised left hemidiaphragm and the mediastinum shifted slightly to the left; smaller left lung.

3 mFEV1 45% predicted, FVC 80% predicted, FEV1/FVC 0.45, with only a small change after salbutamol. Interpret.

Obstructive pattern (low ratio), severe by FEV1 (GOLD 3, 30–49%), with little reversibility: typical of COPD.

MCQAn ECG shows a regular rhythm with 4 large squares between R waves. The heart rate is:A. 50B. 75C. 100D. 150

B. 300 ÷ 4 = 75 beats a minute.

MCQPostural hypotension is a fall within 3 minutes of standing of at least:A. 10 mmHg systolicB. 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolicC. 40 mmHg systolicD. 5 mmHg diastolic

B. 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic.
