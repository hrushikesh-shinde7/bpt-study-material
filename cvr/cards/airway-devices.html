My revision

1PEP: the principle

Positive expiratory pressure (PEP) therapy means breathing out against a resistance, so pressure inside the airways stays above atmospheric pressure during expiration. The usual target with a PEP mask is about 10–20 cmH₂O during the breath out.

  • Splints the airways open: back-pressure stops small, floppy airways collapsing early in expiration (the same idea as pursed-lip breathing).
  • Gets air behind mucus: lung volume rises a little and air moves through collateral channels into blocked units; on breathing out, that air pushes mucus towards the larger airways.
  • Oscillating PEP adds vibration: the expiratory pressure flutters on and off many times a second, shaking mucus off the airway walls and making it less sticky.

The loosened mucus is then cleared with huffs and a cough.

3Technique (PEP mask)

  1. Sit upright with elbows resting on a table (or in a drainage position).
  2. Hold the mask firmly over the nose and mouth (or seal the lips round the mouthpiece).
  3. Breathe in slightly deeper than normal; breathe out actively but not forcefully through the device, keeping the manometer at about 10–20 cmH₂O.
  4. Do about 8–10 such breaths.
  5. Remove the mask; do 2–3 huffs, then a cough to clear loosened mucus; rest with breathing control.
  6. Repeat the cycle until the chest feels clear, usually for about 15–20 minutes, once or twice a day (more during infections).

Flutter or Acapella: the same cycle; with the Flutter, tilt the device until the vibration is felt most in the chest. Clean devices after each use and do not share them (cross-infection, especially in cystic fibrosis).

4Uses of a PEP device (3 marks in S26)

  • Airway clearance in bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis and COPD with sputum: independent, portable, quick.
  • Unstable, collapsible airways (COPD, tracheomalacia): back-pressure keeps airways open during expiration.
  • Lung expansion after surgery: helps prevent and reverse collapse.
  • Better aerosol delivery: a nebuliser can be connected so the drug is breathed in while PEP keeps airways open.
  • Children: play-based breathing practice (bubble PEP).

5Contraindications and precautions

  • Untreated pneumothorax (the main contraindication).
  • Active haemoptysis.
  • Raised intracranial pressure; haemodynamic instability.
  • Recent facial, oral, skull or oesophageal surgery or injury; acute sinusitis; nosebleeds; middle-ear problems or a perforated eardrum.
  • Patients who cannot tolerate the extra work of breathing (very breathless, exhausted).
  • Large bullae: use lower pressures with care.

Test yourself

3 mWrite the principle of PEP and name two devices.

Breathing out against a resistance keeps airway pressure positive (about 10–20 cmH₂O), splinting small airways open and letting air get behind mucus through collateral channels; oscillating devices also vibrate the airways. Devices: PEP mask, Flutter, Acapella (any two).

MCQA patient needs oscillating PEP while lying in a drainage position. The best device is:A. FlutterB. AcapellaC. Incentive spirometerD. Peak flow meter

B. The Acapella works in any position; the Flutter depends on gravity acting on its steel ball.

MCQThe main contraindication to PEP therapy is:A. BronchiectasisB. Undrained pneumothoraxC. Cystic fibrosisD. Post-operative collapse

B. Positive pressure could enlarge the pneumothorax.
