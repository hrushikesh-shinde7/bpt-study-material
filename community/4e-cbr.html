4(e, f)Community-based rehabilitation: principles, the CBR matrix, strategies, the anganwadi worker and the physiotherapist

CBR was asked in 11 of 20 papers: principles four times, the anganwadi worker three times, strategies, approaches, teamwork, and a long answer on a child with cerebral palsy in a village. CBR is not a cheaper copy of hospital rehabilitation; it is rehabilitation that the person, the family and the village own, with professionals as trainers and back-up.

My revision

1What CBR is

Aspects of CBR (S16), three features of CBR (W24), 3 marks; a note on CBR (W20, 7 marks).

  • Definition (ILO, UNESCO and WHO, 2004): "a strategy within general community development for the rehabilitation, equalization of opportunities, poverty reduction and social inclusion of all people with disabilities", carried out by people with disabilities, their families and communities together with health, education, vocational and social services.
  • History: WHO began promoting CBR after the Alma-Ata declaration on primary health care (1978), because most disabled people in low-income countries had no access to institutions; the joint position papers (1994, 2004) widened it from medical care to rights and inclusion; the WHO CBR guidelines (2010) set out the CBR matrix.
  • Compare with institution-based rehabilitation on IBR vs CBR.

Features (aspects) of CBR

  • Takes place in the person's own home, village or town, using local resources and simple technology.
  • The person with disability and the family are active partners and decide goals.
  • Community workers (CBR workers, anganwadi workers, ASHAs, teachers, volunteers) deliver most of the work, trained and supported by professionals.
  • Covers health, education, livelihood, social life and empowerment, not only therapy.
  • Works with existing services (primary health centres, schools, panchayats, government schemes) and refers to institutions when needed.
  • Low cost, wide coverage, sustained by the community.

Benefits of teamwork in CBR

  • Disability touches health, school, work and social life, so many sectors must act together.
  • Few professionals can train and support many local workers (transdisciplinary model, see the team).
  • Shared goals avoid repeated assessments and conflicting advice; families get one consistent plan.
  • Early identification by village workers and quick referral; follow-up close to home.
  • Local resources and schemes are pooled; costs fall and the programme lasts.
  • Community attitudes change when many local people are involved.

5Strategies and approaches

CBR strategies in rural and urban areas (W19, 7 marks); approaches to CBR (S26, 7 marks).

Steps of a CBR programme

  1. Situation analysis: meet leaders, map resources, survey households to identify persons with disability.
  2. Plan with the community: form a CBR committee, set priorities using the matrix.
  3. Train local workers and families in simple assessment, home programmes and referral.
  4. Implement: home visits, camps, school inclusion, aids, livelihood links, self-help groups.
  5. Monitor and evaluate: records, outcomes, community feedback; hand over to local ownership.
SettingStrategies (syllabus 4f)
UrbanUrban health centres and community centres for clinics and camps; clubs, mahila mandals and social centres for awareness, volunteers and funds; ordinary schools for inclusion and screening; industries for vocational training, jobs and workplace adaptation; sports centres for adapted sport; NGOs and corporate social responsibility funds
RuralPrimary health centres, rural hospitals and district hospital infrastructure for screening, treatment and referral; anganwadi workers and ASHAs for identification and home programmes; locomotor aids made from local materials (bamboo crutches, wooden walkers, parallel bars and standing frames, CP chairs) by village craftsmen; panchayat support; self-help groups for livelihood; camps for certificates and aids

Approaches to CBR (S26)

  • Home-based: a trained worker visits and teaches the family a home programme.
  • Centre-based in the community: a village or ward CBR centre or anganwadi where people come for therapy, aids and groups.
  • Outreach from institutions: camps and mobile teams from hospitals, DDRCs and NGOs.
  • Health-system integrated: CBR delivered through primary health care, RBSK and anganwadis.
  • Community development and rights-based: disabled people's organisations and self-help groups lead, linking with livelihood, education and social programmes; the twin-track approach includes disabled people in mainstream programmes while also meeting their specific needs.

6The anganwadi worker in CBR

Role of the anganwadi worker in CBR: 3 marks in S17 and S22, 7 marks in S26.

  • Who: the village-level worker of the Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS, started in 1975), running an anganwadi centre for about 400–800 people in rural and urban areas (300–800 in tribal and hilly areas) with one helper; she serves children under 6, pregnant women and nursing mothers.

7The physiotherapist in CBR and in a special school

Role of physiotherapy and the physiotherapist in CBR (syllabus 4e); the physiotherapist in a school for physically handicapped children (W22, 7 marks).

In CBR

  • Trainer: teaches CBR workers, anganwadi workers, ASHAs, teachers and families simple assessment, positioning, exercises, transfers and when to refer.
  • Assessment and planning at camps and centres; writes simple home programmes with pictures.
  • Designs low-cost aids from local materials; checks fit and safety.
  • Prevention and health promotion: ergonomics, fall prevention, exercise for chronic disease, antenatal and postnatal advice.
  • Referral to institutions for surgery, orthoses and complex care; follow-up.
  • Advocacy for accessibility and inclusion; supervision and evaluation of the programme.

In a school for physically handicapped children (W22)

  • Screen and assess each child (GMFCS, ROM, strength, posture, gait, function); set goals with teachers and parents as part of the individual education plan.
  • Individual and group therapy: stretching, strengthening, balance, gait training, play-based activities.
  • Positioning and seating in class (adapted chairs, tables, standing frames), orthoses and mobility aids, wheelchair skills.
  • Train teachers and attendants in safe lifting, transfers and positioning; train parents in home programmes.
  • Make the school accessible: ramps, rails, toilets, classroom layout; adapted physical education and sports.
  • Prevent secondary problems: contractures, scoliosis, obesity, pressure sores.
  • Plan transition to ordinary schools and later to vocational training; liaise with doctors and the rehabilitation team.

8Worked long answer: CBR for a child with cerebral palsy

From a real paper (S22, 5 + 10)

"6 years old CP child with locomotor disability has been identified during a screening at a village. His educated parents intend to send him school. Enumerate the local resources to be used and plan community based rehabilitation."

Spot the clues before you write

6-year-old with CP and locomotor disability: needs mobility, positioning and aids. village screening: rural CBR, PHC and anganwadi links. educated parents want school: education component is the priority; parents are strong partners.

Local resources (5 marks)

ResourceUse
FamilyDaily home programme, school travel, advocacy
Anganwadi worker, ASHA, ANMFollow-up visits, nutrition, immunisation, referral
PHC, RBSK team, District Early Intervention CentreMedical review, therapy, hearing and vision checks, referral for orthoses and surgery
Village school, teachers, block resource centreEnrolment under inclusive education, special educator support, classroom adaptations
Gram panchayatRamp and toilet at school, funds, transport
Village carpenter and blacksmithCP chair, standing frame, parallel bars, walker
Self-help and parents' groups, NGOsSupport, awareness, funds, advocacy
Government schemes and campsDisability certificate and UDID card, free aids (ADIP), scholarships, travel concessions

CBR plan (10 marks)

  1. Assessment at home and school: type of CP, GMFCS level, tone, contractures, sitting and walking, hand use, communication, vision and hearing, feeding, toileting; ICF summary; family goals (school).
  2. Health: home programme taught to parents and the anganwadi worker: stretching of calves, hamstrings and hip adductors daily; sitting balance, sit-to-stand and supported walking practice; positioning in sitting and lying; standing frame 30–60 minutes a day if he cannot stand; ankle–foot orthoses and a walker through the DEIC or an ADIP camp; review every month by the physiotherapist at the PHC or camp.
  3. Education: enrol in the village school; seat near the door and blackboard; adapted chair and table; ramp, railing and an accessible toilet; teacher training on handling and toileting; peer buddy; extra time and adapted physical education; special educator from the block resource centre.
  4. Social: play with peers, festivals and outings; counselling to remove stigma; parents' group.
  5. Empowerment: parents learn rights under the RPWD Act (free education, accessibility, reservation); link to a disabled people's organisation.
  6. Livelihood (family now, child later): schemes for the family; plan for vocational training as he grows.
  7. Referral and follow-up: to the district hospital for orthopaedic review, botulinum toxin or surgery if needed; record goals and review every 3 months.

LAQA 6-year-old with CP found at a village screening; parents want him in school. Local resources and a CBR plan. (5 + 10, S22)

Resources: family, AWW, ASHA, ANM, PHC and RBSK team, DEIC, village school and block resource centre, panchayat, craftsmen, SHGs and NGOs, schemes (UDID, ADIP). Plan by matrix: assessment and ICF; health (home programme, positioning, standing frame, orthoses and walker, monthly review); education (enrolment, seating, access, teacher training, special educator); social; empowerment (RPWD rights, parents' group); referral and 3-monthly review.

MCQWhich is NOT a component of the WHO CBR matrix?A. HealthB. LivelihoodC. SurgeryD. Empowerment

C. The five are health, education, livelihood, social and empowerment.
