ExamThe Community practical exam: long case, project synopsis and journal

The Community practical is 80 marks: one long case (50), the project synopsis (25) and the journal (5). The long case can come from any of five areas, so prepare a proforma that adds the community questions (job, home, family, schemes) to the usual clinical assessment, and practise presenting it in ICF order.

Syllabus
Practical scheme: long case (rehabilitation, women's health, geriatric, industrial health or health promotion) 50; project synopsis 25; journal 5 (one case each of rehabilitation, health promotion, industrial health, women's health and geriatrics, and documentation of at least one visit to an industry, geriatric home or community assessment)
Priority
Essential. 80 practical marks plus 20 internal assessment.
Asked as
Long case 50 Synopsis 25 Journal 5
Usual split
Long case 50; synopsis 25; journal 5

Internal assessment adds 20 marks. The terminal exam has two long cases of 40 marks each; the preliminary exam has one long case of 50 and the synopsis for 30.

2Long case: a community assessment proforma

Subjective

  • Demographics: age, sex, education, marital status, family (joint or nuclear, who helps), income and socioeconomic status.
  • Chief complaints in the person's words; history; past, medical, drug, obstetric and menstrual history (women); falls history (elderly).
  • Occupation: job title, hours, shifts, postures, loads, tools, breaks, environment; job and task analysis.
  • Home and community: floor level and stairs, toilet type (Indian or western), floor surface, lighting, distance to services, transport.
  • Lifestyle: activity, diet, sleep, tobacco and alcohol, stress.
  • The person's goals; knowledge of schemes and benefits.

Objective

  • Vital signs, height, weight, BMI, waist.
  • Posture, range, strength, flexibility, special tests for the region.
  • Function: ADL and IADL, gait, balance (TUG, Berg), endurance (6-minute walk), fitness tests.
  • Area-specific: pelvic floor (PERFECT) and diastasis recti in women; cognition (MMSE), mood (GDS), frailty in the elderly; ergonomic score (REBA, RULA) and FCE items in workers; disability percentage and aids in rehabilitation cases.
  • Outcome measures to repeat later (NPRS, NDI or Oswestry, ICIQ, FES-I, Barthel).

Then

  • ICF table and one-line functional diagnosis; problem list; short- and long-term goals; plan; home programme; community and workplace changes; schemes and referrals.

4Presenting the long case (5 minutes)

  1. One sentence: "Mrs A, 38, homemaker with two children, leaks urine on coughing and lifting for 2 years…"
  2. Key findings in ICF order: body functions and structures, activities, participation, environmental and personal factors (draw the ICF if asked).
  3. The functional diagnosis in one line.
  4. Two or three priority problems with short- and long-term goals.
  5. The plan: treatment with doses, home programme, ergonomic or home changes, health promotion, schemes and referrals.
  6. Demonstrate one test and one treatment on the patient (for example the PERFECT grading explained and pelvic floor teaching; REBA scoring and a workstation exercise; TUG and a balance exercise).

5The project synopsis (25 marks)

  • Bring two copies, the consent form and the proforma; prepare a short presentation if allowed.
  • Be ready to explain the question (FINER, PICO), design, sample size, criteria, outcome measures, statistics and ethics, and to admit limitations.
  • Full guidance on the project synopsis.

6The journal (5 marks)

  • Five cases: one each of rehabilitation, health promotion, industrial health, women's health and geriatrics, written with assessment, ICF diagnosis, goals, intervention and outcome.
  • At least one visit documented: industry, geriatric home or community assessment (the syllabus field work also lists UHC and PHC visits, adoption of a nearby area and surveys on antenatal care, disability, exercise and health promotion, and prevention of smoking, alcohol and drugs in youth).
  • For a visit, record: date, place, purpose, people met, observations (work tasks and hazards, ergonomic problems, facilities for elders, accessibility), physiotherapy recommendations, and what you learnt.
  • Neat, complete, signed and up to date; examiners may ask about any entry.

7What examiners watch

  • Introduction, consent, explanation, privacy and draping (especially in women's health cases).
  • Safety: BP and warning signs, guarding the elderly, no heavy testing in pregnancy or osteoporosis.
  • Practical, affordable advice that fits the person's home, job and income.
  • Correct, current facts on schemes and laws; honest answers ("I would refer to the doctor") rather than guesses.

Last two weeks

  • Practise the proforma on classmates and family members until it takes under 20 minutes.
  • Rehearse one case from each of the five areas aloud in ICF order.
  • Rehearse the synopsis presentation and the nine common examiner questions.
  • Complete and check the journal; revise the visit report.
