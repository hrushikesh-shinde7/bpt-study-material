6(a)Industrial health: definition, models of industrial therapy, the industrial rehabilitation team, pre-placement and the role of the physiotherapist

This introduction to Chapter 6 was asked in 3 of 20 papers (the physiotherapist in industry, the industrial rehabilitation team and job analysis, and pre-placement), but it frames the most-asked part of the whole paper: work stress, hazards and the worker care spectrum. The key idea is the shift from the traditional medical model (treat the injury in the clinic) to the industrial model (prevent injury and return the worker to the job, with the employer as a partner).

1Industrial (occupational) health

Definition (Joint ILO/WHO Committee, 1950, revised 1995)

Occupational health should aim at the promotion and maintenance of the highest degree of physical, mental and social well-being of workers in all occupations; the prevention among workers of departures from health caused by their working conditions; the protection of workers from risks adverse to health; and the placing and maintenance of the worker in an environment adapted to his or her physiological and psychological capabilities: the adaptation of work to people and of each person to their job.

  • Industrial therapy: the application of physiotherapy (with other disciplines) to workers and workplaces: preventing work injury, treating work-related disorders close to the job, and returning injured workers to safe, productive work.
  • Why it matters in India: most workers are in the informal sector (farms, construction, small units) with little protection; musculoskeletal disorders, injuries and dust diseases are common.

Laws for workers' health in India

  • Factories Act, 1948: health, safety and welfare in factories; notifiable occupational diseases; working hours.
  • Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948: medical care and cash benefits for sickness, maternity, employment injury and disablement for insured workers.
  • Employees' Compensation Act, 1923 (formerly Workmen's Compensation): compensation for injury and listed occupational diseases.
  • Mines Act, 1952; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, which brings many of these laws together.

5Pre-placement in the worker care spectrum

Describe the pre-placement worker care spectrum (S25, 7 marks).

  • Place in the spectrum: the first step, before ability management and long before any injury (see the worker care spectrum).
  • Pre-placement (post-offer) screening: after a job is offered, the worker's health and physical abilities are tested against the essential demands of that job.
  • Aims: place the worker in a job they can do safely; find needs for training, aids or workplace changes; record a baseline for later comparison if injured; reduce injuries and compensation costs.

Components

  1. Job demand analysis of the post (weights, postures, frequency, environment).
  2. Health questionnaire and medical examination (history of back or joint problems, heart and lung disease, vision and hearing).
  3. Musculoskeletal screen: posture, range, strength, flexibility.
  4. Functional tests matched to the job: lifting and carrying to the job's weights and heights, pushing and pulling, positional tolerance (stooping, kneeling, climbing, overhead work), grip, aerobic fitness.
  5. Result: fit; fit with adjustments (training, tools, rotation); temporarily unfit (with a plan to improve); report to the employer in functional terms, not diagnoses.

Rules

  • Tests must be job-related, standardised and safe; the same tests for all candidates for a post.
  • Must not be used to exclude persons with disability unfairly: the employer must consider reasonable accommodation (RPWD Act, 2016).
  • Results are confidential.

7 mRole of the physiotherapist in industrial health settings. (W19)

Define occupational health and the industrial model. Roles: pre-placement screening; job analysis and ergonomic evaluation; injury prevention education; employee fitness and stress management; early on-site care; FCE, work conditioning, work hardening and graded return; hazard advice; records and programme evaluation; inclusion of older and disabled workers. Outcome: fewer injuries, lost days and costs.

7 mDescribe the pre-placement worker care spectrum. (S25)

Worker care spectrum (pre-placement, ability management, disability management, return to work). Pre-placement: after job offer; aims (safe placement, needs, baseline, fewer injuries); components (job demand analysis, health questionnaire and examination, musculoskeletal screen, job-matched functional tests, result and report); rules (job-related, standardised, non-discriminatory with reasonable accommodation, confidential).

LAQDefine industrial therapy. The industrial rehabilitation team. Explain job analysis and its purpose. (2 + 3 + 7 + 3, S22)

Definition (section 1). Team (section 3). Job analysis: job description, job demand analysis with frequency categories and physical demand levels, task analysis with risk factors (ability management page). Purpose: prevention and ergonomic redesign, pre-placement, matching with FCE, work hardening design, light duty, training, compensation.
