3(f)Geriatric evaluation and the role of physiotherapy: assessment scales, functional and fitness assessment, principles of geriatric rehabilitation

Geriatric evaluation was asked in 9 of 20 papers, with 5 long cases about older people with knee pain, stairs, an Indian toilet, falls or a fracture. In every one, the examiner wants a structured assessment (medical, functional, cognitive, mood, social, home), named scales with cut-offs, and a plan that keeps the person independent where they live.

Add grip strength, static balance (single-leg stance) and gait speed. Screen first: BP, heart disease, recent falls, pain; stop if chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.

4Principles of geriatric rehabilitation and the role of PT

Principles of geriatric rehabilitation (W18, 7 marks; W19, 7 marks of the long answer).

  1. Assess comprehensively, including cognition, mood, home and carers; treat the person, not the age.
  2. Function is the goal: independence in the tasks that matter to the person, set with them and their family.
  3. Teamwork: doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, social worker, family.
  4. Mobilise early; avoid unnecessary bed rest (section 5).
  5. Start low, go slow, but progress: older muscle still adapts to overload; allow longer recovery.
  6. Prevent the next problem: falls, pressure sores, contractures, deconditioning, incontinence.
  7. Adapt the environment and use aids: walking aids, raised toilet seat, rails, lighting.
  8. Consider multiple illnesses and drugs: BP, sugar, heart rate response, pain, sensory loss.
  9. Simple instructions, repetition and written or pictorial home programmes; involve carers.
  10. Respect dignity and choice; motivation through meaningful goals.
  11. Continuity: link to community programmes, senior citizens' groups and follow-up.

Role of the physiotherapist in geriatrics

  • Health promotion: exercise groups, walking clubs, fall-prevention classes.
  • Assessment and screening (sections 1–3).
  • Treatment: osteoarthritis, back and neck pain, stroke, Parkinson's, fractures and joint replacement, COPD and heart disease, incontinence.
  • Fall prevention (see falls); osteoporosis care (see menopause and osteoporosis).
  • Home visits, aids and home modification; carer training; palliative care.

5Adverse effects of immobility in the elderly

Adverse effects of immobility in the elderly (S23, 3 marks).

  • Muscle and function: rapid loss of strength and walking ability (hospital-associated disability), contractures; older people regain strength more slowly than the young.
  • Heart and lungs: postural hypotension, falls on first standing, deep vein thrombosis, pneumonia.
  • Bone and skin: bone loss, pressure sores.
  • Bladder and bowel: incontinence, retention, constipation and faecal impaction.
  • Mind: delirium, depression, loss of confidence and fear of falling.
  • Metabolic: insulin resistance, poor appetite and weight loss.
  • Full table on bed rest. Prevention: sit out and walk early, exercise in bed, turning, toileting schedule.

6Worked long case: a widow in an old age home

From a real paper (S17)

"65-years old female, who lost her husband year ago moved into old age home, comes with chief complaints of pain in her left knee and finds difficulty in climbing stairs to reach her room and using Indian toilet. She also complains of loss of balance twice in the past one month. She expresses to lead a quality of life in her remaining days. i) What is your method of assessment in this case? ii) Enumerate the impairments and plan the management."

Spot the clues before you write

left knee pain, stairs, Indian toilet: knee osteoarthritis with weak quadriceps. loss of balance twice in a month: high falls risk; needs a falls assessment. lost her husband, moved to an old age home: grief, possible low mood, new environment. wants quality of life: her goal leads the plan.

Method of assessment

  • History: knee pain pattern, morning stiffness, the two falls (where, when, how, injuries, could she get up), dizziness, vision, drugs, sleep, mood and grief, continence.
  • Examination: BP lying and standing; knee swelling, crepitus, range, quadriceps strength and lag; hip strength; feet and footwear; sensation and vision.
  • Function: Timed Up and Go, Berg, 30-second chair stand, gait, stairs, squatting and rising (Indian toilet), Barthel or Katz.
  • Cognition and mood: MMSE, Geriatric Depression Scale, FES-I for fear of falling.
  • Environment: stairs to her room (rails, lighting), toilet type, bed and chair heights, floor surface.

Impairments

  • Left knee pain and stiffness; reduced knee range; weak quadriceps and hip abductors.
  • Poor static and dynamic balance; slow gait; possible postural hypotension or vision problem.
  • Fear of falling; low mood after bereavement; possibly reduced fitness.

Management

ProblemTechnique and doseWhy
Knee painHeat or ice, TENS for flares; education on OAAllows exercise
Weak quadriceps and hipsStatic quadriceps, straight-leg raise, sit-to-stand from a raised chair, mini-squats, side-lying hip abduction; 10 × 2–3 sets, 3 times a week, progressing with ankle weightsStairs and rising from the toilet need leg strength
Poor balanceProgressive balance training: narrow stance, tandem stance, single-leg stance at a rail, stepping, weight shifts, walking with turns; more than 3 hours a week in total across the programmeMost effective fall-prevention exercise
Stairs and toiletStair training with a rail (up with the good leg, down with the painful one); raised commode seat or western toilet with grab bar; walking stick in the right handReduces knee load and falls
Fear and moodGroup exercise in the home, teach how to get up from the floor, encourage social activities and counselling for griefConfidence and participation
FitnessDaily walks building to 30 minutes, chair-based aerobic exerciseEndurance and well-being

Ask the home to add rails on the stairs, a night light, non-slip flooring in the bathroom, and if possible a ground-floor room.

Practice cases from the papers

  • S19: 69-year-old man of low income, strained family, in a charitable geriatric centre on the second floor, pain on walking and stairs: functional diagnosis and PT (as above; add low-cost aids and group programmes).
  • W16: 75-year-old with wrist stiffness 6 months after a Colles' fracture from a fall: evaluation (range, grip, swelling, complex regional pain, function, osteoporosis and falls risk) and rehabilitation (mobilisation, strengthening, functional use, fall prevention, bone health).
  • S21: 62-year-old with neck and upper back pain for 2 years, living with her husband with domestic help: functional diagnosis and PT for chronic neck pain, posture and activity.
  • W19: principles of geriatric rehabilitation (section 4) and national policies for the elderly (see senior citizens in India).

3 mAdverse effects of immobility in the elderly. (S23)

Rapid loss of strength and walking ability, contractures; postural hypotension, DVT, pneumonia; bone loss and pressure sores; incontinence, constipation; delirium, depression, fear of falling; insulin resistance and weight loss. Prevent by early mobilisation.

7 mGeriatric assessment scales. (W17)

ADL: Katz (6 items), Barthel (0–100). IADL: Lawton (8 items). Cognition: MMSE (30; 23 or less impaired). Mood: GDS-15 (more than 5). Balance and falls: TUG (13.5 s), Berg (0–56; below 45), Tinetti (28; below 19). Physical performance: SPPB, gait speed. Frailty: Fried criteria. Fear of falling: FES-I. Fitness: Senior Fitness Test. One line on use of each.

7 mPrinciples of geriatric rehabilitation. (W18)

Comprehensive assessment; function-based goals set with the person; teamwork; early mobilisation; start low, go slow, but progress; prevent secondary problems; environment and aids; account for comorbidity and drugs; simple instructions and carer involvement; dignity and choice; continuity with community services.

LAQ69-year-old man of low income in a charitable geriatric centre on the second floor with pain on walking and stairs: functional diagnosis and PT plan. (5 + 10, S19)

Assessment as in section 6; likely knee or hip OA with deconditioning. ICF: pain, weakness, balance; walking, stairs; participation in the centre; second floor without lift and low income (barriers), centre staff and peers (facilitators). Plan: pain relief, strengthening, balance, stair training with rail, walking aid, group exercise sessions in the centre, low-cost aids, staff training, fall prevention, follow-up.
