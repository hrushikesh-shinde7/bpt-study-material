2(a–c)Women in India: social issues affecting physical health, legal rights and benefits, and health promotion for rural women

This part was asked in 5 of 20 papers: social issues affecting women's physical function three times, plus two long answers on legal rights and on reaching rural pregnant women. The physiotherapist's angle is always the same: name the social issue, show how it reaches the body (anaemia, early pregnancy, heavy work, no toilet, no time to exercise), and say what the physiotherapist and the law can do about it.

My revision

1Women's health in India: key numbers

  • Anaemia: 57% of women aged 15–49 were anaemic in NFHS-5 (2019–21), up from 53% in NFHS-4.
  • Maternal mortality: 93 per 100,000 live births in 2019–21, down from 130 in 2014–16 (Sample Registration System).
  • Early marriage: about 1 in 4 women aged 20–24 were married before 18 (NFHS-5).
  • Violence: 29% of ever-married women reported spousal violence (NFHS-5).
  • Literacy: female literacy 65% against 82% for men (Census 2011).

3Legal rights and benefits for women

Legal rights and benefits provided by the Government of India (8 marks of the W19 long answer).

Constitution

  • Equality before law (Article 14); no discrimination by sex, with special provisions for women allowed (Article 15); equal pay for equal work as a directive principle (Article 39); just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief (Article 42); one-third of panchayat seats reserved for women (73rd Amendment, 1992); one-third of Lok Sabha and assembly seats (Women's Reservation Act, 2023, to take effect after delimitation).
LawWhat it does
Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (amended 2017)26 weeks of paid maternity leave in establishments covered by the Act; nursing breaks; crèche in larger establishments
Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 (amended 2021)Safe, legal abortion up to 20 weeks, and up to 24 weeks for special categories, by registered doctors
PCPNDT Act, 1994Bans sex determination of the fetus to prevent female foeticide
Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961Giving or taking dowry is an offence
Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005Protection orders, right to live in the shared home, maintenance, protection officers
Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006Marriage of a girl under 18 is prohibited and punishable
Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act, 2013Internal complaints committees at workplaces
Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005Daughters get equal rights in ancestral property
Equal pay (Code on Wages, 2019)No discrimination in wages by gender

4Health promotion for rural women

Strategies of health promotion for rural women (S21, 7 marks).

  1. Know the community: meet the sarpanch, ASHA, ANM, anganwadi worker and self-help group leaders; learn the main problems (anaemia, back pain, pelvic floor problems, pregnancy, smoke, diabetes) and the women's daily timetable.
  2. Use existing platforms: anganwadis, Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Days, self-help group meetings, mahila mandals, PHC and Ayushman Arogya Mandir clinics, temples and schools.
  3. Choose the message: nutrition and iron; antenatal and postnatal exercise; pelvic floor exercise; back care and safe ways to lift water pots, squat and carry; osteoporosis prevention; physical activity (walking groups, yoga); clean cooking fuel; toilet use; menstrual hygiene; screening for BP, diabetes and cancer.
  4. Use the right methods: local language, pictures, demonstrations and practice, songs and role plays, short sessions at convenient times, women educators, home visits for those who cannot come.
  5. Involve men, mothers-in-law and elders so women get permission and time.
  6. Link to schemes and services: JSY, PMMVY, PMSMA, Ujjwala, pensions, PHC referral.
  7. Train local women (ASHAs, self-help group leaders) to continue the programme.
  8. Evaluate: attendance, knowledge before and after, behaviour change (exercise, iron tablets, institutional delivery), and feedback.

5Worked long answer: reaching first-time pregnant women in a village

From a real paper (S22, 5 + 10)

"Write your strategy to approach the rural community for health education on role of Physiotherapy in Primigravidae. Explain your community management for the same."

Strategy to approach the community (5 marks)

  • Permission and partnership: PHC medical officer, gram panchayat, ASHA, ANM and anganwadi worker.
  • Identify primigravidae from ASHA and anganwadi registers and antenatal records.
  • Hold sessions where women already gather: anganwadi, Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Day, the PMSMA clinic on the 9th of the month.
  • Invite husbands and mothers-in-law to one session to win family support.
  • Use the local language, pictures, flip charts, videos on phones and live demonstrations; keep sessions short and practical.

Community management (10 marks)

SessionContent
1. Body changes and postureWhy the back aches; standing, sitting and lying positions; safe lifting of water pots and children; avoiding long squatting; side-lying rest (see pregnancy)
2. ExerciseWalking 30 minutes most days; pelvic tilts, cat–camel, calf and ankle exercises; when to stop (warning signs)
3. Pelvic floorHow to find and train the pelvic floor; prevents leakage and prolapse later (see pelvic floor)
4. Labour preparationBreathing and relaxation, labour positions, what happens in labour, going to hospital in time (JSY, JSSK, ambulance 108) (see labour)
5. After birthEarly pelvic floor and abdominal exercise, feeding posture, back care, spacing of children
ThroughoutNutrition and iron tablets, antenatal visits, danger signs (bleeding, swelling, headache, reduced baby movements)
  • Follow-up: ASHAs trained to repeat the exercises during home visits; pictorial handouts; a WhatsApp group where phones are common.
  • Referral: women with pain, leakage, high BP or bleeding to the PHC.
  • Evaluation: attendance, knowledge test before and after, exercise practice, institutional delivery rate, postnatal back pain and leakage at follow-up.

7 mWhat are the strategies of health promotion for rural women? (S21)

Know the community and its leaders and workers; use existing platforms (anganwadi, VHSND, SHGs, PHC); choose messages (nutrition, antenatal and postnatal exercise, pelvic floor, back care, osteoporosis, activity, clean fuel, toilets, screening); use local language, pictures and demonstrations at convenient times; involve men and elders; link to schemes; train local women; evaluate.

7 mDiscuss various social issues having impact on physical function of women in India. (W21, W24)

Discrimination and nutrition; early marriage and repeated pregnancies; poverty and anaemia; heavy domestic and farm work with double burden; chulha smoke; poor sanitation; low literacy; restricted mobility and decision-making; violence; cultural limits on exercise and sunlight; menstrual and widowhood stigma; each with its physical effect and a PT response.
