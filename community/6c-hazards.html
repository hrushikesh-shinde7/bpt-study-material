6(b iii)Environmental stress at work: physical, chemical and mechanical hazards, pneumoconiosis, vibration and cumulative trauma disorders

Occupational hazards were asked in 12 of 20 papers, with 4 long cases: a vibration-tool worker, a sand-industry worker, a coal miner and a clerk with carpal tunnel syndrome. For every hazard, write three things: what it does to the body, how the physiotherapist treats the effects, and how the workplace prevents it, starting at the top of the hierarchy of controls.

3Chemical agents and the pneumoconioses

Three hazards of chemical agents (S19), byssinosis (S18), asbestosis (W18), sequelae of pneumoconiosis (W20), 3 marks each; sand-industry worker (S16) and coal miner (S21) long cases.

  • Chemical hazards by route: local action on skin and eyes (dermatitis, burns, ulcers, skin cancer); inhalation of dusts, gases, fumes and vapours (pneumoconiosis, asthma, poisoning by carbon monoxide, lead, solvents); ingestion from contaminated hands, food and smoking at work (lead and mercury poisoning).
  • Pneumoconiosis: lung disease from inhaling mineral or organic dust, causing fibrosis.
DiseaseDust and jobsKey points
SilicosisFree crystalline silica: stone quarrying and crushing, mining, sandblasting, sand industry, glass, pottery, slate-pencil and agate workUpper-lobe nodular fibrosis, progressive massive fibrosis; high risk of tuberculosis (silico-tuberculosis); no cure
Coal workers' pneumoconiosisCoal dust: underground miningSimple nodules to progressive massive fibrosis; chronic bronchitis; with rheumatoid arthritis, Caplan's syndrome
AsbestosisAsbestos fibres: asbestos mining, cement sheets, insulation, brake linings, ship-breakingLower-lobe fibrosis, pleural plaques and thickening, clubbing; lung cancer (more with smoking) and mesothelioma, decades later
ByssinosisCotton, flax and hemp dust: textile mills (carding and blowing rooms)Chest tightness and breathlessness on the first day back at work after a break ("Monday chest tightness"), later chronic airflow obstruction
Organic dust lung diseasesBagassosis (sugarcane bagasse), farmer's lung (mouldy hay)Hypersensitivity pneumonitis: fever, cough and breathlessness hours after exposure; fibrosis if exposure continues
Schilling grades of byssinosis
GradeSymptoms
½Occasional chest tightness or breathlessness on the first day of the working week
1Chest tightness or breathlessness on every first day of the working week
2On the first and other days of the working week
3Grade 2 with permanent incapacity from reduced effort tolerance

Sequelae of pneumoconiosis (W20)

  • Progressive fibrosis and restrictive lung disease; progressive massive fibrosis.
  • Tuberculosis (especially with silica); chronic bronchitis and airflow obstruction.
  • Pulmonary hypertension and cor pulmonale; respiratory failure.
  • Lung cancer (asbestos, silica) and mesothelioma (asbestos); pneumothorax.
  • Disability, loss of work and income, early death.

Physiotherapy and prevention

  • PT: breathing control and pursed-lip breathing, airway clearance (active cycle of breathing) when sputum is present, thoracic mobility and posture, graded exercise training as pulmonary rehabilitation, energy conservation, oxygen if desaturating as prescribed, smoking cessation, vaccination advice (see interstitial lung disease and pulmonary rehabilitation).
  • Prevention: substitute safer materials (asbestos bans); wet methods, enclosure, exhaust ventilation; respirators (N95 or better) correctly fitted; pre-placement and periodic examination with chest X-ray (ILO classification) and spirometry; screening for tuberculosis in silica workers; remove affected workers from exposure; compensation under the Employees' Compensation Act (these are notifiable diseases under the Factories Act).

4Cumulative trauma disorders and carpal tunnel syndrome

Causative factors for CTD (W16, 3 marks); CTD with one common example (W22, S24, 7 marks); clerk with carpal tunnel syndrome (W17, long case).

  • Cumulative trauma disorders (work-related musculoskeletal disorders): injuries of muscles, tendons, nerves and joints from repeated small strains without enough recovery.
  • Work factors: repetition, force, awkward or static posture, vibration, contact pressure from tool edges, cold, long hours without breaks, piece-rate pressure.
  • Personal factors: age, female sex, pregnancy and menopause, diabetes, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis, obesity, previous injury, low fitness, smoking; stress.
  • Examples: carpal tunnel syndrome, de Quervain's tenosynovitis, trigger finger, lateral epicondylitis, rotator cuff tendinopathy, tension neck syndrome, hand-arm vibration syndrome.
Stages of a CTD as commonly described
StageFeatures
1Ache and tiredness during work that settles overnight; work not affected; reversible
2Pain starts early in the shift and lasts overnight; sleep may be disturbed; work capacity reduced
3Pain at rest and with light non-repetitive tasks; poor sleep; unable to do even light duties; may last months to years

Carpal tunnel syndrome (the usual "one common CTD")

  • What: compression of the median nerve under the flexor retinaculum at the wrist.
  • Features: tingling and numbness in the thumb, index, middle and half the ring finger, worse at night and with gripping; shaking the hand helps; later weakness and thenar wasting (abductor pollicis brevis).
  • Tests: Phalen's (wrist flexion 60 seconds), Tinel's over the carpal tunnel, carpal compression test; nerve conduction studies confirm and grade.
  • Treatment: night splint holding the wrist in neutral; nerve and tendon gliding exercises; ergonomic changes and breaks; steroid injection; surgery (carpal tunnel release) when there is thenar wasting, constant numbness or failed conservative care.

5Hand-arm vibration syndrome

The S16 long case (see section 8): an automobile worker using vibrating tools without protection.

  • Cause: regular use of vibrating hand tools (grinders, drills, chipping hammers, riveters, chain saws, polishers).
  • Vascular: attacks of finger blanching (white finger) set off by cold, then blue and red on rewarming.
  • Sensorineural: tingling, numbness, poor touch and dexterity; often carpal tunnel syndrome as well.
  • Musculoskeletal: grip weakness, pain in the hands, wrists and elbows.
Stockholm Workshop scale (summary)
ComponentStages
Vascular0 no attacks; 1 occasional attacks of the fingertips; 2 occasional attacks of the distal and middle phalanges; 3 frequent attacks of all phalanges of most fingers; 4 as 3 with skin changes at the fingertips
Sensorineural0SN exposed, no symptoms; 1SN intermittent numbness with or without tingling; 2SN numbness with reduced sensation; 3SN numbness with reduced touch discrimination or dexterity

Protective gear and instruments

  • Tools: low-vibration tools, anti-vibration handles and mounts, tool balancers and suspension so the worker does not grip hard, sharp and well-maintained tools.
  • Gloves: anti-vibration gloves reduce little of the vibration at the frequencies that matter, but warm, dry gloves help prevent blanching attacks.
  • Measurement: vibration meters to estimate daily exposure against the action and limit values; limit time on the tool and rotate jobs.
  • Warm clothing and a warm workplace; no smoking (it narrows vessels).

6Industrial injury, risk assessment and control

Workplace hazards and risk assessment tools (S18, 7 marks); contributing factors for industrial injury (S22, 7 marks).

Factors in industrial injury

FactorExamples
WorkerFatigue, lack of training or experience, haste, alcohol, illness, poor vision or hearing, stress, not using protection
Machine and toolsUnguarded moving parts, faulty or poorly maintained equipment, unsuitable tools
EnvironmentPoor lighting, noise, heat, slippery or cluttered floors, poor ventilation, work at height
OrganisationLong hours, night shifts, production pressure, poor supervision, no safety rules or PPE, poor maintenance
ErgonomicHeavy lifting, awkward postures, repetition, badly designed workstations

Risk assessment

  1. Identify hazards (walk-through, job analysis, injury records, worker interviews).
  2. Decide who might be harmed and how.
  3. Evaluate the risk (likelihood × severity on a risk matrix) and decide controls.
  4. Record findings and act.
  5. Review regularly and after any change or injury.
Ergonomic risk assessment tools
ToolWhat it assesses
RULA (Rapid Upper Limb Assessment)Posture of neck, trunk and upper limbs with force and repetition; score 1–7 (1–2 acceptable, 7 change immediately)
REBA (Rapid Entire Body Assessment)Whole-body postures, load and coupling; score 1–15 (11 or more very high risk)
NIOSH lifting equationRecommended weight limit for a lifting task (23 kg in ideal conditions) and lifting index
Strain Index, OCRARisk of distal upper-limb disorders in repetitive hand work
OWAS, QEC, checklistsWorking postures; quick exposure check by observer and worker
Nordic Musculoskeletal QuestionnaireSymptoms in body regions in the past 12 months and 7 days

7Practice cases from the papers

  • S16, 30-year-old in the sand industry with breathlessness on effort for 6 months, mucoid sputum and patchy consolidation (functional diagnosis 5, PT 10): think silicosis and rule out tuberculosis; ICF with reduced exercise tolerance and job loss risk; PT and prevention from section 3.
  • S21, 40-year-old coal miner for 15 years with breathlessness and cough (task analysis 5, functional diagnosis 5, prevention 5). Task analysis: underground work in cramped space with stooping, kneeling and crawling; drilling (vibration, dust), shovelling and lifting; heat, humidity, poor light, noise; shift work; risk of roof falls, gas and fire. Diagnosis: coal workers' pneumoconiosis with chronic bronchitis. Prevention: water sprays and wet drilling, ventilation and dust extraction, respirators, periodic X-ray and spirometry, job rotation, smoking cessation, compliance with the Mines Act.
  • W17, 35-year-old clerk at a computer for 8 hours with thumb and index tingling and thenar wasting (prevention 5, ergonomics 5, orthosis 5): carpal tunnel syndrome, already with wasting, so refer for nerve conduction and a surgical opinion; education on breaks and wrist posture, nerve gliding; workstation (section 2 of desk work: keyboard at elbow height, neutral wrists, light touch, mouse close); orthosis: a resting or cock-up splint with the wrist in neutral, worn at night and during flares.

8Worked long case: an automobile worker with numb hands

From a real paper (S16, 5 + 7 + 3)

"A 45 year old male working in an automobile company (vibrations) without any precaution comes to the PT department with complains of tingling numbness & pain in bilateral hands. i) Discuss functional diagnosis with clinical reasoning. (5) ii) Give short term goals and management. (7) iii) Preventive gears and instruments. (3)"

Spot the clues before you write

vibrations without any precaution: hand-arm vibration syndrome. tingling, numbness and pain in both hands: sensorineural component, possible carpal tunnel syndrome; ask about white fingers in the cold. 45, automobile company: years of grinding or drilling; his job is at stake.

ICF componentFrom this caseClinical reasoning
Health conditionHand-arm vibration syndrome (sensorineural, possibly vascular) with possible bilateral carpal tunnel syndromeYears of unprotected exposure to vibrating tools
Body functions and structuresTingling and numbness of the fingers, hand pain, reduced touch and two-point discrimination, weak grip and pinch, possible finger blanching in cold, positive Phalen's and Tinel'sVibration damages digital nerves and vessels; tight grip adds median nerve load
Activity limitationsHolding tools steadily, fine tasks (buttons, screws), carrying, working in coldLoss of sensation and grip
Participation restrictionsFull work at the factory; risk of losing his job and incomeThe job itself causes the problem
Environmental factorsBarriers: vibrating tools, no protective equipment, no exposure limits, cold workshop. Facilitators: employer safety officer, ESI and compensation schemesPrevention must change the workplace
Personal factors45 years, earning member of the family, possibly a smokerNeeds to keep working safely
One-line functional diagnosis

A 45-year-old automobile worker with hand-arm vibration syndrome from unprotected tool use, with numbness, pain and weak grip in both hands limiting tool handling and threatening his job.

Short-term goals (4–6 weeks)

  • Reduce pain and tingling; no blanching attacks at work.
  • Improve grip and pinch strength and dexterity.
  • Daily vibration exposure reduced below the action value; protective measures in use.
  • Understands the condition, warm-hands routine and warning signs.

Management

ImpairmentTechnique and doseWhy
Ongoing exposureReduce or stop vibrating-tool work; job rotation; refer to the occupational physician for nerve conduction and vascular testsRemoving exposure is the main treatment
Tingling and numbnessMedian and ulnar nerve gliding, 5–10 repetitions, 3 times a day; night splint in neutral if carpal tunnel signsReduces nerve tension and night symptoms
PainWarm water soaks (not hot, sensation is poor), TENS as neededRelief while exposure is reduced
Weak grip and dexterityPutty and hand-gripper exercises, finger-thumb opposition, pegboard and coin tasks, 10–15 minutes twice a dayRestores hand function
Blanching in coldKeep the whole body and hands warm, gloves, avoid smoking; hand and arm exercises before work to warm upPrevents vasospasm
Sensory lossProtect hands from burns and cuts, check skin daily, sensory re-educationPrevents injury to numb fingers

Preventive gear and instruments (3 marks): low-vibration tools and anti-vibration handles; tool balancers and suspension; regular maintenance; warm, dry gloves; vibration measurement and exposure time limits; health surveillance (section 5).

3 mByssinosis. (S18)

Lung disease from cotton, flax or hemp dust in textile mills (carding and blowing rooms). Chest tightness and breathlessness on the first day back at work, improving through the week; later chronic airflow obstruction. Schilling grades ½, 1, 2, 3. Prevention: dust control, ventilation, respirators, periodic spirometry, job transfer; PT: breathing exercises, airway clearance, exercise training.

3 mEnumerate causative factors for cumulative trauma disorders. (W16)

Work: repetition, force, awkward or static posture, vibration, contact pressure, cold, insufficient rest, piece-rate pressure. Personal: age, female sex, pregnancy, diabetes, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis, obesity, previous injury, low fitness, smoking, stress.

7 mWrite a note on the influence of physical agents on industrial workers' health. (W20)

Heat (cramps, exhaustion, heat stroke), cold (frostbite, hypothermia), light (eye strain, accidents, miner's nystagmus), noise (hearing loss at 4 kHz, stress; 90 dBA for 8 hours), vibration (HAVS, back pain), ultraviolet (welder's flash, cataract, skin cancer), ionising radiation (cancer, sickness; 20 mSv a year limit). For each, one or two prevention measures following the hierarchy of controls.

LAQ40-year-old coal miner for 15 years with breathlessness and occasional cough for a year: task analysis, functional diagnosis, preventive measures. (5 + 5 + 5, S21)

Task analysis: underground environment, postures (stooping, kneeling), tools (drills: dust and vibration), lifting and shovelling, heat, noise, poor light, shifts, accident risks. Functional diagnosis (ICF): coal workers' pneumoconiosis with chronic bronchitis; reduced exercise tolerance, cough; limited heavy work and climbing; risk to employment; dusty environment, smoking. Prevention: elimination and engineering (wet drilling, sprays, ventilation, extraction), administrative (rotation, exposure limits, surveillance with X-ray and spirometry), PPE (respirators); smoking cessation; PT for breathing and fitness.
