4(a)Disability: impairment, disability and handicap, models, types, evaluation and levels of prevention

Disability was asked in 9 of 20 papers, mostly as 3-mark definitions and 7-mark "levels of disability prevention". Learn the ICIDH chain (disease, impairment, disability, handicap) with one example, and hang the three levels of prevention on it: primary stops the impairment, secondary stops it becoming a disability, tertiary stops the disability becoming a handicap.

My revision

1Impairment, disability and handicap (ICIDH)

Define impairment, disability and handicap (W16, W25); full form of "ICFDH", meaning ICIDH (W17); define disability (W20). All 3 marks.

  • ICIDH: International Classification of Impairments, Disabilities and Handicaps, published by WHO in 1980; replaced by the ICF in 2001.
  • Impairment: any loss or abnormality of psychological, physiological or anatomical structure or function (organ level).
  • Disability: any restriction or lack, resulting from an impairment, of ability to perform an activity in the manner or within the range considered normal for a human being (person level).
  • Handicap: a disadvantage for a given individual, resulting from an impairment or a disability, that limits or prevents the fulfilment of a role that is normal for that individual, depending on age, sex, and social and cultural factors (society level).

The ICF (2001)

  • Body functions and structures (impairments), activities (limitations), participation (restrictions), with environmental and personal factors; all interact both ways.
  • Disability in the ICF is the umbrella term for impairments, activity limitations and participation restrictions, arising from the interaction between a health condition and contextual factors.
  • Full explanation on the ICF page in Neuro.

  • Benchmark disability: 40% or more of a specified disability, certified by a medical authority; needed for most reservations and benefits.
  • Other ways to classify: congenital or acquired; temporary or permanent; visible or invisible; mild, moderate, severe, profound.

Primary and secondary disability (S16)

  • Primary disability: the loss of function caused directly by the disease or injury (paraplegia after spinal cord injury; hemiplegia after stroke).
  • Secondary disability (secondary conditions): further problems that develop later because of the primary disability, inactivity or poor care: contractures, pressure sores, obesity, osteoporosis, pain, urinary infections, depression, deconditioning. They are largely preventable, which is a major role of physiotherapy.

4Evaluation of permanent physical impairment of the upper extremity

S23, 7 marks (with types of disability).

  • Done when the condition has reached maximum improvement from treatment; based on measured function, not opinion.
  • The upper limb has two parts: the arm component and the hand component, each worth 90%.
ComponentWhat is measuredValues
Arm: range of motionShoulder, elbow and wrist, each joint 30%; mean percentage loss of range in each arc × 0.3, added across jointsUp to 90%
Arm: muscle strengthMRC grades 0–5 converted to percentage loss; mean loss around each joint × 0.3Up to 90%
Arm: coordinated activitiesTen set activities, 9% eachUp to 90%
Hand: prehensionOpposition 8% (2% for each finger), lateral pinch 5% (holding a key), cylindrical grasp 6%, spherical grasp 6% (large and small objects), hook grasp 5% (lifting a bag)30%
Hand: sensationComplete loss: thumb ray 9%, index 6%, middle, ring and little 5% each; partial loss in proportion30%
Hand: strengthGrip 20%, pinch 10% (dynamometer or clinical)30%
  • Combining formula: a + b(90 − a) ÷ 90, where a is the higher and b the lower value. Arm values (motion, strength, coordination) are combined this way; hand values (prehension, sensation, strength) are added; then arm and hand are combined by the formula. Example: arm 27% and hand 64% give 64 + 27 × 26 ÷ 90 = 71.8%.
  • Extra weightage of up to 10% for persistent pain, infection, deformity, malalignment, contracture, disfigurement, dominant limb (4%) or shortening (2% for each inch beyond the first); the total never exceeds 100%.

5Levels of disability prevention

Levels of disability prevention: 3 marks in W16, W22 and W25; 7 marks in W20 and S26.

LevelAimMeasures
Primary (first level)Prevent the impairment from occurringImmunisation (polio, measles, rubella), nutrition (iodine, vitamin A, folic acid, iron), antenatal care and safe delivery, avoiding consanguineous marriage and genetic counselling, road and workplace safety, helmets and seat belts, control of leprosy and diabetes, healthy lifestyle and exercise, health education
Secondary (second level)Stop an impairment becoming a disability by early detection and treatmentNewborn and developmental screening (RBSK), early treatment of infections and injuries, early physiotherapy, positioning and splinting, early surgery (clubfoot, cataract), hearing and vision screening, screening for diabetic foot
Tertiary (third level)Stop a disability becoming a handicapRehabilitation (medical, vocational, social), aids and appliances, prevention of secondary conditions, inclusive education, accessible buildings and transport, employment, legal rights and schemes, community awareness, CBR

These levels come from the WHO report on disability prevention and rehabilitation (1981) and match the general levels of prevention (see health and disease). The physiotherapist works at all three: health education and safety (primary), early detection and early therapy (secondary), and rehabilitation and inclusion (tertiary).

Test yourself

Answer on paper first, then open the answer.

3 mDefine impairment, disability and handicap. (W16, W25)

ICIDH (WHO, 1980). Impairment: any loss or abnormality of psychological, physiological or anatomical structure or function. Disability: any restriction or lack, resulting from an impairment, of ability to perform an activity in the normal manner or range. Handicap: a disadvantage, resulting from an impairment or disability, that limits or prevents fulfilment of a role normal for that individual. Example: polio, paralysed leg, cannot walk, cannot farm.

3 mNote on primary and secondary disability. (S16)

Primary: loss of function caused directly by the disease or injury (paraplegia after SCI). Secondary: later, preventable problems arising from the primary disability or inactivity (pressure sores, contractures, obesity, osteoporosis, UTI, depression). Physiotherapy prevents secondary disability by positioning, exercise, education and early mobility.

7 mWrite on levels of disability prevention. (W20, S26)

Chain and definitions; primary (prevent impairment: immunisation, nutrition, antenatal care, safety, genetic counselling, health education); secondary (prevent impairment becoming disability: screening, early treatment, early PT and splinting, early surgery); tertiary (prevent disability becoming handicap: rehabilitation, aids, education, accessibility, employment, rights, CBR); PT at each level.
