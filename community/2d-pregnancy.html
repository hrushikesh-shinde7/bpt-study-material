2(d, e)Pregnancy: physiological changes, discomforts, diastasis recti, exercise guidelines and antenatal classes

Pregnancy was asked in 13 of 20 papers, mostly as 7-mark questions: physiological changes (four times), diastasis recti (four times), exercise guidelines and contraindications, aims of obstetric physiotherapy, antenatal classes. Learn the changes system by system, because every discomfort, precaution and exercise rule follows from them.

Hyperemesis gravidarum (W19, 3 marks)

  • Severe nausea and vomiting of pregnancy with dehydration, weight loss and electrolyte imbalance, often needing hospital care; medical treatment is fluids and anti-emetics.
  • PT role: relaxation and slow breathing to ease nausea and anxiety; acupressure wristbands at the P6 point (some women find them helpful); gentle bed and chair exercises and ankle pumps to prevent deconditioning and clots while in bed; early walking as she improves; energy conservation; posture and positioning; emotional support.

3Diastasis recti

7 marks in W16, S22 and S24; 3 marks in S17; assessment for 3 marks in W24.

  • Definition: separation of the two rectus abdominis muscles along the linea alba, from stretching in pregnancy.
  • How common: in first-time mothers, 33% at 21 weeks of pregnancy, 60% at 6 weeks after birth, 45% at 6 months and 33% at 12 months (Sperstad et al., 2016).
  • Risk factors usually listed: multiple or large babies, many pregnancies, weak abdominals, obesity, heavy lifting; the study above found no clear risk factor.
  • Effects: abdominal doming or bulging, poor trunk support, possibly back and pelvic pain, cosmetic worry; rarely hernia.

Assessment

  1. Crook lying; she lifts her head and shoulders slowly until the shoulder blades just clear the bed.
  2. Place the fingers across the midline at the umbilicus, 4.5 cm above and 4.5 cm below; count finger-widths between the muscle edges (or measure with calipers or ultrasound).
  3. Note depth and tension of the linea alba (does it feel firm or do the fingers sink?) and any doming.
  4. Record pain, back and pelvic symptoms, pelvic floor function and function (lifting, getting up).

Management

  • In pregnancy: roll to the side to get up from lying; avoid straining and breath-holding; transversus abdominis and pelvic floor activation; good posture; avoid exercises that make the abdomen dome.
  • After birth: transversus drawing-in with pelvic floor, pelvic tilts, side-lying and four-point exercises; progress to head lifts and curl-ups only when she can keep the abdomen flat (pre-activate transversus first); later, loaded functional training.
  • Splinting the abdomen with crossed hands or a binder for comfort when coughing and getting up in the early days.
  • Correct lifting, constipation care, weight management; refer for surgical repair only if a large gap with hernia or major symptoms persists after a long rehabilitation period.

4Exercise in pregnancy

Guidelines for women exercising in pregnancy (W18, 7 marks); exercises contraindicated in pregnancy (S17, S22, 3 marks).

  • Benefits: less gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia and excessive weight gain, better mood and sleep, less back pain, better fitness for labour and recovery; physical activity is not associated with miscarriage or preterm birth (Canadian guideline, 2018).
  • Dose: at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity over at least 3 days, ideally daily, combining aerobic and resistance exercise (Canadian guideline; ACOG). Women active before pregnancy can continue their usual activity.
  • Intensity: moderate, by the talk test (can talk but not sing) or Borg 12–14, because heart rate is altered in pregnancy.
  • Good choices: walking, stationary cycling, swimming and water exercise, low-impact aerobics, prenatal yoga and Pilates (not hot), light resistance training, daily pelvic floor training.
  • Precautions: warm-up and cool-down; drink water; avoid heat and humidity; avoid lying flat for long after the first trimester (side-lying or inclined instead); avoid breath-holding and heavy straining; avoid end-range and bouncing stretches (lax ligaments); good footwear; eat enough.
ListContents (ACOG, 2020)
Activities to avoidContact sports (boxing, hockey, kabaddi); activities with a high risk of falling (horse riding, skiing, gymnastics, off-road cycling); scuba diving; sky diving; hot yoga or hot Pilates; unaccustomed exercise at high altitude

Exercises usually listed as contraindicated in Indian textbooks: double leg raises and full sit-ups (strain the abdominal wall), prolonged supine exercise after the first trimester, deep squats and lunges at the end of range with pelvic girdle pain, ballistic stretching, heavy lifting with breath-holding, jumping and high-impact work in late pregnancy, and any of the activities in the table.

5Obstetric physiotherapy and antenatal classes

Aims and principles of obstetric physiotherapy (W21, W24, 7 marks); early bird classes (W18, 3 marks); antenatal programme goals and class structure (S25, 7 marks).

Aims of obstetric physiotherapy

  • Help the woman adapt to the physical changes of pregnancy and prevent or treat discomforts.
  • Keep her fit and active safely; control weight gain.
  • Protect the back, pelvis, abdomen and pelvic floor.
  • Prepare her for labour: knowledge, positions, relaxation, breathing, less fear.
  • Help recovery after birth and the return to fitness (see labour and postnatal care).
  • Prevent long-term problems: incontinence, prolapse, back pain, diastasis.

Principles

  • Assess first and screen for contraindications and red flags; work with the obstetrician.
  • Moderate, regular exercise with precautions (section 4); progress with the trimester.
  • Education and self-management: posture, body mechanics, rest positions.
  • Include the partner and family; respect beliefs; group classes for support.
  • Individualise: high-risk pregnancies need tailored or reduced activity.

Early bird and antenatal classes

  • Early bird class (first trimester): changes of pregnancy, nutrition, posture and back care, safe exercise, pelvic floor, warning signs, discomforts of early pregnancy; builds healthy habits early.
  • Antenatal classes (usually from about 28 weeks, weekly for 6–8 sessions, groups of 8–12, partner invited to some):
    • Goals: knowledge of pregnancy, labour and the baby; fitness and posture; relief of discomforts; pelvic floor training; relaxation, breathing and positions for labour; postnatal recovery and baby care; confidence and less fear.
    • Structure of a session (about 90 minutes): welcome and questions (10 minutes); a talk on one topic (20 minutes); exercise: warm-up, low-impact aerobic, strengthening, pelvic floor, stretching (30 minutes); relaxation and breathing practice (20 minutes); summary and handout (10 minutes).
    • Topics across the course: body changes and posture; exercise and discomforts; pelvic floor; signs of labour and when to go to hospital; stages of labour and pain relief; labour positions and breathing (partner session); caesarean section; postnatal care, feeding and baby handling.

6Worked long case: a nurse in her third trimester

From a real paper (W17, 5 + 5 + 5)

"A 25 years, nurse, in third trimester complains of severe heel and knee pain on standing causing difficulty in walking. She has gained nearly 20 kg of extra weight. She wishes to continue with her job till her due date which is one month away. i) Discuss the reasoning for such impairments in this community. (5) ii) Explain the functional diagnosis. (5) iii) Discuss the physiotherapy management for her."

Spot the clues before you write

third trimester: most weight, most laxity, centre of gravity forward. 20 kg gain: above the 11.5–16 kg advised for a normal BMI. nurse: long standing and walking on hard floors, lifting patients. heel and knee pain on standing: overloaded feet and knees. work till due date: plan must keep her working safely.

Reasoning for the impairments (5 marks)

  • Extra weight multiplies the load on the heels and knees with every step.
  • Relaxin loosens the foot ligaments: the arches drop and the feet roll in, straining the plantar fascia (heel pain) and turning the knees in (knee pain).
  • The centre of gravity moves forward; she leans back and locks the knees, overloading the front of the knee.
  • Fluid retention and ankle swelling add pain and stiffness.
  • Nursing work: hours of standing and walking on hard floors, lifting and turning patients, little rest.

A 25-year-old nurse in her third trimester with excess weight gain, flattened arches and lax ligaments, causing heel and knee pain that limits standing and walking at work.

3 mEnumerate exercises contraindicated during pregnancy. (S17, S22)

Contact sports; activities with a high fall risk (riding, skiing, gymnastics); scuba and sky diving; hot yoga; prolonged supine exercise after the first trimester; double leg raises and full sit-ups; heavy lifting with breath-holding; ballistic and end-range stretching; high-impact jumping late in pregnancy. Mention that exercise is avoided altogether with absolute contraindications such as placenta praevia after 26 weeks or pre-eclampsia.

3 mEarly bird classes. (W18)

Antenatal education in the first trimester: changes of pregnancy, nutrition, posture and back care, safe exercise, pelvic floor training, early discomforts (nausea, fatigue), warning signs. Purpose: build healthy habits early, prevent problems, reduce anxiety; later antenatal classes prepare for labour.

7 mDiastasis recti and its management. (S24)

Definition; prevalence (33% at 21 weeks, 60% at 6 weeks postpartum, Sperstad 2016); effects; assessment at three levels during a head lift, finger-widths, depth and doming; management in pregnancy (roll to side, avoid straining, TrA and PFM) and after birth (TrA with PFM, pelvic tilts, progress curl-ups when flat, functional training), splinting, lifting and constipation advice; surgery rarely.

Five discomforts with cause and PT: low back pain, pelvic girdle pain, leg cramps, varicose veins and swelling, carpal tunnel syndrome (or breathlessness, heartburn, constipation, frequency), each with specific advice and exercise (section 2).
