ExamAnswer writing for the Community Physiotherapy paper

All 20 papers from 2016 to 2026 had the same shape: 80 marks in 3 hours, five of six 3-mark questions, five of six 7-mark questions, and two long answers of 15 marks, usually a patient in a community setting. The Community paper rewards answers that move from the person to their work, home and society, so every template here ends with prevention, ergonomics or health promotion.

1The paper and a time plan

The syllabus prints a scheme with 20 MCQs and 5-mark questions, but it was never used in 2016–2026. The real paper:

Read and chooseTick your choices; plan the two long answers5 min
Q1 short answersAny 5 of 6 × 3 = 15About 6 min each (30 min)
Q2 short answersAny 5 of 6 × 7 = 35About 14 min each (70 min)
Q3 and Q4 long answersAny 1 of 2 each × 15 = 30About 30 min each (60 min)
ReviseUnderline keywords; add doses, measurements and prevention15 min
  • About 2 minutes a mark.
  • The long answer gives its break-up (for example 5 + 5 + 5 or 3 + 5 + 7): use the same parts as your headings and give each part space in proportion to its marks.
  • Choose the long case whose setting you know best (worker, woman, elder), not the shortest one.

3Short answers

3 marks (about half a page)

  • A definition, then four to six points as a list or a small table; one example or number; a diagram only if it is quick (the ICIDH chain, the T-score scale).
  • "Enumerate" means a list; "define" needs the standard definition word for word where one exists (WHO health, ICIDH, rehabilitation, ergonomics, CBR).

7 marks (one and a half to two pages)

  1. Definition or introduction (one or two lines).
  2. The body: a table or numbered points, with headings that echo the question.
  3. A diagram if the topic has one.
  4. The physiotherapy role or prevention.
  5. One line of conclusion or evidence (for example "exercise reduces falls by 23%").

  • Always include the ICF (draw the six boxes), a one-line functional diagnosis, short- and long-term goals with numbers, treatment with doses, and a community element (ergonomics, home, schemes, prevention).
  • Theory long answers (for example "IBR vs CBR", "disaster management cycle", "pathomechanics of balance"): use the question's break-up as headings, a diagram, a table, and the PT role.

5Diagrams that earn marks

  • The ICF model filled for the case (every long case).
  • Computer workstation set-up; hierarchy of controls; worker care spectrum.
  • Pelvic floor sling; diastasis recti test; stages of labour.
  • Fun-to-failure steps; balance system; theories-of-ageing tree.
  • Rehabilitation team; CBR matrix; ICIDH chain with levels of prevention.
  • Three tiers of health care; disaster management cycle; START triage.
  • Apple and pear shapes; energy systems; circuit training.

Draw in pencil, label clearly, give a title, and refer to it in the text ("as shown in the diagram").

6Common mistakes

  • Writing a clinical answer with no community part: no job analysis, ergonomics, home, schemes or prevention.
  • "Strengthening exercises" without naming the exercise, dose and progression.
  • Ignoring the break-up and writing one long paragraph.
  • Missing measurements: BMI calculation, waist cut-offs, TUG, PERFECT, REBA scores.
  • Old or wrong facts: PWD Act 1995 is replaced by the RPWD Act 2016 (21 disabilities, 4% jobs); use Asian Indian BMI cut-offs; mention current schemes.
  • Forgetting red flags and precautions (pre-eclampsia, osteoporotic fracture, postural hypotension, hypoglycaemia).
  • Spending too long on 3-mark questions and rushing the long answers.

7The last week

  1. Days 1–2: the four Tier 1 occupational pages (work stress, hazards, ability and disability management); practise one worker long case in 30 minutes.
  2. Days 3–4: women's health (pregnancy, labour and postnatal, pelvic floor, menopause); practise one woman long case.
  3. Day 5: geriatrics (ageing, theories, evaluation, falls, elderly in India); practise one elderly long case.
  4. Day 6: health promotion (delivery system, fitness, obesity) and rehabilitation (disability, rehabilitation, CBR, policies, amputee, disaster).
  5. Day 7: redraw every diagram from memory; write the repeated 7-mark answers (IBR vs CBR, theories of ageing, levels of prevention, work conditioning vs hardening, FCE, communication barriers) in outline.
