My revision

1Energy conservation (S19, S22, W22; 3 marks W17, S25)

Memory hook

The 4 Ps: Plan, Pace, Prioritise, Position

PrincipleExamples
PlanSpread heavy jobs (washing clothes, cleaning, shopping) through the week; do them at the best time of day (often late morning, after airway clearance and medicines); gather everything before starting
PaceSlow, steady movement; rest before becoming very breathless; breathe out on the effort part ("blow as you go"): lifting, standing up, reaching
PrioritiseDrop or share tasks that are not essential; ask family to help with heavy work
Position and ergonomicsSit to bathe, dress, iron and cook; keep things used often between waist and shoulder height; avoid bending, lifting and working with the arms above the head; use a trolley or bag on wheels; long-handled sponge, shower stool, raised toilet seat
Breathing with activityPursed-lip breathing while walking; stairs one step per breath out, resting on landings
EnvironmentAvoid smoke, dust and extreme heat or cold; loose clothes; fan nearby

Ergonomic advice for one-sided lung disease (W22, left lung bronchiectasis with a normal right lung): rest and sleep lying on the right side: the healthy right lung is below, where it gets more blood and air, and the diseased left lung is on top, where it drains. Sit upright at work with the desk at elbow height; plan huffing breaks; avoid dust and smoke. Full answer on the bronchiectasis page.

2Activity pacing (3 marks in S20 and W21)

Pacing means doing activity in planned, manageable amounts with rests, so the patient stays below the level of breathlessness that forces a stop, and finishes the task without "boom and bust".

  • Break tasks into steps: make the bed in stages, sitting between them.
  • Alternate heavy and light tasks through the day.
  • Rest before exhaustion: "stop, rest, recover, restart" when breathlessness reaches a moderate level (Borg about 3–4).
  • Stairs: one step on each breath out, rest every few steps or at the landing.
  • Walking: interval walking (walk a set distance, rest, repeat) and slower speed with pursed-lip breathing.
  • Bathing and dressing: sit to wash and dress; rest between bathing and dressing; dressing gown instead of towelling off.

3Ergonomic advice: a worked example (W22)

From the bronchiectasis notes (6(c)(iv)), where this question was asked.

Ergonomic and energy-conservation advice for left lung bronchiectasis with a normal right lung (7 marks in W22)

  • Sleep and rest lying on the right side. This puts the diseased left lung uppermost, so it drains, and the healthy right lung lowermost, where it gets more blood flow and better gas exchange.
  • Clear the chest in the morning and before work or travel; plan a short huffing break during the day in private.
  • Sit upright at work with the desk at elbow height; avoid long slumped sitting, which squashes the lower chest.
  • Pace tasks, sit for tasks such as cooking and ironing, keep things within reach, use trolleys and avoid heavy lifting (as in COPD energy conservation).
  • Avoid dust, smoke and crowded places during infections; wear a mask.

4Home exercise programmes

A home programme turns treatment into daily habits. A good one has the same five parts for every condition:

  • What: a few exercises the patient can do alone (breathing, airway clearance, walking, strengthening), written down, with pictures if possible.
  • How much: frequency, intensity, time and type, with a simple self-check such as the talk test, the Borg score or SpO₂.
  • Safety: warning signs, when to stop and whom to call.
  • Context: fitted to the home and the job, which are the ICF contextual factors: stairs with no lift, Indian toilets, a hot kitchen, long standing or sitting at work, travel, and who at home can help.
  • Review: a diary or checklist, and a follow-up date.

Each condition's programme is at the end of its notes:

  • Heart and vessels: hypertension, IHD and CABG, heart failure, venous disease and foot care, peripheral arterial disease.
  • Lungs: COPD, asthma, ILD, TB, bronchiectasis, pleural disease.
  • Surgery and other conditions: lung surgery, abdominal surgery, mastectomy and arm care, burns, amputation and stump care, diabetes, obesity.
  • Long-term programmes: cardiac rehabilitation Phases III and IV, pulmonary rehabilitation.

5Monitoring adherence and contextual factors

  • How to monitor: exercise and symptom diaries, step counts on a phone or pedometer, attendance, checking inhaler and breathing technique at each visit, and asking open questions ("How many days did you walk last week?").
  • Common barriers: cost and travel, work hours, fear of breathlessness or chest pain, low mood, poor understanding, family beliefs ("rest is best"), other illnesses (arthritis, diabetes, memory problems).
  • Helps: goals the patient chose; a short written or picture programme in the patient's language; linking exercise to daily routines; family involvement; phone or WhatsApp reminders and video reviews; praise for small gains; reviewing and adjusting the plan.
