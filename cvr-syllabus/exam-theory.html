ExamAnswer writing for the theory paper

Every one of the 20 papers (2016–2026) had the same shape, and almost every long answer asks for ICF, a functional diagnosis or impairments, goals and management. Marks follow structure as much as knowledge. This page turns the notes into answers that score; ICF and goals themselves are in Chapter 5.

1The paper and a time plan

All 20 papers from 2016 to 2026 used this pattern (no MCQs, although the syllabus prints a different scheme): 80 marks in 3 hours, one answer book.

  • About 2 minutes a mark. Never spend 20 minutes on a 3-mark answer.
  • If you write slowly, do the two long answers first, so 30 marks are safe; never leave them for the last 20 minutes.
  • Choose the long-answer option whose condition you know best, not the shorter-looking question.

2Short answers: 3 and 7 marks

3 marks (about half a page)

  1. One-line definition.
  2. Three to five points, a small table, or a labelled sketch (numbers, values, names).
  3. One line of physiotherapy relevance if it fits.
Example: "Ankle–brachial index" (3 marks)

Definition: ratio of ankle to arm systolic pressure, measured with a Doppler. Formula: highest ankle ÷ highest brachial pressure, for each leg. Interpretation: 0.91–1.40 normal, 0.90 or less PAD, below 0.40 severe, above 1.40 non-compressible arteries. Use: diagnosis of PAD and a check before compression bandaging.

7 marks (1.5–2 pages)

  1. Definition or introduction (2 lines).
  2. Principle, classification or "why" (a flowchart is ideal).
  3. The main body as 6–8 headed points or a table: technique with dose, or management by phase.
  4. A labelled diagram where one exists (cycle, positions, curve, timeline).
  5. Precautions and contraindications.
  6. One concluding line (evidence or outcome measure).

Each technique page and condition page has an answer skeleton with marks for the questions actually asked.

3Long answers: the 15-mark case

  1. Read the split (for example 5 + 5 + 5 or 3 + 4 + 8) and give each part space in proportion.
  2. Underline the case clues: age, occupation, earning role, home (stairs, lives alone), day after surgery, drains, settings, test results, what the patient wants.
  3. Functional diagnosis in one sentence, then the ICF table with a "clinical reasoning" column (Chapter 5).
  4. Problem list in order of priority.
  5. Goals: short-term and long-term, SMART, with time frames (Chapter 5).
  6. Management: by phase or by impairment, with technique, dose and reason; precautions and stop criteria; education and home programme.
  7. Outcome measures and discharge or progression criteria in one or two lines.

Write doses with units (repetitions, sets, times a day, minutes, RPE, % HRR); a plan without doses loses marks.

4Presentation that earns marks

  • Headings, numbered points and tables; one idea per line.
  • Underline the keywords listed in each exam corner (for example ICF, FITT, RPE, sternal precautions).
  • Labelled diagrams: ICF model, ACBT cycle, drainage positions, CR phases timeline, rule of nines, spirogram.
  • Always include precautions and stop criteria in management answers.
  • Keep pathology short unless it is asked; spend the space on physiotherapy.
  • Leave a few lines after each answer for additions during revision.

Checklist before you hand in

  • Every part of every split answered?
  • Doses with units? Time frames on goals?
  • Precautions written? Diagram labelled?
  • Question numbers written correctly in the margin?
