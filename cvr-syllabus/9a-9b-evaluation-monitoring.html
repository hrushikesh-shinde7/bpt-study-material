9(a)Basic evaluation

ICU assessment

Write this first in any ICU answer. It shows the examiner you check before you treat.

From the chart and the monitors

  • Diagnosis and course: reason for admission, surgery, days on the ventilator, trend (better or worse since yesterday).
  • Ventilator: mode, FiO₂, PEEP, rate, tidal volume, pressures (see the ventilation page).
  • Vital signs: heart rate and rhythm, BP and mean arterial pressure (MAP), SpO₂, respiratory rate, temperature; intracranial pressure if monitored.
  • ABG and chest X-ray: oxygenation, CO₂ and pH; collapse, consolidation, effusion, pneumothorax, tube position.
  • Drugs: sedation, pain relief, drugs supporting BP, paralysing agents.
  • Blood tests: haemoglobin, platelets and clotting (bleeding risk), lactate, electrolytes, sugar.
  • Lines and drains: what is attached and where (femoral lines limit hip bending).

At the bedside

  • Conscious level: Glasgow Coma Scale; sedation score (Richmond Agitation–Sedation Scale, RASS, −5 to +4); delirium screen (CAM-ICU).
  • Chest: breathing pattern, synchrony with the ventilator, chest movement, auscultation, secretions (amount, colour, thickness), cough on suction.
  • Limbs: range of motion, tone, early contractures, skin and pressure areas, oedema.
  • Strength and function (when awake): MRC sum score of 12 muscle groups (0–60; below 48 = ICU-acquired weakness); bed mobility, sitting balance; ICU Mobility Scale (0 = lying in bed, 10 = walking independently).
  • Pain: self-report if possible, or a behavioural pain scale.

Safety before mobilising a ventilated patient

An international consensus (Hodgson, 2014) uses traffic lights: green = low risk, go ahead; yellow = possible risk, discuss with the team; red = high risk, do not mobilise actively without senior agreement.

NeurologicalDrowsy, calm or restless (RASS −1 to +1); no delirium, or delirium but follows simple commandsVery agitated or combative (RASS above +2); deeply sedated (RASS below −2); raised ICP being actively treated; uncleared spine; uncontrolled seizures
OtherICU-acquired weakness; dialysis lines; femoral venous and arterial lines; drains, catheters, nasogastric tubeUncontrolled active bleeding; unstable pelvic, spinal or long-bone fracture; large open chest or abdominal wound (surgeon decides); femoral sheaths

If a patient is near the limit in several areas at once (for example FiO₂ 0.6, PEEP 10 and SpO₂ 90%, like the W24 case), decide with an experienced ICU doctor before mobilising.

Stop the session if
  • SpO₂ falls below about 88–90% and does not recover with rest
  • Heart rate rises or falls sharply, a new arrhythmia appears, or MAP falls below target
  • Respiratory rate stays above about 35, the patient fights the ventilator, or looks distressed
  • The tube or a line is displaced, or the patient falls

Test yourself

3 mList five safety criteria that make a ventilated patient "green" for out-of-bed mobilisation.

FiO₂ 0.6 or less; SpO₂ 90% or more; respiratory rate 30 or less; PEEP 10 cmH₂O or less; MAP above the lower target with no or low-dose support; calm, drowsy or restless (RASS −1 to +1); no active bleeding or unstable fracture.
